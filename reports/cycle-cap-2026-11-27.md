# Proving-cycle cap (plan M5.11)

Measured on 2026-11-27 at `221ffb9` (rules version 2), with the release `zk-host` of RISC Zero 3.1.0, verify image id `0xc9634ada0eab5e9a7bb0a9ddce14df5634f7dddd2caac79416ae3cd8eda915a8`, `RISC0_DEV_MODE` unset, executor only (no proof). Cycles are the verify program's: user cycles, and proving cycles, each segment padded to its power of two at 2^20, which set the price (`docs/VERIFICATION.md`). The inputs are in [`cycle-cap-2026-11-27/`](cycle-cap-2026-11-27/): game vectors that `zk-host execute` checks against the native core, as `vectors/` are. Wall time and price use M5.9's model: 7.3 s + 2.15 s per segment on a rented RTX 4090, 0.89 USD an hour, a session's set-up and build 2,099 s.

## Summary

- **The worst case is about 590 million proving cycles per level, and 3.51 billion per run, not 2.9 billion.** Every one of the 16 levels can be driven to 481 to 591 million proving cycles by one-cell captures, one every two ticks, for the whole 2,700 ticks. The worst legal run measured, six different levels, takes 3,510,632,448 proving cycles, 3,348 segments: about 2.0 h and 1.78 USD to prove, against the 0.085 USD fee. The analytical bound of the current core is 4.04 billion for a run.
- **Recommended mechanism: a work budget in the core (rules version 3).** The core counts the work of each level in cells, as its grid-wide procedures spend it; the level ends, claim kept, once the count reaches 300,000. A level set bound alone (no rules change) holds but caps a run at about 4 billion cycles, and not settling over-cap runs is ruled out.
- **The value:** a budget of 300,000 per level, which caps a run at **370 million proving cycles** (bound; 324 million measured). No run of the reference playtest's 80,000 bot levels, on the season's levels or on the paid copies, reaches it; the heaviest uses 235,599 (79 %).
- **Testnet-1's grace:** 24 h. With every scoring run settled (owner, 2026-11-27: the v1 payout) and settlement daily during the season, the queue at the end is about one day of runs; 24 h holds 55 runs at the cap and about 400 typical bot runs on one card, margin 2. 12 h holds 26 and about 190.

## 1. What drives the cycles

From the core (`core/src`):

- **A capture** floods the boss's whole region (R4.8-2, R4.8-3), then passes over all 3,600 cells (R4.8-4), whatever it captures. A one-cell capture next to a large field therefore costs as much as a large one.
- **Captures come at most every other tick.** A capture closes a cut, so the player must be cutting at the start of the tick (R4.5-5, `Player::step`); entering the field ends that tick's movement, and a capture leaves the player not cutting. Tick 0 cannot capture either. So a level of T ticks has at most ⌈(T − 1) / 2⌉ captures: 1,350 at 2,700 ticks. Each claims at least one cell, so the region filled at the i-th capture holds at most N0 − i cells, N0 being the level's open cells at start.
- **A minion respawn** whose spawn cell is no longer a rail cell searches the grid in row-major order for the first rail cell (R4.7-7): up to 3,600 rail tests. A minion is destroyed at most once per R ticks, so a level has at most minions × ⌈T / R⌉ such searches.
- **Everything else is per tick and small:** the player's two sub-steps, the boss's edges, three minions' right-hand steps, the hit predicate, one journal event. Standing still for 2,700 ticks on 01 costs 1.9 million cycles in all.

A linear model of user cycles, fitted on 53 executed inputs (the 32 gate-set vectors, the 16 inputs below and five runs on 01 stopped after 0 to 1,200 captures), reproduces the capture-heavy inputs within 0.2 % and the gate set within 5 %:

| Term | User cycles |
|---|---:|
| Per level | about 0.70 million |
| Per tick, by level | 321 (01) to 1,673 (16) |
| Per capture (the 3,600-cell pass, the minions' orientation) | 22,930 |
| Per cell of the boss's region filled | 151.94 |
| Per journal event | 679 |
| Per cell tested by a respawn's search, measured on a level built for it (three searches of 1,681 cells: 507,199 cycles) | about 101 |

Proving cycles are 1.042 to 1.046 times user cycles at these sizes.

## 2. The worst case per level

**The inputs.** A generator, kept out of the repository as the brief asks, plays each level through the core's Recorder: from a rail cell it steps into one open cell and back out onto a claimed cell, capturing that one cell, and it prefers the step out that lands next to another such cell, searching five captures ahead, so that it walks the staircase of a growing corner (right, down, right, down, then up, left on the way back) at nearly two ticks a capture. It checks each move with the core against the boss and the minions over the next six ticks, never commits, never clears, and loses no life. Six run nonces per level, the heaviest kept; the nonce is in each input. The gate set's `NN-worst` and `NN-synthetic` (`vectors/levels/`) make 40 to 46 captures and take 5 to 31 million cycles: they are bot journals, not worst cases.

**The bound** is the model at its maxima: 1,350 captures each filling N0 − i cells, 2,700 ticks at 3,000 user cycles (about twice the heaviest level's fitted 1,673) plus an event each, every possible respawn searching all 3,600 cells, times 1.05 for proving. It is an engineering bound from measured unit costs, not a proof over the code. The searches cannot all be long while the field stays large, so the bound is loose by up to 65 million on the levels with three minions; without them, every level measures at 82 % to 96 % of it.

## 3. The worst legal run

A run draws six different levels of the 16 (README §8.2), so the worst run plays the six costliest. `zk-host execute-run` on the measured six heaviest, `01`, `03`, `07`, `04`, `14` and `02`: **3,367,882,243 user cycles, 3,510,632,448 proving cycles, 3,348 segments**, about 7,206 s (2.0 h) and 1.78 USD on the card. The six highest bounds sum to 4.04 billion. M5.9's 2.9 billion, "16 times the largest input", was 17 % low.

## 4. Rules version 3 and the paid copies

- **The fast cut always on (M5.12)** does not move the worst case, provided rules version 3 keeps R4.5-6's trigger, cutting *at the start of the tick*. A one-cell capture already takes one tick to enter and one to close, and the second sub-step never runs after a close (PIN-04). If instead a player who enters the field could take its second sub-step in the same tick, one tick could enter and close, 2,699 captures would fit in a level, and the worst case would about double. The fast cut also shortens the time a long trail is exposed, which helps humans and bots, not the cost-maximiser. Under the work budget below the cadence no longer matters: the cap holds either way.
- **The paid copies** (owner, 2026-11-27: the 16 maps with 3 more lives and a 5,000 bp target, 2,700 ticks) do not move it either. The generator's inputs lose no life and claim 36 % to 48 % of the field, under 5,000 bp, so on the copies they play out the same: on copies of the 16 levels built here by changing only lives and target, the generator finds the same inputs, and the six heaviest take the same 3,510,632,448 proving cycles in run mode. More lives keep a careless player's level alive longer, but never past 2,700 ticks; the lower target ends more levels earlier, by a clear. Under the budget, an input that crosses 5,000 bp clears sooner and costs less.

## 5. The mechanism

| | How | Rules change | Bound per run | For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Work budget | The core counts work; a level ends once it reaches the budget | Yes, in M5.12 | 370M at 300,000 | Tight, level-independent, deterministic; the client shows it |
| (b) Level-set bound at season open | Every level's worst case times six under the cap | No | 4.04 billion | Valid, but every level is 610 to 690 million: only a cap of about 4 billion, or levels of about 300 ticks, pass |
| (c) Not settling runs above the cap | | No | | Ruled out: strands honest runs |
| (d) A cheaper capture | Fill the cut-off side instead of the boss's (same grid, no rules change) | No | Not provable | Cuts typical runs' cost, but a trail whose two sides meet only far around the field (a ring) still costs a whole region, so it needs (a) anyway |

**Recommended: (a), counted in cells as the grid procedures spend them.** Captures and respawn searches are the only work that grows without a cap other than time, so the count covers both:

- each capture counts the cells its fill marks `BOSS_ZONE` (the boss's region, its body included) plus 150, the share of the 3,600-cell pass (22,930 cycles against 152 per filled cell);
- each minion respawn counts the cells it tests for the rail: its spawn cell, then, if that is not a rail cell, each cell of the row-major search up to the one it takes.

At about 152 user cycles a unit for any capture, whatever its size, and 101 for a search, the count tracks cycles within 1 % on captures, so a budget is a cycle budget, and the cost-maximiser cannot do better per unit than an ordinary player. Other proxies were weighed: a capture count lets a one-cell capture next to a large field cost 20 times a small-region capture, and leaves the respawn searches (up to 65 million cycles a level) uncounted; cells claimed do not track cost at all, since the costliest captures claim one cell.

**Per level, bounded:** 0.70 million + 2,700 ticks × (3,000 + 679) + 153 × (budget + 14,300), times 1.05, where 14,300 is the most one tick can add past the budget (one capture of 3,514 and three searches of 3,601). At 300,000: 61.6 million a level, **370 million a run**, on any valid level, so no season-open check is needed. Measured: the generator, paced to spend the budget over the level, reaches 51.5 to 54.5 million a level (88 % of the bound), and 324,009,984 proving cycles for its six heaviest (`11`, `15`, `16`, `10`, `12`, `14`), 309 segments.

A level that does end there keeps its claim, as at the time limit.

## 7. The value and the grace

Every scoring run is settled (owner, 2026-11-27: the v1 payout), so the grace is sized in runs. Runs one card proves in the grace, margin 2, a session's set-up and build included, as `gpu_bench.py` counts them; the typical runs are the model's (§1) over the playtest's runs, six different levels drawn at random, scaled by 1.06 (four executed bot runs, greedy and bold, came out 3 % to 7 % above the model: 84.9, 59.8, 58.2 and 49.3 million).

- **Settle daily during the season.** The queue at the end is then the runs committed since the last session, about one day of runs.
- **24 h recommended:** it holds 55 runs at the cap, or about 400 typical runs, from the last day; 12 h holds 26 and about 190. 48 h doubles that, and the close and claims wait two days.
- **A flood of cap-size runs** is the residual: on testnet a run's fee is faucet ETH, and one card proves 110 cap-size runs a day. Above that the operator rents a second card, and the budget keeps each run at 12.8 min.

## 9. Reproduce

```bash
cargo build --release --locked --manifest-path referee/zk/Cargo.toml -p zk-host   # at the fixed path
referee/zk/target/release/zk-host execute reports/cycle-cap-2026-11-27/
referee/zk/target/release/zk-host execute-run reports/cycle-cap-2026-11-27/{01,03,07,04,14,02}-max-captures.json
referee/zk/target/release/zk-host execute-run reports/cycle-cap-2026-11-27/{11,15,16,10,12,14}-budget-300k.json
```

The inputs carry no trace file, only `traceKeccak`, which `zk-host` checks. The `budget-300k` inputs emulate the proposed rule on rules version 2: they stop capturing once their work reaches 300,000 and commit on the next tick, a tick later than rule R4.11-4 would end them.

## Not verified

- The bound rests on the fitted unit costs and a per-tick allowance read from the code, not on counting every instruction path.
- The respawn search's cost per cell comes from one purpose-built level (three searches), and no input combines long searches with a large field.
- The search bot's runs were not replayed with the work count.
- The paid copies' worst case was measured on copies built here (lives + 3, target 5,000 bp), not on the copies the plan will ship.
