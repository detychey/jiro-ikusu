# wedgie Training app: timing and RAM (plan M3.9)

The Training app of `wedgie/app/`, with `jiro_core.mpy` of the same commit (18,556 bytes, sha256 `c8f95ab9a7e8f08544de8b659a3bef0ae07f999cfe1c856400f96b37aa36bc66`; round E1's builds had 18,180 bytes, sha256 `092c5a7f91494e2b3f0287cd43da2bc367de848a4ae25e4c8458607674b9468b`). Tools: MicroPython v1.29.0 (commit 0fd6c573) and its qemu port for the MICROBIT board (Cortex-M0, 236 KB heap), qemu-system-arm 8.2.2, arm-none-eabi-gcc 13.2.1, rustc 1.98.0. The wedgie's own numbers are round E1's, below.

## qemu

`python3 -I wedgie/scripts/qemu_app_check.py <firmware.elf> wedgie/app 137760`: the app's `run()`, with the heap filled down to the 137,760 bytes a wedgie reports free with no app, 9,728 of them in 32 holes of 64 to 512 bytes, every block over 1 KB filled once the app has started, and the heap locked over each tick's sample, step and drawing, plays level 1 with scripted inputs (88 ticks, two captures, then Commit), whose best's store fails with the flash full, then level 2 with the same inputs, with a `time` stub whose `ticks_us` wraps 2³⁰ early in each level and jumps 600 s ahead on tick 70. PASS: the journals are the scripted keys' mask changes, native replay gives the app's end (Committed), claim (677 bp), ticks (88) and score (1,049 on level 1, 1,055 on level 2), no tick allocated, and level 2's store saved both bests.

The worst step is a capture tick: the boss-zone fill runs over the open cells, whatever the capture's size. qemu's `show_rect` pushes nothing, so a wedgie adds the push: about 45 µs a row (wedgie.dev/code.md), 4.7 to 6.1 ms for the rows above. Expected on a wedgie: a worst tick of about 15 to 30 ms against the 66.7 ms tick and the 50 ms of round E1, and no overrun in play. The times are with the heap locked over the tick, so every Python frame is on the C stack; a wedgie puts a frame of more than 11 words on the heap, the draw's and those of its own `lcd` module's Python (`show_rect`, `Keys.pressed`). ## wedgie (round E1)

**First try** (wedgie RP2040 at 125 MHz, firmware 0.3.76, MicroPython 1.29.0): "it works but it's buggy". Level 1 froze in play, about 2 minutes after plugging in, while the owner drew a line to finish a capture: the picture stopped, no button did anything. No log. The white player dot was hard to see on the red trail.

- **The freeze was not reproduced.** On qemu, about 50,000 random and adversarial play ticks over 117 levels, 302 captures up to 84 % of the field, C-stack and 64 KB-stack canaries, framebuffer guards and every journal against native replay found no hard fault, hang or exception (`step` 344 bytes, the renderer's exports at most 2.7 KB of C stack with the app's depth, `show_rect` always on the screen).
- **What did hang on qemu,** and is fixed: the tick wait read a due time more than 2²⁹ µs late as up to 9 minutes ahead (`ticks_diff`'s range) and slept that long, with the picture still and the buttons dead. It needs ticks far slower than a wedgie's, so it is not likely E1's freeze; a due time read more than one tick ahead now runs at once, and the due time is a running one, so `ticks_add`'s delta stays small (the absolute one would have overflowed at tick 8,054, past any level's 2,700). The play test's clock jump covers it: the first build hung there.
- **Also changed:** the summary screen is drawn before the USB output, which can block 500 ms a print when a host holds the port open unread, so a level that ends on a capture shows its summary at once instead of seconds later; presses made in the level's last moments and the summary's first 0.5 s no longer skip it; the play loop binds the screen's methods once instead of allocating them every tick; the player is drawn larger, in a black ring.
- **For the next try,** if it freezes again: whether the claim was near the level's target (a clear), whether the screen changed after 30 s, USB power or battery, and the output of `python3 wedgie.py debug` afterwards.

**Second try** (round E1's build, e6198d3): after install the level select showed level 1 of 16; green showed "Jiro training / memory allocation failed, allocating 1148 bytes". The first build had started and played level 1 on the same wedgie.

- **The allocation** was the level file's: the app opened `jiro_levels.bin` at every level start, and a file on the wedgie's littlefs is a 1,148-byte object, 124 bytes and a 1,024-byte cache (MicroPython 1.29.0's rp2 port mounts it with `progsize=256`; `extmod/vfs_lfsx_file.c`). On qemu the e6198d3 build's largest free block after start was 747 bytes (the first build's 3,279), and the wedgie's heap is more fragmented than the old play test's ballast, which left the free bytes in a few blocks and served the files from objects of a few bytes.
- **Fixed:** `jiro.py` allocates the level buffer, reads the bests and keeps the level file open from the start, so a level start seeks and reads into the buffer; nothing in the play path allocates more than a short string. `save.store`, which opens a file too, gets the core's stack's block, idle between levels, which the app frees for it and takes back. The level select shows, and USB prints, the free heap and largest free block at each stage (`run`, `buf`, `imp`, `lcd`, `app`, `sel`, `lvl`).
- **The play test** now models it: a 1,148-byte file object per `open()`, small holes in the free bytes, every block over 1 KB filled once the app has started, and two levels in a row. The e6198d3 build fails it at its first level start with the wedgie's error; this build passes. An ad-hoc run with the heap locked over each tick's step and drawing, the player's ring included, passed: a tick allocates nothing on the heap.
