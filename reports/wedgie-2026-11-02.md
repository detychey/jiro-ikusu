# wedgie Training app: timing and RAM (plan M3.9)

The Training app of `wedgie/app/`, with `jiro_core.mpy` of the same commit (18,180 bytes, sha256 `092c5a7f91494e2b3f0287cd43da2bc367de848a4ae25e4c8458607674b9468b`). Tools: MicroPython v1.29.0 (commit 0fd6c573) and its qemu port for the MICROBIT board (Cortex-M0, 236 KB heap), qemu-system-arm 8.2.2, arm-none-eabi-gcc 13.2.1, rustc 1.98.0. The wedgie's own numbers are round E1's, below.

## qemu

`python3 -I wedgie/scripts/qemu_app_check.py <firmware.elf> wedgie/app 137760`: the app's `run()`, with the heap filled down to the 137,760 bytes a wedgie reports free with no app, plays level 1 with scripted inputs (88 ticks, two captures, then Commit), with a `time` stub whose `ticks_us` wraps 2³⁰ early in play and jumps 600 s ahead on tick 70. PASS: the journal is the scripted keys' mask changes, and native replay gives the app's end (Committed), claim (677 bp), ticks (88) and score (1,049).

The margin is about 10 KB (the first build's, with a smaller test harness, failed below 129,000: the ballast leaves holes it cannot fill, so the margin moves by a few kilobytes with what the test uploads before it). Below it, compiling `jiro.py` at import leaves its code in the heap's large block, and no 64 KB or 18 KB piece remains: an exception, not a freeze. M3.10 is not needed.

**Time.** The app measures with `time.ticks_us()`. Under `-icount shift=3` one microsecond of the qemu port is 195.3 instructions (`wedgie/README.md`, qemu's clock); a wedgie runs 125 instructions per microsecond at one instruction per cycle, more cycles per instruction on loads, taken branches and flash cache misses.

The worst step is a capture tick: the boss-zone fill runs over the open cells, whatever the capture's size. qemu's `show_rect` pushes nothing, so a wedgie adds the push: about 45 µs a row (wedgie.dev/code.md), 4.7 to 6.1 ms for the rows above. Expected on a wedgie: a worst tick of about 15 to 30 ms against the 66.7 ms tick and the 50 ms of round E1, and no overrun in play. Levels 10, 15 and 16 were measured before round E1's player ring, which adds two Python calls and a 3 × 3-cell restore to the draw. All 16 levels started, passed the level-id check, played and scored on qemu with the same inputs (an ad-hoc run of the play test's script with another level picked), every one within 1.45 M instructions a tick, 136 rows pushed and no overrun.

## wedgie (round E1)

**First try** (wedgie RP2040 at 125 MHz, firmware 0.3.76, MicroPython 1.29.0): "it works but it's buggy". Level 1 froze in play, about 2 minutes after plugging in, while the owner drew a line to finish a capture: the picture stopped, no button did anything. No log. The white player dot was hard to see on the red trail.

- **The freeze was not reproduced.** On qemu, about 50,000 random and adversarial play ticks over 117 levels, 302 captures up to 84 % of the field, C-stack and 64 KB-stack canaries, framebuffer guards and every journal against native replay found no hard fault, hang or exception (`step` 344 bytes, the renderer's exports at most 2.7 KB of C stack with the app's depth, `show_rect` always on the screen).
- **What did hang on qemu,** and is fixed: the tick wait read a due time more than 2²⁹ µs late as up to 9 minutes ahead (`ticks_diff`'s range) and slept that long, with the picture still and the buttons dead. It needs ticks far slower than a wedgie's, so it is not likely E1's freeze; a due time read more than one tick ahead now runs at once, and the due time is a running one, so `ticks_add`'s delta stays small (the absolute one would have overflowed at tick 8,054, past any level's 2,700). The play test's clock jump covers it: the first build hung there.
- **Also changed:** the summary screen is drawn before the USB output, which can block 500 ms a print when a host holds the port open unread, so a level that ends on a capture shows its summary at once instead of seconds later; presses made in the level's last moments and the summary's first 0.5 s no longer skip it; the play loop binds the screen's methods once instead of allocating them every tick; the player is drawn larger, in a black ring.
- **For the next try,** if it freezes again: whether the claim was near the level's target (a clear), whether the screen changed after 30 s, USB power or battery, and the output of `python3 wedgie.py debug` afterwards.
