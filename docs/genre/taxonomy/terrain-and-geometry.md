# Mechanics: terrain and geometry

The 44 mechanic families of the axis *terrain and geometry*: field shape and terrain: obstacles, pre-claimed islands, walls, hazards, teleporters, non-rectangular or hex fields, scrolling, 3D surfaces, a field that changes during play. The [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) summarizes the axis and lists the 12 axes. **Games** counts the catalog games that show a family, as the merging agent counted them.

## Interior obstacles and walls

Solid blocks, rocks, pillars or wall segments sit inside the open field. They stop the player's trail, and usually the enemies too, so cuts must be routed around them. In most games they do not count as claimed edge, so a trail cannot be closed on them. Variants differ in how the obstacles appear (fixed, a preset layout per level, random per run, popping in and out), in what else they block (enemies, shots, line of sight) and in whether they can be broken.

## Non-rectangular field outlines

The playfield boundary is a shape other than the default rectangle: a polygon, a curve, a ring around a central object, a maze-like layout, a ship or a symbol. Capture percentages are computed on that shape, and its concavities change where cuts pay off and how loops close. The shape can be fixed per level, change for boss stages, or be chosen as an arena in multiplayer.

Games: 18 · lineages: qix, volfied, jezzball, io-territory

Examples:

- **Water Bugs** (2007): Each level has a different field shape.
- **Patchwork Heroes** (2010): The field is an enemy warship.
- **iSlash** (2010): You slash an arbitrary shape down.
- **Curvebot Evolved** (2011): Later planes have awkward shapes.
- **Fortix 2** (2011): Maze-like level layouts.
- **Qix Galaxy: Space Adventure** (2014): Area is fenced off around a planet.
- **Paper.io 2** (2018): Steam version has 45 differently shaped custom maps.
- **Q\*BALL** (2025): A Q\*bert-style isometric pyramid of cubes.
- **Volfi: Cyber Territory Arcade** (2026): Octagon, Diamond, Cross, Star and Citadel arenas, with a 75%-85% clear target per arena.
- **Territory Arrow** (2026): Squares, locks, stars and winding curves.
- **Line Fight** (unreleased (Steam coming soon)): 19 shaped maps (star, heart, gear, crescent) whose outlines change how claiming works.
- **Paper Loop 2** (2026): Circular arena.

Variants:

- The field is an enemy warship to be cut apart (Patchwork Heroes)
- Later floating planes have awkward shapes (Curvebot Evolved)
- Maze-like level layouts (Fortix 2)
- Territory is fenced off in a ring around a planet (Qix Galaxy: Space Adventure)

- A circular arena (Paper Loop 2)

## Lethal static terrain and traps

Fixed features of the field that are not enemies kill the player on contact: hazard tiles such as lava, placed traps, lethal objects with a danger radius, or a hazard specific to one scene. They do not chase the player. They turn parts of the field into no-go cells that cuts must route around, and some also act as walls. They can be fixed, escalate per level, or accumulate in random cells.

- **Mokoko X** (2022): Traps, towers, crates and bonus effects on the field.

- **Enveloping Darkness** (2025): Getting too close to the central star destroys you; planets are harmless.
- **Kawaii Girls Panic** (2025): Deadly traps alongside enemies.
- **Territory Arrow** (2026): Pink gears end the run on contact.
- **Pirate Xonix** (unknown): Gunpowder barrels are hazards to avoid.

The capture board is shown in 3D or isometric projection through a camera, with the player as a craft, ball or character over it. The capture logic usually stays planar. What changes is how the player reads distances and lines, and sometimes how much of the field is visible at once. This family also holds games described only as '3D' territory capture.

## Pre-claimed starting territory

Play begins with part of the field already claimed, not just the outer border: a filled floor, pre-built sections, or a small home patch inside the field. When the start is an interior patch, the player draws outward from it and closes cuts by returning to claimed area, and the outer border cannot be used to close a cut. In multiplayer, placement rules for each player's patch (corners, minimum spacing, random spawn, shape, size cap) set up the opening.

Games: 11 · lineages: qix, gals-panic, io-territory

Examples:

- **Doodlebug** (1984): Rectangles are drawn up from the already-filled bottom of the screen.
- **Mamba 2** (1992): Starting level 1 has pre-built web sections; starting level 10 has no web.
- **Gals Panic II** (1993): Claiming starts from a rectangle inside the field and cannot connect to the outer border.
- **Vivid Dolls** (1998): Start from a small claimed square inside a blurred image and draw outward.
- **Gal Pani X** (2002 (demo 2000)): Each stage starts in a randomly sized claimed rectangle at the centre.
- **Conquerors** (c. 2019): Two bugs start from small corner bases.
- **SNAPIXEL: Infinite Universe** (2025): Start from a base and expand a safe territory while protecting the player marker.
- **Pauper.Io!** (2026): Each starting territory is at most 3x3 character cells.
- **looprush** (2026): Heart-shaped start at a random spawn kept a minimum distance from other players.
- **Paper IO Game** (c. 2026): Start from a small base.
- **bloom (PlayWeave)** (unknown): Leave a home patch.

Variants:

- Rectangles are drawn up from the already-filled bottom of the screen (Doodlebug)
- Pre-built web sections that depend on the starting level: level 1 has them, level 10 has none (Mamba 2)
- A small claimed square inside a blurred image; captures close by returning to it (Vivid Dolls)
- Claiming starts from a rectangle inside the field and cannot connect to the outer border, a rule kept by later entries (Gals Panic II)
- Each stage starts in a randomly sized claimed rectangle at the centre (Gal Pani X)
- Start from a base and draw lines to expand a safe territory while protecting the player marker (SNAPIXEL: Infinite Universe)
- Two players start from small corner bases (Conquerors)
- Each starting territory is at most 3x3 character cells (Pauper.Io!)
- Heart-shaped start at a random spawn kept a minimum distance from other players (looprush)
- Leave a small base or home patch and return to it (Paper IO Game, bloom (PlayWeave))

## Movement-altering terrain

Terrain cells or patches change how fast or how controllably whatever crosses them moves: slowing, dragging, trapping, sliding without being able to stop, speeding up, or applying a timed status effect. The effect can hit the player only, so some cuts take longer to draw while enemies keep their speed.

Games: 7 · lineages: qix, xonix, volfied, io-territory

Examples:

- **Fortix** (2009): Rivers, mountains and cliffs slow movement and drawing; crossing a fort wall is very slow.
- **Lightfish** (2011): Ice blocks (late game) slow the player but not the enemies.
- **Lightspace** (2026): Ice, mirrors, ion storms, fire and teleporters.
- **GraviNix: Tilt Xonix Arcade** (2026): Rolls on grass, drags in water, sinks in snow, slides on ice without stopping.
- **Marble Land Grab** (2026): Puddles trap and sand slows.
- **Turf Rollers** (2026): Speed pads.
- **Territory Raider** (2026): Fire, ice and lightning patches rotate every 10 stages from stage 11.

Variants:

- Rivers, mountains and cliffs slow movement and drawing, and crossing a fort wall is very slow (Fortix)
- Late-game ice blocks slow the player but not the enemies (Lightfish)
- A tile set of ice, mirrors, ion storms, fire and teleporters (Lightspace)
- The orb rolls on grass, drags in water, sinks in snow and slides on ice without stopping; penguins on ice slide faster than the wolf (GraviNix: Tilt Xonix Arcade)
- Puddles trap and sand slows (Marble Land Grab)
- Speed pads (Turf Rollers)
- Elemental patches rotate every 10 stages from stage 11: ice halves speed for 3 s and makes you slide, lightning freezes you for 1 s but stuns nearby minions for 2.5 s, fire burns the trail back; enclosure removes them (Territory Raider)

## Lane lattice of boxes

The field is a fixed lattice of lanes enclosing boxes or panels. The player and enemies move only along the lanes, a box is captured or filled when its whole perimeter is traced, and two enemies on one lane can trap the player between them. Boxes can be uniform panels or irregular, with perimeter cost and point value differing per box.

Games: 6 · lineages: amidar-painter, gals-panic

Examples:

- **Amidar** (1982): An Amidakuji ladder grid; later levels use more complex grids.
- **Boxer Rebellion** (1982): Pre-set score boxes laid out on the field.
- **Jeepers Creepers** (1982): An irregular grid of rectangles of varying sizes across four named mazes.
- **Potty Painter** (1983): Squares of different sizes, each worth different points.
- **Dancing Eyes** (1996): Player and enemies move only along fixed grid lines, so the player can be trapped between two enemies.
- **Qiks!** (2019): A board of blocks that fill to reveal a pixel-art image.

Variants:

- An Amidakuji ladder grid, more complex in later levels (Amidar)
- Pre-set score boxes laid out on the field (Boxer Rebellion)
- An irregular grid of rectangles of varying sizes across four named mazes: Number One, Scrambler, Brick Wall, Skull Run (Jeepers Creepers)
- Squares of different sizes, each worth different points (Potty Painter)
- A uniform panel grid on which player and enemies move only along grid lines, so two enemies can trap the player (Dancing Eyes)
- A board of blocks that fill to reveal a pixel-art image (Qiks!)

## Field boundary contact rules

Touching the outer edge of the field follows its own rule instead of being a safe rail. In some games it kills: a lethal frame, a crash into the map wall, leaving the board, or only an impact at a steep angle. In others the wall is explicitly safe and stops the avatar or redirects it along the boundary.

Games: 6 · lineages: xonix, io-territory

Examples:

- **陣取り (Evio)** (1993): The big red outer frame kills on contact.
- **Paper-io (MakeCode Arcade)** (2021): Hitting the map wall kills.
- **Qixotic** (2025): Crashing loses.
- **Paper Loop 2** (2026): Steering too steeply into the circular wall eliminates you.
- **looprush** (2026): Leaving the board kills.
- **Pauper.Io!** (2026): Walls do not kill; they stop movement or redirect it along the boundary.

Variants:

- The big red outer frame kills on contact, so the player cannot ride the wall and must steer precisely near it (陣取り (Evio))
- Hitting the map wall kills (Paper-io (MakeCode Arcade)); crashing loses (Qixotic)
- Steering too steeply into the circular wall eliminates you (Paper Loop 2)
- Leaving the board kills (looprush)
- Walls do not kill; they stop movement or redirect it along the boundary (Pauper.Io!)

## Field larger than the screen

One level's field spans more than one screen. The view scrolls (sometimes only once a set share is revealed, sometimes wrapping around) or the field is split into connected screens, so the player cannot see the whole field at once and must manage what is off-screen. Aids include a zoomable camera and a minimap.

Games: 5 · lineages: gals-panic, amidar-painter

Examples:

- **Pepper II** (1982): Each level is four connected mazes spanning four screens, all of which must be filled.
- **Triple Punch** (1982): The grid scrolls horizontally and wraps around.
- **Gals Panic 3** (1995): Scrolling field; once a set share is revealed the view follows the cursor.
- **Gals Panic S - Extra Edition** (1997): Scrolling playfields, with some girls on fixed single screens.
- **panic_Territory Expedition** (2026): Zoomable camera and a toggleable minimap over a map larger than the screen.

Variants:

- Scrolling field larger than the screen; once a set share is revealed the view follows the cursor (Gals Panic 3)
- Scrolling playfields mixed with stages on fixed single screens (Gals Panic S - Extra Edition)
- Zoomable camera and a toggleable minimap over a map larger than the screen (panic_Territory Expedition)
- The grid scrolls horizontally with the player and wraps around (Triple Punch)
- Each level is four connected mazes on four screens, all of which must be filled (Pepper II)

## Shrinking play area

The playable area physically contracts. Either captured cells are removed from the board, or the boundary closes in on a timer. Timer-driven shrinking forces players together to end a match (sudden death, battle royale), or pressures a solo player who can push the walls back by reaching a coverage target.

Games: 5 · lineages: jezzball, io-territory, other

Examples:

- **Crawlco Block Knockers** (2019): Cleared blocks reveal the image and shrink the board.
- **Orbis: Bubble Fill Reflex Game** (2026): Walls close in every 12 seconds; covering 75% pushes them back.
- **Paper Realm 3D** (2026): Sudden Death mode in which the map keeps shrinking.
- **PaperCraft: Territory Capture** (2026): Battle royale with a shrinking zone.
- **Snake Conquest** (2026): Battle Royale with a shrinking safe zone.

Variants:
