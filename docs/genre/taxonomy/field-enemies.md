# Mechanics: field enemies

The 74 mechanic families of the axis *field enemies*: enemies in the open field: bouncing balls, line creatures, bosses with patterns or phases, growing, splitting, shooting, chasing, several bosses. The [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) summarizes the axis and lists the 12 axes. **Games** counts the catalog games that show a family, as the merging agent counted them.

## Mixed enemy roster

Levels draw on a catalogue of enemy types, each with its own movement or attack rule (bouncers, chasers, edge walkers, track riders, lurkers, splitters and so on). The mix changes from level to level and replaces the single Qix-plus-Sparx setup.

Games: 78 · lineages: qix, xonix, volfied, jezzball, gals-panic, other

Examples:

- **Hidden Fields** (1995): 14 enemy types, different on each level.
- **Dancing Eyes** (1996): Slow mice and rabbits that favour different axes, fast squirrels, periodic starfish.
- **Late** (2005): Goo balls, unstable triples, teleporting balls and 'cold-blooded' homing balls.

- **Jezz Mania: Better Jezzball** (2021): Regular, Speeding, Fast, Random, Breaking, Sniper, Father and Baby atoms.
- **Claim (DaNuTree)** (2026): Wanderer, Hunter, Guardian, Nervous and Cutter guards.
- **Neon Slice & Fill** (2026): 36 enemy types, each with its own movement and attack pattern.

## Bouncing ball enemies

Small enemies travel in straight lines through the unclaimed area and bounce off borders, claimed land, walls and obstacles. Contact with the player or the open trail costs a life. In the Xonix line they move only diagonally at constant speed and cannot enter claimed land. In Qix descendants they replace or join the line creature. In Jezzball descendants they are the balls the walls must trap.

Games: 57 · lineages: qix, xonix, jezzball, amidar-painter, volfied

Examples:

- **RollerBall** (1983): The Qix is a single bouncing ball.
- **Xonix** (1984): Sea balls bounce diagonally off land and off each other.
- **Gridd Worz** (1984): Drones, one more per level up to four, bounce around the grid and lay cleared cells back down.
- **Zolyx** (1987): Several balls bounce in the unclaimed area; there is no Qix.
- **Splash!** (1992): Bubbles bounce around the screen; touching one costs a life.
- **Strong Lines** (1994): Enemy balls (white dots), with more added as the player progresses.
- **Xonix (KolibriOS)** (2006): n+1 balls bounce in the unclaimed area at level n.
- **Bix** (2008): Normal and bouncing balls, plus giant balls as a Bix Challenge twist.
- **Pac-Xon** (by early 2010): Ghosts are the bouncing enemies.
- **Corbynball** (2020): Diagonal-only balls that bounce off each other, as in JezzBall.
- **Quarosel** (2023): Each player's balls are used to sabotage the other player.
- **Territory Arcade** (2026): Three bouncing types with steady, readable paces.

Variants:

- A single bouncing ball replaces the Qix (RollerBall); one bouncer and no Sparx or Fuse (Boundarpy).
- Diagonal-only sea balls at constant speed, kept to the unclaimed area (Xonix, Xonix32, the 'flyers' of Xonix X11/Macintosh).
- Balls bounce off each other as well as off walls (Xonix 1984, Corbynball).
- The ball count depends on the level: n+1 balls at level n (Xonix KolibriOS, TIXONIX); 3 at the start plus one per level (Xonix PICO-8); 2 at the start (Antix, Xobox); more added as you progress (Strong Lines).
- Wraps around the screen edges as it bounces through tree rows (Rumba Lumber's pterodactyl).
- Re-skinned bouncers: ghosts (Pac-Xon, Ghost Chaser Xonix), red star-shaped Quixes (AQuix), electric spheres (World Tour Qix), ricocheting meteors (Cosmo Xonix), spikes (TERR1T0RY).
- Giant balls as a Bix Challenge twist (Bix).
- Each player's balls are used to sabotage the other player (Quarosel).
- Bouncers decide which side of a cut fills (Neon Fill); bouncing shapes physically block cuts (NEON CUT); bouncing cores intercept the line (Pixect).
- Each type keeps a steady, readable pace (Territory Arcade); 20 rival ball types with different movement (Stake That Claim).
- On a grid: drones (one per level, up to four) bounce around and lay cleared cells back down (Gridd Worz); bouncing bubbles cost one of three lives on contact (Splash!).
- Bouncer type with unpredictable movement (LandSlicer); drifters that bounce erratically (Gravebloom).

## Homing chasers

Field enemies steer toward the player's current position (the marker, cursor, cutter, bubble or growing wall) instead of bouncing blindly. This forces short cuts and quick returns to safety. The pull is often partial and grows with the level or the claimed area, and some games give each enemy type its own pursuit algorithm.

Games: 45 · lineages: qix, xonix, jezzball, gals-panic, volfied, other

Examples:

- **Night Flight** (1982): A lightning bolt sent by a thunder god chases you.
- **Doodlebug** (1984): At higher levels the Bug turns towards your cursor more often.
- **Qix (ZX Spectrum, PCW type-in)** (1985): Wanders at random early on, homes in while you draw, more often as claimed area grows, and always from screen 4.
- **Thin Ice (described in the Disco No. 1 entry)** (1986): A red seal chases the penguin.
- **Mamba** (1991): The snake tends to chase the spider.
- **Cosmic Bugs** (2004): Some enemies track the cursor to block lines.
- **Neo Xonix** (2010): The 'Hunter' chases the player everywhere.
- **Urbanix** (2010): 'Helisharks' fly after the player.
- **Tarantula vs Snake** (2011): A hungry snake hunts the spider instead of the Qix and Sparks.
- **Jezz Mania: Better Jezzball** (2021): Sniper atoms fly at double speed toward a wall as soon as you start building it.
- **Cherry Gals: Qix Arcade Retro** (2025): Qix crossed with Pac-Man: each enemy type uses a different chase AI.
- **LandSlicer: Slice & Conquer** (2026): The Chaser uses pathfinding pursuit.

Variants:

- Homes more often as the level or claimed area grows: wanders at first, homes while you draw, and always homes from screen 4 (Qix, ZX Spectrum PCW type-in); turns toward the cursor more often at higher levels (Doodlebug); gradually gets smarter (Qix, FM-7).
- A lightning bolt sent by a thunder god chases you (Night Flight).
- Tracks the player only briefly after surfacing (Rumba Lumber's mole).
- Chasers kill on contact on higher levels (Frenzy); Suiveurs (followers) are one of three enemy types (Kicks).
- A snake chases the spider instead of a Qix (Mamba, Mamba 2, Tarantula vs Snake).
- Themed pursuers: cops chasing a crook who grabs money bags (Quirky Crook), predators attacking a farmer (Critter Round-Up), blood bats and bone dragons (Line Knight Fortix), alien ships (LiM Grid Wars), Helisharks (Urbanix), enemy ships (Pirate Xonix), 'shady characters' (Xonix, Atari 8-bit), a red seal chasing the penguin (Thin Ice).
- A Hunter that chases the player everywhere (Neo Xonix).
- Pathfinding pursuit (LandSlicer's Chaser); chase strength set per enemy (RPG Maker MZ Territory Action Plugin).
- Each enemy type uses a different Pac-Man-style chase AI (Cherry Gals: Qix Arcade Retro).
- Tracks the cursor to cut lines off (Cosmic Bugs, Cosmo Bots); the Hunter tracks the player's bubble (Orbis).
- Sniper atoms fly at double speed toward a wall as soon as you start building it (Jezz Mania).
- Red enemy arrows hunt you along the shape (Territory Arrow).
- Hunters as one of the galaxy twists (Bix); Titans join the hunters as levels rise (Space Cutter).

## Shooting enemies and bullet patterns

Regular enemies, and bosses outside their scripted attack sets, fire projectiles at the player and often at the open trail: bullets, spreads, ricochets, breath, missiles or weapons. Harder modes raise the volume up to bullet hell. In many games a hit counts only while the player is drawing.

Games: 35 · lineages: qix, gals-panic, volfied, xonix, other

Examples:

- **Dig Dug II** (1985): Fygars breathe fire.
- **Torch 2081** (1986): The Fireball releases Spitfires that fly straight at you and grow more dangerous in later levels.
- **Gals Panic II** (1993): The Special Edition (1994) adds enemies that shoot and is much harder.
- **Lady Killer** (1993): Being shot by an enemy kills you.
- **Vivid Dolls** (1998): Enemies act like 2D-shooter enemies, each reacting differently; some fire projectiles.
- **Gal Pani X** (2002 (demo 2000)): Bullet-hell bosses: bullet waves, giant lasers, homing missiles, falling projectiles.
- **Fortix** (2009): One dragon type breathes fire; towers shoot once a line is started.
- **Area 99.9%** (2022): Enemies fire bullets; a hit while drawing costs a life.
- **Gensou -Yumegokochi- Illusion -Dreamlike-** (2024): Waves of attacks; about 5x more in Bullet Hell mode; unblockable in HELL Mode.
- **Pixect: Retro Pixel Arcade** (2026): Fired bullets try to intercept the line being drawn.
- **RPG Maker MZ Territory Action Plugin** (2026): 3-way and 5-way spreads, and shots that ricochet off borders.
- **Landgrab - Line Cutting Arcade** (2026): Bosses fire bullets that can hit the player or the open line.

Variants:

- Breath attacks: Fygars breathe fire (Dig Dug II); the pterodactyl starts breathing fire partway through the scene (Rumba Lumber); fire-breathing dragons (Fortix, Line Knight Fortix).
- The Fireball releases Spitfires that fly straight at you and grow more dangerous in later levels (Torch 2081).
- Enemies behave like 2D-shooter enemies, and some fire (Vivid Dolls); fish that shoot (Dancing Eyes); the Special Edition adds shooting enemies (Gals Panic II); monsters attack with weapons, with harder AI (Gals Panic S3).
- Shots aimed at the line being drawn (Pixect); bullets can hit the player or the open line (Landgrab - Line Cutting Arcade).
- 3-way and 5-way spreads, and shots that ricochet off borders (RPG Maker MZ Territory Action Plugin).
- Bullet-hell volume: waves of attacks, about 5x in Bullet Hell mode and unblockable in HELL Mode (Gensou -Yumegokochi- Illusion -Dreamlike-); bullet-hell boss patterns (Project Uncover, Gal Pani X).
- Bullets cost a life only on a hit while drawing (Area 99.9%); being shot kills you (Lady Killer); some enemies shoot bullets (Crawlco Block Knockers).
- Themed shooters: spaceships firing missiles while you capture an arc (Enveloping Darkness), pirates carrying a variety of weapons (Area of Pirates), flying artillery (Fly Trap), jets and heavy bombers (Xonix Wars Territory Capture).
- Precision lasers (TERR1T0RY); a boss's lasers can hit the line in progress (Pix Panic).
- Enemies have attacks to dodge, not only bodies (Pretty Girls Panic! PLUS); projectiles to dodge while drawing (Star Panic: Cosmic Queens).

## Qix line creature

The main field enemy is a line made of several segments (the Qix) that roams the unclaimed area, stretching and contracting as it moves. Touching the marker or the open trail costs a life. It is the defining field enemy of the Qix lineage.

Games: 26 · lineages: qix

Examples:

- **Qix** (1981): The Qix wanders the open field and does not seek the marker.
- **Statix** (1984): The 'Statix charge' gets faster and more aggressive on each new screen.
- **Qixi** (1984): An enemy made of bouncing coloured line segments.
- **Quix (CoCo)** (1984): The Quix, a travelling line, plus other monsters.
- **Qix (Tilt type-in)** (1987): A bouncing 'crazy line' (ligne folle) inside the shrinking box.
- **Trix** (1988): A large pulsating line enemy in the centre.
- **Gotcha** (1993): A bouncing wavy-line creature among the 'meanies'.
- **QIX (Japanese mobile)** (2002): Keeps the Qix's stretching and contracting movement.
- **似非クイックス** (2003): The Qix's tail length is an embed parameter.
- **Get Your Klix on Route 66** (c. 2020): The roaming KLIX is a Route 66-sign-shaped beast, different in each of 12 cities.
- **Mix** (2022): Big green monsters; v1.1-1.2 let them move diagonally.
- **Qixie** (2025): Sawtooth movement, partial homing, sudden speed-ups and zoom-aways.

Variants:

- Wanders the open field and does not seek the marker (Qix, 1981).
- Gets faster and more aggressive on each new screen (Statix).
- Made of bouncing, multicoloured segments (Qixi, Qiks, VGA Kix).
- A fixed five-segment line (Qix, ZX Spectrum PCW type-in); a 'crazy line' inside a shrinking box (Qix, Tilt type-in).
- A large pulsating line in the centre (Trix); a wavy-line 'meanie' among other enemies (Gotcha).
- Tail length is an embed parameter (似非クイックス).
- Moves in a sawtooth pattern with partial homing, sudden speed-ups and zoom-aways (Qixie).
- Diagonal movement added in v1.1-1.2; drawn as big green monsters (Mix).
- Re-themed for each stage: a Route 66-sign-shaped KLIX, different in each of 12 cities (Get Your Klix on Route 66).
- Also the final target, destroyed once the capture goal is met (Game Maker Qix).
- A line-shaped 'QEX' built in a spreadsheet (QEX64, Excel VBA).
- One of the game's two hazards (Mirror Qix).
- Gradually gets smarter over the game (Qix, FM-7).

## Boss plus minions

- **Gal Pani II** (1996): The bosses are monsters holding the girls captive.

- A unique boss with minions on each of 32 levels (Mokoko X); a boss and minions with unique interfering skills on each of 30 stages (Gold Wiper).

- Screen-filling bosses with escorts (ArcField: Neon Territory); a guardian boss with minions in each arena (Volfi: Cyber Territory Arcade); bug enemies plus larger threats (The Zamio).
- Themed boss rosters: radioactive aspic, Zmey Gorynych, an armoured train, mutant bears (Chernobyl Girls).
- Bosses and minions get faster as the stages go on (땅따먹기 (GLORIOGO)).

## Grid-bound chasers

- **Painter Man!!** (2025): AI lions chase the player through the maze.

Variants:

## Patterned and scripted movers

Enemies follow fixed, deterministic paths or rhythms instead of bouncing at random or chasing: right angles, straight lines that never turn, zig-zags, circles, hops, pulses or a set turning rule. Their routes can be learned and cuts planned around them.

Games: 15 · lineages: amidar-painter, qix, xonix, jezzball, volfied, gals-panic

Examples:

- **Amidar** (1982): Enemies travel vertically and take every horizontal turn they reach, as in an Amidakuji draw.
- **Frenzy** (1984): Leptons move in regular, deterministic patterns.
- **Mamba** (1991): One long snake moves fast at right angles.
- **Eyestorm** (2009): Some eyes move in patterns.
- **Neo Xonix** (2010): 'Twins' circle the field, one clockwise and one counter-clockwise.
- **Urbanix** (2010): 'Landrunners' run side to side without ever changing direction.
- **Pac-Xon** (by early 2010): Later levels add ghosts on straight paths.
- **Area 99.9%** (2022): Version 1.2 changed enemy movement from random to patterned.
- **Xonix-Game-Development-Project / xonic-game-cpp-sfml** (2025-2026): Enemies move in circular and zig-zag patterns.
- **GraviNix: Tilt Xonix Arcade** (2026): Horned hares hop, jellyfish drift in pulses, penguins on ice slide faster than the wolf.
- **Orbis: Bubble Fill Reflex Game** (2026): Pulsar enemies pulse in a rhythm.
- **In My Imagination** (2026): Monsters drift or patrol set routes.

Variants:

- Leptons move in regular, deterministic patterns (Frenzy).
- A fast snake on right-angled paths (Mamba, Mamba 2).
- Twins circle the field, one clockwise and one counter-clockwise (Neo Xonix).
- Landrunners run from side to side without ever changing direction (Urbanix); ghosts on straight paths in later levels (Pac-Xon).
- Linear, zig-zag or circular patterns (the tahreem7926/Xonix tutorial clone; KanwalAi's xonic-game-cpp-sfml).
- Creature-themed scripts: horned hares hop, jellyfish drift in pulses, penguins slide faster on ice (GraviNix: Tilt Xonix Arcade).
- Amidar movement: travel vertically and take every horizontal turn reached, as in an Amidakuji draw (Amidar).
- An update changed enemy movement from random to patterned (Area 99.9% v1.2).
- Pulsar enemies pulse in a rhythm (Orbis: Bubble Fill Reflex Game); some eyes move in patterns (Eyestorm).
- Route patrols (In My Imagination); patrolling droids, some predictable and some erratic (Vertex (Komodor)).

## Several main enemies at once
