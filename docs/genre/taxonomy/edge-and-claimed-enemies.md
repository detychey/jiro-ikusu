# Mechanics: edge and claimed-area enemies

The 26 mechanic families of the axis *edge and claimed-area enemies*: enemies on the border or in claimed area: patrollers on the edge, enemies that eat claimed area, pursuers that follow the trail, fuses, enemies that leave the edge. The [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) summarizes the axis and lists the 12 axes. **Games** counts the catalog games that show a family, as the merging agent counted them.

## Border patrollers (Sparx)

Enemies that run along the outer border and the edges of claimed area, and in some games also along drawn or completed lines, but never along the unfinished trail. Contact kills the player. They make the safe lines unsafe, push the player out into the field and force each cut to start and close where no patroller is.

Games: 62 · lineages: qix, xonix, volfied, gals-panic, other

Examples:

- **Qix** (1981): Sparx patrol the border and claimed edges but not the unfinished Stix.
- **Stix** (1983): A quark and an anti-quark walk the inner border of finished fields.
- **Zoo Keeper** (1983): Escaped animals run along the outer edge toward the player, who must jump over them.
- **Zolyx** (1987): Per Sinclair User, a larger blob chases the player round the perimeter.
- **Skyx** (1988): Enemies travel along the lines the player has already drawn.
- **Araña** (1990): A single enemy patrols the border and cannot be trapped.
- **Cacoma Knight in Bizyland** (1992): Some enemies move only along borders and chalk lines.
- **Cubixx** (2009): From stage 2, light nodes run along the cube's edges and along cut edges.
- **Space Cropper** (2014): Sentinels guard the map border Sparx-style; a shield protects the player from them.
- **Pretty Girls Panic!** (2016): The edge is normally safe, but crab enemies can catch the player there.
- **Qixie** (2025): Two Sparks patrol only the outer border in opposing patterns and never follow drawn lines.
- **RetroQix** (2026): Wall-Walker enemies on the border spawn away from the player's start.

Variants:

- Classic Sparx: they patrol the outer border and claimed edges but never the unfinished Stix (Qix, 1981).
- Outer border only, in opposing patterns; they never enter the field or follow drawn lines (Qixie).
- They also run along lines the player has already drawn or completed (Trix, Skyx, VGA Kix, 3Quarter, Fortix's bats).
- They patrol the outlines or the inner border of finished fields rather than the outer frame (RollerBall's Squeezers, Stix's quark and anti-quark).
- From stage 2 they run along a 3D cube's edges and along freshly cut edges (Cubixx).
- They home on the player along the boundary instead of looping blindly (Statix, Qixi, Zolyx's larger blob, RPG Maker MZ Territory Action Plugin, Reef Reveal's coast crabs).
- Sparklers that burn like fuses along the walls (Fill 'Er Up! II); the original Fill 'Er Up! has no border runners at all.
- A dotted caterpillar, faster than arcade Sparx (Styx).
- Sparx are lethal only on the border (quix).
- Mixed rosters in which only some enemies are confined to borders, chalk lines or boundary lines (Cacoma Knight in Bizyland, Lovely Girls).
- A sentinel walks the edge of the player's own land, so the border is never safe (Territory: Claim & Conquer); the border protects from the field creature but not from Sparks (QL-x).
- A single patroller that a capture cannot trap (Araña).
- A shield protects the player from border sentinels (Space Cropper).
- The player jumps over animals running along the outer edge (Zoo Keeper).
- Patrollers spawn away from the player's start, for a fair opening (RetroQix).
- The count is fixed by the game or set by the host: two start in the right-hand corners (Qix ZX Spectrum PCW type-in); up to four Tracers (Kix BBC Micro); an options toggle (Shocker); 0 to 6 sparks (Unveil); a maximum-count embed parameter (似非クイックス).
- Re-skins: edge walkers on a Xonix board (Cosmo Xonix); sea snails along the walls (Mr Super Fish); crabs on the normally safe Gals Panic edge (Pretty Girls Panic!); pink spiders (World Tour Qix); crawlers along the shore (Glowfield); a ring of folder icons as the border (Game Maker Qix).
- Standard Sparx carried unchanged through ports and clones, including a 4 KB build (4kQix).

## Stall fuse

If the player stops or hesitates mid-cut, a fuse appears on the unfinished trail and burns along it toward the marker, costing a life if it reaches it. The fuse can be a spark, a fire or an 'ignition' enemy. It forbids parking in the open field.

Games: 18 · lineages: qix

Examples:

- **Qix** (1981): Stopping while drawing lights a Fuse that burns along the unfinished Stix toward the marker.
- **quix (QIX on Unix)** (c. 1982-1984): A Fuse appears if the player stops off the border, or crosses or doubles back on their own trail.
- **Statix** (1984): Fuses run up the trail if the player hesitates.
- **Kix (BBC Micro)** (1986): A Fuse appears if the player hesitates.
- **Super Qix** (1987): Pausing mid-line spawns a fire that creeps along the line toward the marker.
- **Perestroika Girls** (1993): Standing still too long sets a fire chasing along the player's line.
- **似非クイックス** (2003): An 'ignition' enemy appears on the line if the player stalls mid-cut.
- **ONLINE QIX** (2007): The Fuse is in the starting roster.
- **Game Maker Qix** (2011): A Fuse chases the marker along its dotted Stix trail.
- **Stix (PICO-8)** (2019): Fuses move fast.
- **Mirror Qix** (2024): The Fuze is one of the game's two hazards.
- **Eight Bit Qix** (c. 2026): Stopping mid-line lights a fuse; fuse speed is a difficulty option.

Variants:

- Stopping off the border lights a Fuse on the unfinished Stix (Qix, 1981).
- Crossing or doubling back on one's own trail also lights the Fuse (quix).
- A fire creeps along the line instead of a spark (Super Qix); standing still too long sets that fire (Perestroika Girls).
- The fuse is an 'ignition' enemy (似非クイックス).
- Fast fuses (Stix PICO-8, carried over into Stix Disruption).
- Fuse speed is a difficulty option (Eight Bit Qix).
- The Fuse chases the marker along a dotted Stix trail (Game Maker Qix).
- Kept as one of only two hazards (Mirror Qix).
- Kept alongside the Spiral Death Trap (Qyx).

- **AirXonix** (2000): Striped balls destroy territory already claimed.

- **Hundreds** (2013): Buzzsaws reset the circles' numbers.
- **Mr Super Fish: Hero Fill Block** (2017): Ball Fish Man roams open space and eats wall blocks, un-filling captured ground.
- **Xonix (Pac-Man hybrid, Eques72)** (2022): A destroyer bounces diagonally and erases the inner claimed tiles it bounces off; the outer border is safe.

- **Q\*BALL** (2025): Randomly bouncing q\*balls undo progress, so the player must trap them.

- **Moonlight Territory** (2026): Once the bow is unlocked, wolves chew through walls and collapse territory.
- **Starcutter: Space Arcade** (2026): One boss's trick is eating claimed land.

Variants:

- Striped balls destroy territory already claimed (AirXonix).
- Later levels add enemies that eat finished walls (Pac-Xon).
- Red balls split whole chunks off filled territory and turn them back into dangerous area (Xonix Blast).
- Ball Fish Man roams open space and eats wall blocks, un-filling captured ground (Mr Super Fish: Hero Fill Block).
- A destroyer bounces diagonally and erases the inner claimed tiles it bounces off, while the outer border stays immune (Xonix, Pac-Man hybrid, Eques72); a destroyer erases claimed tiles (Eques72 open-source tutorial clones).
- Breakers chew through captured ground (Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade).
- The Destructor damages claimed territory and the Driller cuts through safe land (LandSlicer: Slice & Conquer).
- Once the bow is unlocked, wolves chew through walls and collapse territory (Moonlight Territory).

- The run ends if territory is destroyed (point line surface).
- Eating claimed land is one boss's trick (Starcutter: Space Arcade).
- A virus steadily eats captured blocks, so mass must be held by capturing again (Scalar Snake).
- Buzzsaws reset the circles' grown numbers (Hundreds).
- Randomly bouncing q\*balls undo progress until trapped (Q\*BALL).

- **Qurvix** (2026): Sparx get faster on later levels, punishing lingering on the border.
- **Arcade Land Grab: Line Game** (2026): The Master and Boss tiers add sparks.

Variants:

- A timer meter releases more Sparx, then turns them into Super Sparx that chase onto the unfinished Stix (Qix, 1981; kept in Qyx).
- From level 7, sub-viruses follow the marker while it draws (Qix, 1989 Taito America Amiga port).
- Running out of time costs the round instead of releasing Super Sparx (Mix).
- The Sparx count rises every few stages (Qix FM-7); Tracers are added on later levels (Alien Blockade).
- A second patroller, running the opposite way, joins from level 2 (Styx).
- Border enemies grow in number and speed (Y) or multiply over time (Reef Reveal).
- Faster Sparx on later levels punish lingering on the border (Qurvix).
- After a set time, a red spark appears that runs only on the barrier (Area 99.9%).
- Sparks appear on completed lines in place of the long-draw mine (Volfied, PC Engine port).

- The spawn interval and the speed-up interval are embed parameters (似非クイックス); starting count, timer and speed are difficulty options (Eight Bit Qix).

## Safe border and claimed ground

Ordinary enemies cannot harm the player on the border or inside claimed territory, so only time spent drawing in the open field is dangerous. Border patrollers, land enemies and dangerous edges are the mechanics that break this baseline.

- **Megami Tensei QIX Persona 3** (2007): The player is invincible while inside their own claimed territory.

- **Virtual Bug Catcher** (2020): The player's area is the red safe zone.
- **Area 99.9%** (2022): The green barrier line is safe from normal enemies and their bullets.
- **Spherical Qix** (2022): Enemies only interfere while the player draws.

- **Blobbed Away** (2025): The player is safe on claimed ground.

- **Territory Raider** (2026): Shield mode: invulnerable while walking the claimed border.
- **Kingdom Cutter: Cut & Conquer** (2026): The player rides out from the castle walls and reconnects to safety to claim.

Variants:

- The player is invincible inside their own claimed territory (Megami Tensei QIX Persona 3).
- Invincibility on the edges was added by a February 2010 update (Reclamation).
- The player is safe on the border even when an enemy touches them there (Coach Tzabar).
- The green barrier protects from normal enemies and from their bullets (Area 99.9%).
- A shield mode makes the player invulnerable while walking the claimed border (Territory Raider).

## Enemy-triggered line fuse

An enemy touching the open line does not kill outright. It lights a spark or bullet that races along the line toward the player, who dies only if it arrives before the cut is closed or retracted to safe ground.

Games: 8 · lineages: volfied, qix

Examples:

- **Volfied** (1989): An enemy touching the line sends a spark toward the ship; closing the area first saves it.
- **Ultimate Qix (Genesis port of Volfied)** (1991): Mode A uses the arcade spark rule under a stage timer.
- **Dino Master** (2005): An enemy touching the open line sends a bullet along it until it hits the player or the player reaches a safe area.
- **Area 99.9%** (2022): An enemy touching the line spawns fast sparks that chase the ship.
- **ArcField: Neon Territory** (2025): A spark chases along the red line instead of instant death on contact.
- **Starcutter: Space Arcade** (2026): An enemy touching the line starts a fuse racing toward the player.
- **Territory Raider** (2026): Sparks race along the open trail; being reached costs a life.
- **VoidMania** (2026): The fuse burns toward the player, who can hold SPACE to retrace to safety before it arrives.

Variants:

- Closing the area before the spark arrives saves the ship (Volfied).
- Mode A keeps the arcade spark rule under a stage timer; modes B and C drop it for instant death (Ultimate Qix).
- A bullet travels the line until it hits the player or the player reaches safe area (Dino Master).
- An enemy touching the line spawns several fast sparks that chase the ship (Area 99.9%).
- Replaces instant death on contact explicitly (ArcField: Neon Territory).
- Holding SPACE retraces the line to safety before the fuse arrives (VoidMania).
- Being reached costs a life rather than ending the run (Territory Raider).

## Trail chasers

Enemies that travel along the player's open trail, or along drawn track lines, and chase the cursor from behind while a cut is in progress. What sets them off is drawing itself, line length or capture size, not stopping, so they cap how far a single cut can go. Closing the capture first escapes them or removes them.

Games: 7 · lineages: xonix, qix, gals-panic, volfied

Examples:

- **Zolyx** (1987): ZX Spectrum only: an open line kept going too long spawns a chaser ball behind the cursor.
- **Skyx** (1988): A spark chases the player as soon as drawing starts and catches them if the area drawn is too large.
- **Trix** (1988): An enemy chases the player along the line being drawn.
- **Just4Qix** (2009): Fire henchmen run along track lines and steer toward the current trail; completing a fill with a fire on your tail douses it.
- **Xonix 3d (myplayyard)** (2011): A ball enemy eats the trail back until it hits the border (player comment).
- **Space Cropper** (2014): Segment-follower monsters chase the player's trail.
- **In My Imagination** (2026): A monster pattern chases the player's trail.

Variants:

- ZX Spectrum only: a chaser ball spawns behind the cursor if an open line is kept going too long (Zolyx).
- A spark chases as soon as drawing starts and catches the player if the area being drawn is too large (Skyx).
- A ball eats the trail back until it hits the border (Xonix 3d, myplayyard).
- Segment-follower monsters chase the player's trail segment by segment (Space Cropper).
- Fire henchmen run along track lines and steer toward the current trail; touching one costs a life, and completing a fill with a fire on your tail douses it (Just4Qix).
- A monster pattern chases the trail (In My Imagination).
- An enemy chases the player along the line being drawn; its source groups it with the Qix fuse but records no stall condition (Trix).

- **Kingdom Cutter: Cut & Conquer** (2026): Roaming monsters can cut off the exposed line.
- **Pulsebound: The Expanse** (unknown): Patrol drones can cut the unfinished chain and damage the shield.

Variants:

- Shears and Demons cut the line, which then reforms between the arrows (Libble Rabble).
- A Pokémon touching the capture line breaks it and the loops reset (Pokémon Ranger).

## Claimed ground as a barrier

Enemies, rivals or their projectiles cannot enter or cross the player's claimed ground, so territory also works as terrain denial, cover and an escape route.

- Enemies crawl both inside the board and outside it (Cellonix).
- The iOS version adds a basilisk that crosses the whole board, alongside separate field and land enemies (Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade).

## Instant death on line contact

Any enemy touching the open line kills the player at once, with no fuse and no escape window. Listed here where a game states the rule explicitly, usually as the harsher alternative to a line fuse.

Games: 2 · lineages: volfied

Examples:

- **Ultimate Qix (Genesis port of Volfied)** (1991): Modes B and C kill instantly when an enemy touches the line.
- **VOLTFIELD: 80s Classic Arcade** (2026): Enemies hunt the live wire, and one touch ends the run.

Variants:

- Modes B and C kill instantly on line contact, while mode A uses the fuse (Ultimate Qix).
- Enemies actively hunt the live wire, and one touch ends the run (VOLTFIELD: 80s Classic Arcade).
