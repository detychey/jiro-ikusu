# Mechanics: controls, time and pacing

The 50 mechanic families of the axis *controls, time and pacing*: input schemes (one button, touch, swipe, analog), time limits, slow motion, pacing devices, pause. The [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) summarizes the axis and lists the 12 axes. **Games** counts the catalog games that show a family, as the merging agent counted them.

## Level countdown timer

Each level, stage, round or life runs against a visible countdown. If the countdown reaches zero before the clear target is met, the player loses a life, the round or the whole game. In some games expiry instead ends the level and scores what was claimed. The length may be fixed, scale with the level, depend on difficulty, or be set by the player or the arcade operator.

Games: 80 · lineages: qix, volfied, xonix, jezzball, gals-panic, amidar-painter, other

Examples:

- **Jolly Jogger** (1982): A burning fuse acts as the time limit.
- **Stix** (1983): Running out ends the game regardless of lives; losing a life refills the timer.
- **Kix (BBC Micro)** (1986): A time-limit bar on each screen; the time left is paid out as a bonus.
- **Zolyx** (1987): 75% must be filled within a time limit.
- **Rampart** (1990): A timed build phase.
- **Ultimate Qix (Genesis port of Volfied)** (1991): Mode A runs under a stage timer.
- **JezzBall** (1992): Capture 75% within a time limit.
- **Paparazzi** (1996): Operator 'Time' DIP: 80/100/120/150 seconds.
- **KBounce** (2000): A 30x(n+2)-second limit; running out of time ends the game.
- **Territory Raider** (2026): A 60-second timer per life; a time-out costs a life.
- **Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade** (2026): An orbital countdown dial of 2-4 minutes per level.
- **Landgrab - Line Cutting Arcade** (2026): A timeout during an over-80% push loses that cut's clear, its points and a life.

Variants:

- Expiry ends the game regardless of lives left, while losing a life refills the timer (Stix).
- The time left is paid out as a bonus (Kix (BBC Micro), Gridder).
- Expiry costs a life instead of the game (Alien Blockade, Strong Lines 1994, Neo Xonix, Xonix Assault, Gravebloom).
- The timer is a 'time pointer' bar in the HUD (Illgen); a plain HUD countdown (Strong Lines 1991, TIXONIX).
- The limit shrinks in later stages (Lovely Girls); a sequel tightens the time pressure of earlier games (Gals Panic 3).
- The limit grows with the level number, 30x(n+2) seconds, and expiry ends the game (KBounce).
- The level timer can be set from 1:00 to 1:40 (Harem Challenge).
- The arcade operator sets the round length by DIP switch (Paradise, Paradise Deluxe, Paparazzi 80-150 s, Pocket Gals V.I.P and Gals Hustler 70-120 s, Penky 40-70 s).
- A 60-second timer runs per life rather than per level (Territory Raider).
- One countdown covers the whole session (Cobweb).
- The limit is strict only on medium and hard difficulty (Coach Tzabar).
- Only some modes or stages are timed: Mode A (Ultimate Qix), the 60-second Time Challenge (Blobbed Away), Endless Dreams at 3 minutes per image (In My Imagination), the bonus stage (Wonder Stick).
- A points gauge, not an area share, must fill before the limit (Urbanix).
- Expiry ends the level and scores what is already enclosed: animals still inside score (Zoo Keeper); the level ends at time-out or when every tile is captured (Live Wire!).
- Expiry loses the round (Mix).
- The timer is a burning fuse (Jolly Jogger) or an orbital countdown dial of 2-4 minutes (Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade).
- Timing out during a push past the 80% target loses that cut's clear, its points and a life (Landgrab - Line Cutting Arcade).
- The clock limits the build phase between battles (Rampart).
- Fixed-length 5-minute rounds (DEIFLOV).
- The play clock always runs, deterministically from power-on (Jezzball NES).
- Fast clears earn a bonus scene (Dancing Eyes).
- Once the target is met, a button runs the remaining clock faster so the level ends sooner (Q\*BALL).

## Timed match, largest territory wins

A match clock ends the round, and the player or team holding the most territory when it expires wins, unless a win threshold ends the match first. This clock is the main pacing device of arena and io-style territory games, and a match usually lasts a few minutes.

Games: 24 · lineages: io-territory, other

Examples:

- **Splatoon (Turf War)** (2015): 3-minute matches; the team with the most inked turf wins.
- **Conquerors** (c. 2019): A round timer.
- **Pyrodrome** (2025): The most territory at the end of the round wins.
- **Qixotic** (2025): The most territory when the timer ends wins.
- **BeastGrid** (2026): Suggested 60 s (Classic) or 120 s (Capture).
- **DroneXtreme: Territory Capture** (2026): 90-second matches.
- **Ink Wars** (2026): 3-minute matches.
- **Loop Nook: Territory Capture** (2026): Blitz: one minute.
- **Paper Land: Territory Grab** (2026): Percentage targets before the clock runs out.
- **Pengu Floe** (2026): A blizzard closes the match; the leader wins.
- **TERRITORY SCRAMBLE** (2026 (unreleased)): The host chooses 1, 3, 5, 7 or 10 minutes.
- **땅따먹기왕 : 꼬리로 둘러싸는 땅따먹기** (unknown (listing dated 2026)): 1-3 minute rounds.

Variants:

- Suggested lengths differ per mode: 60 s in Classic, 120 s in Capture (BeastGrid).
- A short Blitz mode: one minute (Loop Nook: Territory Capture), two minutes beside a 3-minute Team Battle (PaperCraft: Territory Capture).
- A separate 90-second timed mode beside the main mode (Loop: Land Grab).
- Percentage targets must be reached before the clock runs out (Paper Land: Territory Grab).
- A blizzard closes the match and the leader wins (Pengu Floe).
- A 60-second survival round (Marble Land Grab).
- The host picks the length: 1, 3, 5, 7 or 10 minutes (TERRITORY SCRAMBLE); a match length of 2-10 minutes (Splash&Dash).
- Teams paint turf in 3-minute matches (Splatoon Turf War).

## Adjustable game speed

The player sets the game or movement speed, before play or during it, on a numeric scale or from a few presets. Some games set the player's and the enemies' speeds separately.

Games: 17 · lineages: qix, xonix, jezzball, other

Examples:

- **Stix** (1983): Cursor keys adjust movement speed: up/down slower, left/right faster.
- **Antix** (1985): Speed 1-9 chosen before play.
- **Shocker** (1988): Per-player fast/medium/slow and eel speed relative to the man.
- **Araña** (1990): Difficulty sets the speed of both player and enemies.
- **Ballistyx** (1990s): Separate player, ball and enemy speeds.
- **Gotcha** (1993): \+ and - change the game speed during play.
- **Xonix (X11/Macintosh reimplementation)** (1995): Configurable step timeout, default 50 ms (20 moves per second).
- **JezzBall (TI-83, Joe Wingbermuehle)** (1998): Selectable ball speed.
- **firmament i-a** (2001): A 4-step flow speed sets the score multiplier.
- **Xobox (Rockbox plugin)** (2006): Speed 1-10; loop period (11-speed)x10 ms.
- **Corbynball** (2020): Three selectable speed settings.
- **Xonix (Atari 8-bit)** (2024): Game speed selectable from 3 to 9.

Variants:

- Speed changes during play: cursor keys make movement slower or faster (Stix); + and - keys (Gotcha).
- Per-player game speed plus the eel's speed relative to the man (Shocker).
- Separate player, ball and enemy speeds (Ballistyx).
- A numeric scale chosen before play: 1-9 (Antix), 3-9 (Xonix (Atari 8-bit)), 1-10 setting a loop period of (11-speed)x10 ms (Xobox).
- A configurable step timeout, 50 ms by default (Xonix (X11/Macintosh reimplementation)).
- Difficulty sets the speed of both player and enemies (Araña).
- A ball-speed setting (PetsArtXoniX, JezzBall (TI-83)); a speed toggle (JezzballDS); three presets (Corbynball); a slow-pace option (Containment); a menu option (Smiley).
- Game speed is an embed parameter set by the host page (似非クイックス).
- The player picks a maximum flow speed in 4 steps, and a faster setting raises the score multiplier (firmament i-a).

- **Qix** (1981): The Atari 5200 port needs a button press to start drawing.
- **Qixi** (1984): Cursor keys plus space bar or Z.
- **Kix (BBC Micro)** (1986): Hold the draw key to draw slowly; release after starting to draw quickly.
- **Spider Web** (1989): One button starts and stops a strand.
- **LadyLove** (1990): Hold SHIFT while moving along the frame to cut.
- **Coach Tzabar** (1997): Space must be held to leave the border and draw.
- **似非クイックス** (2003): Arrow keys move; Z or X held while moving cuts.
- **Sloppy Ninja** (2013): Arrows move and Space is a dedicated claim key.
- **Space Cropper** (2014): Cuts into space while Shift or J is held.
- **Re: Gals Panic** (2019): Draw on left mouse/Z/Space, skill on right mouse/X/Enter.
- **Smokescreen** (2025): Hold to lay smoke.
- **In My Imagination** (2026): Holding the draw button cuts; releasing triggers a double-speed retrace.

Variants:

- A button press is needed to start drawing on the Atari 5200 port (Qix).
- Holding the draw key draws slowly; releasing it after starting keeps drawing fast (Kix (BBC Micro)).
- Keyboard only: hold SHIFT while moving along the frame to cut into the picture (LadyLove).

## Time-driven escalation

The longer a level lasts, the more dangerous it gets. Enemies speed up or multiply, either continuously or each time a timer expires, which punishes stalling. In some games losing a life resets the escalation.

Games: 13 · lineages: qix, xonix, other

Examples:

- **Antix** (1985): Ball speed rises over time, and another diamond spawns after a while on level 1.
- **Mr Super Fish: Hero Fill Block** (2017): Enemy speed acceleration, removed in v1.4.
- **Qyx** (2022): The stage timer releases Super Sparx.
- **Trappy Pixel** (2023): When the 90 seconds run out, more enemy pixels spawn.
- **Xonix (PICO-8)** (2024): Each time the level timer runs out, another land enemy spawns.
- **TIXONIX** (unknown): Each 60-second expiry adds a land enemy; losing a life removes them and restarts the countdown.
- **Xonix-Game-Development-Project / xonic-game-cpp-sfml** (2025-2026): Continuous mode: +1 enemy every 30 s, speed increase every 20 s.
- **Domain Expansion** (2026): Timed acceleration.
- **Eight Bit Qix** (c. 2026): A configurable Sparx timer.
- **Territory Raider** (2026): Enemies speed up +25% at 20 s left and +50% at 10 s left.
- **Reef Reveal** (2026): Crabs multiply over time, so slow play is punished.
- **Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade** (2026): Survive long enough and the level speeds up and adds hunters.

Variants:

- Another enemy spawns after a while (Antix, a diamond on level 1).
- Enemy speed rises continuously during play (Antix); the acceleration was later removed in v1.4 (Mr Super Fish: Hero Fill Block).
- Each expiry of a repeating level timer spawns another land enemy (Xonix (PICO-8)); the same on a 60-second countdown, where losing a life removes the extra land enemies and restarts it (TIXONIX).
- Expiry of the 90-second level timer spawns more enemy pixels (Trappy Pixel).
- The stage timer releases Super Sparx (Qyx); the Sparx timer is configurable (Eight Bit Qix).
- Enemies speed up in steps as the clock runs down: +25% at 20 s left and +50% at 10 s left (Territory Raider).
- Crabs multiply over time (Reef Reveal); surviving long enough speeds the level up and adds hunters (Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade).
- Timed acceleration (Domain Expansion).
- Continuous mode adds an enemy every 30 seconds and raises speed every 20 seconds (Xonix-Game-Development-Project / xonic-game-cpp-sfml); continuous mode adds enemies over time (tahreem7926/Xonix).

## Clock replenished by play

A clock or survival meter drains steadily, and the player tops it up by playing: closing captures, collecting items or earning bonuses. The run lasts as long as the player keeps capturing faster than the clock drains, so the timer sets the pace instead of only capping the level.

Games: 10 · lineages: amidar-painter, other, gals-panic, qix, xonix

Examples:

- **Demolition Herby** (1982): The fuel gauge drains steadily and is refilled by completing squares.
- **Libble Rabble** (1983): The border is a timer; looping Mushlins and plants adds time.
- **Dancing Eyes** (1996): E adds time and T freezes the timer for 6 s.
- **Star Trigon** (2002): Oxygen drains during the stage; air capsules refill it.
- **Patchwork Heroes** (2010): The timer pauses briefly in proportion to the share of the ship cut away.
- **Xonix Casual Edition** (2023): About one minute per level, which bonuses can extend.
- **Cherry Gals: Qix Arcade Retro** (2025): A per-level timer extendable with extra-time pickups.
- **Glowfield** (2026): Seeds of light give extra time.
- **Starcutter: Space Arcade** (2026): Extra-time crates add to the clock.
- **GALS HUNTER** (2026): Time bonuses keep the run alive across 10 phases.

Variants:

- A fuel gauge drains and completing squares refills it (Demolition Herby).
- The border itself is the timer; looping Mushlins and plants adds time (Libble Rabble).
- Oxygen drains during the stage and air capsules refill it (Star Trigon).
- Each cut pauses the stage timer for a time proportional to the share of the field removed (Patchwork Heroes).
- One continuous clock spans 10 phases, and time bonuses earned in play keep it running (GALS HUNTER).
- Pickups add time to the level timer: seeds of light (Glowfield), extra-time pickups (Cherry Gals: Qix Arcade Retro), extra-time crates (Starcutter: Space Arcade), bonuses (Xonix Casual Edition).
- Item E adds time and item T freezes the timer for 6 seconds (Dancing Eyes).
