# Ideas: multiplayer and competition

The 11 ideas proposed from the axis of the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) *multiplayer and competition*: versus, co-op, .io territory battles, asynchronous ghosts, leaderboards, battle royale, team play. Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Big capture stuns the boss

`multiplayer-and-competition#7` · T3 New core rule · score 12 (core 4, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

A capture of at least S cells sends a shockwave that freezes the boss for a few seconds, so one big risky cut buys a safe window for the next.

**Rule sketch.** Level format v2 adds stun_cells (u16, 0 = off) and stun_ticks (u8), and the boss gets a field stun (u8). At step 3, when a player capture's size is at least stun_cells and stun was 0 at the start of the tick, set stun = stun_ticks. A capture made while the boss is stunned never re-stuns it, so stuns cannot be chained into a permanent lock. At step 4, if stun > 0: decrement it and skip the move and its deflection draw (R4.6-11: a boss that does not move draws nothing). A stunned boss never bumps the trail, but stepping into its body still hits (R4.9-3). Movement then resumes on the global phase (R4.4-2). Optional parry variant: when the shield absorbs a hit (R4.9-15) and that tick's hit came from the boss bumping the trail, set stun = stun_ticks / 2. This needs a per-tick hit-source bit, which is not kept between ticks.

**Inspired by:** Gals Panic 4 (1996), Yoshi Touch & Go (2005), ONLINE QIX (2007), Landgrab - Line Cutting Arcade (2026), Warix (unreleased). **Mechanic families:** [Attacks sent to the opponent's field](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#attacks-sent-to-the-opponents-field), [Direct combat layer](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#direct-combat-layer). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, state-hash, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D14; R4.4-9; R4.6-12; D6 (parry variant).

**Bot effect.** D14: stuns are triggered by the player and are temporary, and the frozen boss stays wherever the seeded deflections left it, so no seed-independent safe zone appears. R4.6-12's divergence bound counts boss moves, so stuns delay divergence by their length, and its tick figure needs a note. Gap: narrows slightly. A frozen boss turns the hardest human task, predicting a moving body, into a static puzzle that humans can solve too. The search bot gains less, since it already predicts the moving boss perfectly. The no-chaining rule stops the search bot from turning stuns into a permanent lock.

**Budget.** 1 byte of boss state (state hash v2), 3 bytes of level data. Per tick: one comparison; a stunned boss skips its edge tests, so a stun saves cycles. Screen: the boss is drawn in a 'frozen' palette colour while stunned, redrawn once when the stun starts and once when it ends.

**Variety.** It counters edge-nibbling with a rhythm the current levels lack: build toward a capture big enough to stun, then spend the frozen window on a deep cut into the boss's quarter. Players weigh many safe small cuts against one that earns a window.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** Cheap and clean in the core:

- One u8 on the boss and three level bytes.
- A stunned tick skips the draw, and since the draw is keyed by tick and stateless, the later deflections are unaffected.
- Movement resumes on the global phase.
- A stunned tick saves cycles.
- D14 holds, since the freeze point is seeded.

The pitch's central safety claim is wrong: 'no re-stun while stunned' does not stop chaining. During a freeze, a trail is completely safe. The frozen boss never bumps it, and minions only walk the rail. So a player, human or bot, lays the next big cut during the freeze and closes it on the first tick that stun = 0. The boss gets at most one move between stuns, which in practice is a permanent lock.

A frozen boss can also be boxed. If stun_ticks x 1-2 cells per tick covers the trail needed to enclose the boss, humans reach search-like claims of about 9,700 bp. That narrows the gap far more than 'slightly', but it also makes stun levels easy. stun_ticks must be tuned against the boss's distance to the rail.

The parry variant needs no persistent bit: boss_hit is known within the tick at step 6. But it changes D6.

*Fixes.* Add a cooldown in the same byte. On a stun, set stun = stun_ticks + cooldown. The boss is frozen while stun > cooldown, and a capture re-stuns only when stun = 0, so there is no new state as long as stun_ticks + cooldown \<= 255. Tune stun_ticks per level against the trail length needed to box the boss, and recalibrate. Amend R4.6-12's tick figure to count only the boss's moves.

*Cost.* Level bytes v2: 475 B (+3). Boss state +1 byte, state hash v2 949 B. RAM: about 1 B. Per tick: one compare; stunned ticks skip both edge tests and the draw, so cycles go down. zk: slightly lower. Screen: a palette swap of the boss at the start and end of a stun. The cooldown fix adds no bytes.

*Conflicts.* D14 (R4.6-12 divergence tick bound); R4.4-9; R4.6-6; R4.6-11; R4.6-12; D6 (parry variant); state hash v1; level bytes v1

**Fairness: concerns, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The boss freezes wherever the seeded deflections left it, draws resume on later ticks, and nothing seed-independent appears. R4.6-12's tick figure needs the noted caveat. Because search already reaches 98.5% of the ceiling, a mechanic that makes the moving boss easier for humans mostly helps humans, so the gap narrows. Scripted bots can use it with a simple 'cut toward the boss while it is stunned' rule, and calibration is straightforward. The no-chaining rule does not hold, though. Step 3 runs before step 4. A player can lay a trail during the freeze (a stunned boss never bumps it), stop one cell short, which is legal under D4's no-fuse rule, and close a capture of at least stun_cells on the first tick where stun is 0. That re-stuns the boss before it ever moves, so the boss can stay frozen for the rest of the level as long as large captures remain. Search will find this exactly, and humans only approximately. The parry variant needs per-tick hit-source state and conflicts with D6, so leave it out.

*Fixes.* Add a cooldown. For example, let the stun field count down 2 × stun_ticks, with the boss frozen only while it is above stun_ticks, and allow a re-stun only at 0. That guarantees stun_ticks of boss movement between stuns at no extra state. Alternatively, require at least M boss moves since the last stun. Add a property test that no journal keeps the boss frozen for more than stun_ticks consecutive moving ticks. Drop the parry variant.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** It adds a clear rhythm: earn a freeze with one big, risky cut, then spend the frozen window on a deep fast cut (A) next to a static body. That is a real decision between many small safe cuts and one that earns a window, and it fits the arcade feel (freeze power-ups). No current level changes the boss's state. It narrows the gap plausibly, by turning prediction into a static puzzle. As written, though, the no-chaining rule fails. D4 allows stopping in the middle of a cut, and a player in the field is safe from minions, who walk the rail. So during a stun a player can draw almost the whole next cut, stop one cell short, and close it on the first tick that starts with stun = 0. The capture at step 3 re-stuns before the boss moves at step 4, and the boss never moves again: a permanent lock that search and skilled humans would both find.

*Fixes.* 1. Count a capture toward a stun only if its cut started while the boss was not stunned. That takes one bit of player state, saved at the cut start, and blocks cuts prepared during the stun. Alternatively, add a refractory period of stun_ticks after a stun ends. 2. Note the move-count basis in R4.6-12's divergence bound. 3. Draw the frozen boss in a palette colour not used by the player (white or grey) or the minions (green).

*Readability.* Excellent. A colour swap on a B x B square is the clearest possible signal at 4 px per cell, with two partial redraws per stun.

## Claim lines that insure against zero

`multiplayer-and-competition#11` · T3 New core rule · score 12 (core 4, fairness 5, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Each level has a crown line below its target. Once your claim crosses it, losing the last life ends the level with your claim kept instead of zeroed, but crossing also wakes a dormant extra minion. The choice is between insuring early and crossing late with a big cut.

**Rule sketch.** Level format v2 adds crown_bp (u16, 0 = off, at most the target) and dormant_mask (u8: minion slots, within count, that start dormant). At level start a dormant slot is destroyed (presence 2) with countdown 0xFFFF, a sentinel that R4.7-9 does not count down. At step 3, when a player capture brings claim_bp from below crown_bp to crown_bp or above, each dormant slot's countdown becomes 15: its spawn cell flashes, and it respawns, oriented, through the existing lifecycle (R4.7-9, R4.7-7). At step 6, if the last life is lost (R4.9-16) and claim_bp ≥ crown_bp, the level ends as 'Over, claim kept' (a new end reason) instead of scoring zero. Below the line, D5 stands. Being crowned is derived from the captured cells, so the only state is the existing countdown field.

**Inspired by:** Domain Expansion (2026), Space Cutter (2026), panic_Territory Expedition (2026), Jezzball Jam (2014), LewdQix (2024), Hexanaut.io (2022), Huaxian Quandi 2 (4399 rehost of Paper.io 2) (2018), Defly.io (unknown), BeastGrid (2026), Line Fight (unreleased). **Mechanic families:** [Checkpoints and soft failure](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#checkpoints-and-soft-failure), [Rising clear threshold](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#rising-clear-threshold), [Area-percentage clear threshold](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#area-percentage-clear-threshold), [King crown anti-runaway](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#king-crown-anti-runaway), [Dominance caps](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#dominance-caps), [Bots fill empty seats](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#bots-fill-empty-seats). **Touches:** level-format, rules, outcome, tools-bots, economy, level-data, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** D5; R4.10-3; R4.9-16; §8.4 level_score; §7.3 Outcome.levelScore; D7; R4.7-9; PIN-17.

**Bot effect.** D14 unaffected: the minion wakes at the player's crossing, and the boss stays seeded. Gap: narrows. The reference mix loses much of its score to out-of-lives zeros (bold clears 3.6 of 6 levels per run), while the search bot clears all six and almost never wipes. Keeping the claim above the line turns some human zeros into points and leaves the search bot's score almost unchanged. The woken minion adds the same difficulty for both.

**Budget.** Level bytes v2 grow by 3 B. No new state: it reuses the u16 respawn countdown with a sentinel value, so the state hash keeps format v1. Per tick: one comparison per capture. The Outcome gains an end-reason value. Screen: a tick mark on the claim bar and a flashing spawn cell.

**Variety.** It splits a level into two phases with different risk rules. Players decide whether to cross the line early with a small cut to insure the claim, or to save a big cut that crosses it late and limits the extra minion's time. Commit stays meaningful below the line and is unneeded above it.

**Merged from:** `level-structure-and-progression#6`. Variants:

- Banked claim tiers: Claim lines every 2,500 bp are drawn on the claim bar. Crossing one banks it: running out of lives no longer scores zero, it scores your last banked line. Near a line, the question becomes whether to push one more cut to bank it before committing.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** Cheap and contract-free. Challenge.sol only sums levelScore and counts endReason == 2, so a new end reason 5 scored by the core needs no Solidity change; only the §7.3 comment and the clients change. Reusing the presence-2 slot works: after_capture ignores destroyed minions, and waking on the crossing is one compare per capture. The flaw is that the 0xFFFF sentinel collides with a legal level. §5 allows any R >= 1 up to 65,535, and with R = 65,535 a minion destroyed at step 3 has countdown 0xFFFF until step 5. The new 'do not count down 0xFFFF' rule then freezes it forever, and the hash cannot tell it from a dormant slot. Also needed: validity that dormant_mask bits lie within count and that crown_bp > 0 whenever the mask is non-zero (otherwise those slots never wake), and the wake delay of 15 belongs in consts.rs and README. Bot effect is credible: bold's zeros (3.6 clears of 6) become claims and search is unaffected, so the ratio narrows. The committer bot must stop committing above the line. Above the crown, Commit loses its purpose; that is a D5 decision for the owner.

*Fixes.* In level v2, require R \<= 65,534 (or R \<= T) so the sentinel cannot occur, or make dormancy a level-derived fact rather than a countdown value. Add validity for dormant_mask within count and crown_bp > 0 when the mask is non-zero. Update the committer policy. Add end reason 5 to §7.3, the web UI and the wedgie summary.

*Cost.* Level bytes +3 B (475 B v2); 0 B of state, state hash stays v1 by layout (dormant = presence 2 with 0xFFFF). One compare per capture; zk +0. New endReason 5; no contract change. RULES_VERSION, vectors, playtest and calibration (mix medians rise, weights fall).

*Conflicts.* D5; R4.10-1; R4.10-3; R4.9-16; R4.7-9; PIN-17; §5 level bytes v1 (R range); §7.3 Outcome.endReason and levelScore comment; D7

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The dormant minion wakes at the player's own crossing, at a level-defined spawn or at the first rail cell in row-major order (R4.7-7), and the boss stays seeded, so nothing becomes a safe zone or precomputable. This goes straight at the largest source of the gap: D5 zeros on the high-weight hard levels. bold clears 3.6 of 6 and committer 2.6 of 6, and on doom the mix's median effective claim is 1,857 bp, against search's 9,580 at a weight of 64,620 bp. Search almost never wipes, so its own scores barely change. Keeping the claim above the line turns human zeros into points, raises the hard levels' medians and so lowers their weights, which cuts search's surplus where it is largest. Draw variance falls too: hard levels stop being 0-or-everything lotteries for humans, so a run that draws 13-16 is less of a coin flip. Bots can play it easily: committer stops committing above the crown, and greedy is unaffected. The extra minion costs both sides alike, and search will usually cross the crown with its clearing capture, so it is a non-issue there. No state hash change, since it reuses the u16 countdown sentinel. The new end reason isn't Cleared, so minClears and settlement's clear count are unaffected. It does weaken D5 above the line (Commit loses its purpose there), which is an owner decision.

*Fixes.* Get the owner's decision on D5. Define the new end reason in §7.3's Outcome and §5's effective claim (a kept claim counts), and check that P11's floor and targets.json's clear rates still hold after the rerun. Have the playtest report show each level's share of zeros before and after.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** The dormant minion that wakes at the crossing is a good idea. It's a mid-level event no current level has, with a flashing 15-tick warning, and it needs no new state thanks to the countdown sentinel. Below the line it creates a real choice: cross early with a small cut to insure the claim, or cross late with a big cut to limit the new minion's time. The insurance, though, kills Commit above the line. Once crowned, losing every life keeps the claim, so X has no purpose except the tick tie-break. Commit is the game's signature decision, so the idea shifts tension to one point rather than adding it. The banked-tiers variant every 2,500 bp nearly removes D5 altogether. It narrows the gap honestly by turning human zeros into points, but it overlaps the overtime idea, which narrows the gap while keeping a push-your-luck after the bank. Despite the category, it isn't a multiplayer mechanic.

*Fixes.* Keep the dormant-minion trigger and drop the insurance. Or make insurance partial: losing the last life above the line keeps the crown_bp value, not the full claim, so playing on above the line still risks something and Commit keeps its meaning. Don't adopt the 2,500-bp tier variant. Don't ship it in the same season as overtime, since they compete for the same role.

*Readability.* Good. A tick mark on the claim bar and a flashing spawn cell on the rail are both clear at 4x4 px. The wake event is easy to read.

## Pace ghost of a best run

`multiplayer-and-competition#1` · T0 Presentation · score 12 (core 4, fairness 5, play 3) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

In Training you race a ghost of your best attempt, or of a friend's attempt sent as a link, on the same level and seed. Its dot and claim bar move beside yours, so you can see at every moment whether you are ahead.

**Rule sketch.** Client only; the core does not change. A Training attempt is fully named by (level bytes, run nonce, levelIndex, journal), and §6.3 already allows any nonce in Training. Browser: a challenge link carries the level name (or its hex), the nonce, the levelIndex and the journal: at most 71 + 3 × 2,700 = 8,171 bytes, about 11 KB in base64url, in the URL fragment. The page loads a second wasm core with the ghost journal and steps it once per live tick, in lockstep. It draws the ghost player's cell as a hollow 4×4 outline, its trail as a dim tint and its claim_bp as a second bar. Both games share the boss start and the deflection key (seed bytes 0 to 8), so they stay identical until the first input that differs and then visibly diverge. At the end it shows both effective claims and ticks used. On the wedgie there is no room for a second core, so it gets a pace line instead. The app samples its best attempt's claim_bp every 45 ticks (60 u16 values, 120 bytes per level in /saves/jiro/) and draws a marker on the claim bar at the best attempt's claim for the current tick. Challenge gets only that per-level pace line, built from the player's own earlier runs on other seeds, never a same-seed ghost.

**Inspired by:** Fly Trap: Territory Game (2026), Glimmer: Capture the Light (2026), Kunitori Returns with Senran Kagura NewWave (2014), AQuix (1997), Retrocade (2026), Jezzball NES (2026), Splix.io (c. 2016 (unconfirmed)). **Mechanic families:** [Ghost racing and recorded replays](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#ghost-racing-and-recorded-replays), [Friend challenges](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#friend-challenges), [Live ranking during play](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#live-ranking-during-play), [Replay-verifiable runs](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#replay-verifiable-runs), [Spectator mode](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#spectator-mode), [End-of-match stats screen](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#end-of-match-stats-screen). **Touches:** presentation.

**Bot effect.** No effect on D14. Shared seeds exist only in unpaid Training, where §6.3 already lets the client choose the nonce, and Challenge seeds stay per run. Gap: neutral to slightly narrowing. A human learns pacing and commit timing from a known-good line on the same seed; the search bot needs no reference.

**Budget.** Browser: one more wasm core instance (grid 3,600 B, capture stack 6,728 B, journal 8,100 B) and one more core tick per frame, well under 1 ms. Wedgie: 120 B of RAM for the active level's pace line, about 1.9 KB of flash for 16 levels, 2 to 4 cells redrawn per tick for the marker. No zk cost and no format change.

**Variety.** An opponent with no new rules: you see where a good attempt stood at 1:00 and decide to push or bank against it. A friend's link turns a Training seed into an asynchronous duel on identical boss starts, which none of the 16 solo levels offers.

**Merged from:** `presentation-wedgie#11`. Variants:

- Pace ghost, splits and replay theatre: In Training, a ghost notch on the claim bar shows the best run's claim at the same tick, with a split at each capture. On the season page, any settled leaderboard run can be watched, with markers on its captures, hits and commits.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 0.** Technically sound. The journal header carries runNonce, levelId and levelIndex, so (level bytes, journal) name the attempt. Equal nonce and index give equal boss starts and deflection keys, so the two games match until the inputs differ. A journal is at most 8,171 B (about 11 KB of base64url), which fits a URL fragment, and a second Recorder in wasm is cheap.
Issues:
(1) A link can name any nonce and levelIndex, including a just-revealed Challenge run's. Friends could then play that run's levels in parallel in an unmodified Training page and send the journals back, to be re-headered as mode 1 by a modified client. That was already possible with modified clients (Training accepts any nonce), but a link makes multi-human search one click within the §7.2 deadline.
(2) The wedgie has no claim bar in play: the field fills the 240x240 screen and nothing else is drawn. A pace marker therefore covers field cells, and the border rows are rail.
(3) The save API stores the whole bests dict, so 16 pace lines of 120 B live in RAM, about 2 KB of the about 17.4 KB free, unless they are saved per level, where each file open costs a 1,148 B object and the stack-lending trick.
(4) Links stop decoding after a RULES_VERSION bump (code 4).
The effect on the bot gap is neutral to slightly narrowing, as stated.

*Fixes.* Derive ghost-race nonces as keccak256('jiro.training' || seed), and accept only links that carry the seed, so that a link cannot target a Challenge nonce, which is keccak('jiro.run' || ...). On the wedgie, show pace as a split on the summary screen, or as a 1 px tint along the inner edge of the border, instead of a claim bar. Store the pace lines per level or accept the about 2 KB of RAM. Show a clear message for links from an older rules version.

*Cost.* Browser: a second Recorder (about 18.5 KB of wasm memory), one extra step per frame (under 1 ms), an 11 KB URL. Wedgie: about 2 KB of RAM for 16 pace lines in the bests dict (or 120 B per level if saved apart), 1.9 KB in /saves, a marker of 2-4 cells per tick. zk, formats and vectors: none.

*Conflicts.* §7.2 real-time play / §7.4 bots (links make parallel play of a revealed run's seed easy, unless Training nonces are domain-derived)

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** Same-seed ghosts exist only in Training, where §6.3 already lets the client pick any nonce. Challenge gets only a claim-versus-tick line from the player's runs on other seeds, which says nothing about the current run's boss. A modified client could load a search journal for the live Challenge seed as a ghost to trace by hand. A modified client can already submit search's inputs directly, though, so this adds no capability. Learning pacing and commit timing from good lines, and the replay-theatre variant over public journals, may narrow the gap slightly.

*Fixes.* Keep the lockstep ghost core out of the honest client's Challenge flow, and build the Challenge pace line only from runs with other nonces.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** An opponent with no new rules. A same-seed asynchronous duel is genuinely new to the game, and seeing a good line's claim at the same tick sharpens the push-or-bank decision and teaches commit timing. Safe for D14, since shared seeds exist only in Training, and roughly neutral on the gap. Two readability problems. First, a dim ghost trail tinted on your own black field looks like a trail, which is the most dangerous thing on screen to misread. Once the runs diverge, the ghost's trail can also cross cells that are CLAIMED on your field. Second, the wedgie part assumes a claim bar, but the wedgie draws no in-play HUD at all. The pace marker therefore needs a new bar, and the only space for one is the border, which is rail where the player and minions walk.

*Fixes.* In the browser, draw only the ghost's hollow outline and a second claim figure: no trail, or a trail in a hue far from red (cyan dots, say). On the wedgie, add the pace line as a 1-px line on the outer edge of the top border, drawn under the entities, or show the ahead/behind split only on the end overlay. Add splits at each capture in the browser HUD, which already shows claim, target and ticks left.

*Readability.* Fine in the browser if the ghost has no trail, or a trail far from red. On the wedgie, a marker at 4 px is readable only if it does not cover rail cells. Use the outer 1 px of the border or end-of-level splits.

## Shield discharge repels the boss

`multiplayer-and-competition#8` · T3 New core rule · score 11 (core 4, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Standing on the rail, press B while holding A to spend your shield charge and turn the boss away from you. The shield becomes a choice between surviving one hit and opening a lane for a deep cut.

**Rule sketch.** No new input bits. Today, A and B together are the same as B (R4.5-7), and A does nothing on the rail. New rule at step 3, before movement: if the player is not cutting, stands on a rail cell, holds no direction, holds A, and B went from released to held on this tick (press_tick = tick, already tracked), and shield charges ≥ 1, then the boss is repelled. Using doubled coordinates to avoid halves, cx2 = 2·bx + B and px2 = 2·px + 1. Invert vx if (vx = +1 and px2 > cx2) or (vx = −1 and px2 \< cx2), and invert vy the same way with y. If at least one component flips, charges −= 1; otherwise nothing is spent. Step 4 then moves the boss with the new velocity through the usual R4.6-3 edge tests, so the body still only covers EMPTY cells. It needs no seed and no new state. Pairs with the minion-bounty idea, which refills the charge.

**Inspired by:** Stix Disruption (2024), Demolition Herby (1982), Paint Roller (Gakken) (1982), Glowfield (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Asymmetric co-op support shooter](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#asymmetric-co-op-support-shooter), [Player-controlled enemies (asymmetric versus)](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#player-controlled-enemies-asymmetric-versus). **Touches:** rules, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D6; R4.5-7; R4.6-2.

**Bot effect.** D14: the push is deterministic and relative to the player, and the boss's later path still follows its seeded deflections, so nothing becomes precomputable. Gap: roughly unchanged, perhaps narrowing slightly. The search bot will find the best moment to use the push, but the push is most valuable to a player who cannot predict the boss and wants it reliably moving away before cutting. That describes humans, and the rule is easy to read on screen.

**Budget.** Zero bytes of state; the state hash keeps format v1 (shield charges, press_tick, vx and vy already exist). Per tick: a few comparisons. Vectors that hold A and B on the rail would change, so this needs a RULES_VERSION bump. Screen: a one-tick flash around the boss.

**Variety.** The shield gains an active use. Players choose between holding the charge as insurance during a long cut and spending it to turn the boss before starting one. Combined with the minion bounty, it creates a small economy of earning and spending the charge.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** Checked against core/src/player.rs and game.rs. B already beats A when both are held (sub_steps), and A does nothing on the rail. press_tick is set at step 1, so testing press_tick == tick at step 3 needs no new state. B must also be held: at tick 0 with no press, press_tick = 0 = tick. The doubled-coordinate test uses integers only and is well defined. When B is odd and the centres line up there is a tie: nothing flips and nothing is spent. D14 holds, because the flip depends only on shared state and the seeded draws continue. Four problems. (a) The sketch skips the deflection draw. R4.6-7 runs before the edge tests on every boss move, so 1 move in 32 inverts a component again right after the repel. If the boss does not move this tick (period > 1), the new velocity waits for its next move and that move's draw. (b) The chord depends on order. Inputs are sampled at 15 Hz, so A must already be held on the tick B's edge lands. Pressing B one tick before A fails without any sign. (c) It revises D6, which says an absorbed hit costs the charge and nothing else; the owner has to decide. (d) The bump to RULES_VERSION 3 regenerates every game vector, because every journal header carries rulesVersion, not only the vectors that hold A and B. With one charge per level this is a once-per-level herding tool. Search will time it perfectly. A human who spends the charge loses the shield as insurance against a D5 zero. So the effect on the gap is ambiguous, not 'slightly narrowing'.

*Fixes.* Make the chord order-tolerant without new state. Trigger when A and B are both held this tick and were not both held under the previous mask (Game.mask before step 1, already state and already in the hash). Say in the rule that step 4's deflection draw still runs after a repel, so 1 move in 32 can undo it. Amend the text of D6. Before recalibrating, give bold and committer a repel policy, for example repel when the boss is heading toward the cut start.

*Cost.* State: 0 B, state hash v1 unchanged. Per tick: about 10 integer compares, plus an 8-neighbour rail check on the trigger tick only. zk: under 0.2% more cycles. RULES_VERSION 2 to 3: every game vector and trace regenerated, plus new repel vectors. Playtest and calibration rerun. Wedgie: a one-tick flash on a few dirty cells.

*Conflicts.* D6; R4.5-7; R4.4-8; R4.6-7

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The flip depends only on the boss and player positions and needs no seed. Seeded deflections keep running on every later move, so nothing becomes copyable and no safe zone appears. The rule needs no cut to be open, so no TRAIL exists and the repel can never make the boss bump the player's own trail. Gap: about neutral. Search is held near its ceiling (98.5%, and the ceiling is area minus B squared), so a once-per-level steering lever is worth at most about 1.5% to it. Today the shield is mostly a human tool, because search rarely gets hit. Making it a spend-or-keep choice gives humans a readable move: about 32 boss moves of the boss travelling away before the next deflection. It also tempts them to give up the insurance that protects them from D5 zeros, so the pitch's claim that it narrows the gap is not established. Scripted bots can play it with a simple trigger (repel when the boss heads toward the planned cut), so it can be calibrated. It adds no per-seed variance to the six-level draw. It touches D6 and R4.5-7, which are owner decisions, and changes vectors that hold A+B on the rail, so RULES_VERSION must be bumped. Paired with the minion-bounty refill, search would get repeated steering; judge that pair separately.

*Fixes.* Add a repel policy to at least one reference-mix bot before recalibrating, and rerun bot-advantage with and without it so the gap is measured, not assumed. Show the boss's new velocity for a few ticks after a repel, so humans can use the window. Get the owner to sign off on the D6 change. Evaluate the minion-bounty pairing on its own, since refills turn a single steer into repeated steering.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** The idea adds a real trade-off. Today the one charge mostly insures against minions on the rail (B held at R4.9-15). Spending it to turn the boss gives up that insurance for a few seconds of open field before a cut, which ties the shield, the rail and the cut together. The push is relative to the player and is still bounded by R4.6-3, so D14 holds, and it needs no new state. Limits: the charge is spent once per level, so on its own the decision comes up once (it only grows into an economy with the bounty idea). The boss comes back off the far wall within about 4 to 8 s at period 1, so the push mainly helps short and medium cuts. The A-held-then-press-B chord is hard to discover and easy to fire by accident if a player rests on A while defending with B. It does not help against minions, which are the main threat on the rail in the hard levels; that makes the trade-off sharper but leaves hard levels no easier. It is distinct from the 16 levels, none of which gives the player any effect on the boss. Spec gap: if a minion hit lands on the push tick, the charge is already gone at step 6. That is correct under the tick order, but it should be pinned and tested.

*Fixes.* Accept the chord in either order: A and B both held on the rail, no direction, with one of them pressed this tick. Keep the flash for about 6 ticks, not 1, and empty the HUD shield pip at the same time. Pin the same-tick minion-hit case in a PIN line. Pair it with a way to refill the charge (the bounty idea or a capture-size refill) so the decision recurs in a level.

*Readability.* Good. The boss body is 8 to 32 px across, so its change of heading is plainly visible. A one-tick flash (67 ms) is too short to notice: use about 6 ticks and a HUD pip. It needs no new palette colour beyond the flash.

## Live pot-share gauge for Commit

`multiplayer-and-competition#2` · T0 Presentation · score 10 (core 4, fairness 4, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

During a Challenge run, a gauge turns the current claim into points: what pressing X now would add to the run, how that compares with your best settled run, and roughly what share of the pot it would earn.

**Rule sketch.** Client only. At run start the Challenge page reads the season's weightsBp, rakeBp and totalQualifiedPoints, the fees collected so far and the player's best settled run (the season page already reads Season). Each tick it computes in integers: bank_now = the sum of finished level scores + claim_bp × weight_bp / 10,000 for the current level, which is 0 while a wipe is pending. It also computes with_rest = bank_now + the calibration's median effective claim × weight / 10,000 for each level not yet played. It shows bank_now against the best settled run (a run counts only if it beats it, D10). It shows a 'qualifies' flag while the run has no clear yet and minClears is 1. It shows an estimated pot share = with_rest × fees × (10,000 − rakeBp) / 10,000 / (totalQualifiedPoints − ownBest + with_rest). Nothing is journaled, and the core's Outcome stays the only score.

**Inspired by:** AQuix (1997), Paper-io (MakeCode Arcade) (2021), Kingpins.io (likely 2026), Volfield (2016). **Mechanic families:** [Live ranking during play](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#live-ranking-during-play), [Segmented score tables](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#segmented-score-tables), [Online leaderboards](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#online-leaderboards). **Touches:** presentation.

**Bot effect.** No effect on D14. Narrows the gap a little. The search bot already weighs what each commit is worth against the risk, using the weights and its own forecast. The gauge gives humans the same arithmetic, so they lose points through their play rather than by guessing what 6,000 bp is worth on a heavily weighted level.

**Budget.** Browser only: a few contract reads at run start, a dozen integer operations per frame, one bar and three numbers. Nothing on the wedgie (Challenge is browser only, D15) and no zk cost.

**Variety.** Commit becomes specific to each level: banking 4,000 bp on a heavy-weight level can beat pushing on a light one. A run that already beats your best can take risks it could not before, and the run's first clear, which qualifies it, has a visible price.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 0.** Client only, with no consensus, device or zk impact. Season.seasons(n) already exposes fees and totalQualifiedPoints, the leaderboard code already reads BestRunRecorded, and the calibration file ships with the client, so bank_now and with_rest are trivial integer maths.

The pot-share figure is unreliable. totalQualifiedPoints counts only settled runs, and settlement is by exception: the operator proves and settles each player's best run, and a heavy run's proof takes hours. Mid-season the denominator lags far behind the committed field, and early in a season it is near 0. The estimate is then inflated toward 'you take most of the pot'. In a paid mode that is a misleading earnings projection.

The formula also has small holes:

- It subtracts ownBest even when the player's best is not qualified, and so not in the total.
- It ignores rollover, which is part of playersPot.
- It ignores the fees future runs will add.
- 'Best settled run' ignores the player's own committed runs that are not yet settled.

*Fixes.* Lead with points: bank_now, with_rest, the player's own best (including their own committed runs, replayed locally from the run store), and the current score at a few ranks. If an ETH figure stays, compute it only with ownBest subtracted when it is qualified and with rollover included, label it 'at current settled standings', and show how many committed runs are still unsettled.

*Cost.* Browser only: about 3 contract or log reads at run start, a dozen BigInt operations per frame, one bar and three numbers. Device: 0 (Challenge is browser-only, D15). zk: 0. Contracts and formats: none.

**Fairness: concerns, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** This is client only and adds no thinking time (there is no pause), so D14 and consensus are untouched. Giving humans the commit arithmetic that search already does narrows the gap a little, and it adds no variance to the draw, since the six slots are known from the run nonce at reveal. The problem is the ETH pot-share estimate, which is unreliable in a paid product. Settlement is by exception and is usually done late, by the operator rebuilding and settling best runs after the season (§7.2, §7.5). Mid-season, totalQualifiedPoints and the player's own best settled run can therefore be near zero or stale, and the formula would show a player taking most of the pot. Fees also keep growing until the season ends. A live, overstated ETH figure during a paid run nudges players toward risk and toward buying more runs.

*Fixes.* Show points, not ETH. Show bank_now, with_rest, the comparison with the player's best committed run (replayed from public journals through the indexer, not just settled runs), and the qualifies flag. If any share figure is shown, compute it from offchain replays of every committed run's best score, label it an estimate, and give it a range. Define the 'wipe pending' case precisely: the current level is counted as 0 only after the last life has gone.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** It shows numbers that already drive the Commit decision (the weights, the best run) rather than adding a decision. Knowing that a run only counts if it beats your best changes the risk you should take across levels. Showing it does narrow the human gap a little, as the pitch says. In play, a money estimate and three numbers during a 15 Hz cut are distracting and shift the feel from arcade to spreadsheet. The pot-share figure is noisy (fees so far, totalQualifiedPoints and the median projection all move), and 'with_rest' can mislead. 'Which is 0 while a wipe is pending' is not a defined state.

*Fixes.* 1. Show only two things during play: the points at stake on this level (claim x weight, lost if the lives run out) and a tick mark for the best settled run on one bar. 2. Move the pot-share estimate and the projection to the screen between levels. 3. Define the current level's contribution as claim x weight, counting as 0 only if the level ends out of lives.

*Readability.* Browser only (Challenge is browser-only, D15), so the wedgie screen is not a factor. In the browser, one bar is readable while standing on the rail. Three numbers and a money estimate cannot be read in the middle of a cut.

## Seeded capture flags

`multiplayer-and-competition#10` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts (proposed as T3) · score 10 (core 3, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: unclear · D14: compliant

Each level lists a few candidate flag spots, and the level seed raises two or three of them. Enclosing a flag pays a claim bonus, so every run asks which flags are worth a detour toward the boss.

**Rule sketch.** Level format v2 adds n candidate flag cells (n ≤ 8; distinct cells, open at level start, checked by validity), flags_up (0 to 3, at most n) and flag_bonus_bp (u16). At level start the raised flags are deck\[0..flags_up\] of a partial Fisher-Yates shuffle over the candidates: for i from 0 while i \< flags_up, j = i + fmix32(keyF XOR i) mod (n − i), then swap, where keyF is the big-endian u32 of seed bytes 17 to 20. Like the boss start, this is a constant of the level and seed, not state. A raised flag counts as captured when its cell is CLAIMED, read from the grid, so no new state is needed. effective_claim = min(10,000, claim_bp + flags_captured × flag_bonus_bp). It is used for the clear test at step 3 (PIN-10) and for scoring (§8.4), and stays 0 when out of lives (D5). The Outcome reports both the area claim and the number of flags. Flags can sit anywhere in the field, including cells the boss crosses, and a flag is taken only by a capture whose region excludes the boss.

**Inspired by:** TERRITORY SCRAMBLE (2026, unreleased), Loop Nook: Territory Capture (2026), Defly.io (unknown), Harapeko Bakka (1994), Tetro Domain (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Objective modes on territory](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#objective-modes-on-territory), [Respawn rules](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#respawn-rules). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, outcome, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D5; D14; PIN-10; R4.1-8; §8.4 level score input; §6.4 seed bytes 17-20.

**Bot effect.** D14: which flags are raised changes with every seed, so no route can be memorized or copied between runs. Gap: likely widens slightly. Choosing an order of captures that takes the flags is a planning problem the search bot solves exactly, though humans can see the flags from tick 0. The scripted bots need a flag-aware variant for calibration.

**Budget.** Level bytes v2 grow by about 20 B. RAM: 3 raised-flag indices (constants of the level). Per capture: at most 3 grid reads. The state hash keeps its format, but the Outcome grows by a flags byte. Screen: one palette colour, at most 3 cells, redrawn on capture.

**Variety.** Routing in place of pure area: a flag deep in the boss's quarter is worth a risky cut, while one near the rail is a cheap bonus. Because the raised set changes every run, the same level asks a different routing question each time, and flags can shorten a clear (fewer ticks for the tiebreak).

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 5.** The core mechanism is sound. The raised flags are a stateless function of keyF (seed bytes 17 to 20 are free), like the boss start. A cell becomes CLAIMED only during a capture, so reading at most 3 grid cells after each capture, plus the clear test at step 3, covers every case without new state. Problems. (a) As stated, the Outcome gains a flags byte. That changes the Solidity Outcome struct, the zk public values (abi.encode(uint16, Outcome)), ZkReferee's decoding and Season. That is a new contract generation and makes this T5, not T3. (b) 'Enclosing a flag' is wrong. R4.8-4 turns every TRAIL cell CLAIMED, so a trail that merely crosses a flag captures it, even on the boss's side. A spike two cells wide, out and back, grabs a deep flag for a handful of cells of area. The routing question becomes 'touch it with a trail and survive', which is cheaper and more bot-friendly than pitched. (c) The level score input of §8.4 and the score ceiling change. Search will take every flag, so most of the added ceiling goes to search, and the gap probably widens by more than 'slightly'. (d) Level bytes v2 have a different length, so the decoder has to read the version before the length. The committed vector level-unknown-version (472 B, version 2, code 21) would then change its code, which forces a RULES_VERSION bump on its own. (e) Validity has to bound flags_up ≤ n and the bonus, so flags alone cannot clear a level, and keep unused slots at zero so the bytes stay canonical.

*Fixes.* Keep the Outcome at its 11 fields. Report the effective claim in claimBp and compute levelScore from it. The flag count can be derived from the public journal, so leave it offchain. That brings the idea down to T2+T3 with no contract change beyond the usual new season. State the trail-crossing rule plainly, or, if real enclosure is wanted, add 3 bits of state that mark a flag as spoiled when it is TRAIL at capture. Cap the total bonus (for example flags_up × bonus ≤ 1,500 bp). Add validity codes 40 and up for the flag fields.

*Cost.* Level bytes 472 to 492 B (v2). Level struct +20 B. Game +6 B of constants, not hashed. At most 3 fmix32 at level start and at most 3 grid reads per capture. zk about 0. As stated, Outcome +1 B, which means changes to the Solidity struct, the guest's public values, ZkReferee and a new Season. RULES_VERSION bump, new level and game vectors, a new ceiling formula in the bot-advantage report. Scripted bots need flag routing before calibration. Wedgie: one palette entry and at most 3 cells.

*Conflicts.* D5; PIN-10; R4.1-8; R4.8-4; §5 level bytes v1; §7.3 Outcome; §8.4 level_score input; §6.4 seed bytes 17-20

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap unclear; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The raised set comes from free seed bytes 17-20, and the boss keeps deflecting, so no game is shared. The main fairness problem is per-seed scoring luck that the per-level weights cannot normalize. Every raised flag pays the same flag_bonus_bp, yet the pitch itself says a rail-side flag is a cheap bonus and a deep one a risky cut. A run whose seed raises three rail-side flags gets free points that another run on the same level does not. Weights only normalize each level's median, so this becomes new variance inside the draw. Gap: the bonus lifts the ceiling to the 10,000 cap. Search, which already captures about 98% of the field, will collect every flag incidentally on its finisher and saturate the cap on most levels, which turns its runs into a race on the fewer-ticks tiebreak. Humans collect flags roughly in proportion to their effective claim (about 0.6 of search's), which is near the current human/search ratio. The ratio may therefore barely move, but the absolute gap grows and planning the capture order is search's strength. Counting flags in the clear test (PIN-10) lets humans clear earlier and avoid D5, which pulls the other way, so the net effect is unclear. The rule sketch also contradicts itself. It says 'taken only by a capture whose region excludes the boss', but a flag counts once its cell is CLAIMED, and TRAIL always converts to CLAIMED. A thin cut through a flag deep in the boss's area therefore takes it, which search will exploit exactly. The Outcome change touches IReferee and the zk public values, so this is effectively T5, not T3. Scripted bots need a flag-aware variant, which is feasible.

*Fixes.* Remove the placement luck. Either stratify the candidates so every raised set has the same difficulty mix (for example one flag per authored ring of distance from the rail), or raise all candidates every run, or scale each flag's bonus by its authored depth. Decide whether cutting through a flag captures it and write the rule once. Keep flags out of the clear test, or cap the total bonus well below the boss-body gap, so search does not routinely hit the 10,000 cap and turn the leaderboard into a tick race. Treat it as T5 because it changes the Outcome and zk public values. Measure with bot-advantage before adopting.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This gives the game spatial goals besides area, so the path a player takes starts to matter. Because the seed raises a different subset each run, the same layout asks a new routing question every time, and nothing can be memorized. That fits D14 well. A flag in open field can only be captured from the side away from the boss, so it pulls players into the long, exposed cuts where the boss, fast cut and shield matter most. It also changes Commit: a player may push for one more flag before banking. It is distinct from the 16 levels. Concerns: (1) Luck in paid play. Candidate spots differ in difficulty, so a run whose seed raised the easy flags scores more on the same level, and season weights only correct for that on average. (2) The bonus counts toward the clear test, so a lucky set of flags can turn a hard level into a clear. (3) The Outcome grows by a byte and the §8.4 score input changes, so contracts and calibration are affected beyond T3. (4) The search bot plans capture order exactly, which widens the gap a little, as the pitch says.

*Fixes.* Have the author list candidates in difficulty-matched groups and raise one flag per group, or draw from pairs that mirror each other, so every raised set has about the same value. Keep flag_bonus_bp small, say at most 300 bp each, and cap the total. Consider counting flags toward score only, not the clear test (PIN-10). Mark captured flags in the claimed colour and show the count in the HUD.

*Readability.* Workable. A flag is one 4x4 px cell, so it needs a palette colour used nowhere else, ideally drawn as a 2-tone glyph inside the cell. Minions only walk the rail, so a flag in the field will not be mistaken for one. A flag under the boss body is hidden until the boss moves off it, which is acceptable. Three cells and one colour cost almost nothing to redraw.

## Two sparks on one keyboard

`multiplayer-and-competition#12` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts (proposed as T4) · score 9 (core 2, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Browser Training for two on one keyboard. In co-op, both players claim the same field with pooled land and lives, and when either closes a cut, both trails are banked. Variants: claim war, where land goes to whoever closes, or hunt, where player 2 steers the boss.

**Rule sketch.** Journal v2 adds mode 3, duo Training, which v1 decoders reject (code 5). Events stay (u16 tick, u8 mask), and mask bit 7, reserved today, says the event is player 2's. Two events may share a tick only as a P1 event followed by a P2 event. Each event must change its own player's mask, and capacity stays 2,700 events in all; a duo level ends as committed at step 2 of the tick after its 2,700th event. The core gains a second Player and a second mask. At step 3, P1 moves and then P2 (order is part of the rules). Co-op: no one may step onto any TRAIL (D4 extended); a capture by either player converts every TRAIL cell, so the other player's open cut is banked too and that player stops cutting, possibly off the rail (R4.5-3). Lives are shared, and one hit erases both trails and returns any player who is cutting to their cut start. Claim war: captured cells are credited to the player who closed the cut, including the rival's open trail; lives are kept per player; a 450 B ownership bitmap marks P2's trail cells, so a boss bump hits the trail's owner. Hunt: P2 holds a direction, and on the boss's next move with cooldown 0, vx or vy is set toward it, then the cooldown is set to 16 moves; seeded deflections are off. The mode is not allowed in Challenge.

**Inspired by:** Moonlight Territory (2026), Cacoma Knight in Bizyland (1992), XONIX 1984 2 player remake (2024), Glowfield (2026), Illgen (1989), Paint Roller (Gakken) (1982). **Mechanic families:** [Simultaneous co-op capture](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#simultaneous-co-op-capture), [Simultaneous shared-field versus capture](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#simultaneous-shared-field-versus-capture), [Simultaneous two-player play](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#simultaneous-two-player-play), [Player-controlled enemies (asymmetric versus)](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#player-controlled-enemies-asymmetric-versus), [Computer rival claimers](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#computer-rival-claimers). **Touches:** journal, input, rules, state-hash, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D8; D15; D4; D5; D14; §6.3 mode and tick order; R4.4-5.

**Bot effect.** No effect on Challenge or D14: the mode is Training only, and journals with mode 3 are rejected by the referee. A scripted bot can play the second spark (its inputs are recorded as P2 events, so a replay stays exact), which also gives a solo co-op practice mode. No effect on the gap in paid play.

**Budget.** The journal buffer stays 8,100 B, because bit-7 events share it. State: about 20 B more for co-op and hunt (state hash v2 adds the second player and mask); claim war adds a 450 B bitmap, which would take about a third of the wedgie's roughly 1.2 KB of stack headroom, so the device may need it compiled out. Browser only in practice: the wedgie has one joystick and four keys. Per tick: a second player step. No zk cost, since the mode is never proved.

**Variety.** Shared-field play the game lacks. In co-op, one player stretches a long trail into the field while the other closes a short cut to bank both. In claim war, you wait for the rival's trail to grow, then close and take it. In hunt, a human steers the boss, so you have to read intent instead of seeded noise.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 5.** Duo is Training-only, never proved, and the referee rejects it, so neither D14 nor Challenge is at risk. But it is a new mode with three rule sets (co-op, claim war, hunt) inside the one core, which also ships to the wedgie and the zk guest. Each rule set needs its own rule text, vectors and bots. Format: when journal v2 becomes valid, the committed vector journal-unknown-version (version 2, code 3) changes its result. That forces a RULES_VERSION bump and a new season, although no Challenge rule changes. The rules that ticks strictly increase (§6.2) and that bit 7 is reserved also have to fork by version. Gaps in the spec: whether shield and invulnerability are per player or shared; whether the players may share or swap cells, and what P1's body is to P2; whether P2 still moves when P1's capture clears the level at step 3; PIN-15's minion predicate, which is defined on one Moves, has to run per player; a boss edge can touch both trails in claim war; what happens when one player's lives run out in claim war; hunt's 16-move cooldown is new state. Wedgie: it has one joystick, so duo is dead code there, and the module's code is loaded into RAM. The claim-war bitmap of 450 B grows Game. `start` builds Game on the stack by value: its peak is 62,784 B of 65,536, leaving about 2.7 KB for interrupt frames (not 1.2 KB). The bitmap also grows the app's fixed RECORDER_BYTES. With both players allowed to close a cut in the same tick, there can be two fills in one tick, which is fine in wasm.

*Fixes.* Ship co-op only at first. Keep journal v1 and state hash v1 for modes 0 and 1, and use v2 only for mode 3. Gate the duo code behind a cargo feature that jiro-natmod and the zk guest leave off. Write out the per-player shield and invulnerability, the cell-sharing rule, the order of two captures in one tick, and PIN-15 for each player. For claim war, keep the ownership map in a browser-only buffer outside Game. Accept the one RULES_VERSION bump that journal v2 forces.

*Cost.* Game about +20 B (a second Player, its mask, the hunt cooldown), plus 450 B for claim war. Journal v2: bit 7 marks P2, two events may share a tick. State hash v2 (about +16 B) for mode 3. Per tick: a second player step, and up to 2× the worst tick when two captures land in one tick (browser only). Core code about +2 to 4 KB, which must be gated off the device and the guest. zk 0. RULES_VERSION bump. Three sets of rule text and vectors, a P2 bot, a two-player keyboard UI.

*Conflicts.* D4; D5; D6; D8; D15; R4.4-5; R4.10-1; §6.1 bit 7; §6.2 event order; §6.3 mode; §6.4 state hash v1

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** Training only. The referee rejects mode 3, and Challenge settlement already requires mode = 1, so there is no effect on paid fairness, D14 or the gap. Hunt mode turning deflections off is acceptable for the same reason. Scripted bots can play the second spark, and its inputs are journaled, so replays stay exact. The real risk is to the consensus surface, not fairness. The decoder, the state hash and the core are the same code the zk guest runs. Bit 7 and same-tick events must stay invalid in modes 0-1 (code 10, strictly increasing ticks). If state hash v2 also changes the hash of solo games, every committed vector's trace moves. By CLAUDE.md that is a behavior change, which forces a RULES_VERSION bump and a new season for a mode no paid run uses. Claim war's 450 B bitmap does not fit the wedgie's stack headroom, but the mode is browser-only in practice anyway.

*Fixes.* Gate the duo code behind a cargo feature that the referee and zk guest builds leave out, so paid replay gains no code paths or cycles. Keep journal v1 and state hash v1 byte-identical for modes 0-1, so no committed vector changes and RULES_VERSION stays. Add decoder vectors proving that bit 7 and a shared tick are still rejected in modes 0-1. Build claim war for the browser only.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** This is the most new kind of situation in this batch, since the game has no shared-field play today. In co-op, one player holds a long trail while the other closes a short cut to bank both. In claim war, a player waits for the rival's trail to grow and then steals it. In hunt, a human steers the boss, so the cutter reads intent instead of seeded noise. All three fit the Qix feel, and hunt is a striking asymmetric twist. Value for this project is lower: it is Training-only and browser-only, two players share one keyboard on a 60x60 precision grid, and it puts a second player plus three sub-rule sets into the referee-critical core for a mode that is never proved. Claim war's 450 B bitmap would need the wedgie build to compile it out, which strains the one-core principle. The spec leaves gaps: who may commit with X in duo, whether an X from one player discards the partner's trail, and how the D5 zero applies with shared lives. 'One hit erases both trails' is harsh but easy to read. The hunt cooldown of 16 moves needs tuning so the boss cannot simply camp the cutter.

*Fixes.* Ship co-op first. Hunt is the cheapest second step, because it adds only a boss-steer input and no second Player, and it is the most distinctive. Drop claim war and its 450 B bitmap. Specify X in duo (either player commits, both trails discarded) and the out-of-lives rule. Let a scripted bot fill the P2 seat so the mode can be played solo.

*Readability.* Not for the wedgie, which has one joystick, so this is browser-only by design. In the browser, two player colours and two trail colours are easy to tell apart. A human-steered boss needs no extra drawing.

## Weekly shared six

`multiplayer-and-competition#14` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts · score 9 (core 3, fairness 3, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Every Challenge run started in the same week of a season plays the same six levels, while each run keeps its own seeds. Players can practise this week's six in Training and compare per-level results with everyone, and nobody can buy a better draw.

**Rule sketch.** New season parameter drawPeriod (uint64 seconds, 0 = per-run draw as today; for example 7 days in a 14-day season, D12). At startRun, Challenge stores epoch = (block.timestamp − start) / drawPeriod (u32) with the run. When drawPeriod > 0, the §8.2 draw uses drawSeed = keccak256("jiro.week" ‖ seasonNumber ‖ epoch), under its own domain tag, in place of runNonce: h = keccak256("jiro.draw" ‖ drawSeed ‖ i), followed by the same forward Fisher-Yates. Level seeds still come from runNonce and levelIndex (§6.4), so no two runs share a game. The referee takes drawSeed, which settlement derives from the stored season number and epoch, so the core still computes the draw and the check by slot (P17) stays as it is. Offchain: the Training page offers 'this week's six' with random nonces, and an indexer of public journals builds weekly per-level boards.

**Inspired by:** Bix (2008), World Tour Qix (2026), Volfield (2016), Glimmer: Capture the Light (2026), Starlasso: Territory Arena (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Weekly shared-board race](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#weekly-shared-board-race), [Time-boxed ranked competitions](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#time-boxed-ranked-competitions), [Segmented score tables](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#segmented-score-tables). **Touches:** contracts, economy, outcome, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** P17; D12; §8.2 draw; §6.4 domain tags.

**Bot effect.** D14 unaffected: level seeds stay per run, so journals still cannot be copied between runs. Gap: neutral to slightly narrowing. Humans gain from practising six known levels; the search bot gains nothing from practice. It also ends the paid re-roll of the draw (§8.2, 'several runs at once'), which favours whoever can afford to buy many runs and keep the best draw.

**Budget.** Contracts: one season parameter, a u32 stored per run, and one keccak at settlement. The core: one more input to the draw. No per-tick cost, and RAM and screen are unchanged. Indexer work for the weekly boards is offchain.

**Variety.** A weekly meta the game lacks: everyone faces the same six, Training becomes targeted preparation, and per-level boards let players compare like for like. Within the week, the run-level choice shifts from 'is this a good draw to keep?' to 'how do I play these six better than last time?'.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 5.** The mechanism is clean: the draw takes drawSeed in place of runNonce under its own tag, level seeds stay per run so D14 holds, and nothing runs per tick. Problems. (a) The contract work is understated. The core computes drawSlot (P17) but cannot get drawSeed from the journal. IReferee.verify and verifyRun therefore need a drawSeed argument, the zk guest must commit it in its public values, and settlement must check it. That means a new ZkReferee, a new guest image, and new generations of Challenge (to store the epoch) and Season (for drawPeriod). (b) Fairness, the real flaw: the weights normalize each level's median, not the ceiling of a six. calibration-1 weights run from 13,692 to 64,620 bp. The six lightest slots sum to about 89.7k and the six heaviest to about 219.8k, so one week's ceiling can be about 2.4× another's. Ranking is by best run (D10) with a pro-rata payout over the whole season, so every serious best run comes from the heavy week, and players who play only the light week, or join late, are out-scored by design. Today each run re-rolls that luck. (c) Knowing the six before paying reverses the premise of §7.2, 'nobody can preview the levels and pay only for easy ones'. That is acceptable only if every player faces the same six. It does end the paid re-roll, and the gap narrows slightly because humans gain from practice and search does not.

*Fixes.* Use one six per season: drawPeriod = length, or a fixedDraw flag. Every run of the season then faces the same six and best-run ranking stays fair. If weekly draws are wanted, split the pot per epoch. Pass drawSeed through IReferee, bind it in the zk public values (+32 B), and check it at settlement against keccak256("jiro.week" ‖ season ‖ epoch). Pack the epoch into an existing slot of the run struct.

*Cost.* Contracts: one Season parameter (uint64); an epoch u32 per run, about 0 to 5k gas if packed into an existing slot, about 22k if it needs a new one; one keccak at settlement; the IReferee signature plus 32 B of zk public values; a new ZkReferee, guest image, Challenge and Season, deployed and reviewed. Core: the draw takes a drawSeed. Per tick, RAM and zk: 0. New draw fixtures, a drawSeed input in game vectors, a rewrite of README §8.2/§8.4 and docs/ECONOMY.md. An offchain indexer for the weekly boards.

*Conflicts.* P17; D10; D12; §7.2 reveal premise; §7.3 IReferee; §8.2 draw; §6.4 domain tags

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. Level seeds still come from runNonce and levelIndex, so no journal can be copied, and knowing the six levels in advance reveals nothing that the public level files don't already show. It removes the paid re-roll of the draw (§8.2), a real win against spend, and like-for-like boards help humans practise while search gains nothing from practice, so the gap narrows slightly. But with drawPeriod shorter than the season, draw luck becomes calendar luck. Ranking is the best run over the whole season. Weights only equalize each level's median (P x 10,000 / max(m, floor)), so a high-weight hard level has a much higher ceiling score, and an epoch whose six include doom, swarm or titan offers a far higher best-run potential than one full of easy levels. drawSeed is keccak of public values, so every epoch's six can be computed when the season opens. Players then know which week is 'rich', and those who can only play the other week are structurally disadvantaged in a pro-rata pot. Contract side: drawSeed must be an input to the referee that is bound in the zk public values or the Outcome, or a settler could pass any drawSeed and the P17 slot check would pass on the wrong draw. That means changing IReferee, Challenge and the guest, which is T5 as labelled. Calibration is unchanged.

*Fixes.* Use one shared six per season (drawPeriod = length), which removes both draw luck and calendar luck. Alternatively, rank and pay each epoch separately, or stratify the weekly draw so every epoch gets the same difficulty mix (for example one level from each weight band). Bind drawSeed, or season and epoch, into the zk public values and check it at settlement. Document that all epoch draws are public at season open.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** This is a meta-structure change. It adds no new situation to the boss, minion, rail, cut, shield or commit loop. The meta value is real: a weekly cadence, Training as targeted practice, like-for-like per-level boards, and an end to the paid re-roll of the draw that rewards spend. Level seeds stay per run, so D14 holds. Downsides: (1) It reduces variety. Everyone plays the same six for a week. With a 14-day season and a 7-day period, at most 12 draw slots appear, with overlaps, so several of the 16 calibrated levels may never be played that season. (2) Ranking is still the best run per wallet across the whole season, so the epochs compete with each other. Players will put their spending into the week whose draw suits them, which brings back a draw-luck choice at season level. (3) Every epoch's draw can be computed when the season opens. That is harmless for practice, but it should be stated. The contract change is small but is T5.

*Fixes.* Derive the weekly sixes from one season-level permutation of the 16 levels, so every level appears before any repeats, and pick drawPeriod so the season covers all 16. Show the weekly boards per epoch, and consider minClears or pot shares per epoch, so weeks do not compete for best-run status. State in §8.2 that weekly draws are public at season open.

*Readability.* No effect in play. The weekly six is browser UI. The wedgie would need the six entered by hand or loaded as a file, which is fine for offline Training.

## Boss erosion of captured land

`multiplayer-and-competition#6` · T3 New core rule · score 8 (core 3, fairness 2, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

The boss gnaws at your land. When it bounces off ground you captured, it sometimes knocks those cells back into open field, so captured land next to the boss is never quite safe and the timing of Commit matters more.

**Rule sketch.** Level format v2 adds erode_log2 (k, 0 = off). In R4.6-3, when an edge test finds the edge blocked and none of its cells is TRAIL: e = fmix32(keyE XOR tick XOR axis), where axis is 0 for x and 0x10000 for y, and keyE is the big-endian u32 of level seed bytes 13 to 16. If e >> (32 − k) = 0, each edge cell that meets all of these becomes EMPTY: it is CLAIMED, not on the border, not an obstacle in the level bitmap (read through the Level, not copied), and not the player's cell, the cut start or a live minion's cell. Captured cells drop by the number eroded. The bounce still happens, and the body still covers only EMPTY cells. Eroded cells touch the body along an edge, so the field stays one 4-connected region and D7's coverage argument still holds. No rail cell stops being one, since erosion only adds open cells. After an erosion, every live minion runs the orientation step (R4.7-3), as after a capture. A clear is still tested only at captures (PIN-10), so erosion never un-clears a level; Commit and the time limit keep the eroded claim. There is no new state: the grid and the captured cells are already in state hash v1.

**Inspired by:** Fly Trap: Territory Game (2026), Live Wire! (1999), XONIX 1984 2 player remake (2024). **Mechanic families:** [Territory stealing](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#territory-stealing). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D5; D14; R4.6-3; R4.1-8; §6.4 seed bytes 13-16.

**Bot effect.** D14: erosion is keyed by seed bytes 13 to 16 and lands wherever the boss bounces, so it creates no safe zone and nothing to precompute. Gap: roughly unchanged. The search bot predicts each erosion exactly, while a human sees where the boss is bouncing and can guess the risk. The mechanic rewards fast, large captures and early banking, which both kinds of player can learn.

**Budget.** At most 8 cell checks and one fmix32 per blocked boss edge, and no fill: a negligible number of zk cycles. RAM: none (the obstacle bit comes from the Level, which replay already holds). No change to the state hash format. Screen: eroded cells redraw as black, a few cells per event.

**Variety.** It reverses the usual strategy. Locking the boss in a tight room, normally the safe play, now makes it bounce off your land more often and eat more of it. Claim is no longer monotone, so Commit becomes 'bank before it bleeds', and a player must choose between shrinking the boss's room and keeping it large and calm.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 3.** Mostly sound:

- Each edge cell is 4-adjacent to a body cell, so eroded cells join the one field region, and D7's argument survives.
- No entity loses its footing, because the player's cell, the cut start and live minions' cells are excluded, and adding open cells keeps every remaining CLAIMED cell's rail status.
- captured cannot underflow, since every eroded cell (non-border, non-obstacle CLAIMED) was a player capture.
- The state hash needs no change.

Three corrections:
(1) 'No RAM' is false as built. Game::step has no Level: the natmod decodes the Level on the stack in jiro_recorder_start and drops it, and Game keeps no obstacle bitmap. Either Game stores the 450-B obstacle bitmap (+450 B per Game copy at the start stack peak, the same headroom problem as a spoil bitmap), or step takes the level, with an API change to Game::step, Recorder::step, the natmod's StepArgs and the wasm binding.
(2) 'No rail cell stops being one' is false for the eroded cells themselves. That is harmless only because of the exclusions. Repeated erosion can leave a player's or minion's cell as a 1-cell CLAIMED island: PIN-18 then parks that minion forever as a static hazard. Also, a player holding a direction along the rail can suddenly start a cut when the next rail cell erodes.
(3) The gap does not stay unchanged. Search predicts each erosion from the key and the boss path; a human learns of it only when it happens. It widens modestly.

The Commit 'bank before it bleeds' design is coherent, since a clear ends the level and erosion only matters before it.

*Fixes.* Pass the level into step, or have the natmod and wasm keep the level bytes and give Game::step a borrowed obstacle-bit lookup, rather than adding 450 B to Game. Pin the island cases in §4.7 (a minion on a 1-cell island stays, per PIN-18) and say that a rail move into an eroded cell starts a cut. Rate-limit by making erode_log2 large enough that erosion stays rare, and calibrate with an erosion-aware scripted bot.

*Cost.* Level bytes v2: 473 B (+1). State hash unchanged (948 B). RAM: 0 with the level-in-step API change, otherwise +450 B per Game copy at start. Per blocked edge without TRAIL: one fmix32 and at most B cell checks (an obstacle bit, the player, the cut start, at most 3 minions). After an erosion: an orientation step for each live minion. zk: \< 0.5 %. Device: negligible. Screen: a few black cells per event.

*Conflicts.* D5; D7 (rationale text: the rail now changes off-capture); D14; R4.1-8; R4.2-3 (the rail no longer changes only on capture); R4.6-3; R4.9-17 rationale; §6.4 seed bytes 13-16; level bytes v1; Game::step / natmod StepArgs API

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. Erosion is a stateless draw from seed bytes 13..16 on blocked boss edges, and the field-connectivity and D7 arguments survive, since erosion only adds open cells next to the body. The cost is negligible and the state hash format is unchanged. The gap widens, contrary to the pitch. A clear is tested only at captures and locks the claim, and search clears 100% of levels with a final capture near the ceiling. So erosion barely touches search's score but taxes exactly the commit and time-out outcomes that the reference mix and humans hit on hard levels. Search also predicts every erosion exactly: it can keep the boss bouncing off the border and obstacles, which never erode, and bank just before an erosion. Humans can only guess. Erosion adds luck to the score of levels that are not cleared, which feeds straight into level scores and the draw. Bots can play it unchanged, and calibration would absorb the mean, but not the per-seed variance.

*Fixes.* If kept, bound the variance: erode only cells captured within the last N ticks, or cap total erosion per level, for example at 200 bp. Measure the per-seed spread of the reference mix's effective claim against the current rules. Reconsider whether making claim non-monotone is wanted at all, given that it penalises only players who do not clear.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** It is cheap: no new state and almost no extra cycles. It adds real tension. Claim is no longer monotone, so Commit becomes 'bank before it bleeds', and cutting near the boss means planning around shapes that change. A clear is tested only at captures, so the final trapping capture still ends the level cleanly, and erosion mostly erodes the claim banked before it. It breaks the genre's promise that captured land is permanent. Humans cannot predict which bounce erodes, so they experience erosion as random loss, while search predicts each one exactly and plans around it. The gap probably widens slightly rather than staying 'roughly unchanged'. The protections in the sketch (player cell, cut start, minion cells, border, obstacles) look correct: the field stays one region, and rail cells only increase.

*Fixes.* 1. Flash eroded cells for one tick (red or white) before they turn black, so small losses are noticed. 2. Limit erosion to a few late or harsh levels, not the ramp. 3. Restate the bot effect honestly as a slight widening. 4. Consider a cap of one erosion event per axis per boss move, so a tightly trapped boss cannot strip a wall in a few ticks.

*Readability.* Yes, as it happens: the cells next to the boss turn black where the player is already looking. Single-cell losses need a flash to be noticed. The falling claim itself is invisible on the wedgie, which has no in-play HUD.

## Boss-trap rooms sealed at the door

`multiplayer-and-competition#4` · T1 Level data v1 · score 7 (core 3, fairness 2, play 2) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Levels split into two or three rooms joined by a gate wide enough for the boss. Seal the gate while the boss is in the small room, and the big room is yours in one cut.

**Rule sketch.** Level data v1 only. Example source: a full-height obstacle wall at x = 19 from y = 1 to 58, except for a gate at rows 26 to 33. The gate is 8 cells, at least B + 2 for B = 5, so the boss can cross. That leaves an 18×58 west room and a 39×58 east room. The boss spawn rectangle sits in the east room (validity 37 rules out a rectangle across the wall), and 1 or 2 minions spawn on the wall's rail. Sealing the gate takes a straight 8-cell cut down x = 19, which is 4 ticks with the fast cut. If the boss is west of the wall when the cut closes, the capture (R4.8) takes the whole east room, about 66% of the claimable cells; if the boss is east, it takes only the west room, about 31%. The seeded deflections (R4.6-6) decide when the boss crosses, so the window differs in every run. Variants: three rooms with two gates of different widths, trading a longer seal against a bigger room; a corner gate far from the player start; a smaller B for more frequent crossings. The open area stays one 4-connected region of at least 1,682 cells (codes 35, 36), and (30, 59) stays a rail cell.

**Inspired by:** Araña (1990), Land Breaker (1999), Rumba Lumber (1984), Milo's Ghosts (2023), Twin Qix (1995), Gals Panic S - Extra Edition (1997), Filltopia (2026), Volfied (1989), Xonix (1984), Gals Panic (1990), Lady Killer (1993), Silhouette Stories (1996), Beaver Dam (c. 2023). **Mechanic families:** [Pocketing and herding walls](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#pocketing-and-herding-walls), [Squeeze capture](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#squeeze-capture), [Enemy-free regions are claimed](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#enemy-free-regions-are-claimed), [Split-field race to quota](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#split-field-race-to-quota), [Turn-based territory duel](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#turn-based-territory-duel), [Big single-capture bonus](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#big-single-capture-bonus), [Interior obstacles and walls](../taxonomy/terrain-and-geometry.md#interior-obstacles-and-walls), [Silhouette target display](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#silhouette-target-display), [Hidden-picture reveal](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#hidden-picture-reveal), [Themed reskin of capture roles](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#themed-reskin-of-capture-roles). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** P11 (the reference mix may need a trap-aware policy).

**Bot effect.** No effect on D14: the window comes from the seeded deflections. Gap: likely widens slightly. The big capture is all-or-nothing timing, and the search bot knows exactly when the boss will be deep enough in the west room to finish the seal. A human has to read the boss's heading and risk the 4-tick seal. A wider gate gives a longer seal and more time for the boss to come back, which turns the seal into a judgment rather than a reflex and keeps the gap closer to today's.

**Budget.** No more than any other level: 472 bytes, a new level id, a playtest and calibration run. Captures cost what they cost today.

**Variety.** A waiting game the current levels lack. You park on the gate's rail while a minion approaches, judge whether the boss's heading will carry it west long enough, and choose between the big one-cut seal and steady nibbling of the small room.

**Merged from:** `capture-rules#2`, `v1-levels#3`, `reveal-and-presentation#7`. Variants:

- Boss-trap alcove levels: Small dead-end rooms hang off the field. When the bouncing boss wanders into one, a short dash across the doorway shuts it in and claims almost everything else.
- Vault with one door: jackpot or grind: A walled vault with a single door stands in the middle of the field. Seal the door while the boss is outside and you take the vault; seal it while the boss is inside and you take everything else at once. Sealing early throws the jackpot away.
- Silhouette trap boards: New levels whose obstacles draw the hollow outline of a figure with two gaps. The boss wanders in and out, and sealing the second gap captures the figure or, if the boss is inside, the whole field around it.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 1.** I built the example (wall at x = 19, gate rows 26-33, boss spawn rectangle (30,20)-(45,35), period 2). It is valid. The reference mix clears 56% (median effective claim 7,519 bp) by nibbling, without ever trapping, so P11 does not block it. The numbers in the pitch are slightly off. The seal is 5 ticks, not 4: the first sub-step into EMPTY is single, because the player is not cutting at the start of that tick. Sealing with the boss in the west room claims 2,270 of 3,314 cells, 68.5% (not about 66%). The real flaw is how often the trap opens. I simulated 2,000 seeds with the player standing still for the whole level. The boss body got fully into the west room in only 54% of runs (median first crossing at tick 692). So about half of all runs never offer the jackpot, which adds per-run luck to a paid score. Wider gates fix it: a 16-row gate at period 2 crossed in 92% of runs (median at tick 420), and a 20-row gate at period 1 in 99.8% (median at tick 174). An 8-row gate at period 1 reached 79%. Captures that shrink the east room also raise the rate. D14 holds: the window is seeded.

*Fixes.* Widen the gate to 12-20 rows, use boss period 1, or both, so the trap opens in at least about 90% of runs. A longer seal (8-10 ticks with the fast cut) is the intended trade-off. Verify the crossing rate per seed range before calibrating, and fix the 4-tick and 66% figures in the level text.

*Cost.* Code: none. Level bytes v1, a new level id, a playtest and calibration. zk cycles about those of an obstacle level. It takes one of the season's 16 slots.

*Conflicts.* P11 (only if a trap-aware policy were added to the reference mix; the current mix already calibrates it)

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: the seal window comes from the seeded deflections. But the idea adds unfair variance and widens the gap. With B = 5, the body crosses an 8-row gate only when its top row is in 4 of about 54 rows as it reaches the wall. The boss spawns in the east room (rule 37), so crossings are rare. On many seeds the jackpot never appears in the roughly 1,300 ticks a run uses; on others it comes early. Whether a run gets a near-free 66% seal is therefore seed luck, which raises per-run variance. Under best-run ranking (D10), with re-rolls bought as extra runs (§8.2), extra variance rewards buying more runs. Search knows every future crossing from the seed, so it knows whether waiting pays. A human sees only the present (2 s with a forecast overlay) and has to guess. Calibration: the reference mix may never find the seal (the P11 conflict the proposal notes). The bot-derived weight may then misprice the level for humans who know the trick, adding draw variance.

*Fixes.* Before shipping, measure the crossing distribution over many seeds with the core. Accept only layouts where the boss crosses several times per level on nearly every seed: a smaller B, a wider gate or several gates, and rooms closer in size so the jackpot is smaller. Add a scripted seal policy to the playtest set and compare the mix's clear rate with it before calibrating.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** The core move, sealing a gate while the boss is on the far side, is already what cross, serpent, maze and labyrinth are built around. Serpent and labyrinth are four corridors joined by 8-12-cell gates, so from any boss position the player already chooses between a small near seal and a large far one. Cross has 8-cell gates for B = 6. A single wall at x = 19 with a 66/31 split is a simpler restatement, not a new situation. The waiting game is also risky. A B = 5 body has to line up with an 8-row gate in a 58-row wall to cross, so crossings may be rare. The level could reduce to parking on the gate until the deflections cooperate, with the time limit running. It costs nothing in code and stays seed-fair under D14, but it adds little the 16 levels lack.

*Fixes.* Make it distinct or drop it. The 'vault with one door' variant is a free-standing room you must first annex from the border, and sealing its door with the boss inside takes the whole outside, which existing border-attached rooms cannot offer. Or combine the gate with something new, such as a sentry island at the door or a gate exactly B + 1 wide, so crossings are rare and the jackpot is a gamble. Measure the boss's crossing rate in a playtest before committing to a level.

*Readability.* Good. A wall, a gate and the boss's side are all obvious at 4x4 px. It needs no new art.

## Rival wall-builder

`multiplayer-and-competition#5` · T3 New core rule · score 7 (core 2, fairness 2, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

A rival patroller on the rail sometimes launches a straight wall into the field. If the wall lands, the land it encloses is lost to you. You can dive in and cut the wall to kill its builder, let it land and use it as new rail, or close your own cut early and take the half-built wall for yourself.

**Rule sketch.** Level format v2 gives each minion slot a kind (0 = patroller as today, 1 = builder) and adds the level fields launch_log2 (k, 1 to 8), wall_period and arm_ticks (default 15). A builder walks the rail like a minion (R4.7) and hits the player on contact. At step 5, on each tick a builder moves while it is patrolling with arm = 0 and no wall: r = fmix32(keyR XOR tick XOR (slot \<\< 16)) >> (32 − k), where keyR is the big-endian u32 of level seed bytes 9 to 12. If r = 0 and the cell on its right (PIN-06) is EMPTY, the builder sets arm = arm_ticks and stops (drawn flashing). When arm reaches 0, it launches: root = its cell, dir = its right, len = 0. Every wall_period ticks at step 5, the head cell root + dir × (len + 1) is tested. If it is EMPTY and outside the boss body, it becomes TRAIL and len grows by 1. If it is CLAIMED, the wall completes. If it is the player's TRAIL or a boss body cell, the wall breaks: its cells go back to EMPTY and the builder patrols again; on the player's TRAIL, the player is also hit, resolved at step 6. A TRAIL cell belongs to the rival exactly when it lies on the segment root + dir × 1..len, so no new cell state is needed. On completion, if the player is cutting, their trail is erased and they return to the cut start without losing a life. Then the R4.8 capture runs, with its size added to rival_captured instead of captured cells, and stranded minions are destroyed (R4.7-8). The player may step onto a rival wall cell, an exception to R4.5-4: the wall breaks, the entered cell becomes the player's TRAIL, and the builder is destroyed with countdown R. A player capture made while a wall grows treats the wall as TRAIL in the fill and claims it, credited to the player. A boss bumping a rival wall cell bounces and breaks the wall, with no hit (R4.6-3 split by owner). claim_bp still counts only the player's captures (R4.1-8), so rival land lowers the ceiling, and builder levels set lower targets.

**Inspired by:** Fly Trap: Territory Game (2026), Territory Arrow (2026), Coconut's Coco-Net (2026), Paper.io (2016), looprush (2026), Turf Rollers (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Computer rival claimers](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#computer-rival-claimers), [Territory stealing](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#territory-stealing), [Trail cut eliminates its owner](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#trail-cut-eliminates-its-owner), [Trail cut as a setback, not death](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#trail-cut-as-a-setback-not-death). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, state-hash, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D4; D5; D7; D14; R4.5-4; R4.6-3; R4.8-1; R4.9-1; R4.9-17; §6.4 seed bytes 9-12.

**Bot effect.** D14: launches are keyed by seed bytes 9 to 12, so they differ in every run and cannot be precomputed. Gap: likely widens slightly. The search bot knows every launch from the key before the builder arms, and plans breaks and wall theft exactly. The 15-tick telegraph gives humans one second of the same knowledge, which keeps the reactive play fair, but taking a wall optimally is a planning problem the search bot wins. The scripted playtest bots need a new rule, for example: break a wall within N cells of the rail, otherwise avoid it.

**Budget.** State: about 3 B per builder slot (arm, dir, len) plus rival_captured (u16), under 12 B in all. State hash v2 (+11 B) and level bytes v2 (+5 B). Per tick: one fmix32 per builder move, and segment-membership tests that cost O(1) each, at most 8 per boss edge. Each rival completion is one more capture, about 0.55M zk cycles (clear-the-level costs about 0.56M more than fast-cut). Five per level add about 2.8M cycles, roughly 9% of the heaviest level's 31M. Screen: one palette colour for rival walls. Drawing rival land in its own colour needs a 450 B client-side shadow bitmap (presentation only); otherwise the HUD shows the rival's share.

**Variety.** A second agent that claims by your own rules, which makes area a race. New choices: go on offence and break a wall with the boss nearby, accept the loss in exchange for new rail deep in the field, or close your cut while a wall is half built and take it as your own wall.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 3.** It can be built, and the 'TRAIL owned by segment' trick does avoid a fifth cell state. But the rule surface is large and the sketch has holes:
(a) Every player capture's pass turns all TRAIL into CLAIMED, so each growing rival wall anywhere is claimed. The sketch never resets that builder's arm, len or dir afterwards.
(b) A head that meets another builder's wall (up to 3 builders) is neither EMPTY, CLAIMED nor the player's TRAIL, so its outcome is undefined.
(c) Whether arm counts ticks or moves, and whether the builder stays at its root while the wall grows (which the absence of a root field implies), is unpinned.
(d) A rival capture's pass also claims other builders' growing walls, credited to rival_captured, and may strand builders, so it needs the same reset rule.
(e) R4.6-3's boss edge test becomes an owner lookup per cell (up to 3 segments x B x 2 edges) on the hottest boss path.

Cost on the device: each rival completion is a full fill plus a 3,600-cell pass, about 10 ms on the RP2040 (the measured worst capture step is 10.3 ms). Three completions plus a player capture in one tick come to about 40 ms of step, plus draw and up to 240 pushed rows (about 11 ms). That is about 50+ ms of the 66.7 ms tick, under it but with a thin margin, and it must be measured.

Gap: the launch key is seeded (good for D14), but search knows every launch, break and steal in advance, while a human gets a 1 s telegraph. Wall theft is a planning problem search wins, so the gap widens. Every capture also claims every growing wall, which turns rival walls into free rail spurs deep in the field. That is a strong and unintuitive exploit the scripted bots must also learn.

*Fixes.* Allow one growing wall per level at a time: a level-wide active-builder index, which makes ownership a single segment test. Reset a builder to patrolling whenever any of its segment's cells stops being TRAIL (after any capture or break). Define another wall's TRAIL as a break. Pin arm to count ticks, with the builder stationary at its root while it arms and while the wall grows. Measure the worst tick (rival plus player captures on one tick) on qemu before committing to it.

*Cost.* Level bytes v2: about 477 B (+5, kind bits included). State hash v2: about 959 B (+11). RAM: about 12 B. Per tick: one fmix32 per builder move, plus segment-membership tests in the boss edge test (at most 3 x B x 2). Per rival completion: about 0.5M zk cycles and about 10 ms on the RP2040. Five completions add about 2.8M (+9 %) to a heavy level, with the price about flat. Worst device tick: about 50+ ms if 3 rival captures and 1 player capture land on one tick. Screen: one palette colour, plus an optional 450-B client-side shadow.

*Conflicts.* D4; D5; D7; D14; R4.2-3; R4.5-2; R4.5-4; R4.6-3; R4.8-1; R4.8-4; R4.9-1; R4.9-17; PIN-10; §6.4 seed bytes 9-12; state hash v1; level bytes v1

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The launches are stateless draws keyed by seed bytes 9..12, and the patrol only positions the launch. The idea fails on fairness and complexity. A rival wall that completes runs a full R4.8 capture of whichever side lacks the boss, which can be half the field. Where the boss happens to be at that moment is seed luck, so each run's achievable ceiling varies a lot, and scores are claim_bp of the original claimable area. That is large unfair variance within each level, and over the six-level draw. The gap widens. Search knows every launch from the key before the 15-tick telegraph and plans wall breaks and wall theft exactly. Reading and racing several agents' walls is precisely where humans fall behind. The rule surface is large: TRAIL ownership by segment membership, exceptions to R4.5-4 and R4.6-3, a forced cut abort, rival captures, and the fill treating rival walls as the player's wall. Each of these means golden vectors and a source of parity bugs. Each rival capture costs about 0.55M zk cycles, roughly +9% on heavy levels. The scripted bots need new heuristics, and the weights would hinge on them.

*Fixes.* If it is pursued, measure claim over claimable minus rival_captured, so that luck in rival land does not move the score ceiling. Cap rival captures by size, or make a completed wall claim only its own cells rather than running a fill. Drop wall theft and the forced cut abort to shrink the rule surface. Prototype it in Training only, and measure the per-seed spread of search's claim before it goes near Challenge.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** It adds a lot of new decisions and situations. Area becomes a race. The player can break a wall under boss pressure, accept the lost land in exchange for new rail deep in the field, or steal a half-built wall by closing a cut onto it. No current level has anything like a second claimer. The price is many special cases: an exception to R4.5-4, ownership worked out from segment geometry, trail erasure without losing a life on completion, a boss bounce that splits by owner, captures credited to the rival, and minions stranded by rival captures. The rules start to feel ad hoc, and so would teaching them. Rival land is not visible on the device, and the player cannot see the lowered ceiling during play. Each rival capture costs about 0.55M zk cycles, roughly +9% at five per level on the heaviest level. Search plans breaks and thefts exactly, so the gap widens, as the pitch admits.

*Fixes.* 1. Cut the rule count. Drop the trail-erase on completion: a wall's head waits while the player is cutting. Keep R4.6-3 unchanged: the boss bounces off rival walls as it does off TRAIL, without a hit, and only a head growing into the boss's body breaks the wall. 2. Keep breaking (the player enters a wall cell) and stealing (the fill treats the wall as TRAIL), because they carry the decisions. 3. Draw rival walls in a palette colour distinct from the red trail, and flash the armed builder. 4. Show the target as already reduced on the start overlay. 5. Ship it on two or three dedicated levels only.

*Readability.* Mostly. A growing wall in its own colour and a flashing builder read well at 4 px per cell. Rival-owned land looks identical to the player's blue unless a 450-B shadow is kept on a device with about 18 KB of RAM free, and with no in-play HUD the rival's share cannot be shown.
