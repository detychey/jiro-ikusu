# Ideas: modes and meta

The 9 ideas proposed from the axis of the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) *modes and meta*: game modes (puzzle, time attack, endless, daily, editor, user levels), meta-progression, collections, economy, gacha. Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Daily shared seed

`modes-and-meta#3` · T0 Presentation · score 13 (core 4, fairness 5, play 4) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Every UTC day, everyone gets the same level and the same boss in Training. You chase your best claim or your fastest clear, while a ghost marker and claim bar show where your best attempt, or a friend's, stood at this moment.

**Rule sketch.** Training only, client-side. Day d = whole UTC days since 1970-01-01, as a u32. The daily run nonce is keccak256('jiro.daily' ‖ d), a client-only domain tag. The level of the day is the season level at draw slot draw(nonce)\[0\], from the core's own §8.2 draw (the wasm draw() export), played at levelIndex 0 in a mode-0 journal with that nonce. Every player therefore gets the same boss start and the same deflection draws. There are two boards. Claim ranks by effective claim (claim_bp, or 0 when out of lives), ties to fewer ticks. Sprint ranks cleared attempts only, by fewest ticks used. The first 3 attempts of the day are marked 'counted' and later ones 'practice' (honour system). A streak counts consecutive days with a clear. Pace ghost, browser: a second Game replays your best journal of the day, or a friend's from a link (day plus journal bytes in base64url), in lockstep. It draws only the ghost's player marker (a hollow 4×4 square) and its claim bar, because the ghost's boss diverges from yours once the trails differ. There is no server: boards are local and shared between friends.

**Inspired by:** bloom (PlayWeave): the same daily seed for every player, VOLTFIELD: 80s Classic Arcade (2026): Daily Volt with 3 attempts, Starcutter: Space Arcade (2026): the same sectors for everyone, Blobbed Away (2025): Time Challenge, Territory: Claim & Conquer (2026): Time Attack, NEON PANIC: Neon Arcade (2026): daily-challenge streaks, Air Xonix: Remastered (2020), Neon Divide (2026), NoSpace Arena (2026), Fly Trap: Territory Game (2026), Bix (2008). **Mechanic families:** [Daily shared challenge](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#daily-shared-challenge), [Time attack and score attack](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#time-attack-and-score-attack), [Seeded deterministic play and replays](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#seeded-deterministic-play-and-replays), [Missions, streaks and seasons](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#missions-streaks-and-seasons), [Weekly shared-board race](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#weekly-shared-board-race), [Leaderboard eligibility rules](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#leaderboard-eligibility-rules). **Touches:** presentation.

**Bot effect.** Keeps the gap, since this is Training only and no money is involved. It does not breach D14, because Training already accepts any run nonce (§6.3). A public daily leaderboard would be won by search bots, because the day's seed is known for 24 hours (§7.4); that is why the boards stay local and friend-shared.

**Budget.** Browser: a second Game of about 10.4 KB for the ghost, and one keccak per day. zk: 0. Screen: one hollow 4×4 square and a 2-px bar, redrawn only when they change.

**Variety.** A game players can compare move for move. Sprint rewards the fastest clear over the biggest claim, the opposite of Challenge, where ticks only break ties. Racing a ghost changes risk appetite in the middle of a level: behind the ghost at tick 600, push; ahead, press X and bank.

**Merged from:** `modes-and-social#3`. Variants:

- Daily shared seed with first-try streaks (Training): Training only. Every UTC day everyone gets the same level on the same seed, with a 'daily six' that mirrors a Challenge run. Your first finished attempt is recorded separately from your best, and a streak counts the days you played.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 0.** It is clean. jiro-wasm's journal_hash() computes keccak256 of arbitrary input bytes, so keccak256('jiro.daily' ‖ d) needs no new export, and draw() already exists. The Training page already takes a nonce, and Training accepts any nonce (§6.3). The client-only tag cannot collide with the consensus tags 'jiro.level', 'jiro.run' and 'jiro.draw'. The ghost Game in lockstep is the same second wasm instance as in the take-over idea, not 10.4 KB, but that does not matter in a browser.

Boards and ghosts must be keyed by RULES_VERSION and level id: a rules bump makes earlier journals unreplayable.

The bot assessment is honest. A public daily board would belong to search, so local and friend-shared boards are right.

*Fixes.* - Key the boards and ghost files by rules version and level id.
- Keep the 'jiro.daily' tag documented in web/README as non-consensus.

*Cost.* - Consensus: none (zk +0; formats, vectors and RULES_VERSION unchanged).
- Browser: one extra wasm instance and one keccak per day.

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** This is Training only, and the client-only domain tag 'jiro.daily' keeps daily nonces apart from Challenge nonces, which only the contract derives as keccak('jiro.run' ‖ entropy ‖ runId). Every daily game is known in advance and repeats all day, so players can memorise it, but nothing carries over to paid play, where D14 gives every run a fresh boss path. The boards are local and shared between friends, which correctly concedes that a public board would go to search bots. The Sprint board ranks by fewest ticks, the opposite of Challenge's objective, but it is unpaid, so it skews nothing. The draw uses the core's own draw export, so the level of the day comes from the shipped season pool. Bots need no calibration for it, and it adds no variance to the paid draw.

*Fixes.* A friend's link should carry journal bytes that the receiver replays with the core to get the claim and ticks, never a claimed number. That is implied, so state it. Label the boards social, since search-assisted journals cannot be told apart.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** The Sprint board brings a real new objective: the fastest clear. That favours big fast cuts and aggressive pacing. Challenge does the opposite, ranking on claim and using ticks only to break ties. The pace ghost changes mid-level risk appetite against a known line. A shared daily seed is a proven habit hook, and it costs nothing in consensus: it is Training only, and §6.3 already accepts any run nonce. The variety from day to day comes only from the boss start and the deflection draws on the same 16 levels; the daily-six variant, which mirrors a Challenge draw, adds a free rehearsal of the paid format. Local, friend-shared boards are the right call, since search would own any public board on a seed known for 24 hours. The 'counted' attempts rest on an honour system, and an open friend journal can be copied. That is harmless without money, but it makes friend comparisons soft. Most of the ghost and link machinery duplicates the replay take-over and duel ideas.

*Fixes.* Share the ghost, the link encoding and the journal storage with the replay take-over and duel ideas; this idea's own content is then the day-to-nonce derivation and the two boards. Rank the Claim board on effective claim with D5's zero, exactly as specified. Lead with Sprint, and with daily-six as a variant. Hold a friend's journal behind its hash until you have played your counted attempts, so it can't be copied.

*Readability.* Browser: fine.

## Per-level medal tiers in Training

`modes-and-meta#4` · T0 Presentation · score 13 (core 4, fairness 5, play 4) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Optional feats (clear untouched, capture 40% in one stroke, box the boss) and an album with one original picture per level, which shows under the cells you capture and is framed bronze, silver or gold by how far past the target you finish.

**Rule sketch.** Presentation only. A feat is a pure function of a replayed level (level bytes, nonce, journal), checked by the shell after each tick from values the core already holds: Game.captured, lives, shield charges, the mask, minion presence, end reason and ticks used. The same checks therefore run in the browser and on any public Challenge journal. Examples: Untouched (cleared with lives equal to the level's lives and the shield charge unused); Single stroke (one capture whose captured delta at step 3 is at least 4,000 bp of claimable); Boxed (the level ends with claimable − captured ≤ 4 × B² open cells); No sprint (cleared without A held while cutting); Stranded (a capture destroys a minion, presence 1 → 2); Last breath (X with 1 life left and claim ≥ target − 500); Quick clear (cleared before tick 450). Album: each of the 16 levels has an original 60×60 picture at 4 bits per cell, made for Jiro (D13). The picture is drawn under captured cells instead of plain blue. At level end the album keeps the best frame: bronze = cleared, silver = claim ≥ 8,500 bp, gold = claim ≥ 9,500 bp. A clear ends the level at the capture that crosses the target (PIN-10), so gold requires one final capture from below the target to 9,500 bp or more: the boss-boxing cut. A season wall on the season page replays committed Challenge journals (public calldata, P5) and lists each wallet's feats and gold frames, with no contract change.

**Inspired by:** GraviNix: Tilt Xonix Arcade (2026), Trappy Pixel (2023), Gals Panic S - Extra Edition (1997), Spider Web: Akai's Journey (2015), Fortix (2009), Bix (2008), Territory Arcade (2026), Cacoma Knight in Bizyland (1992), Megami Tensei QIX Persona 3 (2007), CuteQix (c. 2023-2024). **Mechanic families:** [Per-level star ratings](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#per-level-star-ratings), reward images](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#escalating-reward-images). **Touches:** presentation.

**Bot effect.** Keeps the gap on payouts, because nothing paid depends on feats. Bots can farm season-wall feats from their own runs, so feats stay cosmetic. Gold frames reward exactly the skill where search beats humans by about 2,000 bp on cleared levels: a single capture that overshoots the target. If they teach it, human Challenge scores rise and the gap narrows slightly.

**Budget.** About 16 B of feat state; an album save of about 64 B. Per tick: a handful of integer compares. Screen: captured cells take the picture colour in the existing dirty-cell redraw, with no extra redraws. zk: 0.

**Variety.** Self-imposed constraints (no shield, no fast cut, a single stroke) change the routes on familiar levels. The album gives a reason to overshoot instead of stopping at the target. Bronze, silver and gold make the 16 levels worth replaying after they are cleared.

**Merged from:** `scoring-and-risk#2`, `level-structure-and-progression#2`, `reveal-and-presentation#6`. Variants:

- Training medals and picture tiers: Each Training level awards up to three medals: cleared, cleared without losing a life, and cleared at 9,000 bp or more. Captured cells reveal an original picture whose final tier shows only at 9,500 bp.
- Journey map with medals and star gates: An optional Journey mode lays the 16 levels out as four worlds of four on a map. Each level earns up to three stars (clear, no life lost, a big finish), stars open the next world, and each star lights a tile of that world's mosaic.
- Training mural with tiered tiles: Training keeps a mural of the season's 16 levels as a 4x4 grid. Each tile fills in with every cell you have ever claimed on that level, and its frame upgrades at a clear, at a high claim, and when you box the boss into a pocket its own size.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 0.** The feats are pure functions of what the shell already sees after each tick: lives, shield, invulnerability, cutting, claim, target, minion states, end and ticks, and the client knows the mask it sampled. 'Single stroke' works from the claim_bp delta, since only one capture happens per tick. The PIN-10 reasoning on gold is right.
Gaps:
(1) 'Boxed' needs claimable: compute it at level start.
(2) 'No extra redraws' means that newly captured rail cells also take the art colour. The field boundary then loses its solid frame, and minions and the player walk on art. Keep the rail in the frame colour with #1's grown-rectangle repaint on capture ticks.
(3) Gold at 9,500 is close to search's own mean on the B8 levels (doom 9,580, titan 9,588, against ceilings of 9,809), so human golds there will be rare. Set tiers per level.
(4) The season wall needs an indexer: commitRun calldata (call traces for smart-account wallets), reveal events for the nonces, and the season's level bytes. The §7.5 rebuild-from-chain pipeline can feed it, with no contract change.
It shares the R4.1-4 'Drawn as' issue with #1, and it duplicates #1's art pipeline, so build one.

*Fixes.* Share [presentation#1](presentation.md#picture-reveal-under-claimed-land)'s pipeline (rail kept in the frame colour, obstacle flag). Derive claimable at level start. Set gold and silver per level relative to the target and the ceiling. Feed the season wall from the operator's chain rebuild (§7.5) instead of replaying every run in the browser.

*Cost.* About 16 B of feat state and a save of about 64 B. Per tick: a few integer compares. zk, formats and vectors: none.

*Conflicts.* R4.1-4 ('Drawn as' is tagged §4 rule text)

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** Feats are pure functions of replayed journals and pay nothing. Gold at 9,500 or more teaches the boss-boxing final cut, where search beats humans by about 2,000 bp, so if anything it narrows the gap a little. The public season wall will be bot-dominated: search's mean claim is at least 9,580 on every level, so its runs earn gold nearly everywhere. That is cosmetic, but it puts the bot advantage on display and could discourage human players.

*Fixes.* List the wall's feats per wallet with no ranking, or leave the Challenge wall out and keep medals in Training.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** The feats map directly onto the core verbs and change how the 16 known levels are played. No sprint and Untouched (shield unused) reshape how the fast cut and the shield are used. Single stroke and Boxed reward big fenced cuts. Stranded teaches capturing a minion's stretch of rail, a subtle rule few players find. Last breath is a feat built around Commit timing. These are self-imposed constraints that create new decision contexts on existing content, and all can be checked from state the core already exposes, with no consensus change (T0). The season wall over public journals is free social proof, and nothing paid depends on feats. Weak point: gold at 9,500 is near the ceiling (9,809-9,952). Search averages 9,580-9,918, and the human journals ended at 8,444 and 8,644, so gold is effectively out of reach for most humans.

*Fixes.* Set the frame tiers relative to each level: silver at target + 1,000, gold at about ceiling − 400, or a flat 9,000. Limit feats to 3 per level, chosen to fit it (for example Stranded only on levels with minions, Quick clear tuned per level), so the list does not become a checklist. Share the picture pipeline with the picture-reveal idea instead of building it twice. Make Quick clear's tick bound per level from the reference mix's clear ticks.

*Readability.* Fine. Feats and frames go on the end-of-level overlay, which already shows text, and the album is a menu screen. The pictures under captured cells carry the same caveats as the reveal idea: keep the semantic colours and the frame blue out of the art, and draw starting obstacles in the frame colour.

## Proven level codes

`modes-and-meta#6` · T0 Presentation · score 13 (core 4, fairness 5, play 4) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Share a level you drew in the editor as a code that proves it can be cleared: the code carries your clears on three seeds the level itself chose, the importer checks them by replay, and you can race the author on those seeds.

**Rule sketch.** Client-only, using the existing formats (level bytes v1, journal v1). The editor's Export requires proof: the author clears the level once on each proof nonce P_i = keccak256('jiro.proof' ‖ levelId ‖ i), for i = 0, 1, 2, in mode-0 journals at levelIndex 0. The seeds come from the level's own id, so the author cannot pick a lucky boss start or deflection key, though they may retry each seed. The code is base64url of the level bytes (472 B) followed by the three journals (71 + 3 × eventCount bytes each). Import runs the core's replay on the three journals: journal codes 1-11, validity 20-39, levelId 12, codes 13 and 14. It then shows 'proven: 3 of 3 cleared, best 8,920 bp', or rejects the code with the first error. The recipient plays the level under fresh random nonces, or races the author on P_0..P_2, where the author's journals are exact ghosts because the game is the same. Optionally, the browser runs the shipped scripted policies (greedy, bold, committer from tools/src/bots compiled to wasm) on 20 seeds and shows their clear rate as a difficulty estimate. Imported levels go on a local shelf next to the 16 levels and play unscored, as non-season levels already do.

**Inspired by:** Ballistyx (1990s): validate and test-play before saving, levels exported as images, Delta Light (2020): editor maps shared as a text string, Lightspace (2026): community levels, Ink Fill (2026): stages verified by replay. **Mechanic families:** [Level editor and user levels](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#level-editor-and-user-levels), [Seeded deterministic play and replays](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#seeded-deterministic-play-and-replays). **Touches:** presentation, level-data, tools-bots.

**Bot effect.** Keeps the gap: Training only. A proof shows that a clear is reachable, not that a human made it (§7.5): a search bot could prove levels no human can clear. That is why the bot-mix estimate appears next to the proof. The idea also tries out, at no cost, the fix for TODO problem 6 of a future Superchallenge ('completed once' needs proof).

**Budget.** Code size: 472 B plus three journals. A typical clear is 100-400 events, 0.4-1.3 KB each; the worst case is 8,171 B each. Import: three replays, milliseconds in the browser. The optional bot estimate is browser-only (20 bot games, under a second in wasm). zk: 0. Screen: editor and import dialogs.

**Variety.** Player-made layouts that come with a validity and clearability guarantee. Author-versus-player races on identical games. Level-making becomes a design puzzle: making a level that is hard but provably clearable.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 0.** It uses the existing formats and the core's replay unchanged, with no consensus impact. Problems. (a) 'The author cannot pick a lucky boss start' is false. P_i comes from levelId, and the author can grind the id by flipping an irrelevant obstacle bit or moving the time limit by a tick until all three seeds are easy. The levels are unscored, so this is harmless, but the claim has to go. (b) Proofs expire. Each journal header carries rulesVersion, so after any RULES_VERSION bump every code's journals fail with code 4, and a later level format v2 breaks them as well. (c) Size. The worst-case code is 472 + 3 × 8,171 = 24,985 B, about 33 KB in base64: fine as a file, too long for many chat and URL paths. (d) Replay checks levelId (code 12) but not the nonce. The importer must itself check mode 0, levelIndex 0 and runNonce = P_i. (e) The bot estimate needs the scripted bots moved out of the jiro-tools binary into a crate that builds for wasm. (f) A search bot can prove levels that no human can clear; the bot-mix estimate is the right counterweight.

*Fixes.* Drop the anti-grinding claim and say the code proves clearability on 3 seeds the author attempted. Have the importer check the header (mode 0, levelIndex 0, runNonce = P_i). Strip the 71-byte headers from the code, since every field is derivable, which saves 213 B. Show proofs made under an older rulesVersion as unproven, but still offer the level. Make import browser-only. Move the scripted bots into a shared library crate before the wasm estimate.

*Cost.* Client only. Code: 472 B + 3 × (71 + 3 × events), typically 1 to 4 KB, at worst 24,985 B. Import: 3 replays, milliseconds in wasm. Optional: 20 bot games in wasm, plus the bot crate split. zk 0, no vectors, no consensus change. Editor export, an import dialog and a local shelf.

*Conflicts.* §6.3 rulesVersion header

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** Training only and unscored, with no Challenge or contract surface. It uses the existing v1 level bytes and journals under mode 0, so a proof journal can never pass as a paid one. The proof nonces are fixed per level by design, so everyone shares the same three games and an author's journal can be replayed by anyone to tie a race. That is acceptable only because nothing is scored. The new 'jiro.proof' tag follows the §6.4 rule that every derivation has its own domain tag. The pitch is honest that a proof shows a clear is reachable, not that a human made it, and the scripted-bot estimate beside it reuses the calibration policies, a fair difficulty signal. The core's strict decoders (codes 1-39, 12-14) already handle untrusted codes. Worst-case codes are about 33 KB of base64, too long for some sharing channels. It gives a concrete test of the future Superchallenge's 'completed once needs proof' problem.

*Fixes.* Call it 'verified by replay' rather than 'proven', and note that a bot may have made the clears. Never build a scored or onchain board on the fixed proof seeds. If Superchallenge reuses this, proof seeds must stay separate from scoring seeds. Consider compressing or truncating the shared code, or sharing one journal plus a hash.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This gives the most variety for the least cost. User layouts reach situations the 16 levels do not, such as odd pockets, chokepoints and rail shapes that change minion patrols. The proof stops shared levels that cannot be cleared, and the identical games on P_0..P_2 give exact ghost races against the author with no new rules. It changes nothing in consensus, keeps all of D14 (the proof seeds come from the level id), and is a free trial of the 'completed once' proof that the Superchallenge needs (TODO problem 6). It adds no new mechanic, only layouts within the v1 knobs. Weak points: (1) Codes can be large. Three worst-case journals are about 24 KB before base64, which is too big for a chat message or QR code. (2) An author can change one obstacle cell to get a new level id and new proof seeds, so 'the level chose the seeds' is weaker than it sounds, though harmless in Training. (3) A search bot can prove levels no human can clear, which the bot-mix estimate partly offsets.

*Fixes.* Compress the code with the browser's native CompressionStream, or require one proof seed plus the bot-mix estimate to shorten it. Show the author's ghost by default in races. Label the bot-mix clear rate as the difficulty estimate, and treat the proof as evidence that a clear exists. Accept that layout tweaks can re-roll the seeds, or bind the proof seeds to a timestamped id if that ever matters.

*Readability.* The same as the shipped levels, so it is fine on screen.

## Fixed-path puzzle rooms

`modes-and-meta#12` · T3 New core rule · score 11 (core 3, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Authored Training rooms where the boss follows a fixed, learnable bounce path and every trail cell spends ink from a budget: work out the boss's loop, then claim the room with the fewest, best-placed cuts.

**Rule sketch.** Level v2 puzzle fields. fixed_start u8: when 1, the boss starts at the spawn rectangle's first candidate, with vx and vy from bits 0 and 1 of this field, instead of seed bytes 0..4 (§6.4). deflect_off u8: when 1, R4.6-7 draws nothing. ink u16: 0 means unlimited; otherwise the number of cells that may become TRAIL over the whole level. cut_limit u8: 0 means unlimited; otherwise the number of cuts that may start. New player state: ink_left (u16) and cuts_left (u8), in state hash v2. At step 3, a sub-step from the rail into EMPTY is ignored, like a step onto the own trail (R4.5-4), when cuts_left = 0 or ink_left = 0. Starting a cut spends a cut, and each cell that becomes TRAIL spends 1 ink. A trail erased by a hit or by X refunds nothing. Replay rejects a mode-1 (Challenge) journal on a level with fixed_start or deflect_off, with a new code, so seed-independent levels never reach paid play (D14). A room is scored in stars by ink left at the clear: 1 star for a clear, 2 for 30% left, 3 for 50% left; bests are kept locally. A pack of 30 rooms is authored as sources in levels/src/rooms/, the editor gets the fields, and solutions are shared as journals, which replay on everyone's copy of the room because the room does not depend on the seed.

**Inspired by:** Spider: The Secret of Bryce Manor (2009): Precision mode with limited silk, JezzBall Classic (2011): 100 puzzle rooms, Pup 'n' Sweep: as few sweeps as possible, Linsips (2026): a set objective instead of a percentage, Stealin (2016): several solutions per level, Kingdom Cutter: Cut & Conquer (2026): ladder of compact skill levels, Eyestorm (2009), Filler 2 (2009), iSlash (2010), JezzBall Classic (2011), Hundreds (2013), Ink Fill (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Puzzle levels](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#puzzle-levels), [Challenge and mission levels](../taxonomy/modes-and-meta.md#challenge-and-mission-levels), [Fewest-cuts scoring](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#fewest-cuts-scoring), [Offline-generated, simulation-verified stages](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#offline-generated-simulation-verified-stages), [Per-level star ratings](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#per-level-star-ratings), [Limited number of cuts per level](../taxonomy/movement-and-drawing.md#limited-number-of-cuts-per-level). **Touches:** level-format, rules, state-hash, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D14; R4.6-5; R4.6-6; R4.5-2; R4.5-4; §6.4 boss start.

**Bot effect.** Keeps the paid gap, since the core rejects rooms in Challenge. Search solves rooms trivially and solutions can be copied, so room boards are for fun, not for ranking. A fixed bounce path that humans can learn is the deliberate opposite of D14, and is allowed only because nothing is paid.

**Budget.** State +3 B. Per tick: one compare and a decrement on cut steps. zk: 0 in practice, because rooms never settle. Screen: an ink gauge and a cuts counter, redrawn only when they change. Rooms are ordinary level files of 478 B.

**Variety.** A mode built on planning instead of reflexes. The player learns a fixed boss loop, then places the minimal set of cuts. Ink makes long, safe, wall-hugging cuts expensive, and short daring cuts across the boss's path become the efficient solution.

**Merged from:** `modes-and-social#4`. Variants:

- Fixed-seed puzzle packs with search-proven par (Training): Training only. Hand-made puzzles fix the boss's whole path, so you can study it and plan cuts. Objectives include 'clear in 3 cuts', 'clear with 1 life by tick 900' or 'commit between 80 and 82%', with bronze, silver and gold medals against a par a bot has proven reachable.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 3.** A sound Training-only concept, but the rule sketch has holes.

1. fixed_start is contradictory. 'When 1' together with 'vx and vy from bits 0 and 1 of this field' means a value of 1 always gives vx = +1, vy = -1. And the position half is redundant: a one-cell spawn rectangle (x0 = x1, y0 = y1) is valid v1 data and already fixes the start, since index mod 1 = 0. Only the direction (seed byte 4) and the deflections need new fields.
2. Running out of ink mid-cut is undefined. The sketch blocks only the start of a cut. Without a matching rule, a TRAIL to EMPTY sub-step at ink_left = 0, or a fast cut's second sub-step with 1 ink left, underflows. With the rule, the player freezes mid-cut and can leave only through X, which ends the level, or through a hit. Rooms then softlock unless the authors plan around it.
3. The level decoder should accept both versions: v1 (472 B, code 20 today) and v2 (477 B, not 478). The 16 levels then keep their ids.
4. Even so, state hash v2 (+3 B) changes every trace. That means a RULES_VERSION bump, all vectors regenerated, and a new season and calibration run, with the 16 levels unchanged.
5. The new mode-1 rejection code is only a backstop. Settlement already requires each level id to be one of the season's 16.

With deflections off and a fixed direction, a room's game is seed-independent, so shared solution journals replay on any copy. The boss loop changes after each capture and each TRAIL bounce, which is good puzzle material.

*Fixes.* - Drop fixed_start. Use a one-cell spawn rectangle plus boss_dir u8 (0 = seeded, 1-4 = fixed diagonal) and deflect_off u8.
- Specify ink exhaustion: either an EMPTY step at ink 0 is ignored and the room must be authored so that this cannot strand the player, or reaching ink 0 lapses the cut back to its start.
- Make the decoder accept both v1 and v2.
- Keep the mode-1 rejection as a backstop.
- Accept the RULES_VERSION bump and hash v2.

*Cost.* - Format: level v2 at 477 B (v1 still accepted). State +3 B, state hash v2 at 951 B.
- Per tick: one compare and a decrement per cut sub-step; zk about 0 cycles.
- Versioning: RULES_VERSION bump, all vectors and traces regenerated, playtest and calibration rerun, new season.
- Tools: editor and level-tool fields, 30 room sources.

*Conflicts.* D14 (by design, Training only); R4.5-2; R4.5-4; R4.6-5; R4.6-6; R4.6-7; §6.4 boss start; §5 level bytes v1 (code 20 length)

**Fairness: concerns, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** The rooms are meant to break D14, with a fixed, learnable, copyable boss path, and the core fences them off: replay rejects mode-1 journals on levels with fixed_start or deflect_off. The fence is the right design, but it has gaps. (1) It does not cover ink or cut_limit, so a later season could ship seeded levels with an ink limit into Challenge without a separate review. A tight ink budget against random deflections rewards foresight, so it would widen the search-human gap, and nobody has calibrated it. (2) Season.open cannot check level validity. A room listed by mistake in a season's 16 would make every run that draws its slot unsettleable, 6 runs in 16, with the fees stranded in the pot. (3) State hash v2, with ink_left and cuts_left, changes the paid trace format for every level. That means a RULES_VERSION bump and a new season, but it is fair. Training rooms rightly have no paid boards, since search solves them and solutions can be copied. Nothing to calibrate in paid play, and the draw is unaffected if the fence holds.

*Fixes.* Reject mode 1 on any level with a puzzle field away from its v1 default: fixed_start, deflect_off, ink ≠ 0 or cut_limit ≠ 0, under one new replay code. Admit ink or cut_limit to Challenge only through a separate decision, with playtest bots taught to use them and calibrated. Make the level tool's season check (the operator's step before open) reject puzzle levels.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This adds the most new decisions of the mode ideas. An ink budget makes long, safe, wall-hugging cuts expensive and rewards short, daring cuts across the boss's path, and a cut limit forces big closures. Planning is a real change of pace from the 16 reflex levels, and deterministic retries suit a Training-only puzzle. The rule sketch, however, overreaches and has a hole. (1) Determinism needs no core change: a room published as level bytes plus a fixed run nonce already replays identically on every retry, because deflections depend only on the key and the tick. deflect_off adds only a clean, periodic loop, which is nice but optional, and it is the part that contradicts D14. (2) It costs T3 and a state hash v2 for everyone, including paid play, to carry mechanics that are Training-only. (3) The spec doesn't say what happens to a mid-cut step into EMPTY when ink_left = 0: the u16 underflows, or the player is stranded with no rule. (4) The Challenge rejection is redundant, because Challenge only plays the season's 16 levels. Adding 5 bytes to 472 gives 477, not 478. Captures change the boss path, so 'learn the loop' holds only until the first cut, but retries are deterministic, so this stays fair.

*Fixes.* Ship rooms as v1 level bytes plus a fixed nonce (T1). Count ink and cuts in the client from the core's per-tick state, and have the client end the attempt and grade stars when a budget is exceeded. That needs no core, format or state-hash change. If blocking at zero ink proves essential later, specify the mid-cut case (the step is ignored, like stepping onto the own trail) before it becomes a T3 rule. Prefer authoring rooms over a deflection-free boss, by choosing nonces whose early path reads cleanly.

*Readability.* In the browser, an ink gauge and a cuts counter are easy.

## Rule dials, with Training presets and season mutators

`modes-and-meta#7` · T2 Level format v2 · score 11 (core 4, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: unclear · D14: compliant

Levels gain a few dials on existing rules: deflection rate, shield charges, fast-cut speed, minion hand, invulnerability length. Training offers Calm and Ruthless presets, and each Challenge season can give the same 16 layouts a different feel, such as minions circling the other way or no shield at all.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 are v1's 472 bytes with version 2 and 6 bytes appended (478 B). Each dial parameterizes an existing rule, and its v1 value is the default, so a v2 level with default dials plays exactly like its v1 twin (under a different level id). (1) deflect_shift u8, 25..28 (v1: 26): R4.6-7 becomes r = fmix32(key XOR tick) >> deflect_shift, inverting vx on r = 0 and vy on r = 1, so each axis deflects on 1 move in 128, 64, 32 or 16. Uses: (a) Training presets patch a v2 copy of a level, which plays unscored. Calm: shield 3, invuln 60, deflect_shift 25, lives +2, boss period +1. ## Trade-off card before each Challenge level

`modes-and-meta#10` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts · score 9 (core 2, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Before each Challenge level you are offered three cards drawn by the run's seed. Each pairs a boon with a cost, such as an extra life for a 9,000 bp claim cap, or three shield charges for no fast cut. You pick one or none to suit the level and your style.

**Rule sketch.** The level seed's free bytes 9..12 give k as a big-endian u32. Offer i, for i = 0, 1, 2, is card fmix32(k XOR j) mod 10, with j starting at i and stepping by 3 until the card is not already offered (at most 30 steps). The player picks one offer or none, in a new journal v2 header byte, card (0 = none, 1..3 = offer index), set before tick 0, so no in-play input is involved (D8 untouched). ## Run-wide lives pool in Challenge

`modes-and-meta#9` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts · score 7 (core 2, fairness 3, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

A Challenge run carries a small reserve of spare lives across its six levels: one saves you when you would lose your last life, and every clean clear adds one, so playing the easy draws cleanly pays for the hard ones later in the run.

**Rule sketch.** New season parameter reserveLives, 0..5 (0 keeps v1 behaviour). A run carries a reserve r, with r = reserveLives before level 0. At R4.9-16 (step 6), when a hit would take the player's last life and r > 0, r drops by 1 and the player keeps one life. Every other effect of a lost life still applies: the trail is erased, the player returns to the cut start, and invulnerability lasts 30 ticks. Otherwise the level ends out of lives as now (D5). At the end of a level cleared with no life lost (lives equal to the level's lives), r += 1, capped at 5. Level k starts with the r that level k − 1 ended with. Core: Game::new takes reserve_in; state hash v2 adds reserve (u8); the Outcome adds reserveOut (u8). Referee: verifyRun replays the six levels in order and feeds each level's reserveOut into the next; the zk guest already replays all six in one execution. verify, and settleLevel in the contract, take reserveIn, and the contract checks it against the stored reserveOut of the run's previous level, so the fallback settles levels in order. ## Steer the boss against a scripted cutter

## Seed-generated level layouts

`modes-and-meta#11` · T3 New core rule · score 6 (core 2, fairness 2, play 2) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Two of the season's 16 slots hold a generator instead of a fixed layout: whenever the draw lands there, the run's seed builds a fresh obstacle layout with the knob set of an authored level, so no one has seen that board before.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v3 are a generator spec, for example 32 B: version 3, a layout-family mask (bars, combs, blocks, rooms), a range K of rectangle counts, and one knob set copied from an authored level (B, spawn rectangle, boss period, minion count, period and R, T, lives, target). Its id is keccak256 of the spec, so the season puts it in levelIds\[14\] and levelIds\[15\], weights it like any slot, and the draw check of P17 is unchanged. In Game::new, before validation, a v3 level is expanded once from the level seed's free bytes 13..16 (key g). The draws are fmix32(g XOR c) for c = 0, 1, 2, …: the family, then K, then K rectangles stamped into the obstacle bitmap. The rectangles stay outside the boss spawn rectangle grown by B and off row 58 near the player start (x 27..33), and the minion spawns are the first rail cells in row-major order from the spec's candidate list. Then the validity checks of §5 (35-39) run. On failure, the attempt counter advances to fresh draws; after 16 attempts the generator uses the spec's fallback layout, an open field, which is always valid. So generation is deterministic and always ends. Replay checks the journal's levelId against the spec's hash (code 12) as now. The Outcome is unchanged. In Training, a Generated entry plays endless fresh layouts under random nonces, in the browser.
