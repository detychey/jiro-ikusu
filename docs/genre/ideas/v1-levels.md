# Ideas: new levels on v1 knobs

The 7 ideas proposed from the lens *new levels on v1 knobs*: new levels and level sets that use only the knobs of level bytes v1 (tier T1). Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Slow giant, fast patrol: hide in the field

`v1-levels#10` · T1 Level data v1 · score 13 (core 5, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

The boss is huge but slow, and three fast minions sweep the rail, so the rail is the dangerous place. Halfway through a cut, standing on your own trail, no minion can reach you: you hide in the open field, where only the slow boss can come for you.

**Rule sketch.** Open field. B = 8, spawn rectangle (20, 20)-(31, 31), boss period 5 (3 cells a second). 3 minions at (30, 0), (0, 30) and (59, 30), period 1, R = 30. T = 2,700, lives 3, target 7,500. Minions walk only on rail cells (R4.7-1) and hit only by sharing, swapping or passing cells (R4.9-8), so they cannot hit a player standing on TRAIL. Stopping mid-cut is allowed (R4.5-8, D4), so the only threat to a parked cut is the boss bumping the trail (R4.9-2). Stepping off the rail is also safe from a minion that enters the cell just left (R4.9-12). The risk is the cell the cut closes on. Seed unchanged; period 5 is inside the 1-6 range that M1.5's divergence test covers (R4.6-12).

**Inspired by:** Xonix (1984), Cubixx HD (2011), Qix (1981). **Mechanic families:** [Edges that become dangerous](../taxonomy/edge-and-claimed-enemies.md#edges-that-become-dangerous), [Border patrollers (Sparx)](../taxonomy/edge-and-claimed-enemies.md#border-patrollers-sparx), [Land enemies on claimed ground](../taxonomy/edge-and-claimed-enemies.md#land-enemies-on-claimed-ground). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept. Scratch playtest: greedy clears 99.0 %, bold 76.2 %, committer 52.8 %. Search vs a skilled human: about the same, or narrower. Waiting in the field is an intuitive strategy that a human can apply with slack, because the boss moves only once every 5 ticks. The scripted bots never hide; search does when it pays.

**Budget.** RAM: none. Per tick: 3 minions at period 1 is the heaviest per-tick class, but the boss moves on only 1 tick in 5. Screen: unchanged.

**Variety.** On every current level the rail is safe and the field is dangerous. This level inverts that using only the periods, and turns the D4 pause mid-cut into a tactic: when to step off the rail, where to wait, and when to close.

**Core engineer: feasible, 5 of 5, tier 1.** Every claim checks against the rules. Minions walk only CLAIMED rail (R4.7-1). A player standing on TRAIL can't be shared, and swap needs a = P_k, which is a TRAIL cell while cutting. R4.9-12 covers the cell just left. So a parked cut is threatened only by the boss bumping TRAIL (R4.9-2) and by the closing cell. Period 5 lies within M1.5's tested 1-6 range, with 540 boss moves (>= 400). Validity: the spawn rectangle (20,20)-(31,31) with B = 8 (x1 \<= 51), and border spawns that are rail. I ran a quick D14 check that the idea omitted: 200 random keys, B = 8, period 5, 2,700 ticks on the open field. Per-seed body coverage was 68-96 % (median 87.5 %) and the union was 100 %, so the boss-free areas are seeded, not fixed. Novelty is moderate: 08-storm already has period-1 minions, and only the slow boss makes parking viable. The mix mean of about 64.5 % fits slot 7 (62 %).

*Cost.* RAM none. Per tick 3 minions at period 1, with the B = 8 boss's edge tests on only 1 tick in 5, lighter than 16-doom (24.7M on 16-worst). 2 gate vectors, playtest and calibration. Screen unchanged.

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** Boss period 5 gives about 540 moves per level, above the 400 of R4.6-12 and inside the periods 1-6 that M1.5 tests, so D14 holds. Unlike the other level pitches, this one reports no boss-only coverage for B = 8 at period 5. Hiding on the player's own trail is a state that is safe only from minions (R4.9-8, R4.9-12). It is not a safe zone: the seeded boss still threatens the trail. The tactic is intuitive and slow-paced (the boss covers 3 cells a second), so humans can use it with slack. Search's edge is exact timing of the close against three period-1 minions. Those minions are unseeded, deterministic and visible, so humans can learn that timing too, and the gap stays about the same. The calibration risk is that scripted bots never hide. The mix's median then measures a game played from the rail, and humans who hide may find the level easier than its weight implies.

*Fixes.* Before Challenge, give a scripted policy a behaviour that parks on its own trail, or validate the weight against human journals. Report the boss-only coverage at period 5, as the other level pitches do.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** Inverts the game's basic geography (rail safe, field dangerous) with periods alone. The D4 mid-cut stop becomes a tactic: dodge a head-on minion by stepping into the field, park, then time the landing between passing minions. Distinct: no level has a boss slower than period 3, or minions as the main threat. The rule basis is correct (R4.7-1, R4.9-8, R4.9-12). Concerns: it is easy (greedy 99 %) at target 7,500. A period-5 boss puts so little clock on a parked cut that the field becomes a near-free refuge, which flattens the 'where to wait' decision.

*Fixes.* Raise the target to about 8,500, to force more landings. Or use boss period 4, so a parked cut has a clock and the choice of waiting spot matters. Keep R = 30, so stranding minions buys little.

*Readability.* Unchanged and good: a large slow boss, and fast single-cell minions on the border.

## Street grid: seal segments to pen the boss

`v1-levels#2` · T1 Level data v1 · score 13 (core 4, fairness 4, play 5) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Nine city blocks turn the field into streets 7 cells wide, and every capture is a cut of that street network. Two short seals pen the boss in its street segment and claim everything else. Three blocks hold sleeping guards that wake when you touch their block.

**Rule sketch.** Obstacles: 9 blocks of 10x10 cells with top-left corners at (x, y), x, y in {8, 25, 42}. Streets are 7 cells wide at 1-7, 18-24, 35-41 and 52-58 on both axes. 900 obstacle cells, claimable 2,464. B = 5. Spawn rectangle (18, 1)-(20, 54): anywhere along the left-centre street. Boss period 1. 3 minions spawned on blocks, at (8, 8), (51, 8) and (34, 34). Each block is its own rail component, so each guard circles its block (R4.7-3) and cannot reach the player until a capture joins its block to the border's rail. Minion period 2, R = 2,700: a guard stranded at tick t would respawn at t + R - 1 >= 2,700, after the last tick (R4.7-9), so stranding is permanent. T = 2,700, lives 3, target 8,000. A seal is a cut from a block face, or the border, across a street to the next face: 7 trail cells. Two seals isolate a corner intersection. Two seals at both ends of the segment the boss is in capture everything else. Touching a block wakes its guard. Claiming every street cell around a block without touching it strands the guard (R4.7-7). Seed unchanged.

**Inspired by:** Amidar (1982), Kid Grid (1982), Cacoma Knight in Bizyland (1992), Xonix (1984). **Mechanic families:** [Lane lattice of boxes](../taxonomy/terrain-and-geometry.md#lane-lattice-of-boxes), [Pocketing and herding walls](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#pocketing-and-herding-walls), [Terrain-locked enemies](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#terrain-locked-enemies), [Enclosed enemies are destroyed or captured](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#enclosed-enemies-are-destroyed-or-captured). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept. Every street is at least B wide. In the boss-only simulation, 99.6 % of open cells come under the body in each seed, no cell stayed uncovered over 200 seeds, and keys part after a median of 9 moves. Guards are unseeded like every minion, but they sit on islands the player chooses to wake. Scratch playtest: greedy clears 99.5 %, bold 71.9 %, committer 51.0 %. Search (8 runs): 100 % clear, median 9,841 bp of a 9,898 bp ceiling. Search vs a skilled human: the gap widens a little. Picking the fewest exposed cells that isolate the boss's current sub-network is a min-cut problem, which a two-capture lookahead solves every time. Humans read the grid well, but more slowly against a period-1 boss. The reference mix seals streets only by accident.

**Budget.** RAM: none. Per tick: like 13-trio (3 minions at period 2); guards circling islands cost the same as border minions. Seals are short cuts, so captures are frequent and small, with the same per-tick bound as any level. Screen: 900 static cells, drawn once.

**Variety.** The first field that is a network of corridors rather than an open area. Captures are graph cuts, and the boss's position in the network is the thing to read. Touching a block has a cost, because it wakes the guard, while enveloping a block removes its guard for good.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 1.** I checked the geometry. Blocks span 8-17, 25-34 and 42-51 with 7-cell streets between them: 7 + 10 + 7 + 10 + 7 + 10 + 7 = 58. 900 obstacle cells, claimable 2,464 (>= 1,682). The spawn rectangle (18,1)-(20,54) keeps the 5x5 body in the x 18-24 street at every y (y1 = 54 = 59 - B). The minion spawns (8,8), (51,8) and (34,34) are block corners and so rail cells. (30,58) is open, so the player start is rail. Each block's rail (36 perimeter cells) is a separate 4-component, so a guard circles its block until a cut closes on it, and its trail cells become a claimed bridge. R = 2,700 is legal (a u16 >= 1), and t + 2,699 > 2,699 makes stranding permanent under PIN-17. Enveloping a block takes all its cells off the rail, so R4.7-8 destroys the guard. Caveats. (1) The guard is always moving, not 'asleep'. 'Waking' really means joining its block's rail to the network. A seal that starts from a block face first requires a cut closing on that block, so most two-seal plans touch at least one guard. (2) The reference mix in levels/targets.json is bold + committer, not greedy. Their mean of about 61.5 % fits slot 7 (62 %), so it would replace a mid-ladder level. (3) The search gap widening via min-cut lookahead is credible.

*Cost.* RAM none. Per tick about 13-trio (3 minions at period 2, boss period 1, B = 5). Fills of at most 2,464 cells. Adds a gate-set worst and synthetic vector pair, playtest and calibration. Screen: 900 static cells drawn once.

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** The boss is seeded and covers 99.6 % of open cells in each seed. The guards are unseeded, but they sleep on islands until a capture merges their block into the border's claimed component. Waking them is the player's choice, not a safe zone. The geometry checks out: blocks at 8-17, 25-34 and 42-51, streets 7 wide (at least B = 5), 2,464 claimable cells (at least 1,682), and R = 2,700, which makes stranding permanent. As the pitch says, the gap widens a little. Picking the cheapest seals around the boss's current street segment is a min-cut problem timed against a period-1 boss, and search computes it exactly. The fairness risk is calibration. The reference mix seals streets only by accident, so its median measures a different game from the one a human who reads the grid plays. If humans clear it far more easily than bold and committer do, its weight over-rewards whoever draws it. Scripted bots can play it (greedy 99.5 %, bold 71.9 %, committer 51.0 %), so it calibrates, and a mix clear rate of about 61 % fits the middle of the ladder.

*Fixes.* Before Challenge, give a scripted policy a street-sealing behaviour, or check the mix's median against human journals, so the weight reflects the intended play. Consider boss period 2: humans get time to read the street network, which also shrinks search's timing edge on the min-cut.

**Play: strong, 5 of 5.** Several new situations from existing knobs alone. (1) A corridor network with islands: captures become graph cuts, and the boss's position in the network is the thing to read. (2) Guards bound to islands, which reach the player only once a seal joins their block to the main rail. (3) Permanent stranding through R = 2,700, which I checked fits the u16 field. Touching a block is a real cost-benefit choice. Clearly distinct from comb, serpent, maze and labyrinth (path-like corridors), cross, and ring (one block in an open field). It reads perfectly on the device. Concerns: the two-seal knockout around the boss's segment can swing a level in seconds, and target 8,000 is easy (greedy 99.5 %). The min-cut lookahead widens the search gap slightly, which the pitch states honestly. 'Waking' is a misnomer: guards always circle their block, so players need to see which guards are still confined to their island.

*Fixes.* Dim guards whose block is still a separate rail component (client only), so 'asleep' and 'awake' read at a glance. Raise the target to about 9,000, so the endgame of pinning the boss in one segment is required rather than optional.

*Readability.* Excellent: 40 px blocks, 28 px streets, single-cell guards circling high-contrast islands. 900 static cells are drawn once.

## Topology season: one field shape per slot

`v1-levels#14` · T1 Level data v1 · score 12 (core 4, fairness 3, play 5) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

A season whose 16 levels are 16 different kinds of field, instead of mostly the open field with harder knobs, so each 6-level Challenge draw shows about five different kinds of board.

**Rule sketch.** No core change: 16 level sources in levels/src, new level ids, a new targets.json ladder, a playtest report, a calibration file and a new season file. levelIds stays bytes32\[16\]. Proposed slots, ordered by the scratch-playtest clear rate of the reference mix (mean of bold and committer): 01 open, 02 sentry, 03 tree (tuned harder), 04 comb, 05 stones, 06 posts, 07 hide, 08 city, 09 blitz, 10 vault, 11 cull, 12 labyrinth, 13 octagon, 14 letterbox, 15 track, 16 doom. The measured mix rates of the new levels are: tree 83.6 %, stones 69.1, posts 67.5, hide 64.5, city 61.5, blitz 53.6, cull 52.3, vault 52.1, octagon 34.6, letterbox 27.5, track 25.8. Ten of today's 16 levels have no obstacles; this set keeps five current levels. Bank and finish stay Training-only until the reference mix has a banker policy and their calibration has been checked against human journals. Each level's knobs (B, periods, minions, lives, target) are then retuned to hit its ladder rate within the 5 % tolerance. Sources and the boss-only checker are in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-jiro-ikusu/22a02834-c154-5172-b422-1bcaf068740f/scratchpad/work/ (gen_levels.py, sim_boss.py).

**Inspired by:** Amidar (1982), Dancing Eyes (1996), Star Trigon (2002), Pac-Xon (by early 2010). **Mechanic families:** [Alternating or varied stage types](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#alternating-or-varied-stage-types), [Staged introduction of new enemies, hazards and rules](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#staged-introduction-of-new-enemies-hazards-and-rules), [Time-boxed ranked competitions](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#time-boxed-ranked-competitions). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots, economy.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept. Every new layout passes the same boss-only checks: no open cell stayed uncovered over 200 seeds, and keys part after a median of 9-12 moves, as on 01-open. Calibration risk: the reference bots cut only fully open rectangles, so on topologies built around anchors and seals (stones, city, vault, tree, track) their medians do not track what humans can do. Adding a seal-aware scripted policy to the mix is a tools change, and it reruns every level's playtest and calibration. Search vs a skilled human, over the whole set: about the same as today. Letterbox, blitz, tree and vault should narrow the gap; city, track and cull widen it. Search cleared every new level it played (8 runs each on city, vault, letterbox, track and finish) at 98-99 % of the claim ceiling.

**Budget.** RAM: none; level files only. Proving cost per run follows the level mix: blitz lowers it, while tree, cull, hide and track sit in the 3-minion or period-1 classes like today's hard levels. The 16-colour screen and partial redraws are unchanged.

**Variety.** Variety per run rather than per level. With today's ladder, a draw is mostly open fields; with this one, a run crosses corridors, islands, loops, mazes and fields where the danger is inverted.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 1.** T1 is right. All 13 scratch sources in work/lv were compiled by the level tool under level bytes v1, and every knob is in range (city/posts/cull use R=2700, tree B=2, blitz T=600 with 9 lives). levelIds stays bytes32\[16\], with no core, format or vector change. Claims I checked: 10 of today's 16 levels have no interior obstacles (01-04, 07, 08, 13-16), and the set keeps open, sentry, comb, labyrinth and doom. The scratch bold report agrees with the quoted rates (tree 90.3 % bold, letterbox 37.0 %, track 33.0 %). The main problem is calibration, which the pitch understates. The weight is P x 10,000 / max(m, floor), and m is the mix median. On seal topologies the mix underrates what a human can see. Vault: wait by the 8-cell door and close it with a fast cut (about 4 ticks) while the boss is inside. That takes the whole outer ring, about 87 % of the claimable cells, in one capture, and clears 8,000 bp at once. Yet the mix clears vault only 52.1 % of the time, so vault would get a high weight and be easy for a human who knows the trick. Track: a B=8 boss in a 9-wide ring moves almost along rails, and two 9-cell cuts isolate a segment. Seal-dependent levels go from 6 of 16 to about 10 of 16, so the draw decides more of the ranking, against README §8.4. Smaller points. Tree at 83.6 % is already inside slot 03's band (85 +/- 5), so it may not need tuning harder. Vault (52.1) is ordered above cull (52.3), which does not matter once knobs are retuned. targets.json's ladder can stay as it is. Settlement submits level bytes, so replacing levels/src must keep the old bytes for runs of earlier seasons. Tree is a maze like 10-maze, today's heaviest level (29.6M user cycles), so a new gate set must re-measure the heaviest level. Bank and finish as Training-only extras contradict README §8.1 ('All 16 levels') unless Training gets its own list. Search still reaches 98-99 % of the ceiling, so the roughly 2x gap stays.

*Fixes.* Before a paid season uses the set, either add a seal-aware scripted policy to the reference mix (one that closes a door or neck while the boss is on the far side), or keep vault, track, city, stones and tree out of Challenge until testnet human journals have checked their weights. Keep a level directory per season, so old seasons' level bytes stay available for settlement. Build a gate set for the new ids and measure the heaviest level's zk cycles. Give bank and finish an explicit Training-only list, or drop them.

*Cost.* RAM 0; per-tick work 0 (no core change); level files 16 x 472 B with new ids; format versions unchanged; vectors: no forced regeneration (vectors/levels embed the old bytes), plus a new gate set to re-measure cycles; zk cycles: blitz (T=600) is at most about 22 % of a full level, while tree and cull are at about today's 30M ceiling and must be measured; playtest 16 levels x 1,000 runs x 2 policies, calibration file, about 15 h of bot-advantage shards; wedgie app level pack rebuilt

*Conflicts.* README 8.1 'All 16 levels' (bank/finish as extra Training-only levels); README 8.4 purpose (draw normalization) at risk from seal-level miscalibration

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. There is no core change and the seeded deflections still apply. I re-ran sim_boss.py on the scratch sources. Every open cell is under the body for some seed, and coverage within one level is 0.83 to 1.00, except tree at 0.48 (a B = 2 boss in 3-wide corridors). Coverage varies with the seed, so there is no safe zone that is the same for every seed. But the union-over-seeds check cannot rule out cells that the boss almost never reaches. Search reaches 98-99 % of the ceiling on every new level it played, the same as today. Because the mix is re-laddered to the same clear rates, the pot-share ratio should stay about where it is: tree, letterbox, blitz and vault narrow the gap, while city, track and cull widen it. The real risk is calibration fidelity. The mix (bold, committer) only cuts open rectangles, so its median does not track a skilled human on: the seal and anchor boards (stones, city, vault, tree, track); and the levels with R = 2700, where minions never come back once stranded (city, posts, cull). The weightsBp for those slots are therefore wrong for humans, in an unknown direction, and the draw decides whether a run gets them, which is luck. Today's set already shows this: a human cleared labyrinth at 8,444 bp in 163 ticks, against a mix median of 6,353 and a weight of 18,888. This set adds more such slots. Letterbox, track and octagon are bimodal for bold (median 0, 55-67 % deaths), so their weights rest on committer's commits and land near trio and titan (about 25-30k). Tree's 9,700 target puts its median near 9,735 and its weight near 12,300, below maze's 13,692, so the max/min weight spread grows from 4.7x to about 5.2x. Sixteen kinds of board also add player-specific variance (a maze specialist's run depends on whether mazes are drawn), which no weight can remove. Scripted bots can play every level; the rates were measured.

*Fixes.* - Make the seal-aware scripted policy a precondition for any paid season, not a follow-up. Add it to the targets.json mix and rerun playtest and calibrate for all 16 levels. Until then, keep stones, city, vault, tree and track Training-only.
- Add acceptance numbers to the calibration: the max/min weightsBp (today 64,620 / 13,692 = 4.7x) and the spread of claims per seed on each level. Refuse a set that widens either.
- Replace the union coverage check with a per-seed floor, for example the 5th percentile of coverage within one level, and look again at tree.
- Collect a few human Training journals on the new boards before a season opens (the human baseline that M4.13 lists as needing the user).

**Play: strong, 5 of 5.** This one adds the most variety of the ten without touching the core. Today 10 of the 16 levels are the open field with harder knobs, so a 6-level draw mostly replays one situation. The proposed sources in scratchpad/work/lv/src create real new situations for the existing loop. Stones: short cuts anchored on pillars. City: block-to-block seals, and with respawn 2700 the minions never return, so you strand them on purpose. Vault: a boss pen with doors you can seal. Track: a 10-wide ring where an 8-cell boss races you around a central block. Tree: a 4-wide maze with a 2-cell boss and a 9,700 bp target, which turns the finisher into a sealing puzzle. Octagon: staircase rails that change both the bounce angles and the minion patrols. Posts: minions on 1-, 2- and 3x3-cell posts become a stationary mine, a shuttler and a circler inside the field, removed by capturing around them. Hide: a slow 8-cell boss with fast-respawning minions, so the danger sits on the rail. Cull: fast minions that never come back. Blitz: 600 ticks and 9 lives. It uses no new rules, so the feel is unchanged. The real weakness is fairness, and the pitch states it honestly: the reference bots cut only open rectangles, so the topologies built on seals (stones, city, vault, tree, track) will be over-weighted relative to how skilled humans find them. In a paid draw that makes those slots jackpot draws.

*Fixes.* Add the seal-aware scripted policy to the reference mix before the set goes into a paid season. Until then, ship it as a Training season, or keep the seal-heavy slots at conservative weights. Keep 01 open as the opening slot. Retune tree's 9,700 bp target together with its knobs so that slot 03 does not ask beginners to box a 2-cell boss into a pocket of about 100 cells. Check with the boss-only checker that the posts minions behave as designed: the single-cell post minion stands still per PIN-18 and the 2-cell one shuttles.

*Readability.* Very good. Everything is static obstacle geometry drawn in the existing claimed colour, at 4 px per cell. Tree's 4-cell corridors and octagon's staircase edges read clearly at 240x240. The posts are 1-3 cells (4-12 px), small but static and high-contrast. No new colours or glyphs.

## Letterbox band: one-axis squeeze

`v1-levels#4` · T1 Level data v1 · score 11 (core 4, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

The upper half of the field is solid, leaving a 58 x 30 band with an 8 x 8 boss in it. Almost every cut is the same 30-cell slice from floor to ceiling, so the whole level comes down to one question: how close to the boss do you dare to slice?

**Rule sketch.** Obstacles: every interior cell with 1 \<= y \<= 28 (1,624 cells), leaving rows 29-58 open. Claimable 1,740, above the 1,682 floor of validity rule 36. B = 8, spawn rectangle (5, 29)-(46, 51), boss period 2. 2 minions at (20, 28) and (40, 28) on the band's ceiling, period 2, R = 60; they patrol the band's whole boundary. T = 2,700, lives 3, target 8,000. A vertical slice from the bottom border to the ceiling crosses rows 29-58: 30 trail cells, about 15 ticks at fast cut. It captures the side the boss is not on (R4.8). Seed unchanged.

**Inspired by:** Xonitix (2018), Antix (1985), Shocker (1988), Cubixx (2009). **Mechanic families:** [One-dimensional field](../taxonomy/terrain-and-geometry.md#one-dimensional-field), [Field size and resolution](../taxonomy/terrain-and-geometry.md#field-size-and-resolution), [Subtractive cutting](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#subtractive-cutting). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept. The band is 30 cells high, more than B; in the boss-only simulation 99.6 % of open cells come under the body in each seed, and keys part after a median of 10 moves. Scratch playtest: greedy clears 98.5 %, bold 37.0 % (62.9 % deaths), committer 18.0 % (82 % commit). At boss period 1, bold fell to 5.6 %, so the boss period is the difficulty dial. Search (8 runs): 100 % clear, median 9,465 bp of a 9,632 bp ceiling. Search vs a skilled human: I expect the gap to narrow, but this is untested with humans. With only one useful axis, a human's rule of slicing as the boss heads away captures much of what search finds, and search's remaining edge is exact timing. The mix dies often here, so the calibrated weight will be high: check it against human journals before Challenge.

**Budget.** RAM: none. The cheapest field: half the grid is static, and fills cover at most 1,740 cells. Per tick: like a level with 2 minions at period 2. Screen: 1,624 static cells, drawn once.

**Variety.** No current level has a field with only one useful axis. Every decision collapses to where and when to slice, which reads instantly on the 240 px screen.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 1.** The numbers check out: 58 x 28 = 1,624 obstacle cells, 30 x 58 = 1,740 claimable (just above the 1,682 floor of rule 36). The spawn rectangle (5,29)-(46,51) fits B = 8 (x1 \<= 51, y1 \<= 51). The minion spawns at y = 28 touch row 29, so they are rail, and their orientation step faces E/W along the ceiling with the field on the right. The player start is rail. Strategy: vertical slices alone cap the claim at about 8,620 bp, because the boss strip must stay >= 8 wide x 30 tall. The 9,632 ceiling and search's 9,465 need horizontal cuts at the end, so it is not purely one-axis. Target 8,000 forces the last slice to within about 3 columns of the boss's width, which is where the skill sits. The mix mean (bold 37 %, committer 18 %) is about 27.5 %, slot 13 (28 %). Bold's 63 % deaths still leave the pooled median effective claim above the 500 bp floor, since committer rarely dies, but confirm this with playtest --check. Variety is narrow by design. The idea flags the human-gap claim as untested, which is right.

*Cost.* RAM none. The cheapest field: fills cover at most 1,740 cells, 2 minions at period 2, boss period 2. Adds 2 gate vectors, playtest and calibration. Screen: 1,624 static cells, drawn once.

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: the B = 8 boss is seeded and covers 99.6 % of the band in each seed. With only one useful axis, the human heuristic (slice while the boss heads away, keeping more than 8 cells of margin for a 15-tick fast slice) comes close to what search does. The 9,632 bp ceiling leaves search little room above that. Search's remaining edge is seeing deflections along the band during a slice. The boss's x velocity flips on 1 move in 64, which is about 11 % over a 15-tick slice at period 2, and humans cannot see it coming. The gap therefore probably narrows, but humans have not tested it. The risk is calibration. The mix dies often (bold 62.9 % deaths, committer banks in 82 %), so its median effective claim, and therefore the high weight, follows the bots' deaths. A careful human may find one-axis slicing easy, so on a high-weight level the weight could over-reward whoever draws it. That is draw variance. Bots can play the level.

*Fixes.* Check the mix's median against human journals before Challenge, as the pitch says. If humans clear it much more often, have bold and committer slice with a margin rather than accept a weight inflated by bot deaths, and tune difficulty with the boss period (keep 2).

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** A clean one-axis squeeze. One sharp, instantly readable decision: how close to the boss to slice. But it is a subset of open-field play rather than new situations: essentially 03-heavy (B 8, period 2) in half a field. Horizontal and L-shaped cuts remain possible, so it is not strictly one-dimensional. The large spread among the bots (greedy 98.5 % against bold 37 % and committer 18 %) makes its calibrated weight sensitive, as the pitch notes. Half the 240 px screen is static solid, which is a lot of dead screen on the device.

*Fixes.* Use it as a teaching level early in a ladder, where its single decision is a virtue. For a later slot, use B 6-7 at period 1 rather than only raising the speed. Alternatively, centre the band (rows 15-44), so slices can start from either side and the two minions threaten two separate faces. Check the weight against human journals before Challenge.

*Readability.* Excellent. A 30-row band with an 8 x 8 boss reads instantly, and the static half is drawn once.

## Racetrack loop: two seals trap the train

`v1-levels#5` · T1 Level data v1 · score 11 (core 4, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

A huge island leaves a loop 9 cells wide, and an 8 x 8 boss runs laps around it like a train. The first seal only turns the loop into a dead end. The second, placed right behind the train, claims every part of the track it is not in.

**Rule sketch.** Obstacles: an island at (10, 10)-(49, 49), 1,600 cells, so the open field is a ring 9 cells wide. Claimable 1,764. B = 8, which leaves one cell of lateral play. Spawn rectangle (1, 50)-(51, 51), on the bottom straight. Boss period 1. Under R4.6-3 the body turns every corner by itself and laps in about 200 moves. It reverses only when a seeded deflection (R4.6-7) flips its component along the track, or when it meets a seal. 2 minions at (0, 30) and (59, 30), period 1, R = 45. T = 2,700, lives 3, target 8,500. A seal is a 9-cell cut across the ring. Seal 1, from the border to the island, captures only its own trail, because the ring stays connected; it also bridges the island and lets the minions onto it. Seal 2 splits the remaining corridor and captures the part without the boss. Seed unchanged.

**Inspired by:** Space Cropper (2014), Lock 'n' Chase (1981), Xonix (1984). **Mechanic families:** [Orbiters](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#orbiters), [Non-rectangular field outlines](../taxonomy/terrain-and-geometry.md#non-rectangular-field-outlines), [Pocketing and herding walls](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#pocketing-and-herding-walls). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept. The boss-only simulation covered every open cell in each of 200 seeds. Two keys part after a median of 11 moves (maximum 132): the narrow ring absorbs some lateral deflections, but enough still act. Scratch playtest: greedy clears 93.7 %, bold 33.0 %, committer 18.6 %. Search (8 runs): 100 % clear, median 9,489 bp of a 9,637 bp ceiling. Search vs a skilled human: the gap widens a little. The value of seal 2 depends on leading a target that moves 1 cell per tick, which search simulates exactly and a human must estimate, though the plan itself is simple to read.

**Budget.** RAM: none. Per tick: like 08-storm (2 minions at period 1, boss period 1). Fills cover at most 1,764 cells. Screen: 1,600 static cells.

**Variety.** A loop topology. The first cut changes the field's shape without claiming anything, the boss is a predictable train with rare seeded reversals, and the second cut is a timing shot that must lead a moving target.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 1.** I checked the geometry. The island (10..49)^2 is 1,600 cells, claimable 1,764, and the ring is 9 wide. The spawn rectangle (1,50)-(51,51) keeps the 8x8 body in the bottom straight. The minion spawns (0,30) and (59,30) touch the open ring, so they are rail. 'Turns every corner by itself': in a 9-wide corridor the vy state cycles through 4 phases, and the body spends 4 consecutive moves at x = 50-51 in a corner square. One of those is the (y = 50, vy = -1) phase, which goes up, so it always turns. My quick simulation (200 random keys, B = 8, period 1, 2,700 ticks) covered >= 99.9 % of the ring in every seed and 100 % in the union. Along-track reversals come only from deflections of the along-track component (about 1 in 64 moves), as claimed. Seal 1 joins the island rail to the border rail, so the minions (period 1, as fast as the player) then patrol about 400 rail cells. Seal 2 has to close within about 29 track cells of seal 1 to reach 8,500: a timing shot. The mix mean is about 25.8 %, slot 13-14. Two period-1 minions on a single 236-cell border rail before seal 1 make the opening dangerous. Covered by the playtest numbers.

*Cost.* RAM none. Per tick about 08-storm (2 minions at period 1, boss period 1, B = 8), whose worst vector is about 21.2M cycles. Fills of at most 1,764 cells. 2 gate vectors, playtest and calibration. Screen: 1,600 static cells.

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: every open cell is covered over 200 seeds, and two keys part after a median of 11 moves. But the level's score hinges on seal 2, a shot that must lead a period-1 train, and one thing breaks it: a deflection along the track. That comes on 1 move in 64, about 8 % over a 5-tick, 9-cell seal close behind the train. Search sees it coming and a human cannot. So the gap widens, as the pitch says. Outcomes are also all-or-nothing: either seal 2 lands for 8,500 bp or more, or the run dies or banks low. That adds per-level variance on top of the draw. Seal 1 also bridges the island, so the two period-1 minions reach it, which stacks the risk. Scripted bots can play it (greedy 93.7 %, bold 33 %, committer 18.6 %), so it calibrates. But the mix's low median gives it a high weight, which amplifies that variance.

*Fixes.* Use boss period 2, which halves the reversal risk per seal and gives humans time to lead the train, or B = 7 for more lateral play. Consider lives 4 or a target of 8,000 to make the outcome less all-or-nothing. Calibrate with a scripted policy that times seal 2, so the weight is not set by bots that never take the shot.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** New topology and a new kind of cut. The first seal reshapes the field without claiming anything, turning the loop into a dead-end tube where the train becomes a piston. The second is a leading shot behind a moving target, then repeated shrinking of the tube. Distinct from 12-ring, a 21-wide open ring around a 16 x 16 block. The train metaphor reads at a glance. Concerns: the first seal is a no-claim chore every game. B 8 with 1 cell of lateral play makes any cut in front of the boss fatal, so the only cut type is 'behind the train', repeated. Leading a period-1 target is hard for humans (bold 33 %), and the search gap widens a little.

*Fixes.* Consider boss period 2 for a mid-ladder slot, with period 1 kept for a hard variant. Lives 4 or a target near 8,000 if the human clear rates come in low. Optionally make the first seal pay a little, for example with an island notch, so it is not pure setup.

*Readability.* Excellent: 36 px corridor, a 32 px boss, a 160 px static island drawn once. The boss's heading is obvious from its motion.

## Tree maze: every seal cuts off a subtree

`v1-levels#6` · T1 Level data v1 · score 11 (core 4, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

The field is a perfect maze of corridors 3 cells wide. Because it has no loops, any 3-cell cut across a corridor disconnects it and claims the whole branch the boss is not in. You close in on a small boss, cutting off branches as you go, while the minions follow the walls.

**Rule sketch.** Obstacles: walls 1 cell thick on the lines x = 4k and y = 4k (4 \<= x, y \<= 56), around a 15 x 15 lattice of rooms. Rooms are 3 cells wide (x = 4i+1 to 4i+3 for i \< 14), and the last row and column of rooms span 57-58. A randomized depth-first search from a fixed tool seed opens the passages between neighbouring rooms, so the open area is a tree. The ASCII source is the level, not the generator (P10). 777 obstacle cells, claimable 2,587. All walls form one claimed component with the border (checked), so the player and the minions can walk every wall face. B = 2, spawn rectangle (29, 29)-(30, 30) in the centre room, boss period 1. 3 minions at (30, 0), (0, 30) and (59, 30), period 1. The right-hand rule makes them wall-followers over the whole maze. R = 45. A stranded minion respawns at its spawn, or, if that is no longer rail, at the first rail cell in row-major order (R4.7-7), which lies in what is left of the maze. T = 2,700, lives 3, target 9,700. Seed unchanged.

**Inspired by:** Pepper II (1982), Grid Riders (2026), Lock 'n' Chase (1981), Make Trax (1981). **Mechanic families:** [Movement confined to the line network](../taxonomy/movement-and-drawing.md#movement-confined-to-the-line-network), [Grid-bound chasers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#grid-bound-chasers), [Capture by disconnection](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#capture-by-disconnection), [Pocketing and herding walls](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#pocketing-and-herding-walls). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept, with a caveat. In the boss-only simulation the 2 x 2 boss visits only 49 % of the maze in one level, but every cell in some of 200 seeds, and keys part after a median of 12 moves. So each game has large boss-free branches, but which branches they are depends on the seed. Scratch playtest: at target 9,000 the scripted bots cleared 97-99 % in about 310 ticks. At 9,700, greedy clears 92.1 %, bold 90.3 %, committer 76.8 %. It is easy because even a fully open rectangle cut across a corridor is a seal. Search vs a skilled human: the gap narrows. The key move is obvious, and everyone gets near the ceiling, which leaves search little room. The flip side is low skill expression, so the level belongs at the easy end of the ladder, or needs lives 2 and target 9,800.

**Budget.** RAM: none. Per tick: 3 minions at period 1, the class of 10-maze, whose worst vector is the heaviest today (29.6M user cycles at T 2,700). Captures are frequent, and their fills shrink as the maze is cut. Screen: 777 static cells; walls 1 cell thick are legible at 4 px.

**Variety.** No current level has this pursuit structure. Captures become subtree cuts, and the player moves by walking wall faces through a maze. The minions are true wall-followers, and their respawns crowd into the last branches.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 1.** The geometry holds. Lines x, y = 4k for k = 1..14 give 14 rooms 3 wide and a 2-wide last room (57-58), 15 x 15 in all. An unopened lattice has 1,428 wall cells, and 224 tree openings of 2-3 cells remove about 650, which gives the stated 777. In a perfect maze no lattice point can lose all 4 walls, since that would make a cycle, so there are no isolated pillars. The walls are the dual spanning tree, so they are 4-connected to the border, and every wall cell touches a room, so it is rail. The rail is therefore the border cycle plus wall trees hanging inward. Minions trace both sides of 1-thick walls, which D7 coverage handles (bands one cell thick were tested). The spawn (29,29)-(30,30) keeps the 2x2 body in the centre room, and B = 2 fits the 2-wide last row and column with no lateral play. The minion spawns and the player start are rail. Points to watch. (1) Cycles: each capture costs a 3,600-cell pass plus a fill of the boss's region. 3-cell seals make captures very frequent, and 10-maze's class is already the heaviest (31.2M SP1 / 29.6M RISC Zero on 10-worst). Fills shrink as the maze is cut, but a 2,700-tick, capture-heavy journal could top 10-worst, which would raise the per-run proving price. (2) Wall dead-ends with period-1 wall-following minions trap a player on the rail. The escape is a 3-cell cut, always available. (3) The mix mean of about 83.6 % fits slot 3 (85 %). The low skill expression is honestly flagged. D14: the boss-free branches depend on the seed, not seed-independent safe zones.

*Fixes.* Before adopting it, run the M2.9 synthetic policy ('as many captures as it finds') on this level in the zk executor. If it exceeds 10-worst, raise the target or cut T so that the heaviest proof stays within today's bound.

*Cost.* RAM none. Per tick the 10-maze class (3 minions at period 1). zk cycles may reach or pass about 31M for capture-heavy journals, so measure it. Fills of at most 2,587 cells. 2 gate vectors, playtest and calibration. Screen: 777 static cells.

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds, with a real caveat. The 2 x 2 boss visits only about 49 % of the maze in a level, so each game has large boss-free branches, but which branches they are depends on the seed. The maze is fixed and the boss spawns in the centre, so deep leaves may still be safer in most seeds. 'Every cell in some of 200 seeds' does not rule that out. The gap narrows: the key move, a 3-cell seal across a corridor, is obvious, and everyone gets near the 9,984 bp ceiling, which leaves search little room. Bots play it easily (greedy 92.1 %, bold 90.3 %, committer 76.8 %). Calibration is stable: high, tightly grouped medians give a low weight and little draw variance. The flip side is low skill expression: the level separates players poorly, which is fine for an easy slot. Proving cost stays in 10-maze's class.

*Fixes.* Report a floor on how often the boss visits each cell across seeds, not just 'some seed', to rule out leaves that are safe whatever the seed. Adopt lives 2 and target 9,800, as the pitch suggests, and place the level at the easy end of the ladder.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** A branching tree, where 10-maze and 11-labyrinth are essentially paths. The 1-cell walls make the player and three period-1 wall-followers share a network of wall tops: dodging at junctions is a genuinely new movement situation. But every corridor cut is a seal and every capture is good, so the decisions are obvious and skill expression is low (bots near the ceiling, as the pitch admits). A 15 x 15 perfect maze is dense at 240 px: the branch structure, and so the size of a capture, is hard to read at speed. The last row and column of rooms are irregular (2 wide). The level is in the heaviest cycle class.

*Fixes.* Use fewer, bigger rooms (e.g. an 8 x 8 lattice, 5-wide corridors, B 3), for legibility and for longer, riskier seals. Open one or two loops (an imperfect maze), so some cuts do not seal and reading the topology becomes a decision again. Otherwise apply the pitch's lives 2 / target 9,800 and place it at the easy end of a ladder.

*Readability.* Walls 4 px thick between 12 px corridors are visible, but tracing which branch connects where in a 225-room maze is slow for a human. The picture reads, the topology does not.

## Bank it: a level that never clears

`v1-levels#11` · T1 Level data v1 · score 8 (core 2, fairness 2, play 4) · gap: unclear · D14: compliant

**Rejected:** the fairness judge found it unacceptable in Challenge. The reference mix's median claim falls under the claim floor of [`levels/targets.json`](../../../levels/targets.json), so its weight clamps at about 15 times an ordinary level's, and drawing it would decide rankings. It also fails `playtest --check` (P11). It stays possible as a Training-only board.

The target is 100 %, which no play can reach, so the level ends only when you press Commit, run out of time or lose your last life. Every extra percent is carved closer to a small, fast boss, and the decision is when to bank.

**Rule sketch.** Open field. Target 10,000 bp. The boss's B x B body only ever covers EMPTY cells (R4.6-1), so the claim never exceeds (3,364 - 16) x 10,000 / 3,364 = 9,952 bp, and the clear condition (PIN-10) never fires. The level ends by X (R4.10-2, claim kept), by the time limit (R4.10-4, claim kept) or with the last life (R4.10-3, which scores 0 by D5). B = 4, spawn rectangle (20, 20)-(35, 35), boss period 1. 2 minions at (0, 30) and (59, 30), period 2, R = 60, so idling on the rail is not safe and X is the only free way to bank. Lives 2, T = 2,700. Seed unchanged.

**Inspired by:** VOLTFIELD: 80s Classic Arcade (2026), Claim (DaNuTree) (2026), Kingdom Cutter: Cut & Conquer (2026), Spider: Rite of the Shrouded Moon (2015). **Mechanic families:** [Push past the quota or bank](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#push-past-the-quota-or-bank), [Exit gate and optional overstay](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#exit-gate-and-optional-overstay), [Configurable or escalating clear threshold](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#configurable-or-escalating-clear-threshold). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots, economy.

**Decisions it reopens:** D10; P11.

**Bot effect.** D14 kept. Scratch playtest: greedy, which never commits, dies in 41.8 % of runs and reaches the time limit in 58.2 %, with a median effective claim of 9,304 bp. Bold dies in 99.2 %. Committer banks at its first lost life, median 1,893 bp. Search (8 runs) reaches 9,952 bp, the exact ceiling, in every run, then commits or runs out the clock. Search vs a skilled human: the gap widens strongly. Late micro-cuts in a small field next to a fast boss are what search does best, and humans will bank earlier. Calibration breaks: the mix's median effective claim falls under the 500 bp floor of targets.json (P11), so the weight would be clamped at P x 10,000 / 500, about 15 times an ordinary level's. The level is unusable in Challenge until the reference mix includes a banker policy (commit at a claim threshold). A season that includes it also cannot use minClears = 6 (D10). Training first.

**Budget.** RAM: none. A game that is not committed always runs all 2,700 ticks, so a typical proof costs like a full-length level of its class (2 minions at period 2). Screen: unchanged.

**Variety.** Today Commit is an emergency exit. Here it is the central decision, weighed continuously against a risk that rises with every percent.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 1.** The arithmetic is right. With B = 4, 16 cells always stay EMPTY, so the claim is at most 3,348 x 10,000 / 3,364 = 9,952, and PIN-10 never fires. A target of 10,000 is legal (rule 34). But it cannot join the 16-level ladder as things stand, and the problem goes beyond Challenge weighting. (1) `playtest --check` rejects any level whose mix median effective claim is at or below floor_bp (tools/src/playtest.rs:751), so this level fails P11 outright, not just at calibration. (2) P11's clear-rate curve is non-increasing, about 15 % at level 16 within ±5 pp. A 0 % clear rate has no valid slot. (3) Training plays the season's 16 levels, so 'Training first' needs a 17th, extra-ladder level: a product change to the level select and the JL01 level file. (4) Even with a banker policy in the mix, the level rewards exactly what search does best: micro-cuts next to a fast, small boss, with search reaching 9,952 every run. Its weight multiplies that edge, so whichever runs draw it would decide the ranking, against §8.4's purpose. (5) Under D10, seasons with minClears = 6 can't include it. Minor point: X is not the only way to bank. Reaching the time limit keeps the claim, and parking mid-cut is safe from minions, though not from a period-1 boss.

*Fixes.* Ship it as a Training-only extra level outside the season's 16 (a level-select entry with no weight). Before any ladder or Challenge use: add a banker policy to tools/src/bots, amend P11 and targets.json to exempt never-clear levels from the clear-rate curve, and cap such a level's weight (for example, compute it with a floor near a typical level's median) so that it cannot dominate a run.

*Cost.* RAM none. A game that is not committed runs all 2,700 ticks, so each proof costs about a full-length level of the 04-pair class (04-worst is about 21.8M cycles). Tools: a new bot policy and a targets.json schema exception. Screen unchanged.

*Conflicts.* P11; D10; §8.4 purpose: normalize difficulty

**Fairness: unacceptable, 2 of 5; gap unclear; D14 compliant.** This is fine for Training, but not for Challenge as specified. D14 holds: the boss is seeded as on any level. The problem is calibration and variance. The mix's median effective claim falls under the 500 bp floor, so the weight clamps at P x 10,000 / 500, about 15 times an ordinary level's. A run that draws this level (37.5 % of runs) could score up to about 20P on it, dwarfing the other five levels. The draw would then decide rankings, which is the opposite of what §8.4 intends. *Fixes.* Keep it Training-only. If a version goes to Challenge, set a target the claim can reach, just under the ceiling (for example 9,500), so the level can be cleared.
