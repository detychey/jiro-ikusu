# Ideas: reveal and presentation

The 6 ideas proposed from the axis of the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) *reveal and presentation*: image reveal, silhouettes, themes, music, colour coding, visual feedback, juice, screen layout. Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Boss path forecast overlay

`reveal-and-presentation#1` · T0 Presentation · score 12 (core 4, fairness 5, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

A faint dotted track shows where the boss will be over the next two seconds on the current board. If it would strike your trail, the trail cell it would hit blinks with a countdown, so you see the short-term future the search bot already computes.

**Rule sketch.** Client only, after each processed tick, never in the core's step and never journaled. The shell (no_std) copies the Boss (already Copy: pos, vx, vy, period, deflection_key) and calls the core's existing pub Boss::step(&grid, t) for t = tick+1 .. tick+H on the unchanged current grid. It records the body's top-left on each moving tick and stops at the first call that returns true, where a leading edge holds TRAIL. That gives the predicted contact tick tc and its edge cells. It also copies Minions (Copy) and runs Minions::step / right_hand_step for each live minion's next 3 moves. The forecast is exact while the grid does not change (no new trail cell, no capture), and it is recomputed every tick, so it always shows the true future of the board as it stands. Drawing: one 1-px dot at the body's centre cell per predicted move (a dim palette colour); a ghost outline of the body at tick+H; the TRAIL cells of the predicted contact blink, with ceil((tc - tick)/15) s shown; 1-px arrows on each minion's next cells. H defaults to 30 ticks (2 s); Training has a slider from 0 to 90 ticks, and Challenge uses one client-release constant for everyone. In the browser, the music's intensity follows tc - tick (danger-reactive music). Seeds: the core already derives the deflection key from seed bytes 5..8; nothing new.

**Bot effect.** D14 is untouched. The future still depends on the run's seed and can only be computed inside that run, as it can today. The gap narrows: humans get the 2-second slice of exact boss foresight that the search bot uses to keep cuts safe. The bot keeps its long-horizon planning and frame-exact timing. Calibration does not move, because bots never render, so season weights stay put while human scores rise. Its inverse, spotlight darkness, would widen the gap, because the bot reads the full state; keep that out of Challenge.

**Budget.** No core state and no consensus code; it reuses pub Boss::step and Minions::step. Per tick: at most H x 2 x B edge reads (480 at H=30, B=8), plus H fmix32 calls and 9 minion moves. Zero zk cycles. RAM: about 2H + 20 bytes in the shell. Screen: at most about 2H dot cells restored and drawn per tick, partial redraws only.

**Variety.** Cutting becomes reading instead of reacting. Players can thread a cut through the window the boss leaves, stop mid-cut (D4) when the countdown is short, or bait a bounce. Minion arrows make rail ambushes readable on the 3-minion levels.

- Boss path forecast overlay: A faint dotted ghost shows where the boss will go over the next 24 ticks, with a mark where a seeded quarter-turn will happen. A human player can then read the same near future a search bot computes, and plan the cut around it instead of guessing.
- Boss path forecast: A fading dotted line shows where the boss will go over the next couple of seconds, deflections included, and turns red where it would hit your current trail. Players plan around the boss's real future instead of guessing.
- Boss path forecast with trail impact countdown: A dotted ghost shows where the boss will travel over the next three seconds if no more trail is drawn. Where that path meets the open trail, the trail cell blinks with a countdown, so the player can see whether the cut will close in time.

The seeded future that the search bot already simulates becomes something you can read.
- Foresight lens: A faint outline shows where the boss and the minions will be over the next two seconds if you keep your current input. When holding course would get you hit, a warning such as 'hit in 1.3 s' appears, and the region your cut would capture if you closed it straight ahead is tinted.
- Exposure forecast and capture preview: While a cut is open, the trail cells the boss would hit first if you stopped now glow, and a counter shows how many ticks the line is safe. In the browser, a ghost readout also shows the bp the cut would capture if closed straight ahead, and whether that capture would clear the level.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 0.** I checked this against the code. Boss is Copy, Boss::step(&Grid, u16) is pub, and it returns true when a leading edge holds TRAIL. Its bounce off TRAIL is the real one. Minions is Copy, and right_hand_step is pub. The boss reads only the grid, so the forecast is exact for as long as the player adds no trail and makes no capture. That is the case when the player stands still mid-cut (D4), so the countdown is exactly 'if you stop now'. Stopping at the first true call is right, because a lost life erases the trail. Only with B held and a shield charge left does the trail stay (R4.9-15), so the path could be continued as a bounce for the shielded case. Nothing reaches consensus. D14 is untouched: the future is per seed and per tick, and anyone can already build this from the open client. Every Challenge player gets the same H. Bot-made weights do not move. Human scores will rise more on boss-heavy levels than on minion-heavy ones, and weights built from the next season's player journals absorb that. This is nearly the same feature as [human-edge#1](human-edge.md#boss-turn-tells) and covers part of #4 (minions), so build one overlay.

*Fixes.* For even B, take (x+B/2, y+B/2) as the body's centre. Label the overlay 'if you stop now'.

*Cost.* Core: none. zk: 0 cycles. Formats and vectors: none. A jiro-shell function plus a wasm export. Per tick: at most H Boss::step calls on a 12-byte copy (at most 1,440 edge reads at H=90 and period 1), with a 2H-byte position buffer. Browser: negligible.

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The forecast is computed for one run and one tick at a time. It runs the core's own Boss::step and Minions::step on copies: Boss and Minions are Copy, and Boss::step is pub in core/src/boss.rs. So nothing carries over between runs and no rule is written twice. The gap narrows. Greedy keeps a safety margin while search predicts, and this overlay gives humans 1-2 s of that exact boss foresight. Search keeps long-horizon planning, frame-exact timing and its padding compute, so the 1.29x lead of search over greedy shrinks but does not close. Calibration does not move, because bots never render. Human medians will rise unevenly, though. The help is largest where the boss is the main threat (period-1 bosses, large B: rush, storm, titan) and smallest on minion-heavy levels. Human relative difficulty therefore drifts away from the bot-derived weights. That is a draw-variance effect to measure, not a blocker. One concern: 'one client-release constant' lets a client update change H mid-season, while §8.4 makes scores comparable only within a season.

*Fixes.* Pin H and the overlay set per season, in the season's client config, and do not change them before the season closes. Ship the overlay before the testnet season whose journals will feed later weights. In browser Challenge, keep the overlay always on, not behind a held key. Keep spotlight-style hiding out of Challenge, as proposed.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** This adds a reading layer: a cut can be threaded through the window the boss leaves, and a bounce can be baited. It also narrows the gap with the search bot without touching consensus, and it reuses pub Boss::step and right_hand_step, so the cost is real but small. But the trajectory can only change at a trail contact, which is already a hit. So a 2 s forecast is effectively exact for every cut shorter than 2 s, and at fast cut (2 cells/tick) that is almost every cut on a 58-wide field. The 'nerve' half of the cut/commit loop turns into a lookup: players will watch the dots, not the boss. The deflection quarter-turn stops being felt as the boss's character. Human clear rates would jump unevenly across the 16 levels, most on the period-1 levels, while the bot-calibrated ramp (95% to 15%) stays put. That is fair, because everyone has the overlay, but it flattens the felt difficulty curve. Danger-reactive music is a nice, separable touch. It overlaps heavily with [human-edge#1](human-edge.md#boss-turn-tells) (tells) and [edge-and-claimed-enemies#1](edge-and-claimed-enemies.md#minion-patrol-and-respawn-forecast) (minion arrows).

*Fixes.* Ship the trail-contact blink and countdown and the turn tells, not the full dotted path. Or cap H near 8-10 boss moves in Challenge and keep the 0-90 slider in Training only. Show tells only for deflections that actually change the trajectory (R4.6-10 cancels some). Drop the minion arrows here and leave them to the minion forecast idea. Keep the danger-music cue.

*Readability.* A countdown number has no room at 4x4 px cells. Blinking the threatened trail cells in one palette entry reads well. Fine in the browser.

## Run postcard from public journals

`reveal-and-presentation#5` · T0 Presentation · score 12 (core 5, fairness 5, play 2) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Every Challenge run, yours or any ranked player's, renders as a six-tile card. Each tile is a level's final board with its picture, claim, weight, points and end reason, rebuilt from the journals in the commit calldata.

**Rule sketch.** Browser only. Inputs: the run's six journals (P5: they are in commitRun calldata), the run nonce from the reveal, and the season's 16 level bytes and weights. The wasm core replays each level offchain, the same computation the referee makes, to its final grid and Outcome; the draw slots come from the core (P17). Tile k is the final 60x60 grid at 1-2 px per cell: claimed cells in the level's mosaic, the boss's final body, and a corner glyph for committed, cleared, out of lives or time. The caption gives claim %, weight, points, ticks, the biggest single capture and the number of cuts, counted from the capture ticks during the replay. A thin frame colour derived from the wallet address gives cosmetic identity with no meaning. The season page shows one postcard per ranked player, and a click opens the existing replay viewer at that level.

**Inspired by:** Power Styx (1988), Cacoma Knight in Bizyland (1992), Superhex.io (2017 or earlier), Paper.io 2 (2018), World Tour Qix (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Meta-picture across rounds](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#meta-picture-across-rounds), [End-of-match stats screen](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#end-of-match-stats-screen), [Cosmetic-linked territory colour](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#cosmetic-linked-territory-colour). **Touches:** presentation.

**Bot effect.** D14 is untouched and the gap is unchanged. It makes the §7.4 lever 'public journals that anyone can replay and inspect' practical: a search bot's signature, near-ceiling claims on all six levels at about 1,330 ticks each, shows at a glance on the leaderboard.

**Budget.** Browser only: six replays of at most 2,700 ticks each in wasm, tens of ms. No zk cycles, no contract change. It reads calldata through the dApp's existing RPC or indexer path.

**Variety.** A run becomes one picture to compare and share. Players see how the top runs ended each level (a trapped boss, an early bank), which teaches strategy without any rule change.

**Core engineer: feasible, 5 of 5, tier 0.** This is almost entirely existing code. web/src/chain/calldata.ts already recovers a run's six journals from its commitRun transaction. It handles a direct call, a call embedded in a smart-account call, or, through debug_traceTransaction, a trace, and checks them against the stored commitment. The replay page already replays a committed run with the wasm core, and the core already returns drawSlot and the Outcome.

The extra statistics (biggest capture, number of cuts) come from diffing captured between ticks during the replay. There is no consensus or contract impact.

Two practical limits:

- A season page with one postcard per ranked player costs per player one log lookup, one transaction fetch, six replays of up to 2,700 ticks, and, for smart-account commits, a trace call that public Arbitrum RPCs often do not serve. With hundreds of players this needs lazy rendering and caching.
- Ranked runs are settled, so they are verified. If unsettled runs are ever shown, label them unverified.

*Fixes.* Render postcards lazily, by viewport or on click. Cache by runId, since the journals are immutable. Fall back to a 'journals not retrievable on this RPC' tile when a smart-account commit needs a trace and the RPC does not serve one.

*Cost.* Browser only. Per postcard: about 1-3 RPC calls (getLogs, getTransaction, and debug_traceTransaction only for smart-account commits) and six wasm replays of up to 2,700 ticks, tens of ms. Per season page: N times that, so render lazily. zk: 0. Contracts and formats: none.

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** This is presentation only (T0). It replays journals that are already public in commitRun calldata (P5) with the same wasm core, and takes the draw slots from the core (P17). It has no consensus or zk impact. D14 is unaffected: the deflections already make copied events useless, so making top runs easier to inspect lets players learn strategy without letting anyone reuse inputs. It puts §7.4's social lever, public journals that anyone can replay, into practice and slightly helps humans learn, so the effect on the gap is neutral to slightly narrowing. It adds no variance to the draw. Two things to watch. First, the bot signature is not proof, since a proof shows only that a score is reachable and search can vary its padding, so the page must not label anyone a bot. Second, a season page that replays every ranked player's six levels is hundreds of replays and should render lazily.

*Fixes.* Derive the capture statistics from the per-tick change in captured cells, which the core already reports, rather than adding hooks to the core. Show each run's status: committed, settled, or late (missed the deadline). Render postcards lazily and cache them by journal hash. Read smart-account commits from traces, as §7.2 notes. Never present a run as a suspected bot.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** It is a good, cheap social feature and an anti-bot lever. Search's near-ceiling, uniform runs become visible at a glance. It adds no decisions or situations to play, only indirect learning from other players' end states, so it scores low on the play-value rubric. It does not harm play. It depends on a mosaic or picture-reveal feature that does not exist yet.

*Fixes.* 1. Build it on the existing replay path: replayRunFile in web/src/pages/challenge.ts and ReplayView. 2. Fall back to the claimed or open colours when no mosaic exists. 3. Lazy-render the cards on the season page, so it does not run six replays for every ranked player at load. 4. Drop the wallet-derived frame colour, or keep it out of the palette meanings so it is not mistaken for an end reason.

*Readability.* In the browser, 60x60 tiles at 1-2 px per cell read well as shape thumbnails, and the glyphs and captions carry the detail.

## Figure-weighted claim

`reveal-and-presentation#8` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 3, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

A glowing figure silhouette sits somewhere new on each run's board, and its cells count double or triple toward the claim. You choose between slicing off the figure and taking bulk area.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add three fields: a 16x16-bit figure stamp (32 B), a figure weight w (u8, 1 to 4; 1 turns the figure off), and an anchor rectangle x0, y0, x1, y1 (4 B) for the stamp's top-left, with the stamp inside the border. At level start, the anchor is candidate number (u32 BE of level seed bytes 9..12) mod count, in row-major order, the way the boss start is picked (§6.4). The figure set F is the stamp's cells at that anchor that are EMPTY at level start. claimable_w = claimable + (w - 1) x |F|, constant for the level. In the capture pass (R4.8-4), each cell turning CLAIMED adds 1, plus (w - 1) if (x - ax, y - ay) is inside the stamp and its bit is set. claim_bp = captured_w x 10,000 / claimable_w (R4.1-8), used for the clear (PIN-10) and the score; the §8.4 formula is unchanged. State hash v2: the captured and claimable fields become weighted (at most 3,364 + 3 x 256 = 4,132, which fits u16); the anchor is not hashed, since it is seed-derived like the deflection key. Presentation: the figure glows on the open field in one dim colour, claimed figure cells reveal the picture in full colour, and other claims take a muted fill. Variant: clear when a share of F is claimed, the silhouette target of the 80% picture games.

**Inspired by:** Gals Panic (1990), Youjuu Club (1990), Gals Borderline: Daily Life of the Battle Princesses (2026), Fantasia (1994), Lady Killer (1993), Super Model (1994), Super Qix (1987), Jolly Jogger (1982), Kid Grid (1982), PIX (Sega Genesis / Mega Drive) (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Silhouette target display](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#silhouette-target-display), [Hidden-picture reveal](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#hidden-picture-reveal), [Silhouette-only coverage](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#silhouette-only-coverage), [Area-percentage clear threshold](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#area-percentage-clear-threshold), [Marked bonus regions](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#marked-bonus-regions), [Code-generated art and audio](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#code-generated-art-and-audio). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, state-hash, presentation, tools-bots, outcome, economy.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.1-8; R4.8-4; PIN-10; §5 level bytes v1 (v2 adds 37 bytes); §6.4 (seed bytes 9-12 gain a use); state hash v1; D5; §8.4 level_score formula; §5 level bytes v1; P11 (claim floor must use the weighted claim); D13 (art must be original).

**Bot effect.** D14 is strengthened: one more seed-keyed element, so each run's value map differs and cannot be memorized. The figure is a value zone, not a safe zone. The gap is roughly kept: search ranks regions by value as easily as by area, scripted bots need a figure-weighted greedy, and humans get a visible sub-goal. It may widen slightly, because valuing exact regions rewards exact planning.

**Budget.** \+37 level bytes; about 40 B of core RAM (stamp and anchor). On capture, one bounds check and one bit read per changed cell, inside the existing pass; captures are a few dozen per level, so well under 1% more zk cycles and nothing on other ticks. Screen: one extra colour, and figure cells are redrawn only on capture.

**Variety.** It adds a value map distinct from area: a small cut can be worth a lot. The figure's place relative to the boss start and the minion spawns changes each run, so the opening plan differs from run to run.

**Merged from:** `presentation#12`, `wild-combinations#11`. Variants:

- Figure-weighted claim (the picture counts): A level can mark a figure on the field whose cells count k times toward the claim, and its outline is visible from the start. The player chooses between claiming the figure and claiming the background, and tries to keep the boss off the figure.
- Figure-weighted claim with picture reveal: Each level hides an original picture, and its figure is outlined faintly on the field. Cells inside the figure count double for score but not for the clear, so you choose between the figure's cells, often where the boss roams, and the easier background.

**Core engineer: feasible, 3 of 5, tier 3.** Well specified. The anchor draw mirrors §6.4's boss start: at most 43 x 43 = 1,849 candidates, so the modulo bias is negligible. claimable_w is at most 4,132 (u16), captured_w x 10,000 fits u32, and the per-cell stamp test runs only on cells the capture pass changes, well under 1% of zk cycles. About 40 B of state is fine, though Level copies grow by 37 B each. Skeptical points: (1) Seed bytes 9 to 12 are also claimed by [items-and-power-ups#6](items-and-power-ups.md#enclosable-supply-caches). Give one idea 13 to 16. (2) Counting weighted cells toward the clear (PIN-10) changes each level's difficulty per run, depending on where the figure lands relative to the boss start and its haunt. That adds run-to-run variance the calibrated weight only averages, and all 16 targets need re-checking. (3) The (claimable - B^2) ceiling and P11 become weighted, so the playtest and bot-advantage tools change. (4) A 1-bit stamp reveals a one-colour silhouette, not a 'full colour' picture. Bots: search values regions by weight as easily as by area, so the gap is roughly kept and may widen slightly, as stated. D14 is strengthened.

*Fixes.* Coordinate seed bytes with [items-and-power-ups#6](items-and-power-ups.md#enclosable-supply-caches) (for example, the anchor from bytes 13 to 16). Consider the score-only variant: PIN-10 and P11 on raw cells, weighted claim only in levelScore, so the 16 targets keep their clear rates. Add validity codes: anchor x1, y1 at most 43; w from 1 to 4; at least one stamp bit when w > 1.

*Cost.* Level bytes +37 B (509 B v2). About 40 B of core RAM. One bounds check and one bit read per changed cell in each capture pass; under 1% zk. State hash v2 by field semantics (captured and claimable weighted). Screen: +1 palette colour. RULES_VERSION, vectors, playtest, calibration, target re-check.

*Conflicts.* R4.1-8; R4.8-4; PIN-10; P11; §5 level bytes v1; §6.4 seed bytes 9-12 (also claimed by [items-and-power-ups#6](items-and-power-ups.md#enclosable-supply-caches)); state hash v1 (field semantics); §8.4 level_score inputs; D13

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds and is somewhat strengthened: a seed-keyed anchor (bytes 9-12) adds per-run variety, and the figure is a value zone, not a safe zone. The main fairness problem is static placement luck. With w up to 4, the figure can be about 25% of the weighted claim (768 extra on 3,364), and the seed decides for the whole level whether it touches the rail near the player's start (30, 59) or sits in the centre under the boss's spawn. The boss start is also seeded, but deflections even its influence out over the level, while the figure's position persists. Because the weighted claim also drives the clear (PIN-10), the anchor shifts a run's clear odds on that level. Calibration takes the median over seeds and leaves that variance in each paid run. Gap: search ranks regions by weighted value and times its final capture so the boss pocket holds no figure cells, so it stays near its ceiling. Humans get a visible sub-goal, but the figure often sits where the boss roams. A mild widening is likely. Scripted bots can play it: greedy's rectangle score can count weighted cells. D13 requires original art. The level format, state hash and P11 floor all change.

*Fixes.* Use the weights for score only and keep the clear on raw claim (the pitch's second variant). Cap w at 2. Constrain the anchor rectangle so that every placement is about equally far from the rail and the boss spawn, or draw it from a few symmetric candidate spots. Have the playtest report each level's claim spread by anchor and reject levels whose per-anchor clear rates differ by more than the targets.json tolerance. Calibrate with a figure-aware greedy.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** Adds a value map that differs from area, which none of the 16 levels has. Today every cell is worth the same, so the best cut is always the biggest safe one. A weighted figure creates situations where a small cut beats a large one. Because the anchor is seeded, the plan changes from run to run, and that is good for D14. It also gives the presentation a real purpose (the reveal of an original picture), and the claimable_w normalization keeps 10,000 bp as the maximum. Costs are bounded: +37 level bytes, about 40 B of core RAM, and work only on capture ticks. Concerns: (1) Counting the weight toward the clear makes difficulty vary by seed. A figure anchored near the player start (30, 59), away from the boss, is a cheap jackpot, and one under the boss's spawn is nearly unclaimable. That is per-run luck in a paid mode, beyond the luck the seeded boss start already brings. (2) Search values regions exactly, so the gap may widen slightly, as the proposal says. (3) D13: the art must be original.

*Fixes.* Use the variant where figure weight counts for score but not for the clear, so clear difficulty stays area-based and calibration stays stable. Constrain the anchor rectangle so the figure never overlaps the boss spawn rectangle or touches the bottom border near (30, 59). Limit the revealed picture to 2-3 palette entries. Teach scripted greedy bots a value-per-cell rectangle score.

*Readability.* Good. A 16x16-cell stamp is 64x64 px, an easily read silhouette in one dim colour on black. Revealed cells redraw only on capture.

## Seeded board orientation

`reveal-and-presentation#11` · T3 New core rule · score 9 (core 4, fairness 3, play 2) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Each run plays a level in one of up to eight rotations and mirrors, drawn from the seed, so a known layout comes at you from a new angle while the start stays at the bottom centre.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add orientations, a u8 bitmask over the 8 symmetries of the square (identity, 3 rotations, 4 mirrors); bit 0, the identity, must be set. At level start, s is the k-th allowed orientation, with k = (bits 2..4 of seed byte 4) mod popcount(mask). Before validity and the boss start, the core maps through s: the obstacle bitmap (x -> 59 - x and the like keep the border), the boss spawn rectangle (transform the body's cells, then take the new top-left: x' = 59 - x - (B - 1) for an x mirror, so the rectangle stays a rectangle), and the minion spawn cells, which keep their slot order. Seed bytes 0..4 then pick the boss start in the transformed rectangle's row-major order. The player start stays (30, 59) (PIN-01). Validity rules 35-38 are invariant under isometries. Rule 39, the player start as a rail cell, is checked at load for every allowed orientation, so every seed gives a valid board, and the level tool rejects masks that fail. Presentation: the reveal picture stays upright, and a small compass glyph on the start overlay shows the orientation.

**Inspired by:** Smiley (1992), Spherical Qix (2022), Claim (DaNuTree) (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Camera framing and control](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#camera-framing-and-control), [Shuffled picture order](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#shuffled-picture-order). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.1-7; R4.6-5; §5 level bytes v1 and validity rule 39; §6.4 (seed byte 4 bits 2-4 gain a use); D14 (extends what the seed decides).

**Bot effect.** D14 is strengthened: up to 8 boards per level, on top of the boss deflections, so routes cannot be learned per layout. The gap widens slightly: humans lose part of the layout memorization they build in Training, which the search bot never needed. Because the minions' clockwise rule and the bottom start are fixed, orientations play differently; calibration samples them through the seeds anyway.

**Budget.** \+1 level byte. Level load: one transform pass over 3,600 bits and at most 8 rail checks, a few tens of thousands of cycles once per level, well under 1% of a heavy level's ~30M. Nothing per tick, no new RAM, no screen cost.

**Variety.** The 16 levels play as up to 128 boards. An obstacle bar that sat next to the start may now be far from it, which changes the opening, so players read the layout fresh each run.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** Clean and cheap. The 8 symmetries map the border onto itself. A B x B body square maps to a square, so the spawn rectangle maps to a rectangle of top-left cells (for example x' = 60 - B - x on an x mirror; for rotations, swap the roles of x and y). Validity rules 35-38 are invariant under isometries (connectivity, area, the body fitting, and rail cells, which are defined on 8-neighbours). Only rule 39 is not, because (30, 59) stays fixed, and checking it for every allowed orientation is right.

No buffer is needed: Grid::level_start can read the obstacle bit at the inverse-transformed cell. Nothing runs per tick, and nothing new is in the state hash, since the grid and the boss position already reflect the transform.

Two problems:
(1) Modulo bias. Bits 2..4 of byte 4 give 0..7, and 0..7 mod popcount(mask) is badly biased for popcount 3, 5, 6 or 7 (for 3: 3/8, 3/8, 2/8).
(2) Fairness. Weights are per level (uint16\[16\]), not per orientation. The rules are not isometry-invariant: the boss tests x before y, direction priority is up, down, left, right, minions always circle clockwise, and the start is fixed at the bottom. So orientations are different games with different difficulty, and the draw adds luck the weights do not normalize.

The gap widens slightly, as stated.

*Fixes.* Draw k from a whole free seed byte or a u32 (for example a byte in 9..31 mod popcount; the bias is at most 7/256, or 2^-29 with a u32) instead of 3 bits. That also avoids colliding with the twin-boss idea's bits 2..3 of byte 4. Have playtest report clear rates per orientation, and have the level tool keep in the mask only orientations within the targets.json tolerance of the level's mean.

*Cost.* Level bytes v2: 473 B (+1). Level load: one inverse-mapped pass of 3,600 bits plus at most 8 start-cell rail checks, a few tens of thousands of cycles once per level (\< 0.1 % of about 30M). Per tick: 0. RAM: 0. State hash unchanged (948 B). Screen: a compass glyph. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors regenerated, playtest per orientation.

*Conflicts.* R4.1-7; R4.6-5; §5 level bytes v1 and validity rule 39; §6.4 seed byte 4 bits 2-4 (collides with the twin-boss idea's bits 2-3); D14 (extends what the seed decides); §8.4 weights are per level, not per orientation

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds and is formally strengthened. The marginal value is small, though, because the deflections already ensure no two runs share a game and no safe zone exists. The extra cost falls on humans. Layout-specific openings learned in Training are a human skill that search never needed, so the gap widens slightly. The fixed bottom-centre start and the clockwise minion rule make orientations genuinely different in difficulty: an obstacle bar next to the start in one orientation can be far from it in another. That adds orientation luck within a level, which calibration averages into one weight but individual runs still feel. Over six levels it adds unfair variance to the draw. The rule sketch is sound: validity rules 35-38 are invariant under isometries, rule 39 is checked for every allowed orientation, and the rectangle transform is right. Load cost is negligible. Seed byte 4 bits 2..4 collide with the twin-boss idea's bits 2..3.

*Fixes.* Have the level tool measure the reference mix's clear rate per allowed orientation, and reject masks whose orientations differ by more than the targets.json tolerance. Let Training pick or cycle orientations so players can practise each board. Default masks to the identity on levels where orientation changes the opening a lot. Coordinate the byte 4 bit allocation with the other seed users.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** It adds layout variety and works against memorisation, but adds no new decisions or mechanics. It replays the 16 levels at new angles. Many levels are nearly symmetric (open, sentry, rush, storm, trio, titan, swarm, doom), so on them it changes little more than where the minions spawn. On the asymmetric levels it can change difficulty systematically. comb's boss spawns in the bottom band, next to the player start, and a 180-degree rotation moves it to the far side. A Challenge run with one fixed weight per level then makes the orientation drawn a matter of luck. It also takes away the route mastery that humans build in Training, which search never needed, so the gap widens, as the pitch says.

*Fixes.* 1. In playtests, measure the clear rate of each allowed orientation, and have the level tool reject a mask whose orientations fall outside the level's tolerance band. 2. Alternatively, allow only the left-right mirror, which keeps the spawn regions' distance from the start. 3. Let Training choose the orientation.

*Readability.* Yes. Boards are drawn as before, and a compass glyph on the start overlay is enough.

## Slow-cut premium

`reveal-and-presentation#9` · T3 New core rule · score 7 (core 2, fairness 2, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

A cut drawn without the fast-cut key is a careful claim: it shows in gold instead of blue and counts half again toward the claim, so every cut pits speed against value.

**Rule sketch.** The player gains a field cut_fast (u8). It is set to 0 when a cut starts (R4.5-2, step 3) and to 1 on any tick that takes the fast cut's second sub-step (R4.5-6). The capture counter counts half-cells: at a capture, captured_halves += size x (3 if cut_fast = 0, else 2). claim_bp = min(10,000, captured_halves x 5,000 / claimable) (R4.1-8), used for both the clear and the score. Holding B (slow shield) keeps a cut careful. State hash v2: +1 byte for cut_fast; the captured field becomes half-cells (at most 10,092, which fits u16). Commit still discards an unfinished cut. Presentation: the trail is orange while careful and turns red at the first fast sub-step. Careful captures are drawn gold: the client keeps a 450-B bitmap of careful cells, updated from a 'last capture careful' flag the core exposes on capture ticks. The HUD shows the bonus the current cut would earn.

**Inspired by:** Qix (1981), Jin (1982), Statix (1984), Frenzy (1984), Eight Bit Qix (c. 2026), Moonlight Territory (2026), Stix (1983), Styx (1983), qixxy (2016), Territory: Claim & Conquer (2026), Neon Divide (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Two draw speeds (Fast/Slow Draw)](../taxonomy/movement-and-drawing.md#two-draw-speeds-fastslow-draw), [Single fixed draw speed](../taxonomy/movement-and-drawing.md#single-fixed-draw-speed), [Slow-draw score multiplier](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#slow-draw-score-multiplier), [Exposure-scaled score multiplier](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#exposure-scaled-score-multiplier), [Colour-coded claims](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#colour-coded-claims), [Painted-state coding and completion feedback](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#painted-state-coding-and-completion-feedback), [Long-excursion exposure risk](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#long-excursion-exposure-risk). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, outcome, contracts, economy, tools-bots, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** D6; P4; R4.5-7; §8.4 level_score; §7.2 run score bound; P11; R4.1-8; R4.3-2; R4.5-6; R4.8-4; PIN-10; state hash v1.

**Bot effect.** D14 is untouched. The gap widens slightly. A careful cut takes twice as long, so its trail is exposed twice as long, and the search bot knows exactly when the boss will reach the trail: it takes the bonus on nearly every safe cut, while humans must gamble. With the boss forecast overlay, most of that difference shrinks. Search's ceiling rises from about 9,900 bp to the 10,000 cap. Scripted bots need a rule, such as cutting carefully when the boss is far.

**Budget.** \+1 byte of state; one flag write per tick; one multiply per capture; zk cycles about unchanged. Client RAM +450 B. Screen: one extra colour.

**Variety.** Today the fast-cut key has no cost. This gives every cut a speed-against-value choice, the slow draw of the classic games. It brings patient careful cuts that stop mid-cut (D4) to wait out the boss, and leaves the board as a record of how it was taken.

**Merged from:** `movement-and-drawing#13`, `scoring-and-risk#9`, `human-edge#8`. Variants:

- Careful-cut bonus: A cut drawn entirely at the slow shield pace pays a bonus on top of its claim. For each cut, the player chooses between the fast line, safe through speed, and the slow line, exposed but more valuable, with the one-hit shield as its insurance.
- Slow-line double score: A capture whose whole trail was drawn with B held counts its cells twice for score. The slow, shielded line becomes the valuable one, and the fast A line becomes the safe way home.
- Slow-cut premium (warning case): The classic slow line: a cut drawn entirely with B held counts its captured cells twice toward the claim. It is the purest nerve mechanic in the catalog, and the clearest example of a mechanic that pays bots more than humans.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 3.** The integer arithmetic is fine: captured_halves is at most 10,092 (u16), times 5,000 fits u32, and cut_fast is a flag written in Player::step. The trouble is that it redefines claim (R4.1-8), which everything is built on. 'Careful' means plain normal speed, so any cut without A counts 1.5x toward the clear. A 7,500 bp target then needs only 5,000 bp of area. Every level's clear rate shifts, and the 16 targets, the clear-rate ramp in levels/targets.json, the P11 floor and the (claimable - B^2) ceiling must all be re-derived. Contracts are not touched: Challenge.sol only sums levelScore and counts endReason 2, and the 10,000 cap keeps the 393,210 bound. Bot effect: a careful cut doubles exposure time, and search knows exactly when the boss reaches a trail. It will take the premium on nearly every cut and hit the 10,000 cap on most levels, while humans gamble. This likely widens the gap, as the warning variant admits. The client needs a 450 B careful-cell bitmap.

*Fixes.* Apply the premium to the score only. Keep PIN-10's clear, P11 and Outcome.claimBp on raw cells, and make levelScore = min(10,000, weighted claim) x weight / 10,000. Then targets and clear rates stay, and no contract changes (update the §7.3 comment). Drop 'contracts' from touches. Pin that cut_fast is set only when the second sub-step is actually entered.

*Cost.* \+1 B of core state (state hash v2, 949 B preimage) and one flag write per tick; one multiply per capture; zk ~0. Client +450 B bitmap, +1 palette colour. As stated, a full retune of the 16 targets and a recalibration; with the score-only change, a recalibration only.

*Conflicts.* D6; R4.1-8; PIN-10; P11; R4.5-6; R4.5-7; R4.8-4; state hash v1; §8.4 level_score; levels/targets.json

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 is untouched. The rule pays for exactly what search does best: knowing when a longer exposure is safe. A careful cut keeps its trail out twice as long per cell, so it has about 33% more exposure per unit of claim than a fast cut. Search knows the boss's full seeded trajectory and the minions' patrols, so it takes the premium whenever it is safe. Humans gamble, most of all on period-1 boss levels (05-16). The cap at 10,000 limits search's upside (doom 9,580 to 10,000, about +4%) and compresses the top for everyone, but human gains come only at the risk of more D5 zeros. On the high-weight hard levels (weights 28,591-64,620 bp) that pushes medians down and weights up, which feeds search. The 1.5x also counts toward the clear (PIN-10), so it changes every level's real difficulty. The 16 targets and the clear-rate bands of levels/targets.json would all need re-tuning, and calibration would hinge on what policy the bots use for careful cuts. Bots can play it: greedy already computes boss reach and can cut carefully when the margin is large. Its own catalog entry calls it the clearest warning case, and I agree. Add the client's 450 B bitmap and the state hash v2 for a mechanic that widens the gap.

*Fixes.* Don't use it in Challenge. If kept, make it Training-only (T0/T3 behind a Training-only flag), or apply the premium to score only and never to the clear, with a per-level ceiling, and pair it with the boss forecast overlay. Measure a careful-cut search bot against a careful-cut mix on levels 13-16 before considering it for a season.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** The decision is excellent: the classic slow-draw trade-off, and it gives the fast-cut key a cost it lacks today. Every cut becomes speed against value, and D4's no-fuse rule lets careful cuts pause to wait out the boss. Few ideas add a choice to every cut on every level. The rule sketch undercuts it, though. Careful cells count 1.5x toward claim_bp, which also drives the clear (PIN-10), so an all-careful player clears 7,500 bp at about 50% of the area and hits the 10,000 cap at about 67%. The HUD claim stops matching the visible area, which breaks the genre's 'percent of field' feel and retunes all 16 levels at once. The cap also turns the premium into a clear accelerator rather than a score lever, since search already sits near 9,900 bp. The proposal admits the gap widens: search knows exactly when the boss will reach the trail and takes the bonus on every safe cut, while humans must gamble. It touches a wide surface: the state hash, Outcome semantics, the §7.2 score bound and P11's claim floor.

*Fixes.* Keep the clear on plain area (PIN-10 and R4.1-8 unchanged) and apply the premium to score only, as the classic games do. That takes a separate score accumulator, or a level_score bonus term, not a weighted claim_bp. Ship it alongside a boss-forecast overlay, or not at all, so humans can judge exposure the way search does. Give scripted bots a rule such as cutting carefully when the boss's distance to the trail exceeds a threshold.

*Readability.* Good. An orange to red trail change at the first fast sub-step, and gold for careful cells against the normal claimed fill, are each one palette entry and clear at 4x4 px. The 450-B client bitmap is affordable on the browser.

## Boss smear spoils the picture

`reveal-and-presentation#10` · T3 New core rule · score 6 (core 2, fairness 2, play 2) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Wherever the boss passes, the hidden picture smears grey. Ground claimed before the boss spoils it counts double, so you race to enclose clean ground in the boss's path while spoiled ground is safe but cheap.

**Rule sketch.** The core adds a 450-B spoiled bitmap (1 bit per cell). At level start it holds the boss's initial B x B body. Step 4: after each axis move of Boss::step that changes the position, set the bits of the B cells of the new leading edge, at most 2B bit-sets per move; over time this covers every cell the body has been on. Capture pass (R4.8-4): each cell turning CLAIMED adds 2 to captured_halves if it is not spoiled, else 1. claim_bp = captured_halves x 5,000 / claimable, so 10,000 only when every claimed cell was clean. Level targets are retuned lower in the level data (around 6,000-6,500 bp). State hash v2 adds the 450 B. Presentation: spoiled open cells are drawn with a 1-px grey speckle over black, so the boss's wanderings show as a smear. Claimed clean cells reveal the mosaic in colour, and claimed spoiled cells the same tile desaturated (restoration in reverse).

**Inspired by:** Gals Panic (1990), Cacoma Knight in Bizyland (1992), Chromonia (2026), World Tour Qix (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Restoration reveal (before/after)](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#restoration-reveal-beforeafter), [Bad-image swap](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#bad-image-swap), [Themed cover layer](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#themed-cover-layer), [Reverse reveal (claims cover the picture)](../taxonomy/reveal-and-presentation.md#reverse-reveal-claims-cover-the-picture). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.1-8; R4.4-9 and R4.6-3 (the boss step now writes state); R4.8-4; PIN-10; D5 (the 75% default target); state hash v1.

**Bot effect.** D14 is strengthened: the spoil map follows each run's seeded boss path. The gap widens: the search bot knows where the boss will go and can enclose clean ground just before it arrives, while humans see only where it has been. The boss forecast overlay offsets part of this. The reference mix needs a spoil-aware heuristic, or the weights skew.

**Budget.** Core RAM +450 B. Per boss move, at most 2B = 16 bit-sets; the capture pass adds one bit test per cell. zk: an estimated +0.5-1% cycles, against about 11k cycles per tick. The state hash preimage grows from 948 to 1,398 B (traces and tests). Screen: at most 2B newly spoiled cells per boss move, partial redraws.

**Variety.** It adds time pressure that is not a clock: early claims are worth more, and which region to take first depends on where the boss has and has not been. The board becomes a readable history of the boss.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 3.** The rule is deterministic and the arithmetic fits: captured_halves \<= 6,728 fits a u16, and 6,728 x 5,000 fits a u32. The setting rule is also right: after each axis move that changes the position, set the bits of the new leading edge, plus the initial body. That marks every cell the body has ever covered.

The costs are larger than the pitch says.

(1) Gameplay. With deflections the body passes over about 95 % of the interior in a 2,700-tick level (R4.6 rationale), so late in a level almost every claim is worth half. Claim becomes dominated by elapsed time and by the seeded boss path, which raises variance between runs and makes calibration noisier.

(2) Gap. Search knows the boss's future path exactly and encloses clean ground just ahead of it, while a human sees only the smear behind it. The gap widens, as the pitch admits. The claim that spoiled ground is 'safe' is wrong: where the boss has been says nothing about where it goes next.

*Fixes.* Preferred: make the smear presentation-only (T0). Keep a client-side 450-B spoil bitmap in the browser, drawn from the boss positions the core already exposes. That keeps the readable history with no consensus cost and no gap. If the scoring stays, retune all 16 targets with a spoil-aware reference bot.

*Cost.* State: +450 B in Game; state hash v2 1,398 B (+450). Per boss move: at most 2B = 16 bit-sets. Per capture: +3,600 bit tests (about +20-40k cycles). zk: about +0.5-1 % (estimate). Level data: all 16 targets retuned.

*Conflicts.* R4.1-8; R4.4-9 and R4.6-3 (the boss step now writes state); R4.6-11 (no boss-derived state); R4.8-4; PIN-10; D5 (75 % default target); state hash v1

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: the spoil map follows each run's seeded boss path, and no fixed safe zone appears. The gap widens, though. Clean ground is worth double only until the boss passes over it, and search knows the boss's whole future path while humans see only its past. Search can therefore claim exactly the cells the boss is about to cross. The rule also rewards speed, and rushing is where humans die (D5). It adds luck within each level: a boss that happens to wander widely early spoils more ground, so the same play scores differently on different seeds. Seed variance of this kind does not average out over only six levels. Targets would have to be retuned to about 6,000-6,500, against the 75% default of D5, and claim_bp changes meaning everywhere (R4.1-8, Outcome.claimBp). The cost is real: +450 B of core state and a state hash preimage of 1,398 B. The scripted bots need a spoil-aware heuristic, or the weights skew.

*Fixes.* Ship it as T0 presentation only: draw the smear and the desaturated mosaic from a client-side bitmap, and keep claim_bp unchanged. That keeps the readable board history at no consensus, fairness or RAM cost. If a scoring version is wanted at all, try it only in Training first, and measure seed variance of the score at fixed play before any season uses it.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** It adds a valuation layer, early clean ground versus spoiled ground, so it acts as a soft time bonus. The deflections already carry the boss over about 95% of the interior in a level, so by mid-level almost everything is spoiled. The region-choice decision is real but short-lived, and it reduces to 'capture big, early', which the time limit already encourages. It changes claim semantics globally: half-points, targets retuned to about 6,000, and a claim the player can no longer estimate by eye. That clashes with the genre's simple promise that area is area. It also devalues the final trap capture, which by then is mostly spoiled ground. As the pitch admits, it widens the bot gap. It adds 450 B of state and grows the state hash by 47%. The cost is high for a modest decision.

*Fixes.* 1. Keep the smear as a T0 overlay that shows the boss's recent history, with no effect on scoring. That keeps most of the 'readable history of the boss' benefit at zero consensus cost. 2. If the scoring is kept, make it a per-level knob on one or two levels, not a global claim rule, and use a second solid colour for spoiled claimed cells instead of desaturation.

*Readability.* A 1-px grey dot inside a 4x4 black cell is faint, and after a minute the whole field is noise that reduces contrast for the green minions and the red trail.
