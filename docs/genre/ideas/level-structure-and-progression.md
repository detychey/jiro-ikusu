# Ideas: level structure and progression

The 7 ideas proposed from the axis of the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) *level structure and progression*: stages and progression: stage goals, boss stages, difficulty ramps, level select, world maps, unlocks, continues, story structure. Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Lesson ladder

`level-structure-and-progression#3` · T1 Level data v1 · score 13 (core 5, fairness 5, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Seven short Training-only lessons each teach one rule: close a cut, fast cut, shield, bank with Commit, seal a door, strand a minion, finish big. Each has a one-line hint and an instant retry.

**Rule sketch.** Seven new level-bytes v1 files, Training only and never in a season, each with a hint card (client text). L1 'Close a cut': B=3, spawn (44,4)-(54,12), boss period 4, no minions, lives 9, target 3,000. L2 'Fast cut': B=4, spawn (20,20)-(34,34), period 2, lives 5, target 5,000; hint 'hold A while cutting'. L3 'Shield': B=3, period 4, 1 minion on the bottom border, minion period 3, R 90, lives 3, target 5,000; hint 'hold B as it touches you, one charge'. L4 'Bank it': open field, B=6, period 1, lives 1, target 9,500; hint 'X keeps your claim; losing your only life scores 0' (D5). L5 'Seal a door': three obstacle bars at y=15, 30, 45 from x=1 to x=50, leaving 8-cell doors at x=51..58, B=6, spawn (20,17)-(34,23), target 6,000; hint 'wall off a door to take the whole band'. Open area 3,214 cells, one 4-connected region, so the level is valid. L6 'Strand': 2 minions on the bottom border, minion period 3, R 150; hint 'capture the field in front of a minion to remove it for 10 s'. L7 'Big finish': open field, B=5, period 3, lives 5, target 7,500; hint 'plan the last cut to overshoot'. All validity codes 20-39 hold (player start on the rail, open area at least 1,682 cells). A greedy-bot playtest only checks that each lesson can be cleared; no calibration is needed.

**Inspired by:** Claim (DaNuTree) (2026), Space Cropper (2014), Starlasso: Territory Arena (2026), XonixMinimal (2026), Reef Reveal (2026), JezzBall (Matswm86 Godot Android remake) (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Tutorials, practice and level hints](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#tutorials-practice-and-level-hints), [Staged introduction of new enemies, hazards and rules](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#staged-introduction-of-new-enemies-hazards-and-rules), [Instant level retry](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#instant-level-retry). **Touches:** level-data, presentation.

**Bot effect.** None on paid modes: the lessons are never in a season. Slightly narrowing for humans who take them, because L4 and L7 teach the two things search exploits and the reference mix lacks: banking under D5 and the big final capture.

**Budget.** 7 x 472 bytes of level files in flash. Hint text a few hundred bytes. No RAM beyond the loaded level, no per-tick work, no zk cycles.

**Variety.** The 16 levels assume the player already knows rail, cut, fast cut, shield and Commit. The ladder introduces one element at a time and gives Commit an explicit lesson.

**Core engineer: feasible, 5 of 5, tier 1.** The data checks out. L5: bars at y = 15, 30 and 45 over x = 1..50 meet the left border and leave 8-cell doors at x = 51..58. The open area is 3,364 - 150 = 3,214 cells in one region. Spawn (20,17)-(34,23) with B=6 covers rows 17-28 inside the band of rows 16-29 (codes 25 and 37 hold). L1's spawn with B=3 stays within x1 \<= 56 and y1 \<= 56. L3, L4, L6 and L7 leave their spawn rectangles unstated, a data detail. The tooling is a bigger point than the sketch says. `level build` needs NN-name sources with NN from 01 to 16 per directory, so the lessons need their own directory (for example levels/lessons/src). Placing them in levels/src would fire CLAUDE.md's playtest and calibration rule and break make_app_levels.py, which pairs exactly 16 levels with calibration-1.json. Wedgie: make_app_levels.py must append the lessons at weight 10,000. read_levels indexes every level at start, about 130 B of heap each, so about 1 KB of the roughly 17.4 KB free. The bests dict gains 7 keys, and the level select needs paging. L6's hint is loose: a minion is stranded only when its cell loses every open 8-neighbor. No rules, format or vector change, and no effect on paid modes.

*Fixes.* Use a separate lessons directory with its own build target. Extend make_app_levels.py (or ship a second level file). Add paging to the wedgie level select. Reword L6's hint. Run a greedy playtest per lesson.

*Cost.* 7 x 472 = 3,304 B of level data in the app's files (within the 256 KB app limit). About 1 KB of wedgie heap for the index and the bests keys. 0 per-tick work, 0 zk, no format bump, RULES_VERSION unchanged, no recalibration.

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** The lessons are Training only and never in a season, so D14, calibration and draw variance do not apply to paid play. The one paid-mode effect is good: L4 (banking under D5) and L7 (overshooting the final capture) teach the two levers where search beats the reference mix most, so humans who take them should close part of the gap. The fully specified levels hold up. L5: three 50-cell bars give 3,364 - 150 = 3,214 open cells, joined through the x = 51..58 doors, and the B = 6 body at spawn (20,17)-(34,23) stays within rows 16 to 29. L1 and L2's spawn rectangles satisfy x1, y1 ≤ 59 - B. L3 and L6 omit the boss spawn rectangle and the minion cells, so their validity cannot be checked yet. On L3, a bottom-border minion must not spawn on or next to the player start (30, 59), or it hits on tick 0. A greedy-bot clearability check is enough.

*Fixes.* Fill in L3's and L6's boss spawn rectangle and minion spawn cells, keep the L3 minion away from (30, 59), and run the level tool's validity check on all seven. Make the season tool reject lesson level ids, so a lesson can never be committed in a season's levelIds by mistake.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** It adds no new mechanics or situations for experienced players. But it makes the existing decisions discoverable, and several of them are non-obvious: losing your last life scores 0 (D5), the single shield charge, door sealing, stranding, and the big overshoot finish. That makes the 16 levels' variety reachable for humans, and it is the cheapest item here with a clear benefit to the human-versus-bot gap. The layouts check out: L5's bars leave 3,214 open cells in one region, and the B=6 spawn fits between the bars at y=15 and y=30. Two lessons can't confirm their own lesson through the level format. L4 'Bank it' is passed by committing, not clearing, so a player who commits sees no success. L7 'Big finish' passes on any clear at 7,500, overshoot or not. L4's period-1, B=6 boss with a single life is harsh for a lesson.

*Fixes.* Add client-side lesson goals and stars that the level format can't express: L4 passes on a Commit at 6,000 bp or more, L7 earns a star at 9,000 bp or more. Ease L4 to boss period 2 so its lesson is about banking, not survival. Keep hints to one or two lines of about 30 characters for the wedgie. Give L6 a hint about where to cut relative to the minion's heading.

*Readability.* Good. It reuses the existing visuals. One-line hints fit the 240 px screen, and instant retry is a client feature.

## Free-order run board

`level-structure-and-progression#1` · T0 Presentation · score 12 (core 5, fairness 5, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

After the reveal, the six drawn levels lie face up as cards. The player picks which one to play next: warm up on a favourite, face the hardest while fresh, or keep it for last, and can forfeit a level with X at tick 0.

**Rule sketch.** No core, journal or contract change. At reveal the client already knows all six levels (draw slots) and their level seeds, keccak256("jiro.level" || runNonce || levelIndex). Each journal is keyed by its own levelIndex, commitRun takes the six journals in levelIndex order, and the deadline sums ticks used, so play order is invisible to the chain. Client: a board of six cards (levelIndex k, slot, name, weight, B, minions, lives, target). Picking a card plays that levelIndex and writes its journal under it. The X-at-tick-0 forfeit is already legal (PIN-07). On a page reload the client closes every unfinished level as section 7.2 'Closing a level early' says. Time on the board is not in the ticks, so it uses up commitSlack (120 s for six transitions). The board therefore shows the slack left and auto-starts the lowest unplayed levelIndex after 10 s idle, or sooner if the slack would run out. Caveat: the planned 'fresh entropy per level' upgrade (section 7.4) reveals seeds one level at a time. The board would then have to request each level's entropy when its card is picked, or fall back to a fixed order.

**Inspired by:** Gals Panic 3 (1995), Paradise Deluxe (199?, 1994 or later), LadyLove (1990), Gals Borderline: Daily Life of the Battle Princesses (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Free choice of stage or character order](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#free-choice-of-stage-or-character-order), [Level select and replay of reached stages](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#level-select-and-replay-of-reached-stages). **Touches:** presentation.

**Bot effect.** Neutral for the search bot, whose score does not depend on play order. A human gains a little from warm-up and from choosing when to face the hardest level. Neutral to slightly narrowing. D14 is unchanged because seeds and journals are the same.

**Budget.** Browser only, since Challenge is browser-only (D15). No RAM on the wedgie, no per-tick work, no zk cycles. Six cards drawn once between levels.

**Variety.** Adds the first run-level pacing decision. Today the six levels play in a fixed order and the run has no shape the player controls.

**Core engineer: feasible, 5 of 5, tier 0.** Play order is invisible outside the client. Level seeds are keccak256('jiro.level', runNonce, levelIndex). commitRun takes the journals in levelIndex order, and settlement checks each Outcome's levelIndex and drawSlot. The deadline sums ticksUsed. web/src/run/play.ts's closeLevel already closes any unplayed level with an X at tick 0. So the core, the formats, the contracts and settlement are all untouched. Client costs: the run store must record a status per levelIndex instead of a 'next level'. Board time comes out of commitSlack (120 s = 60 s of latency plus 6 x 10 s of transitions; today's TRANSITION_MS is 3 s), so the 10 s idle auto-start keeps inside the budget. Free order gives no new information: every seed is known at reveal anyway, and Training could already replay a drawn level under the Challenge nonce within the slack. The designed per-level entropy upgrade (§7.4) would force a reveal per card or a fixed order, as the sketch says.

*Fixes.* None needed. Compute the auto-start delay from the remaining slack, not a fixed 10 s, so a slow reveal transaction cannot make the run late.

*Cost.* Browser only (D15): six cards drawn between levels and a store change. 0 wedgie RAM, 0 per-tick work, 0 zk, no format, rules or vector changes.

*Conflicts.* §7.4 fresh entropy per level (designed upgrade)

**Fairness: ok, 5 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** It changes no core, journal, seed or contract, and journals are keyed by levelIndex, so D14 and settlement are untouched. Play order is invisible onchain. The draw is unchanged and the player already sees all six levels at reveal, so no new information leaks and the draw adds no variance. Search's score does not depend on order. Humans gain a little from warming up and choosing when to face the hardest level, so the gap narrows very slightly. Practice on later levels (§7.2) is no easier than in fixed order, because practice time must still come from ticks used or commitSlack. Two risks need handling. Board time eats commitSlack, which the 10 s auto-start and the slack display cover. The board also conflicts with the planned fresh-entropy-per-level upgrade, which the pitch notes. There is nothing for bots to calibrate.

*Fixes.* Keep the auto-start and the slack display as specified. Before fresh entropy per level is designed, decide whether picking a card requests that level's entropy or the board falls back to fixed order, so the upgrade does not silently break the board.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** Cheap and harmless to consensus, but it adds almost no decision. The six levels are scored independently and nothing carries between them, so order only affects warm-up and fatigue. It adds nothing to the boss, minion, rail, cut, shield or Commit loop, and the X-at-tick-0 forfeit already exists. It even adds a new way to lose a run. Board time eats commitSlack, which forces a 10-second auto-start timer and turns dithering in a menu into a deadline risk. It also conflicts with the planned fresh-entropy-per-level upgrade. Its one real benefit is psychological: seeing the run and facing the feared level when ready.

*Fixes.* Show all six drawn levels as a read-only preview at reveal and keep the fixed order, which gives the 'read the draw' benefit without the slack timer. Give free order real stakes only alongside a run-level resource, such as the boosters allocated at reveal.

*Readability.* Not applicable to the wedgie, since it is a browser-only Challenge screen. Six cards in the browser are easy to read.

## Season levels drawn from a calibrated library

`level-structure-and-progression#5` · T1 Level data v1 · score 12 (core 4, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Each season draws its 16 levels from a library of generated layouts, grouped into four themed worlds such as bars, combs, rings and islands. Every layout is verified by the playtest bots before it ships, so seasons feel fresh without any rule change.

**Rule sketch.** New offline tool, jiro-tools level generate --motif \<bars|comb|ring|islands|cross|spiral> --seed \<s>. A tool-side PRNG, outside the core, draws an obstacle bitmap from a motif grammar (k bars with gaps, teeth from one wall, an annulus with n doors, n rectangles). It also draws knobs from ranges: B 4..8, boss period 1..3, 0..3 minions on rail cells the tool picks, R 45..90, lives 2..5, target 7,500..9,000. Filters, in order: (1) level validity codes 20-39; (2) the reference mix's clear rate over 200 seeds inside the band for its intended slot, and the median effective claim at or above the floor (P11); (3) search on 20 seeds reaching at least 0.95 of the ceiling, so the level is solvable and not luck-bound; (4) a no-input run over 50 seeds in which the boss body covers at least 90 % of open cells, so no seed-independent safe zone exists (D14); (5) worst-case zk cycles at or under the current 30M maximum. Survivors are written as ASCII sources to levels/src/lib/ (P10). A season's 16 = 4 worlds x 4 levels, one motif per world, with non-increasing target rates (P11). The same generator is the one the deferred Superchallenge mode needs (TODO.md).

**Inspired by:** Ink Fill (2026), Bix (2008), Territory Raider (2026), Hundreds (2013), Critter Round-Up (2008), Land Breaker (1999), Glimmer: Capture the Light (2026), Landgrab - Line Cutting Arcade (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Offline-generated, simulation-verified stages](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#offline-generated-simulation-verified-stages), [Worlds, chapters and themed stage groups](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#worlds-chapters-and-themed-stage-groups), [Procedurally generated levels](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#procedurally-generated-levels), [Fixed-length handcrafted campaign](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#fixed-length-handcrafted-campaign), [Time-boxed ranked competitions](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#time-boxed-ranked-competitions), [Weekly shared-board race](../taxonomy/multiplayer-and-competition.md#weekly-shared-board-race). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots, economy, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** P10; P11; §8.1.

**Bot effect.** Keeps the gap. Search is layout-agnostic: it clears all 16 current levels 100 % of the time. Rotation removes a human's memorized openings each season, but the season's 16 levels are public at open and can be practised in Training. Filter (3) rejects levels only search can clear, and filter (4) guards D14.

**Budget.** Offline CPU only. Search costs up to 749 ms per game second, so about 30 min of CPU per finalist; the scripted mix runs first as a cheap filter. Each season's set needs the usual playtest and calibration. No runtime, RAM or screen change.

**Variety.** Today's 16 levels vary only by bars, combs, a cross and a central block. Motif worlds bring new geometry each season (doors, annuli, islands, spirals) and a themed four-level arc.

**Merged from:** `modes-and-social#8`. Variants:

- Rotating season playlists from a calibrated library (paid): Paid mode. Each season's 16 Challenge levels are drawn from a larger library of calibrated levels, spread across difficulty tiers. The paid pool changes every season, or every week, while Training ships the whole library for practice.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 1.** This is offline only, so no consensus is touched.
Corrections:
(1) The tools assume exactly 16 sources. levels.rs requires NN 01-16, playtest requires 16 non-increasing clear_rate_pct entries, and calibrate requires levels 01-16. A library and per-season sets therefore need tools work (a set directory with its own targets.json), and levels/src/lib/ is not accepted today.
(2) The bot_effect mislabels filter (3). Requiring search to reach at least 0.95 of the ceiling checks solvability; it does not 'reject levels only search can clear'. Filter (2), the mix's band, does that.
(3) Filter (4), boss coverage of at least 90 %, can reject corridor motifs on purpose: a B x B body never enters a corridor narrower than B. Test the threshold on the current 16 first, since maze, labyrinth or comb may fail it.
(4) Filter (2) at 200 seeds has about 3.5 pp of standard error at 50 %, against the ±5 pp tolerance. It is only a pre-filter, and the P11 check stays at 1,000 seeds.
(5) Seasonal rotation means a new jiro_levels.bin, so the wedgie app has to be rebuilt and reinstalled each season.
The compute is about right: 20 seeds x about 1,350 ticks x 50 ms of search per tick is about 22 min per finalist.
This also revives the generator that README §5 defers with Superchallenge. That is fine offline, but say so in §5 and P10.

*Fixes.* Add level sets (a directory per set with its own targets.json) to the level, playtest and calibrate tools. Validate filter (4)'s threshold on the 16 current levels and per motif. Treat filter (2) as a cheap pre-screen before the 1,000-seed check. Document the offline generator in README §5 and P10. Plan a wedgie app release per season.

*Cost.* No runtime, RAM or screen cost. Offline per finalist: about 22 CPU-min of search, 200 mix seeds and one RISC Zero executor run for cycles. Per season: a 1,000-seed playtest, calibration and a wedgie app rebuild. Tools: SEASON_LEVELS generalization. Format and vectors: none.

*Conflicts.* P10; P11; README §5 (map generator deferred)

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** The generator runs offline and outputs data only. Each season's 16 levels are still fixed at open and calibrated as now, and filter 4 explicitly guards D14. Search plays any layout equally well, so rotation does nothing to it. Humans lose their memorized openings, but the 16 levels are public at open and playable in Training, so the gap widens only slightly early in a season, and that fades. Two filters need changes. (a) 90% no-input coverage still allows up to 10% of open cells, at least 168 cells or about 1,000 bp, that the boss body never reaches, such as pockets narrower than B. Those are seed-independent safe zones, which D14 forbids. (b) Filter 3, search at 0.95 of the ceiling or better, rejects exactly the layouts whose geometry keeps search below the ceiling, which are the ones that would narrow the gap. Four-level motif worlds let a draw over-sample one motif, which helps specialists in a way weights do not normalize, as with any level set. The weekly-rotation variant needs weekly seasons, because levelIds are fixed for a season.

*Fixes.* Tighten filter 4: union coverage over seeds of at least 99% of open cells, and no pocket the boss cannot reach larger than a few cells, with the threshold measured on today's 16 levels. Replace filter 3's ceiling ratio with a solvability and seed-variance test (search clears at least 95% of seeds with a small claim spread), and prefer candidates with a low search / mix-median ratio. Rotate the pool only at season boundaries.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** Geometry is the main way this genre creates new situations, and the 16 levels barely use it. Most are open fields ramped by knobs, plus bars, a comb, a cross and ring's single central block. Annuli with doors (seal the boss in or out), scattered islands as mid-field anchors, and spirals give new capture shapes and boss-trapping plays with no rule change (T1), and seasonal rotation keeps paid play fresh. The filters are sensible: validity, clear-rate band, search solvability, a D14 no-safe-zone check by boss coverage, and a zk cycle cap. The bot gap stays about the same. What the filters cannot measure is whether a board is fun or legible: generated boards can be bland or noisy. The output as ASCII sources fits P10 only if a designer reviews and edits them.

*Fixes.* Treat the generator as a drafting tool. A designer curates and edits finalists, and each world gets one hand-tuned signature level. Add readability and play constraints to the grammar: doors and corridors at least 3 cells wide, at least B+1 wide where the boss is meant to travel, and no 1-cell slivers or single-cell islands. In the clear-rate filter, also reject boards where the boss rarely leaves one sub-region, which makes them luck-bound. Share the motif grammar with the endless-climb generator, so there is one generator.

*Readability.* Good if the grammar keeps features of at least 3 cells. Blue walls on black read well at 4 px. Spirals and 1-cell doors become busy or nearly invisible on the 240×240 screen, so cap their density and width.

## Escalation at claim thresholds

`level-structure-and-progression#8` · T3 New core rule · score 11 (core 4, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Some minions sleep until your claim passes a marked line, then announce their return with a flashing spawn. On some levels the boss speeds up past another line. The player chooses which capture crosses each line, and when.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 adds wake_bp, u16, per minion slot (0 = awake at start), plus rage_bp, u16 (0 = off), and rage_period, u8 (>= 1). Level start: a slot with wake_bp > 0 starts as presence 2 (destroyed) with a countdown of 0xFFFF and skips the orientation step (PIN-16). R4.7-9 decrements it each tick, and it cannot reach 0 within T \<= 2,700, so no special case is needed. At step 3, during a capture: with claim_before and claim_after known, each slot with claim_before \< wake_bp \<= claim_after gets countdown = R, and respawns R - 1 ticks later at its spawn cell or the first rail cell in row-major order (R4.7-7, R4.7-9). Its spawn cell flashes during the countdown. If rage_bp > 0 and claim_before \< rage_bp \<= claim_after, the boss move period becomes rage_period; the phase stays global (R4.4-2). Claim is monotone, so each line fires once, and state hash v1 already holds presence, countdown and boss period: no state-hash change. Design rule: wake_bp \<= target - 3,000, so the escalation plays out and cannot be skipped by a clearing capture.

**Inspired by:** Territory Raider (2026), Project Uncover (2026), VoidMania (2026), Gal Pani X (2002), Qix (ZX Spectrum, PCW type-in) (1985), Gal Pani X (2002 (demo 2000)), Territory Arcade (2026), point line surface (2026, Early Access), Styx (1983), Claim (DaNuTree) (2026), CUT Arcade (2026), GALS HUNTER (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Boss phases at capture thresholds](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#boss-phases-at-capture-thresholds), [Aggression scales with the player's territory or lead](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#aggression-scales-with-the-players-territory-or-lead), [Boss shrinks with free space](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#boss-shrinks-with-free-space), [Telegraphed attacks and readable tells](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#telegraphed-attacks-and-readable-tells), [Size-versus-speed archetypes](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#size-versus-speed-archetypes), [Staged introduction of new enemies, hazards and rules](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#staged-introduction-of-new-enemies-hazards-and-rules), [Per-level enemy ramp](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#per-level-enemy-ramp), [Dynamic rank](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#dynamic-rank). **Touches:** rules, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D9; D14; R4.3-3; R4.6-1; R4.6-7; R4.6-12; §5 level bytes v1; PIN-16; PIN-17; R4.7-9.

**Bot effect.** Probably widens the gap slightly. Search sees both lines and times the crossing exactly, for example crossing with a capture that strands the woken minion's respawn far from its next cut. Humans can learn the same idea but execute it less precisely. The ceiling is unchanged, the weights still normalize, and the wake_bp \<= target - 3,000 rule stops anyone from skipping the escalation. Measure before shipping.

**Budget.** 7 bytes of level format. Work only at captures (a few comparisons), none per tick. No new state, so the hash stays v1, but vectors are regenerated for the rule. Screen: one flashing cell per waking minion.

**Variety.** Gives each level a calm opening and a harder second act, and makes the order and size of captures a pacing decision rather than pure area-grabbing.

**Merged from:** `field-enemies#5`, `wild-combinations#3`. Variants:

- Boss gears at claim thresholds: The claim bar carries one or two marks. When a capture pushes your claim past a mark, the boss changes gear: it gets faster, jumpier or smaller. The size of each capture now decides when the fight changes, not only how much land you hold.
- Boss that shrinks and quickens at claim thresholds: At set claim thresholds the boss sheds size and gains speed, so the late game is a small, fast body that slips into the pockets you left open. One cut big enough to clear from below the threshold skips the phase entirely.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** This is cheap, and the trick is valid. A sleeping minion is a destroyed slot with countdown 0xFFFF, which cannot reach 0 within T ≤ 2,700. Boss period, presence and countdown are already in the v1 hash, and the work runs only at captures. Corrections:
(1) The budget is wrong: 3 × u16 wake_bp + u16 rage_bp + u8 rage_period = 9 bytes, not 7.
(2) 'wake_bp ≤ target − 3,000 so the escalation cannot be skipped by a clearing capture' is false. Single captures above 30% happen (maze's 32% capture in the wedgie report, captures up to 84% in the qemu fuzz), and a clear at step 3 then ends the level before any wake plays out. That is harmless, but the guarantee must be dropped.
(3) Rage has little headroom. A move period cannot go below 1, and levels 5 to 16 already run the boss at period 1, so rage only works on levels 1 to 4 unless later levels are re-authored with slower bases, which shifts their baseline difficulty.
(4) Pin whether rage_period applies from step 4 of the same tick.
(5) The flashing spawn can lie. The respawn cell is chosen when the countdown ends (the spawn, else the first rail cell in row-major order), so a capture during the countdown can move it.
(6) The level-start step must skip the orientation step for sleeping slots (PIN-16 edit), and the hash's countdown field holds 0xFFFF minus elapsed ticks, which is fine.

*Fixes.* Count the bytes: 9 B in level v2. Drop the 'cannot be skipped' claim, or accept skipping as a legitimate big-capture reward. Pin: rage takes effect at step 4 of the crossing tick; the client flashes the cell R4.7-7 would pick at the current tick and updates it after each capture. Validity: rage_period ≥ 1, and a wake_bp > 0 only on a slot with a minion. To give later levels a second act, re-author their base periods, then recalibrate.

*Cost.* Level v2 +9 B. No new state, hash v1 unchanged. A few comparisons per capture, nothing per tick. zk 0. Screen: one flashing cell per waking minion. RULES_VERSION bump (new rule), vectors regenerated, playtest and calibration.

*Conflicts.* PIN-16; PIN-17; R4.7-9; R4.3-3; R4.6-1; §5 level bytes v1

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: escalation is deterministic, driven by claim, and the boss is still seeded. The anti-skip rule does not do what it claims. wake_bp \<= target - 3,000 only stops captures under 3,000 bp from skipping the escalation. The measured search pads about 1,200 ticks, then plays a setup capture and a finisher, and the finisher typically runs from below the target to about 98% claim, often well over 3,000 bp. It can therefore cross wake_bp (and rage_bp) and the target in a single capture, ending the level at step 3 before any woken minion or raging boss matters. Humans and the scripted mix take more, smaller captures and do play through the escalation, so the gap widens. The scripted bots need no new policy, since they simply meet the extra minions, so calibration is feasible and reflects step-by-step play. Draw variance is per level and calibrated.

## Exit gate with discounted overtime

`level-structure-and-progression#7` · T3 New core rule · score 11 (core 4, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Reaching the target opens the gate instead of ending the level. You can leave at once with X, or stay and carve more at half value, knowing that losing your last life still scores zero.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 adds overtime_bp, u16; 0 keeps today's behaviour (PIN-10). With 1..10,000, a capture at step 3 that brings claim_bp >= target opens the gate. That is derived from the monotone claim, so there is no state field, and the level continues to steps 4-6 on that tick: a gate-opening capture no longer wins the tick (PIN-09). End conditions: X at step 2 gives endReason 2 Cleared if the gate is open, else 1 Committed, and discards an unfinished cut (R4.4-7); time limit gives 2 if open, else 4; no lives gives 3 and a score of 0 even after the gate (D5 kept). Reported claim when the gate is open: claimBp = target + (claim_bp - target) x overtime_bp / 10,000; levelScore uses it as today. Only an X or time-out with the gate open counts as a clear for minClears (D10). Suggested overtime_bp 5,000. Presentation: the border flashes when the gate opens, and the bar shows banked versus overtime claim.

## Twin bosses and the dividing wall

`level-structure-and-progression#10` · T3 New core rule · score 11 (core 3, fairness 3, play 5) · gap: unclear · D14: compliant

Some levels have two bosses. Besides clearing by area, you can win by building a wall that separates them: a long, dangerous cut, or a few short ones that close the doors in a central barrier.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 adds boss_count (1..2) and a second record: B2 (2..8), spawn rectangle 2, period 2. Seed: boss 2's start from bytes 21..24 and bits 2..3 of byte 4; deflection key 2 from bytes 25..28. At step 4 both bosses run R4.6-7 and R4.6-3 in index order. Their bodies ignore each other and may overlap; neither is in the grid. Hits from either boss are hits. Capture: mark boss 0's body BOSS_ZONE and fill (R4.8-2, R4.8-3). If boss 1's top-left cell is not BOSS_ZONE, the bosses are separated: mark boss 1's body, fill again, then make the pass. On separation, the level ends at step 3 as Cleared with claimBp = max(claim_bp, target). Walls only grow, so separation is permanent and is checked only at captures. State hash v2: +7 bytes for boss 2. Level design: 'gate' layouts with a central obstacle bar and 2-3 doors, each wider than B.

**Inspired by:** Domain Expansion (2026), Stake That Claim (2026), Volfied (1989), Gals Panic S2 (1999), VoidMania (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Defeat, trap or separate the enemies](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#defeat-trap-or-separate-the-enemies), [Alternate win routes besides the area goal](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#alternate-win-routes-besides-the-area-goal), [Boss in every stage](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#boss-in-every-stage), [Boss stages at intervals, world ends and finale](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#boss-stages-at-intervals-world-ends-and-finale). **Touches:** level-format, rules, state-hash, outcome, tools-bots, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** D9; R4.6-5; R4.6-7; R4.8-2; R4.9-2; R4.9-3; R4.10-1; PIN-10; §6.4 seed bytes; state hash v1.

**Bot effect.** Narrows the gap if the split pays only the target. Search already reaches about 9,700 bp by area and will ignore the split, while humans get a flat-reward route on levels where their clear rates are low. Honest caveat: two bosses make area play harder for everyone, and search adapts best. On twin levels the area gap may widen, so the net effect depends on how often humans complete the split. Measure with a split-seeking scripted policy.

**Budget.** \+1 boss move per moving tick: at most 16 edge-cell reads and one fmix32, an estimated +2-4 % zk cycles at period 1 (unmeasured). Capture work unchanged, since each cell is pushed once in total. State hash 948 -> 955 bytes, about 10 B of RAM, 8 bytes of level format. Screen: a second B x B square redrawn partially.

**Variety.** Adds a second, very different way to win: one decisive, high-risk cut or door-closing sequence against two bouncing bodies, where today every level ends only by area.

**Core engineer: feasible, 3 of 5, tier 3.** The capture logic is sound. Each body covers only EMPTY cells and its cells are 4-connected to each other, so if boss 1's top-left cell is not BOSS_ZONE after the fill from boss 0, then no cell of its body is, and that one test decides separation. The second fill pushes only cells the first did not reach, so each cell is pushed at most once in total and the 3,364-entry stack cannot overflow. R4.8-2 already anticipates several bosses.

Ending the level at separation keeps D7's one-region invariant: the field can never be in two regions during play. max(claim_bp, target) does not cap search, whose separating capture can still trap both bosses and keep about 9,700 bp. Seed material is free: bytes 21..28 and bits 2..3 of byte 4.

The risk is design, not feasibility. Separation is a flat-reward shortcut that can be cheap. On a gate layout, closing a door claims almost nothing (both sides still hold field), so 2-3 short door cuts timed while the bosses sit on opposite sides give a clear at the target within about 100 ticks. On any layout without a bar, one floor-to-ceiling cut at the right moment does the same. Search ignores it (9,700 beats 7,500, and the ticks tiebreak only splits equal scores). Humans and the bots will converge on 'wait, then split'. Twin-level scores will cluster at exactly the target, and the weights are calibrated wrongly unless the reference mix includes a split-seeking policy.

The player's body-collision check and the boss-bump hits must loop over both bosses. M1.5's D14 divergence test must cover key 2.

*Fixes.* Reserve seed bytes in a single registry: bits 2..3 of byte 4 are also claimed by the seeded-orientation idea. Have the level tool restrict twin bosses to layouts where separation needs a real cut, for example a minimum door width and count. Add a split-seeking scripted policy to the reference mix before calibrating. Run M1.5's divergence property test on key 2.

*Cost.* Level bytes v2: about 480 B (+8: count, B2, rectangle 2, period 2). State hash v2: 955 B (+7). RAM: about 8 B in Game, negligible even with the copies made at start. Per boss-moving tick: one more fmix32 and at most 2 x B = 16 edge reads, estimated +2-4 % zk cycles at period 1 (unmeasured), with no price change. Capture: one extra cell test, plus a second seeding only on separation. Device: microseconds. Screen: a second B x B square with partial redraws.

*Conflicts.* D9; R4.6-5; R4.6-7; R4.8-2; R4.9-2; R4.9-3; R4.10-1; PIN-10; §6.4 seed bytes 21-28 and byte 4 bits 2-3 (collides with the seeded-orientation idea's bits 2-4); state hash v1; level bytes v1

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap unclear; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. Boss 2's start, direction and deflection key come from unused seed bytes (21..28 and byte 4 bits 2..3), and nothing seed-independent appears. The door geometry is fixed per layout, but whether closing the doors separates the bosses depends on where the two seeded bodies are, so it is an opening plan rather than a safe zone. Seed byte 4 bits 2..4 are also claimed by the orientation idea, so seed-byte allocation needs coordinating. *Fixes.* Add a split-seeking scripted policy to the reference mix, or at least to calibrate, before twin levels enter a season. Closing the last door has two outcomes. If the bosses are on opposite sides, they are separated and the level clears. If they are on the same side, the empty half is captured for a big area gain. Either way the cut pays, and the decision becomes reading which side each boss is on and timing the closing cut. That is a new situation, not added difficulty. Two bouncing bodies also change area play itself, with crossing danger zones and gaps that open and close. Separation ends the level at once, so the field is never left as two regions. That keeps D7's single-region coverage argument intact, which is elegant. The reward is capped at the target, which stops search from farming it. The split is mostly impractical on open fields, so its variety depends on purpose-built gate layouts. All 16 current levels have one boss, so this is fully distinct.

*Fixes.* 1. Draw boss 2 in its own palette colour, or with a 1-px mark, so the player can read which boss is where when they are close to each other or overlapping. 2. *Readability.* Yes. Two B x B squares, 8-32 px across, are the most legible objects on the screen. Doors in an obstacle bar are clear at 4 px per cell. The only weak spot is the moment the two same-coloured bodies overlap.

## Hunt clear: strand quota

`level-structure-and-progression#11` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 3, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: narrows · D14: needs-seeding

On hunt levels, every minion you strand by walling off the field in front of it counts. Reach the quota and the level clears even if your area is short, but hunting means timing your cuts around the patrols.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 adds hunt_goal, u8 (0 = off, 1..9). New state: strands, u8 (state hash v2, +1 byte). At step 3, each live minion destroyed by R4.7-8 adds 1. Dormant minions (see the wake thresholds idea) are not live and don't count. If hunt_goal > 0 and strands >= hunt_goal after the capture, the level ends at step 3 as Cleared with claimBp = max(claim_bp, target), the same lift as the twin-boss split. Stranded minions still return after R ticks (R4.7-9), so a quota above the minion count needs waves. Presentation: a strand counter; in the browser only, the client may highlight minions standing on rail that the current cut would wall off (a client-side fill, never on the wedgie).

**Inspired by:** Lightfish (2011), Blobbed Away (2025), Moonlight Territory (2026), PIX (Sega Genesis / Mega Drive) (2026), Boundarpy (c. 2019). **Mechanic families:** [Defeat, trap or separate the enemies](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#defeat-trap-or-separate-the-enemies), [Alternate win routes besides the area goal](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#alternate-win-routes-besides-the-area-goal), [Capture every designated target](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#capture-every-designated-target). **Touches:** level-format, rules, state-hash, outcome, tools-bots, presentation.

Today no seed-independent journal can clear a level, because reaching 75 % means cutting into the boss's ground. A fixed, precomputed journal would therefore clear at claimBp = target on most seeds, failing only when the boss happens to bump a short trail. The bot-gap reasoning is otherwise right: with the reward capped at the target, search keeps about 9,700 by area.

*Cost.* Level bytes v2: 473 B (+1). State: +1 byte, state hash 949 B. Per tick: nothing; one increment per destroyed minion. zk: negligible. Minion patrols are seed-independent today: fixed spawns, a fixed heading rule and a global phase, and before the first capture each minion's cell at every tick is a function of the layout alone. Small rail-hugging captures that strand a minion are pockets away from the boss, so their outcome rarely depends on the seeded boss. Respawns are deterministic too (spawn cell or the first rail cell in row-major order, R ticks later). That is the kind of precomputable route D14 rules out, and strand farming of respawns makes it repeatable.
