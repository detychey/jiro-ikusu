# Ideas: field enemies

The 9 ideas proposed from the axis of the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) *field enemies*: enemies in the open field: bouncing balls, line creatures, bosses with patterns or phases, growing, splitting, shooting, chasing, several bosses. Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Mixed-speed minion roster

`field-enemies#3` · T2 Level format v2 · score 12 (core 5, fairness 4, play 3) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Minions on one level no longer share a pace. A slow sentinel and a fast runner patrol the same rail, so the gaps between them open and close on a rhythm the player can learn to read.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 replace the single minion move period (offset 464) and respawn delay R (offset 465) with one period byte and one u16 R per slot, in slot order. Unused slots keep the zero rules, and codes 30 and 31 are checked per slot. R4.7-10 reads the minion's own period (tick % period_i == 0), and R4.7-8 sets a destroyed minion's countdown to its own R_i. State hash v1 already stores a move period in each minion slot, so its layout does not change. A fast minion can catch a slow one: they share a cell and pass through each other, which the rules already allow after respawns. Level bytes grow by 6 bytes to 478.

**Inspired by:** Territory Arcade (2026), Dancing Eyes (1996), Kicks (1989), Jezz Mania: Better Jezzball (2021), RPG Maker MZ Territory Action Plugin (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Mixed enemy roster](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#mixed-enemy-roster), [Size-versus-speed archetypes](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#size-versus-speed-archetypes), [Selectable enemy difficulty and configuration](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#selectable-enemy-difficulty-and-configuration). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** §5 level bytes v1 (offsets 464-466).

**Bot effect.** D14 is untouched: minion motion does not depend on the seed, so nothing becomes precomputable that was not already. It keeps the gap. Minions are visible on the rail and fully deterministic, so a human reads the rhythm of the gaps as easily as a bot. Scripted bots already dodge minions by position and need no change. The playtest and calibration are rerun for the new levels.

**Budget.** 6 more level bytes. No new state, no extra work per tick, no zk change. Screen: optional per-speed colours, one palette entry each.

**Variety.** All 16 levels give their minions one shared period, so the spacing along the rail stays fixed until a capture. Different periods move the safe stretch around the rail, widening and narrowing it on a cycle of lcm(p_i) ticks. New decision: wait on the rail until the fast minion laps the slow one and cut in the wide gap that follows, or go now through a narrower one. It also allows a cleaner difficulty ramp, such as one fast hunter instead of three medium patrols.

**Core engineer: feasible, 5 of 5, tier 2.** Clean. R4.3-4 already lists a move period per minion, and state hash v1 already writes a period into each minion slot (minion_bytes in hash.rs), so the hash layout is unchanged. The arithmetic is right: offsets 464-466 (3 B) become 3 periods and 3 u16 delays (9 B), +6 B for 478 B. Minions overlapping is already legal and the hit predicate is per minion. Nothing seeded changes, so D14 is unaffected, and greedy's minion margin uses current positions, not the period. Corrections: (1) Rewriting R4.7-8 and R4.7-10 to per-slot values is an edit to §4 rule text after first implementation, which CLAUDE.md defines as a behavior change. RULES_VERSION is bumped even though v1-equivalent levels replay identically, and 'touches' should list rules. (2) The gap pattern does not cycle every lcm(p_i) ticks. Relative spacing on a closed rail of length L drifts by (1/p_fast - 1/p_slow) cells a tick, so the cycle is a lap time on the order of L x p_fast x p_slow / (p_slow - p_fast), and every capture resets it. (3) Format v2 changes all 16 level ids and needs: per-spawn period syntax in the levels/src ASCII sources, the editor, web/src/level.ts and the wedgie's make_app_levels.py record size.

*Fixes.* List the rules touch and bump RULES_VERSION for the rule-text edit. Specify the per-slot zero rules (codes 30 and 31 per slot, in slot order). Correct the cycle claim. Add a per-spawn period glyph or annotation to the ASCII sources.

*Cost.* Level bytes +6 B (478). Minions struct +6 B. No new state, no hash change, no extra work per tick, 0 zk cycles. Format v2 with regenerated vectors/format, new level ids, levels/README.md, a playtest rerun and recalibration. A palette entry if speeds get colours (the wedgie maps every colour to the nearest of 16 fixed ones).

*Conflicts.* §5 level bytes v1 (offsets 464-466); R4.7-8; R4.7-10

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** D14 is untouched. Minion motion was already independent of the seed, and the seeded boss still separates runs. Mixed periods do not create a safe zone either: the gap pattern of period lcm(p_i) lies on the rail, not in the field. The gap is mostly neutral, because minions are visible and their positions easy to read. There is one slight widening I would name. With a shared period the spacing between minions stays fixed, so a human can judge a landing spot from where the minions are now. With mixed periods the safe stretch moves, so landing safely means projecting several clocks over the length of the cut. A simulator does that for free. A small period ratio such as p and 2p keeps it readable, and a ratio such as 1 and 5 does not. The pitch says the scripted bots need no change, which is not quite true. Greedy keeps a fixed MINION_MARGIN of 3 cells whatever the minion's speed, so the mix will be hit more by fast minions near the landing, and the playtest will overstate how hard those levels are. Draw variance is fine once the levels are recalibrated.

*Fixes.* Scale greedy's minion margin with each minion's speed over the cut's expected length, as Caution already does for the boss. Cap the ratio of minion periods within a Challenge level (for example max/min of 2 or less) in the level tool's checks before a season opens. Show the speeds in the presentation (one colour per period).

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** This is a cheap, honest T2 knob that adds a timing decision. Today the gap between minions on the rail is fixed until a capture, so the player reads one constant spacing. With per-slot periods the gaps grow and shrink on an lcm cycle: wait for the fast minion to lap the slow one and cut in the wide gap, or go now through a narrow one. It also gives cleaner level identities, such as one fast hunter, or a slow sentinel plus a fast runner, instead of N identical patrols. It does not clash with anything and needs no rule change. But it is still mostly a difficulty-shaping knob: the player's verbs and the minions' role as obstacles stay the same, and all minions still circle clockwise. It is much stronger paired with ramming (#4) or hunters (#11), where speed tells the player what a minion can be made to do.

*Fixes.* Colour-code minions by period so speed is visible at once, not learned by watching. Consider a per-slot phase offset ((tick + phase_i) % period_i) so designers can set the starting rhythm without moving spawns. Ship it together with #4 or #11, so the per-slot period carries meaning and is not only a tempo. Keep at least one Challenge level per speed mix within the playtest's calibrated range.

*Readability.* Good. Minions stay 1-cell sprites on the rail, as today. Speed is not readable from a still frame, so one palette entry per speed class (2-3 entries) is advisable. Gaps opening and closing are easy to see because minions only walk the rail.

## Rail hunters

`field-enemies#11` · T3 New core rule · score 12 (core 3, fairness 4, play 5) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Hunter minions patrol like the others while you walk the rail. Once you start a cut, they turn around whenever that brings them closer to you, and pace up and down the stretch of rail nearest your cut. A short cut lands next to a waiting hunter; a long one gives the boss more time.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add a type byte per minion slot (0 patrol, 1 hunter). Minion state adds a sense bit, stored in bit 2 of the heading byte, so the state hash goes to v2. Sense 0 is clockwise, the right-hand rule of R4.7-2; sense 1 is counter-clockwise, the mirror rule (left, straight, right, back). Step 5, for a hunter that moves this tick while the player is cutting: let n_s be the cell its current sense and heading lead to, and n_o the cell the opposite sense leads to from the reversed heading (heading + 2 mod 4). If the Manhattan distance from n_o to Pn, the player's cell after step 3, is strictly less than from n_s, the hunter flips its sense, reverses its heading and moves to n_o. Otherwise it moves to n_s. While the player is not cutting, a hunter keeps its current sense. At spawn, at respawn and after every capture, the sense resets to clockwise and the orientation step runs as today (R4.7-3). The mirror rule traces the same field boundary in the other direction, with the field on the left, so the coverage property (PIN-14) still holds whenever the player is not cutting.

**Inspired by:** Gapper (1986), Moonlight Territory (2026), Gravebloom (2026), Xonix (Pac-Man hybrid, Eques72) (2022), Lovely Girls (1998). **Mechanic families:** [Draw-triggered aggression](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#draw-triggered-aggression), [Grid-bound chasers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#grid-bound-chasers), [Homing chasers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#homing-chasers), [Trail hunters](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#trail-hunters). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D7; PIN-14; R4.7-2; R4.7-3; R4.3-4.

**Bot effect.** D14 is untouched: hunters react to the player, not to the seed. It keeps the gap. The behaviour is reactive and visible (the hunter shadows your cut along the rail), so a human can bait it with a feint as easily as a bot can, though search will feint with exact timing. Scripted bots plan cuts away from the minions' current cells. They must instead predict the shadow point, the rail cell nearest the head of the cut, which is a cheap heuristic; otherwise they will land on hunters and the playtest will overstate the difficulty.

**Budget.** One level byte per slot. No new RAM (bit 2 of the heading byte). Per hunter move while cutting: one extra rule evaluation (up to 4 rail tests) and two Manhattan distances; negligible zk cost. Screen: a distinct minion colour.

**Variety.** Today minions ignore the player, so a cut's landing spot is chosen against a fully predictable patrol. Hunters contest the landing spot: the nearest shore is guarded. Players choose between a long cut that lands far from the hunter and a feint that drags the hunter one way before the real cut closes on the other side. D4's freedom to stop mid-cut gains a use: stand still to hold a hunter in place.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 3.** Cheap in bytes and cycles, but its coverage claim is unproven, and D7's history shows that this exact kind of orientation detail trapped minions on 1.2% of boards. (1) The orientation conditions are not mirror images. R4.7-3 accepts a heading with open field 'behind its right'. After a reversal, that cell is ahead-left of the new heading, not behind-left, which is what a mirrored rule would need. So the left-hand trace from a corner cell, such as a border corner that touches the field only diagonally, is not shown to follow the boundary. (2) Sense resets to clockwise only at spawn, respawn and capture. A cut that ends in a lost life leaves a hunter in sense 1, walking left-handed indefinitely with no left-hand orientation rule ever applied, and PIN-14 is claimed for exactly that not-cutting state. (3) Greedy Manhattan distance ignores the rail's topology. Across an obstacle a hunter can settle at a local minimum far along the rail from the cut and oscillate there, flipping every move. That is the intended 'pacing', but it should be tested on obstacle levels. (4) 'Bit 2 of the heading byte' keeps the hash width but changes its meaning, which is still state hash v2. The reaction is visible to humans, so the bot gap is neutral, but scripted bots must predict the shadow point.

*Fixes.* Reset sense to clockwise and run R4.7-3 whenever a cut ends for any reason (capture or lost life), so the left-hand rule only ever runs while the player is cutting. Define a mirrored orientation (field on the left, or ahead-left after a reversal) for the flip. Extend core/tests/minion_coverage.rs to hunters flipping on the M1.7/M1.14 board sets before freezing the rule.

*Cost.* Level bytes +3 B (a type per slot). No RAM: the sense bit sits in the heading byte (state hash v2, same 948 B). Per hunter move while cutting, up to 4 extra rail tests (8 neighbour reads each) and 2 Manhattan distances: below 0.5% of zk cycles. One minion colour. RULES_VERSION bump, coverage tests, vectors, bots, playtest, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* D7; PIN-14; R4.7-2; R4.7-3; R4.3-4; R4.9-17

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** D14 is untouched, because hunters react to the player, not to the seed. The rule is simple to model: the hunter heads for the rail cell nearest the head of the cut (greedy in Manhattan distance), and it shows on screen, so a human can feint as well as a bot in kind, if not in timing. The gap stays about the same. Coverage (PIN-14) is kept while the player is not cutting, and the mirrored rule still traces the boundary. A hunter left counter-clockwise after a lost life or a commit (no capture, so no reset) also still covers the rail. The main fairness risk is calibration. Scripted bots plan against the minions' current cells with a 3-cell margin, so they will land on hunters, the playtest will overstate the difficulty, and the hunter levels will be overweighted. Each greedy flip is local, so a hunter can pace back and forth at a local minimum on a rail with obstacles. That is fine, but the vectors must pin it.

*Fixes.* Before calibration, give greedy and bold a shadow prediction: the rail cell nearest the head of the cut, and how far a hunter can travel during the cut at its period. Then prefer cuts that close outside that reach. Add vectors for the pacing case and for a hunter left with sense 1 after a lost life. Give hunters a distinct colour, as budgeted.

**Play: strong, 5 of 5.** This is the best fit with the existing verbs. Hunters contest the landing point of every cut, the most important decision in the loop, and give each existing tool a new role. Fast cut A becomes a sprint to outrun a period-1 hunter parallel to the rail before turning to land. D4's stop mid-cut holds a hunter in place, at the price of trail exposure to the boss. Feints pull a hunter one way before the real close. Long cuts land far from the hunter but give the boss more time. That ties minions and boss into one dilemma. The behaviour is reactive and visible, so humans can read and exploit it as well as a bot, and D14 is untouched. It costs no RAM (a spare heading bit) and one evaluation per hunter move. The tie rule keeps hunters pacing around the nearest point instead of jittering. It is fully distinct from the 16 levels, where minions ignore the player. Caveats: on folded rails (comb, maze), Manhattan distance can have local minima that leave a hunter pacing inside a tooth, which is acceptable and even exploitable. Several period-1 hunters could make some landings impossible.

*Fixes.* Limit Challenge levels to one or two hunters, or hunter period >= 2 when more than one is present, so a landing always exists. Scope the PIN-14 coverage property explicitly to ticks when the player is not cutting. Teach bold and committer the shadow-point heuristic before any playtest, or clear rates will overstate difficulty. Pair with per-slot periods (#3) so levels can field one fast hunter among slow patrols.

*Readability.* Good. A hunter is a 1-cell minion on the rail with a distinct colour (one palette entry). Its pacing around the point nearest the cut head is visible movement, not a hidden state, so a player soon sees who is guarding the shore.

## Ramming slow minions from behind

`field-enemies#4` · T3 New core rule · score 12 (core 4, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Catch a minion from behind on the rail and you knock it off: it vanishes and returns at its spawn a few seconds later. Slow minions can be run down and fast ones cannot, so clearing the rail becomes something you do, not only something you wait for.

**Rule sketch.** Changes the minion hit predicate (PIN-15) at step 5. Before minion i moves, it is knocked out if all of these hold: it is live; its cell a equals Pn, the last cell the player entered at step 3 (n >= 1); and the player's direction resolved this tick (R4.5-1) equals the minion's heading. A knocked-out minion is destroyed with its countdown set to R, through the lifecycle of R4.7-8 and R4.7-9, and respawns at its spawn or at the first rail cell in row-major order (R4.7-7). It does not move this tick and cannot hit the player. Every other contact is a hit as today: a head-on swap or share, or a side entry onto a minion that does not move this tick. On the rail the player moves one cell per tick, and a minion moves when tick % period == 0. So only minions with period >= 2 can be run down on a straight stretch; a period-1 minion can be caught only where the player cuts a corner of its patrol. No new state: a destroyed minion is already representable (presence 2), so the state hash layout is unchanged. RULES_VERSION bump, new PIN-15 text and new vectors.

**Inspired by:** Demolition Herby (1982), Make Trax (1981), Triple Punch (1982), Game Maker Qix (2011). **Mechanic families:** [Enemies killable by touch](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#enemies-killable-by-touch), [Respawning enemies](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#respawning-enemies), [Immune enemy types](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#immune-enemy-types), [Grid-bound chasers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#grid-bound-chasers). **Touches:** rules, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.9-4; R4.9-8 (PIN-15); R4.7-8.

**Bot effect.** D14 is untouched. It keeps the gap and may narrow it a little. Ramming is local and visible (chase what is in front of you) and needs no prediction beyond a few cells, so humans can do it as well as a bot. Search will time its rams exactly before its finisher. Scripted bots ignore ramming until bold and committer gain a ram step, and until then the reference mix understates how easy slow-minion levels are for humans. Calibration must play a mix that rams.

**Budget.** No RAM. One comparison per live minion per tick at step 5; negligible zk cost. Screen: a knock-out flash on one cell.

**Variety.** Today minions are pure obstacles, and the only answer is to wait for a gap. Ramming adds an aggressive verb: spend 2-5 seconds of the clock hunting a minion to buy R ticks of clear rail, or cut now. Combined with per-slot periods, the level tells the player which minions can be hunted and which cannot.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 3.** Well specified and cheap. Today a player who enters a minion's cell from behind on a tick the minion moves forward is not hit (no share, swap or pass), and on a non-move tick it is a share hit. The ram turns both into a knock-out, consistently. player.direction already holds the resolved direction, and presence 2 already encodes a destroyed minion. A direction equal to the minion's heading means the player came from behind, except when the heading came from an orientation step (after a capture at step 3 of the same tick, or at respawn) rather than a move. That is acceptable if pinned. Gaps to pin: (1) R4.7-9 counts the countdown down 'from the tick of its destruction on'. For a knock-out during step 5, state whether that tick's decrement applies, so that the minion respawns at t+R-1 or at t+R. (2) Period-1 minions can be rammed by tailgating at gap 1 wherever the player's path along the rail is shorter than the minion's, not only at 'corners'. That is fine, but the pitch's 'fast ones cannot be run down' overstates it. (3) Committed vectors that contain a share hit from behind will change outcome. Explain the moved outcomes. (4) It weakens D3's stated purpose ('keeps minions dangerous'), which deserves an owner note. The bot gap is about neutral, since a ram is local and visible. The scripted mix needs a ram step before calibration, or the reference mix will understate how easy levels with slow minions are.

*Fixes.* Pin the respawn tick of a rammed minion. Say that the heading tested is the minion's heading at the start of step 5, after any orientation step at step 3. Add a ram step to greedy, bold and committer before rerunning the playtest and calibrate.

*Cost.* No RAM, no state, no format change. Per live minion per tick, about 3 comparisons at step 5; zk change below 0.1%. RULES_VERSION bump, new PIN-15 text, regenerated game vectors, playtest rerun and recalibration. A 1-cell flash on screen.

*Conflicts.* D3 (rationale); R4.9-4; R4.9-8 (PIN-15); R4.7-8; R4.7-9 (PIN-17)

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 is untouched, since minions are not seeded and the boss still drives divergence. The rule is local and visible: the player chases the minion directly in front. It gives humans an answer to minions, and minions are where the reference mix falls furthest behind search: trio 20.2%, swarm 21.4% and doom 11.1% clear rates, against 100% for search. Search will time its rams exactly, but it is already at the ceiling, so the gap most likely narrows. Two fairness traps remain. (1) The predicate compares the player's direction with the minion's stored heading, so at rail corners, or right after a capture's orientation step (R4.7-8), a ram the player expects can become a hit. Search never makes that mistake and a human will, so the heading must be visible and the corner cases pinned. (2) Until bold and committer can ram, the mix understates how easy slow-minion levels are. Calibration would then give those levels too much weight, and drawing them would favour humans who ram: that is variance the draw creates. Note also that the rule changes two of today's cases. A rear entry onto a minion that does not move this tick was a share hit. A rear entry onto a minion that moves away was no hit. Both become knock-outs, and vectors must cover both.

*Fixes.* Give bold and committer a ram step (follow a minion with period 2 or more along its heading when it is within a few cells) before any calibration that ships this rule. Draw the minion's heading, and pin the knock-out predicate for a minion that turned this tick or was re-oriented at step 3. Add vectors for both of today's rear-entry cases.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This gives the player an aggressive verb on the rail. Minions change from pure obstacles to prey when slow (period >= 2) and stay hazards when fast, and the rule is easy to explain: hit from behind and it dies, meet it head-on and you die. It creates real choices: spend 2-5 s of clock chasing a minion to buy R ticks of clear rail, or cut now. Respawn at a known spawn cell, where a respawn onto the player hits (PIN-11), adds a follow-up risk instead of a free win. It reuses existing state (presence 2) and the existing lifecycle, so the cost is small. One caveat: it is a global rule change. 7 of the 16 levels (sentry, heavy, pair at period 3; comb, serpent, rush, cross, trio at period 2) get easier at once, and the reference mix (bold, committer) will not ram, so calibration understates human ease until those bots learn to. The fiction is a twist on the deadly-Sparx convention, but it reads naturally on a rail game.

*Fixes.* Gate ramming behind a per-level flag (or per slot, with #3) so the existing 16 levels keep their tuned clear rates, and add ram-centred levels on purpose. Colour-code rammable minions. Add a ram step to bold and committer before calibrating any season that uses it. Consider a short grace period, or a visible respawn telegraph at the spawn cell, so a ram followed by a respawn onto the player does not feel arbitrary.

*Readability.* Good. It is a single-cell event on the rail with a one-cell knock-out flash. Which minions can be rammed is not obvious from a still frame, so give slow and fast minions distinct colours (1-2 palette entries). The rule is about direction, which a player feels at once on the rail.

## Wind-up and charge

`field-enemies#6` · T3 New core rule · score 11 (core 4, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Now and then the boss stops and glows for a second, then charges at full speed in a straight line, bouncing off walls, for a couple of seconds. The wind-up is the safest moment to cut right beside it, and closing a capture during it stuns the boss for longer.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add a chance c (0..63, 0 disables), a wind-up W (8..30 ticks) and a charge length D (8..40 moves). Charge key K2 = big-endian u32 of level seed bytes 9..12. Boss state adds hold (u8) and charge (u8), with a 'stunned' flag in bit 7 of hold, so state hash v2 grows by 2 bytes. Step 4, in order: (1) If hold > 0: decrement it; the boss neither moves nor draws. When hold reaches 0, set charge = D and clear the flag. (2) Else if charge > 0: the boss moves this tick whatever its period, skips the deflection draw and runs the R4.6-3 edge tests (a bump into TRAIL still hits); then charge -= 1. (3) Else, on a tick with tick % period == 0, draw q = fmix32(K2 XOR tick) >> 26. If q \< c, set hold = W and the boss does not move this tick; otherwise it makes a normal move with the R4.6-7 draw. Stun: at step 3, a capture while hold > 0 and the flag is clear adds W to hold (so hold \<= 60) and sets the flag, once per wind-up. The charge direction is the velocity at wind-up, so its path is a plain bounce path that the client draws during the wind-up.

**Inspired by:** GraviNix: Tilt Xonix Arcade (2026), Moonlight Territory (2026), Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade (2026), Glowfield (2026), Volfied (1989). **Mechanic families:** [Charging dashers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#charging-dashers), [Telegraphed attacks and readable tells](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#telegraphed-attacks-and-readable-tells), [Territory-only boss](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#territory-only-boss), [Per-boss attack scripts](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#per-boss-attack-scripts). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D14; R4.3-3; R4.4-2; R4.6-6; R4.6-12; §6.4 seed bytes 9-12.

**Bot effect.** D14 gains a second seeded stream (when the boss charges), so runs diverge sooner. R4.6-12's divergence test must be rerun with charges, because a charge suspends deflections for D moves. The gap: the wind-up gives humans a 0.5-2 s warning and a fixed charge path, the same information search uses for that window, so the window itself is fair. Search also knows every future wind-up from the key and plans its finisher around the next stun; humans do not. Net, it keeps the gap, widening it slightly when c is high. Showing the time to the next wind-up (an extension of the boss forecast) would close that.

**Budget.** 2 bytes of state, 3 level bytes. One extra fmix32 per boss move, under 1% more zk cycles on the heaviest level. Screen: the boss recoloured during the wind-up and a dotted charge line of up to 58 cells for W ticks.

**Variety.** Today the boss never stops, so cuts near it are always the riskiest. A wind-up turns the boss into an opportunity: race in with fast cut A beside a frozen boss, close before the charge, and earn the stun for a second capture. Players must also keep clear of the charge line, a short-range threat unlike the slow diagonal drift.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** The mechanics are consistent and cheap: hold and charge fit in 2 bytes (hold of 60 or less fits under the bit-7 flag), seed bytes 9-12 are free, and the K2 draw is stateless. Issues: (1) c from 0 to 63 against q from 0 to 63 allows wind-ups on 63 moves in 64, a boss that is almost always frozen or charging, which suppresses deflections. The R4.6-12 divergence bound (N = 400) no longer holds as stated, and M1.5's property test must be redefined for moves taken under hold or charge. (2) A frozen boss runs no edge tests, so during W (up to 30 ticks, plus up to 30 more of stun) a fast cut at 2 cells a tick can lasso it: out to the boss, around its body, and back beside the outgoing line, about 50 to 80 cells. That claims almost the whole field in one cut, which today only search's exact timing reaches (search sits at 0.985 of the ceiling, which is claimable minus B^2). Paradoxically this narrows the gap, but it makes a level's outcome hinge on a seeded wind-up landing near the rail: high variance between runs that weights cannot normalize. (3) 'Its path is a plain bounce path that the client draws' is only a forecast. Any trail or capture made during the wind-up changes the bounces, so the client must recompute it every tick. (4) Charging 'whatever its period' and holding both break R4.4-2 and R4.6-10 ('never skips a move'), which need rule text.

*Fixes.* Restrict c to 0..8 (at most 1 move in 8) and W to a range below the typical boss-to-rail distance divided by 2, as level validity rules. Restate R4.6-12 to count only drawn moves, and rerun M1.5 with charges. Recompute the charge forecast every tick. Teach greedy that a charging boss moves every tick, since its margin assumes 1/period.

*Cost.* Boss state +2 B (state hash v2, 950 B), level bytes +3 B. One fmix32, about 20-30 RV32 instructions, per non-hold boss move: well under 0.5% of zk cycles. RAM +2 B per Game copy. Screen: recolouring the boss and a dotted line of up to 58 cells, so up to about 58 extra rows pushed (about 2.6 ms on the wedgie) during a wind-up. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors, playtest, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* D14; R4.3-3; R4.4-2; R4.6-6; R4.6-10; R4.6-12; §6.4 seed bytes 9-12

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** For D14 it adds a second seeded stream (K2 from seed bytes 9-12), so runs diverge sooner. Hold and charge suspend the deflection draws, though, so R4.6-12's divergence argument and its 400-move bound must be derived again and retested with charges. The gap widens through foreknowledge. Search knows every future wind-up from K2, and a frozen boss, extended up to 60 ticks by a stun, is the best moment for a lasso finisher: a trail around the boss's body that claims everything else. Search can stand at the nearest rail cell before each wind-up and run stun then lasso exactly. A human sees each wind-up only 8-30 ticks ahead and must improvise the same combo, so the window is reactive for humans and scheduled for search. The charge is also a surprise threat to any trail on its line, which search never meets unprepared. The pitch suggests showing the time to the next wind-up 'as an extension of the boss forecast', but no boss forecast exists in the repository or in the catalog, so that fix has to be built. Bots can be scripted: Caution's reach must cover a possible charge, and bold should react to a visible wind-up. A high c turns the draw into a lottery between calm and chaotic boss levels unless the calibration is right.

*Fixes.* For Challenge, require the client to show a countdown to the next wind-up, computed from K2. The client already holds the seed, so this gives humans what search already knows. Cap c, W and D for Challenge levels, and consider dropping the stun extension or limiting a stunned hold to about W. Extend Caution::margin for a charge, and teach bold to cut beside a visible wind-up. Rerun the R4.6-12 property test with charges before freezing the rule.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This is a classic telegraphed boss pattern that gives the boss a rhythm. Today the boss never stops, so cutting near it is always the worst option. Here the wind-up becomes the moment to race in with fast cut A beside a frozen boss and close in time for the stun bonus, while staying off the drawn charge line. It adds risk and reward around the boss without touching the minions, and it is clearly different from all 16 levels. There is a real flaw, though. The charge moves once per tick whatever the boss's period, and 12 of the 16 levels already have boss period 1. On those levels the charge is no faster than normal and only suspends deflections, which makes it more predictable, not more dangerous, so the wind-up becomes a pure gift (a freeze plus a stun). The bot gap note is honest: search sees every future wind-up from the key.

*Fixes.* Make the charge faster than any normal period: two R4.6-3 moves per tick during the charge (as fast cut does for the player). Then it threatens on period-1 levels too. Recompute and redraw the charge line every tick of the wind-up, because the player's TRAIL can change the bounce path. Show wind-up progress (2-3 boss colour steps) so humans know when the charge starts. Extend the boss forecast with time to the next wind-up to close the search gap. Cap c low on Challenge levels.

*Readability.* Very good for the wind-up: recolouring a 2-8-cell square boss (8-32 px) is the most readable signal on the screen. The dotted charge line (up to 40 one-cell dots) is readable but overlays the field and costs partial redraws for W ticks, which is acceptable. It needs 1-2 boss palette entries and one for the dots.

## Boss line of sight

`field-enemies#9` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 3, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

The boss watches the rows and columns its body spans. If it sees the head of your cut with no claimed land in between, it turns toward you. Obstacles and your own land become cover, and showing yourself on purpose can pull the boss away from where you want to cut next.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add a gaze mode g (0 off, 1 toward, 2 away). Step 4, on a boss-move tick, after the R4.6-7 deflection draw and before the edge tests, if the player is cutting: let (px, py) be the player's cell. If x \<= px \< x + B and py \< y, scan the cells (px, y - 1) up to (px, py + 1). If none is CLAIMED, the boss sees the player and sets vy = -1 (g = 1) or vy = +1 (g = 2). The same applies below the body (py >= y + B: vy = +1, or -1 for g = 2), and on the x axis when y \<= py \< y + B. TRAIL does not block sight, since it is the cut itself; CLAIMED does. The player matches at most one axis, because it is never inside the body. R4.6-3 then runs unchanged: a turn toward a blocked edge is undone, and a turn into TRAIL hits, as with any deflection (R4.6-10). No new state, since vx and vy are already in the hash. RULES_VERSION bump. Presentation: small markers where the boss's row and column bands meet the border, lit while the player is in view.

**Inspired by:** Gridder (1983), Late (2005), Claim (DaNuTree) (2026), Narrow the Siege (2022), Torch 2081 (1986). **Mechanic families:** [Sight-based detection and stealth](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#sight-based-detection-and-stealth), [Homing chasers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#homing-chasers), [Draw-triggered aggression](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#draw-triggered-aggression), [Evasive enemies that flee enclosure](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#evasive-enemies-that-flee-enclosure). **Touches:** rules, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D14; R4.6-2; R4.6-6; R4.6-10.

**Bot effect.** D14 still holds: deflections continue on 1 move in 32, so the player cannot lock the boss into a loop, and seeds still differ per run. But the boss's path now also depends on the journal, which gives a planner a lever. It widens the gap slightly: steering the boss with a deliberate sighting pays most to whoever can simulate where the turned boss goes next. Search is already at 98.5% of the ceiling, so its gain shows mostly as fewer lost lives rather than more claim. The border markers and the boss forecast make the lever readable to humans. Mode 2 (away) is closer to neutral, because it makes cuts in line with the boss safer for everyone. Scripted bots need a line-of-sight check before choosing a cut.

**Budget.** One level byte, no state. Up to 57 cell reads per boss move, only while cutting: typically 1-2% more zk cycles, up to about 5% on a period-1 level cut continuously. Screen: 4 border markers.

**Variety.** Today the boss is blind: where you cut relative to it matters only through its trajectory. Sight adds positioning. Cut outside its rows and columns, hide behind an obstacle, or bait it: show yourself far away to turn it, step out of line, then make the real cut behind it. Obstacle levels gain a second purpose as cover.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 3.** Implementable statelessly as written, but the key safety claim is false. The sight override runs AFTER the R4.6-7 draw and replaces vy (or vx) on every boss move while the player is in view, so deflections on that axis no longer matter. In mode 2 (away), a cutting player in the boss's column band with no CLAIMED cell between them pins the boss against the far wall: each move sets vy away, the edge test blocks it and inverts vy, and the next move sets it away again. The boss can then only slide along x, and the player can cut the whole near side safely while tracking the band. That is a player-made safe zone available in every run: not a copyable journal, but a universal strategy that trivialises mode-2 levels, and search will exploit it perfectly. In mode 1 (toward), staying in view always ends in a TRAIL bump, so it works as homing, which is fine. 'TRAIL does not block sight' combined with mode 2 strengthens the pin, because the player's own trail in the band never breaks line of sight. Cost is as stated: up to 57 reads per boss move while cutting, a few percent of zk cycles at worst.

*Fixes.* Make sight edge-triggered: turn the boss only on the boss move on which the player enters view on that axis. That needs 2 bits of state ('in view' per axis), so state hash v2. Alternatively, apply sight only on moves whose existing draw r falls in a band (for example r \< 16). Drop mode 2 or limit it to Training. Add a line-of-sight check to the scripted bots.

*Cost.* Level bytes +1 B. No state as sketched, or +1 B of state (state hash v2) with the edge-trigger fix. Up to 57 cell reads per boss move while cutting: about 1-2% of zk cycles typically, up to about 5% (about 1.5M cycles) on a period-1 level cut continuously. Worst wedgie tick about +0.1 ms. 4 border markers. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors, bots, playtest, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* D14 (partial lock of one axis); R4.6-2; R4.6-6; R4.6-10; R4.6-12

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 mostly holds, but its proof must be redone. The gaze sets vx or vy after the deflection draw, so while the player is in view the draw on that axis has no effect. R4.6-12's argument that two runs 'test opposite edges' on the first move whose draws differ then fails for that axis. The other axis still deflects, so runs still diverge, but the 400-move bound and the property test must be derived again. With g = 2 a player who keeps the head of the cut in the boss's band pins the boss against the far wall: a learnable technique that holds one axis under the player's control. It is not a seed-independent safe zone, because the player must follow the boss's seeded motion on the other axis, but it reduces what the seed decides. On the gap: g = 1 widens it. Being seen pulls the boss onto your trail, which humans will misjudge and search will not. g = 2 narrows it, since cutting in line becomes safer for everyone. The pitch's 'widens slightly' fits mode 1. Scripted bots can play it with a line-of-sight check.

*Fixes.* Allow only g in {0, 2} on Challenge levels and keep g = 1 for Training. Derive R4.6-12 again with the gaze override and rerun the M1.5 divergence property test with journals that keep the boss in view. Draw the boss's row and column bands while cutting, not only the 4 border markers.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This gives the boss awareness without randomness or new state, and it reads like the targeting of classic arcade ghosts. Where you cut relative to the boss becomes a positional game: stay out of its rows and columns, use obstacles and your own land as cover, or bait it by showing yourself far away and then cutting behind it. Obstacle levels (comb, cross, maze, labyrinth, ring) gain a second purpose as cover, which uses existing level data better. It is distinct from all 16 levels. There is an exploit in the sketch. Sight is tested on every boss move, after the deflection draw, so a player who stays in the band overrides that axis completely. In mode 2 (away) the boss is pinned against the far wall on one axis for as long as the player keeps level with it, which cancels D14's deflections on that axis and makes in-band cuts nearly free. In mode 1 (toward) it is a steady pull toward the cut head while in band, which is fine but harsh with large B.

*Fixes.* Make sight edge-triggered: turn only on the boss move where the player enters view (1 bit of state, 'was seeing'), not on every move while in view. That stops wall-pinning in mode 2 and homing in mode 1, and keeps baiting as a deliberate single action. Optionally limit sight range (for example 20 cells) so long-range bait is a choice with a cost. Keep the 1-in-32 deflections after sight is resolved, or at least not overridden on every move.

*Readability.* Good. The boss is a large square, so whether the 1-cell player is level with its 2-8-cell rows or columns is easy to judge at a glance. The border markers B cells wide confirm it. Cover is ordinary CLAIMED cells, already visible. It needs one palette entry for the lit markers.

## Sparks shed by the boss

`field-enemies#7` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 3, fairness 3, play 4) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

The boss sheds small sparks that ricochet through the open field and kill your trail just as the boss does. A spark caught in a captured region dies, so every cut is both a land grab and pest control. Leave the sparks alone and the field fills up.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add a spark cap k (0..4), initial sparks k0 \<= k, a spawn interval S in ticks (0 = no spawns) and a spark period p >= 1. Spark key K3 = big-endian u32 of seed bytes 13..16. State adds 4 spark slots of (live, x, y, vx, vy), so state hash v2 grows by 20 B. Step 4, after the boss moves: (a) On ticks with tick > 0 and tick % S == 0, if fewer than k sparks are live, the lowest free slot spawns at the boss body's centre cell (x + B/2, y + B/2), an EMPTY cell. vx comes from bit 0 and vy from bit 1 of fmix32(K3 XOR tick). At level start, the k0 initial sparks spawn the same way, drawing fmix32(K3 XOR (0x80000000 | i)). (b) Each live spark i moves on tick % p == 0 exactly as a B = 1 boss (R4.6-3), with its own draw r = fmix32(K3 XOR ((i + 1) \<\< 16 | tick)) >> 26 under the R4.6-7 rule. Sparks ignore the boss body and each other. A spark bumping TRAIL hits the player (a new case of R4.9-1). A player sub-step onto a spark's cell while cutting is a hit and does not happen (as R4.9-3), so sparks only ever stand on EMPTY cells. Capture (R4.8): the fill still seeds only from the boss body; after the pass of R4.8-4, every spark whose cell is now CLAIMED is destroyed and its slot freed.

**Inspired by:** Xonix (1984), Torch 2081 (1986), Jezz Mania: Better Jezzball (2021), Volfied (1989), Eyestorm (2009). **Mechanic families:** [Bouncing ball enemies](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#bouncing-ball-enemies), [Spawner enemies](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#spawner-enemies), [Reinforcements over time](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#reinforcements-over-time), [Boss plus minions](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#boss-plus-minions). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.4-9; R4.8-4; R4.9-1; §6.4 seed bytes 13-16.

**Bot effect.** D14 is strengthened: spark spawns and turns are seeded, so runs diverge faster. The gap moves both ways. Sparks tax waiting: search pads half the spare ticks on the rail to bank compute, and a field that fills while you wait makes that padding costly for it as well as for humans, which narrows the gap. But several small, fast, seeded movers are much harder to track by eye than to simulate, which widens it, sharply with k >= 3 or p = 1. Keep Challenge levels at k \<= 2 and p >= 2 and leave the swarms to Training. Scripted bots need a spark-aware safety check; greedy's reach test can treat each spark as a B = 1 boss.

**Budget.** 20 B of state and 4 level bytes. Per tick: up to 4 sparks x (2 edge tests + 1 fmix32), about 2% more zk cycles with 4 sparks at p = 1. Capture: 4 cell checks after the pass. Screen: four 1-cell sprites, 8 partial redraws per move, one palette entry. The Game struct grows by 20 B against a stack margin of about 1.2 KB on the wedgie.

**Variety.** Today the boss's region always survives a capture, and the only enemy you can remove is a stranded minion. Sparks give the field a population to manage. You can aim a cut so the region you take holds two sparks, accept a smaller capture because it clears the field, or rush the claim before the cap is reached. The boss becomes a source of threats, not a single threat.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 3.** Sound core design. Sparks move as B=1 bosses through R4.6-3, the three draw domains are disjoint (K3^tick, K3^((i+1)\<\<16|tick) with tick \< 2^16, and K3^(0x80000000|i)), seed bytes 13-16 are free, and the body's centre cell is always EMPTY because the body only covers EMPTY cells. Gaps: (1) The player hit covers only 'a sub-step onto a spark's cell while cutting'. The sub-step that STARTS a cut (cutting is false) onto a spark cell is not covered, and that would let TRAIL lie under a spark and break the invariant 'sparks only on EMPTY'. Bead idea #8 covers this case and this one must too. (2) 'Keep Challenge levels at k \<= 2 and p >= 2' is advice, not a rule. Nothing stops a season from shipping k=4, p=1. (3) The readability cost is real on the device: E1 already found a 4x4-px white player dot 'hard to see on the red trail', and four 1-cell movers on a 16-colour panel will be worse. (4) The Game grows by 20 B. Game is moved several times on the 64 KB stack, which peaks at 62,784 B, so expect about 60-100 B of extra peak against a margin of about 1.2 KB. That is fine alone, but it stacks with every other T3 idea, and the natmod's extra code (about 1 KB) comes out of a heap with about 12 KB left at level start. Scripted bots need spark-aware safety, or calibration overstates difficulty.

*Fixes.* Extend the hit rule to any player sub-step into a spark's cell, including the one that starts a cut. Make k \<= 2 and p >= 2 level-validity rules for any level a season may include, or a v2 range rule. Treat sparks as B=1 bosses in greedy's reach margin before the playtest.

*Cost.* State +20 B (state hash v2, 968 B), level bytes +4 B. Per tick, up to 4 x (2 single-cell edge tests + 1 fmix32), plus 4 compares per player sub-step and 4 cell checks per capture: about 1-3% more zk cycles at k=4, p=1. Game +20 B (about 60-100 B of extra stack peak). About 1 KB of natmod code. Screen: 8 cell redraws per spark move, one palette entry. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors, bots, playtest, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* R4.4-9; R4.8-4; R4.9-1; R4.9-3; §6.4 seed bytes 13-16

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 is strengthened, since spark spawns and turns are seeded from K3. On the gap I agree with the pitch: it widens. Each spark is in effect a 1-cell boss with its own deflection draws, drawn as a 4x4 px sprite on a 240 px screen. Search simulates them exactly. Humans must track the boss and up to 4 small movers by eye, each of which ends a cut on contact. The waiting tax narrows the gap only a little, because search's padding exists to bank compute and its 892857 units per tick are generous. The ceiling does not change, since the fill still seeds only from the boss, so search will stay near 0.985 while human scores fall. Scripted bots can play it (greedy's reach test can treat each spark as a B = 1 boss), so calibration is possible. Fairness then depends on the caps the pitch suggests, which nothing in the format enforces. Two rule gaps: the first sub-step from the rail onto a spark's cell starts a cut, so whether that counts as 'while cutting' must be pinned. The 20 B of extra state also shrinks the wedgie's roughly 1.2 KB stack margin, which matters for Training.

*Fixes.* Enforce k \<= 2 and p >= 2 for Challenge levels in the level tool's pre-season checks, not just as advice, and leave swarms to Training. Pin the case of a cut that starts onto a spark's cell. Add the spark reach test to greedy and bold before calibration. Draw sparks in a high-contrast colour, with a short motion trail in the browser.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** This gives the field a population to manage and adds a reason not to stall. Since D4 allows stopping mid-cut and the rail allows waiting indefinitely, the game has little to stop turtling, and sparks that build up to a cap tax waiting for humans and search alike. Every cut becomes both a land grab and pest control: aim the enclosure so it swallows sparks, take a smaller claim that clears two, or rush before the cap. Sparks are B = 1, so they reach 1-wide corridors and comb teeth that big bosses cannot. That revives obstacle levels whose narrow pockets are boss-safe today. Spawns on a fixed tick clock (tick % S) can be learned, while directions stay seeded, so D14 holds. It is distinct from all 16 levels. The cost to the game's identity is moderate: one big readable boss becomes a boss plus a swarm, and tracking several 1-cell fast movers by eye is where humans fall behind simulation, as the pitch says.

*Fixes.* Keep Challenge levels at k \<= 2 and p >= 2 as proposed, and leave k >= 3 or p = 1 to Training. Flash the boss for a tick before it sheds, so spawns are telegraphed and not only clocked. Give sparks one high-contrast colour unused by minions and the trail. Give greedy's safety check a spark-as-B=1-boss model before calibration. Consider a max lifetime per spark so a spark stuck in the boss region does not hold a slot forever.

*Readability.* Weakest of the batch on the wedgie. A spark is a 4x4 px dot, as small as a minion, but moving diagonally in open field instead of along the rail, so two or more at p = 1 are hard to track on a 1.3-inch screen. At k \<= 2 and p >= 2, with a dedicated bright colour, it is readable. Redraw cost (8 partial redraws per move) is fine.

## Wake beads

`field-enemies#8` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 4, fairness 3, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

The boss drops glowing beads along its path, leaving a tail of its last dozen positions. Cutting through its wake, today the safest cut, now means threading between the beads, and a capture that swallows part of the tail cuts it short.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add a tail length K (0..16) and a spacing S (2..8 boss moves). State adds a ring of K bead cells (u16 cell index, 0xFFFF = empty) and a head index, so state hash v2 grows by 33 B. Step 4: on a boss-move tick with (tick / period) % S == 0, after the boss moves, write the body's centre cell (x + B/2, y + B/2) to ring\[head\] and advance head mod K, overwriting the oldest bead. Beads only land on EMPTY cells, because the body covers only EMPTY cells. Step 3: a player sub-step onto a bead cell while cutting, or to start a cut, is a hit and does not happen (as R4.9-3; with fast cut there is then no sub-step 2, PIN-04). So a bead never lies on TRAIL. The boss passes over beads. Capture: after the pass of R4.8-4, every bead whose cell is now CLAIMED is cleared. Beads survive a lost life. With S >= 2, the beads leave gaps that a 4-connected trail can thread. The oldest bead, the next to vanish, is drawn dimmest.

**Inspired by:** Qix (1981), Xonix Blast (2012), Mamba (1991), Space Cutter (2026), Clax (1985). **Mechanic families:** [Qix line creature](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#qix-line-creature), [Snake and segmented-body enemies](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#snake-and-segmented-body-enemies), [Mines and mine layers](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#mines-and-mine-layers), [Ambushers and lingering area hazards](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#ambushers-and-lingering-area-hazards). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.4-9; R4.5-2; R4.8-4; R4.9-1.

**Bot effect.** D14 is unchanged: the beads follow the seeded boss, so their layout differs in every run. It narrows the gap slightly. Beads are static, visible and fully readable, so the routing difficulty they add is seen as well by a human as by a simulator. They also punish the shadow-the-boss cut that both search and greedy rely on most. Search routes around them cheaply. Greedy's rectangle planner needs a bead check or it will hit them, so its clear rates drop until it learns, and calibration must wait for that.

**Budget.** 33 B of state and 2 level bytes. Per tick: a bead check on each player sub-step while cutting (up to 2 x K comparisons) and one write every S boss moves; K checks per capture. About 1% more zk cycles. Screen: up to 16 static cells, 2 redraws per drop, 3 palette entries for brightness by age.

**Variety.** Today the space behind the boss is the safest place to cut, which flattens most levels into the same pattern. A tail turns that space into a maze that changes as the boss moves. Players choose between cutting fast ahead of the boss, threading its wake carefully, or severing the tail with a capture to clear a lane for the next cut.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 3.** Consistent. Beads are written from the body's centre, so they land only on EMPTY cells; the player can neither enter nor start a cut onto one, so they never lie on TRAIL; captures clear them; and the ring has a fixed 16 entries, so the hash width is fixed whatever K is. S >= 2 is needed and enough for consecutive beads on a diagonal run, because a 4-connected trail cannot cross an 8-connected diagonal line, which S = 1 would draw. Caveats: (1) With bounces the boss can sit in place on one or both axes, so beads can cluster or repeat a cell. A tight pocket can then hold a bead blob that walls off a narrow lane. That is acceptable, and every capture clears it. (2) A bead hit costs a life, like touching the body (R4.9-3). That is harsh but consistent. (3) '3 palette entries for brightness by age' does not hold on the wedgie, whose lcd.color snaps every colour to the nearest of 16 fixed ones. Expect one or two distinct shades. (4) Greedy's rectangle planner must check beads, or its clear rates fall and calibration must wait. D14 is unchanged, and the beads are static and visible, so the bot gap is neutral or slightly narrower.

*Fixes.* None required. Use a single bead colour (or two) on the wedgie. Add a bead check to greedy and bold before the playtest.

*Cost.* State +33 B (16 x u16 + head, state hash v2, 981 B), level bytes +2 B. Per tick, up to 2 x 16 compares on player sub-steps while cutting and one ring write every S boss moves; 16 checks per capture. Under 1% more zk cycles. Game +33 B (about 100-150 B of extra stack peak with copies). Screen: up to 16 static cells, 2 redraws per drop. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors, bots, playtest, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* R4.4-9; R4.5-2; R4.8-4; R4.9-1; R4.9-3

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 is unchanged: the beads follow the seeded boss path, so they differ in every run. I disagree that this narrows the gap. Beads remove the safest cut for humans (shadowing the boss), and threading a 4-connected trail through gaps between beads 1-2 cells wide is a precision task at 15 Hz. With fast cut at 2 cells a tick, overshooting by one sub-step hits a bead. Search loses nothing: it routes around beads exactly, and its score is fixed by the ceiling, which beads do not change because capture clears them. So the human mix loses claim and lives while search stays at about 0.985, and the ratio widens. Visibility helps humans, so the widening is mild, much less than with moving threats. Scripted bots need a bead check in greedy's rectangle planner, and calibration must wait for it. Otherwise the bead levels are overweighted and drawing them becomes lucky.

*Fixes.* Use S >= 3 on Challenge levels, so that the gaps fit a human's input precision, or make fast cut stop before a bead instead of hitting it. Add the bead check to greedy and bold before calibration. Correct the bot_effect to 'widens slightly', with the reason.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** The diagnosis is right: cutting behind the boss is the dominant safe pattern, and changing that helps variety. But the answer is a passive hazard. Beads are static mines in the boss's recent wake that only hurt if you step on them. The new decisions are mostly routing: cross the dotted wake at a gap, cut ahead of the boss, or capture part of the tail to clear a lane. Severing the tail with a capture is the most interesting part. With S >= 2 the wake is always threadable by a 4-connected trail, so it mostly adds precision and difficulty, especially with fast cut A, rather than new situations. The boss covers 95% of the interior over a level, so beads are scattered across recent paths, not only directly behind it. The boss itself and the minions are unchanged. It is distinct from the 16 levels but sits close to sparks (#7), which do the same job (hazards that captures clean up) with more dynamics.

*Fixes.* Use one bright bead colour plus a single dimmer tone for the oldest bead, not 3 brightness levels, so beads stay visible on the wedgie. Keep S >= 3 on Challenge levels so fast-cut crossings remain fair. Consider making a bead block a sub-step (as the own trail does, D4) instead of hitting, and let a capture that swallows the tail clear the whole tail: that turns beads into a routing and timing puzzle instead of a mine field. Or merge the idea into #7 as a level option.

*Readability.* Fair. Beads are static 1-cell dots, easy to see in a bright colour, and drop redraws are cheap. Ageing over 3 brightness levels is the weak point: dim beads on the dark field at 4x4 px on a 1.3-inch screen will be missed, and those are the beads that hit. Two tones at most.

## Boss finale slot

`field-enemies#13` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts · score 8 (core 2, fairness 4, play 2) · gap: neutral · D14: compliant

Every Challenge run ends on a boss level. The sixth level comes from a small season pool of boss stages, where earlier bosses return with one extra trick each (a charge, sparks, turrets or a twin). Runs get a climax, and new enemy mechanics enter paid play one slot at a time.

**Rule sketch.** Season parameters add finaleIds (bytes32\[4\]) and finaleWeightsBp (uint16\[4\]), with a count f (1..4). The draw of §8.2 runs its Fisher-Yates loop for i = 0 to 4 only, giving 5 of the 16 main levels. Slot 5 is 16 + (h mod f), where h = keccak256("jiro.finale" ‖ runNonce) read as a 256-bit big-endian integer, under a new domain tag. The core returns slot indices 16..19 for finale levels in its Outcome, and settlement's draw check (P17) reads finaleIds for those slots. Finale levels use whatever level format and rules the season's rules version supports. Each is calibrated on its own from the playtest mix, so its weight normalises it like any other level. Training lists the finale pool as extra levels.

**Inspired by:** Starcutter: Space Arcade (2026), Volfied (1989), Lazy Drawer: Line Survivors (2026), Cosmo Xonix: Space Arcade (2026), Gotta Xonix (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Boss encounters in a campaign](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#boss-encounters-in-a-campaign), [Returning upgraded bosses](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#returning-upgraded-bosses), [Signature-trick bosses with a combined finale](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#signature-trick-bosses-with-a-combined-finale). **Touches:** contracts, economy, outcome, level-data, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** P17; §8.2 draw; §8.4 levelIds and weightsBp.

**Bot effect.** Neutral by itself; D14 is untouched. Its value is containment. A mechanic that widens the gap, such as sparks or twin bosses, affects one level in six, with its own calibrated weight, instead of the whole pool of 16, and its playtest can be judged before it reaches the main pool.

**Budget.** Core: one extra keccak per run at draw time, nothing per tick. Contracts: two more fixed arrays in Season.open (about 5 storage slots) and a slot-range branch in the draw check, which needs a new Season generation and a new season-file schema. No device change, since the device plays Training only.

**Variety.** Today a run is six random levels with no arc. A fixed finale gives the run a shape: bank early, knowing the last level has a particular kind of boss. It adds a run-level decision, how much risk to take in levels 1-5 before the boss level. It also gives seasons a theme, since the boss pool can rotate from season to season.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 5.** There is a structural gap, and the argument for it is weak. (1) The core computes the draw (P17), and draw() in seed.rs takes only runNonce. Slot 5 = 16 + (h mod f) needs f, a season parameter the core never sees. Either IReferee.verify/verifyRun and the zk public values gain f, or f must be a constant. (2) With f constant and finaleIds allowed to repeat, the contract cannot recover the slot by searching finaleIds for keccak(level) as it does for levelIds. It must instead check finaleIds\[drawSlot - 16\] == levelId. (3) Slot 5's draw changes, which changes drawSlot in committed Outcomes at levelIndex 5, so RULES_VERSION is bumped. (4) The 'containment' claim is backwards. A single new-mechanic level placed among the 16 already appears at most once per run, in 6/16 = 37.5% of runs. A finale puts a mechanic level in 100% of runs. (5) The 'run arc' decision is illusory. Levels score independently (a sum), lives do not carry over, and the only coupling across levels is the deadline/ticks tiebreak, so there is no reason to 'bank early' for level 6. (6) The finale pool still needs the new mechanics in the core, under the season's single rules version. All of this needs a new Season generation, a new season-file schema, ECONOMY.md and settlement changes, for something the existing 16-slot pool already does.

*Fixes.* If kept: fix f = 4 as a core constant (finaleIds may repeat, all disjoint from levelIds), check finaleIds\[drawSlot - 16\] in settlement, and bump RULES_VERSION for the draw. Better: ship boss-variant levels inside the 16-level pool (T1/T2 plus the mechanic's T3) and let weights normalise them, with no contract change.

*Cost.* Core: Fisher-Yates i = 0..4 plus one finale keccak, the same number of keccaks as now and nothing per tick. Contracts: a new Season generation, finaleIds (4 slots) plus packed finaleWeightsBp and f (about 5-6 storage slots, roughly 110-130k gas once at open), a slot-range branch in the draw check, a new season-file schema, docs/ECONOMY.md, contracts/abi. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors, separate calibration of the finale levels.

*Conflicts.* P17; P4 (IReferee inputs if f is variable); §8.2 draw; §8.4 levelIds and weightsBp; §7.2 settleRun check 3

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap neutral; D14 compliant.** D14 is untouched: every finale level is still played under its own level seed at levelIndex 5. The draw stays fair. With f in {1, 2, 4} the keccak value has no modulo bias, and with f = 3 the bias is negligible. Every run now gets exactly one finale, which spreads a new mechanic's effect evenly over the runs instead of leaving it to whether the draw holds 0, 1 or 2 levels with it. That is a fairness gain. The containment claim needs a correction, though. Compared with putting the mechanic on one level of the main pool (6/16 = 0.375 such levels per run), a finale exposes every run to it once, so a mechanic that widens the gap reaches all runs. The containment holds only compared with spreading the mechanic over many of the 16 levels. Scripted bots can calibrate finale levels like any other level. Their weights must use the same P scale as the main 16, or the finale would count for more or less than the other slots. It needs a new Season generation, an Outcome change and an extension of P17, which costs a lot but adds no fairness risk.

*Fixes.* Have calibrate compute finaleWeightsBp with the same P as the main pool, and check that a median run on the finale scores P. Admit a mechanic to the finale pool only after its playtest shows a search-to-mix ratio on that level close to the main pool's (about 2.0). Correct the containment claim: every run gets one finale level, against 0.375 levels per run for a mechanic on a single main-pool level. Prefer f >= 2, so the finale is not one fixed level that can be over-practised.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** This is a rollout and run-structure device more than play variety. It adds no new decisions to the boss, minions, rail or cut loop; the play comes from whatever finale levels it holds. The claimed run-level decision is not real: levels are independent (lives, shield and claim reset each level, and run score is a plain sum), so knowing the finale does not change optimal risk in levels 1-5. The climax is mostly presentation. Its genuine value is containment, exposing new mechanics on one known slot with its own weight. But a season can already put 1-2 new-mechanic levels in its 16-level pool with calibrated weights, which contains them nearly as well with no contract change. For that, it costs a new Season generation, a new season-file schema, P17 changes and an Outcome change: a T5 bill for little play.

*Fixes.* Get containment through the existing season pool first: include new-mechanic levels among the 16 levelIds with calibrated weightsBp. If a finale is still wanted, give it a real run-level stake, such as carrying unused shield charges or a lives bonus from levels 1-5 into the finale (a scoring and Outcome change worth its own design), or present the last drawn level as a finale in the client (T0) without changing the draw.

*Readability.* No screen impact of its own; readability depends entirely on the finale levels' mechanics. A finale banner or boss intro is client-only.

## Tethered twin bosses

`field-enemies#14` · T3 New core rule · score 7 (core 2, fairness 2, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

Two smaller bosses share the field, joined by a crackling tether that sweeps across it as they move. Split them into separate regions and the tether snaps for good, but both regions then stay open. Keep them together and you must dodge a moving line to herd both into one small region.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add a second boss: a size B2 (2..8) and a spawn rectangle, sharing the move period. Validity (new code): the two bodies cannot overlap from any pair of spawn cells. Seed: the second boss's start index comes from bytes 21..24, vx and vy from bits 2 and 3 of byte 4, and its deflection key from bytes 25..28 (R4.6-5 and R4.6-7, per boss). State adds a second Boss and a tether bit, so the state hash goes to v2. Step 4: boss 0, then boss 1, moves by R4.6-3. Each treats the other's body cells as solid in its edge tests: it bounces, with no hit. Because boss 1 tests against boss 0's new position, the bodies never overlap. Then, while the tether bit is set, walk the integer (Bresenham) line from boss 0's body centre to boss 1's, at most 60 cells: any TRAIL cell on it hits the player, and CLAIMED cells do not stop the line. Capture (R4.8-2 already seeds from each boss): fill from boss 0 first. If boss 1's top-left cell is now BOSS_ZONE, they share a region. Otherwise clear the tether bit for good, since open regions never merge. Then seed from boss 1 and finish the pass. Moving onto either body is a hit (R4.9-3). The ceiling becomes claimable - B0^2 - B1^2, or less once the bosses are split.

**Inspired by:** Volfied (1989), QWYX (c. 2026), Domain Expansion (2026), Qix (1981), Styx (1983). **Mechanic families:** [Paired and multi-part bosses to separate](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#paired-and-multi-part-bosses-to-separate), [Several main enemies at once](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#several-main-enemies-at-once), [Qix line creature](../taxonomy/field-enemies.md#qix-line-creature). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-format, level-data, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** D9; D14; R4.3-3; R4.6-1; R4.6-5; R4.8-2; R4.9-1.

**Bot effect.** D14 is strengthened by a second seeded boss, but R4.6-12's divergence argument and the M1.5 property test must be restated for two bodies that bounce off each other. It widens the gap. A line whose ends follow two seeded bouncers is the hardest threat in this list to predict by eye and trivial to simulate, so search gains the most. The mitigations are slow periods and a boss forecast for both bodies. Scripted bots must test each planned cut against the area the tether sweeps, which greedy's reach test does not do today.

**Budget.** About 12 B of state (second boss and tether bit) and 7 level bytes. Per boss move: the second boss's 2 edge tests, mutual body tests (up to 4 x 8 comparisons) and a line walk of up to 60 cells, about 5-10% more zk cycles on a period-1 level. Capture: one extra cell check. Screen: the tether redraws up to 60 cells per boss move, as much as a large body, so on the wedgie it suits only periods >= 2.

**Variety.** Today every level has one boss, so the end game is always 'shrink one region'. Twins add a strategic fork. Snap the tether early for a safe middle game at the cost of a lower final claim, or keep the bosses together, dodge the sweeping line and herd both into one pocket for a high clear. The line also brings the defining threat of the lineage, a moving segment across the field, onto Jiro's grid.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 3.** The movement order is sound. Boss 0 tests boss 1's old body and boss 1 tests boss 0's new body, one axis at a time, so the bodies never overlap and each still covers only EMPTY. The capture order also works by calling mark_boss_zone twice: fill from boss 0, check boss 1's top-left for BOSS_ZONE, then fill from boss 1 only if it is still EMPTY. Six problems.
(1) Leaving two open regions breaks the one-region invariant that D7's coverage argument and PIN-14 rely on. The former trail becomes a 1-thick CLAIMED wall with field on both sides, so the 4-connected rail component C spans both regions' boundaries. A right-hand minion traces only one of them, R4.7-5 calls it trapped, and core/tests/minion_coverage.rs fails. Minions of one region never reach a player in the other.
(2) The line is tested only on boss-move ticks. At period >= 2, a fast U-cut that crosses the line and closes between two moves is never hit, although the screen shows the line through the trail.
(3) 'Body centre' is undefined for even B.
(4) The mutual body test is an O(1) rectangle intersection, not 4x8 comparisons, so the line walk dominates the cost: about 15 RV32 instructions per cell for at most 59 cells, roughly 1k cycles. That is about 8-12% of the ~11k-cycle average tick at period 1.
(5) R4.6-12's argument still holds, because the other body is identical in both runs up to the first differing move, but M1.5's test must cover the pair. The score ceiling in reports/bot-advantage becomes claimable - B0^2 - B1^2.
(6) The tether is not a grid cell, so the shell's dirty-cell diff needs an overlay. Up to about 120 cell redraws and 60 pushed rows per move add 3-6 ms to the 22 ms worst tick, which is fine at period >= 2.
Of this batch, this idea widens the bot gap the most.

*Fixes.* Restate PIN-14 and R4.7-4 per open region (C = the rail cells on the boundary of the minion's region) and rerun minion_coverage on split boards. Walk the line on every tick while the tether bit is set and the player is cutting, not only on boss moves. Define the centre as (x + B/2, y + B/2), rounded down. Use a rectangle-intersection test for the mutual blocking. Take seed bytes 21..28 as stated, but register the vx/vy bits centrally in §6.4, since byte 4 bits 2-3 are also claimed by the island and bouncer proposals. Keep twin levels out of Challenge until bot-advantage has been measured on them.

*Cost.* State +8 B (a second Boss of 7 B plus the tether bit; Game grows about 12 B, so the start stack grows about 40 B). State hash v2 = 956 B. Level v2 +5-7 B. zk +8-12% on a period-1 twin level: the line walk is about 1k cycles per tick and the second boss's edge tests about 150. Wedgie +3-6 ms per move tick for the tether redraw. RULES_VERSION bump, level v2 and hash v2 vectors, a tether-sweep test in greedy, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* D9; D14 / R4.6-12 (restate for two bodies); D7 / PIN-14 (two open regions); §5 code 35 one-region invariant; R4.3-3; R4.6-1; R4.6-5; R4.8-2; R4.9-1; R4.9-3; R4.4-9; §6.4 seed byte 4 bits 2-3 (shared with other proposals)

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The second boss gets its own start, direction and deflection key from free seed material, and the per-boss divergence of R4.6-12 still applies. M1.5's property test must be restated for two bodies that bounce off each other.

The tether is the problem:

- **Splitting is a bot-only move.** A trail closed against the border is a 4-connected wall, so any 8-connected Bresenham line between the two bodies must cross a TRAIL cell unless an obstacle lies on it. In an open field the split is impossible at boss period 1. At period p, the last cells of the cut must be drawn between two boss moves, exactly while the line passes through the remaining gap. Search can do that; humans cannot. The pitch's 'snap early' choice is therefore open only to bots.
- **Keeping the bosses together is unreadable.** The player must avoid a segment that runs through CLAIMED walls and joins two deflected bodies that also bounce off each other. It is the least readable threat in this batch.
- **D7 breaks after a snap.** Two open regions then persist, which breaks the one-region premise of D7's coverage argument (PIN-14, validity code 35). A rail component that touches both regions is no longer covered by one patrol. D7 is missing from the conflict list.
- **Weights inflate.** Scripted bots would need a full simulator of the tether's sweep. If they clear these levels poorly, the §8.4 weights rise and search collects them, so the gap in pot share grows beyond the gap on the level itself.
- **Ambiguity.** The sketch doesn't say whether the line is walked on every tick or only on boss-move ticks.

*Fixes.* - Ship twin bosses without the tether for Challenge: two seeded bosses, with R4.8-2 filling from both. Or keep the tether Training-only.
- If a tether stays: let CLAIMED cells stop the line, walk it only on boss-move ticks, require a period of at least 3, and forecast both bodies.
- Add D7, PIN-14 and validity code 35 to the conflicts, and restate the coverage proof for two open regions.
- Reserve seed material jointly with island start and land bouncer, which also claim bits 2-3 of byte 4.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** This adds the most new play in the batch. Every level now ends with 'shrink one region'; twins add a fork: split them early, or herd both into one pocket while dodging a moving line. Splitting is a real skill moment. The trail is 4-connected, so the 8-connected tether line can't slip through it: any cut that separates the bosses must close its gap while the tether crosses that gap or crosses claimed ground. The capture at step 3 runs before the boss's line test at step 4, so the final step is safe. That is a tense 'thread the needle' close, and the pitch undersells it. Three costs weigh it down. (1) It widens the bot gap the most of any idea here, by the sketch's own account: two seeded deflecting bouncers move both ends of a line that kills any TRAIL it crosses, which a human can't predict by eye and search simulates exactly. That cuts against brief constraint 7. (2) The tether passes through CLAIMED walls and still kills trail on the far side. That feels arbitrary, and a player will reasonably expect walls to give shelter. (3) It rewrites D9 (one boss), needs state hash v2 and restating R4.6-12, and costs 5-10% more cycles. Almost every level from 05 on runs the boss at period 1, where the line redraw is too heavy for the wedgie. So it can't go on the hard end of the ladder, which is where its variety would matter most.

*Fixes.* 1. Let CLAIMED cells shield the line: test and draw only the EMPTY and TRAIL cells on it, so walls protect you and the threat reads as 'open field only'. 2. Or make the tether hit only the cut head (the player's cell) and the cell entered this tick. The tether then becomes a sweep you dodge, not area denial, which narrows the gap. 3. Restrict twins to boss periods of 2 or more. 4. Ship it with the T0 two-body forecast. 5. Consider removing seeded deflections from the twins and keeping D14 through their seeded start positions, so their mutual bounces stay readable.

*Readability.* The bodies are fine. A Bresenham line in one bright colour is readable at 4x4 px, but it is jagged and up to 60 cells long. Redrawing it on every boss move costs up to about 120 cell writes, which the wedgie only manages at periods of 2 or more. Where the line crosses claimed land it must contrast with the claimed fill, or with the revealed image if T0 adds one.
