# Ideas: controls, time and pacing

The 10 ideas proposed from the axis of the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) *controls, time and pacing*: input schemes (one button, touch, swipe, analog), time limits, slow motion, pacing devices, pause. Sorted by score. The [index](../IDEAS.md) lists every idea by tier and score, and [`README.md`](../README.md#the-taxonomy-and-the-ideas) explains the judges.

## Overtime after the clear

`controls-time-and-pacing#1` · T3 New core rule · score 12 (core 3, fairness 4, play 5) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Reaching the target no longer ends the level at once. A short overtime clock starts, and every further capture adds claim that stays at risk until you bank it with X or the clock runs out. A hit in overtime still ends the level as Cleared, but you lose the unbanked part.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add overtime O (u16 ticks, 0 = the v1 behaviour, O \<= T) and refill E (u8 ticks per overtime capture). State (hash v2, +4 bytes): overtime_left u16 (0 = not in overtime) and banked_cells u16. Step 3: when a capture brings claim_bp >= target and overtime_left = 0, O = 0 clears the level as today (PIN-10). Otherwise set banked_cells = captured cells and overtime_left = O, and the level goes on. A capture during overtime adds its cells to the captured cells (at risk) and sets overtime_left = min(O, overtime_left + E). Step 2: X in overtime ends the level Cleared with the full claim, and an unfinished cut is discarded as in R4.4-7. Step 6: in overtime, a hit that the invulnerability and shield steps do not absorb erases the trail and ends the level Cleared, with claim_bp computed from banked_cells. No life is lost, so the D5 zero never applies once the target has been crossed. Step 7: in overtime, overtime_left -= 1. At 0, or after tick T - 1, the level ends Cleared with the full claim. On the crossing tick a hit gives exactly today's result, because banked = the crossing claim. Outcome: endReason 2 for every overtime end, claimBp as above, and ticksUsed includes the overtime ticks. The levelScore formula is unchanged, so the contracts are untouched. Optional variant: the boss runs at period max(1, P - 1) during overtime. Nothing new is seeded.

**Decisions it reopens:** D5; PIN-10; PIN-09; R4.10-1; R4.10-3; R4.4-8; §7.3 Outcome.claimBp; §8.4; D10; §7.3 Outcome; §7.3 endReason semantics; R4.9-16; Outcome.endReason (§7.3).

**Bot effect.** Narrows. Search already reaches 0.985 of the ceiling (claimable - B^2), and overtime does not raise that ceiling, so search can gain at most about 1.5%. **Variety.** The crossing capture becomes a decision: cross early with a small capture and harvest under pressure, or build one big finisher. - Second picture after the clear: Reaching the target no longer ends the level. The first picture completes and a second one starts to show in new claims. Commit banks at any time, but if you lose your last life after the clear you keep only the target, not your overshoot.
- Overtime after the clear: On overtime levels, reaching the target banks your clear but does not end the level: the boss speeds up and the minions come back. Every further capture raises your claim until you press X. If you run out of lives, you fall back to the banked clear instead of zero.
- Overtime after the clear: Reaching the target no longer ends the level. It banks the clear and opens overtime: keep cutting against an angrier boss to raise your claim and press X to bank whenever you like. Lose your last life in overtime and you keep the banked clear, but the surplus is lost.

Points to pin: (1) whether step 7 decrements overtime_left on the crossing tick itself (with O = 1 the level ends that tick, after the hits); (2) X in overtime returns endReason 2, not 1, so §7.3's endReason text changes. (3) Refill abuse: a 1-cell nibble capture takes about 3 ticks, so with E > 3 a player can keep overtime alive until T - 1 with near-zero risk. That is bounded by T but turns overtime into a safe harvest. Bot effect: plausible narrowing on cleared levels. The two human journals crossed at 8,444 and 8,644 bp, and search can gain at most about 1.5%. It does nothing for D5 zeros. Typical proofs grow by the overtime ticks, so the price per proof rises for runs that use it. The at-risk tint needs a client per-cell flag, not just a palette entry.

*Fixes.* Pin the step-7 decrement on the crossing tick. Give refills a minimum capture size, or drop E and use a fixed O, to stop nibble-farming. Remove 'contracts' from touches and update §7.3's endReason description. Budget 450 B of client bitmap for the at-risk tint.

*Cost.* Level bytes +3 B (475 B v2); state +4 B (state hash v2, 952 B preimage). A few compares per tick; worst-case ticks and zk cycles unchanged, typical runs longer. Client 450 B bitmap and one palette entry. RULES_VERSION, vectors, an overtime policy for the scripted bots, playtest and calibration.

*Conflicts.* D5; PIN-09; PIN-10; R4.10-1; R4.10-2; R4.10-3; R4.4-7; R4.4-8; R4.9-16; §5 level bytes v1; state hash v1; §7.3 Outcome.endReason semantics; D10 (ticks tiebreak)

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: nothing is seeded and nothing becomes a safe zone. Gap: narrows. Search is at 98.5% of a ceiling that overtime doesn't raise, so it gains at most about 1.5%. Humans who cross at 8,400-8,600 bp (the two committed journals, labyrinth in 163 ticks and open field in 333) can harvest toward the ceiling with small captures. Today only a perfect finishing cut gets there, and search's main lever on cleared levels is that one huge finisher (9,580-9,918 bp per level). Calibration amplifies the effect a little: the mix's medians rise on the levels it mostly clears (01-09), their weights fall, and search's points there shrink. It does nothing for the zeros on 13-16, where most of search's surplus comes from. Draw variance falls: the size of a human's finishing cut, which depends on where the boss happens to be, matters less. No new zero risk, since a hit in overtime keeps the banked clear, consistent with D5. Bots can play it easily: keep cutting rectangles, and press X when a minion or the boss's reach gets close. Costs: typical human journals get longer, so cycles and proving cost per verified run rise even though the 2,700-tick worst case holds. Outcome.claimBp semantics, the state hash and the level format change.

*Fixes.* Give the scripted bots an explicit overtime policy and recalibrate. Measure the change in proving cycles on the median human-like run, not only the worst case. Leave the 'angrier boss' variant out of paid seasons until measured, since a faster boss in overtime favours exact timing.

**Play: strong, 5 of 5.** The best fit for Jiro-ikusu's core identity. It takes the Commit push-your-luck decision that D5 creates and adds a second, nested round of it inside every cleared level. The crossing capture becomes a choice (cross early and harvest, or build one big finisher). Each overtime cut risks all unbanked cells, and X becomes a cash-out. The overtime phase is the genre's tensest endgame: a small region with the boss in it, a long rail with minions, and the E refill driving the pace. The outcome now needs a clear banked versus full distinction, and a longer ticksUsed shifts the fewer-ticks tie-break.

*Fixes.* Use the optional boss speed-up (period max(1, P - 1)) on high-tier levels so overtime feels distinct. Make the HUD show banked and at-risk claim as two segments of the claim bar. Give scripted bots a simple banking policy (bank when a minion is within k rail cells or after n overtime captures), and recalibrate. State in README §4.10 how a Cleared-by-hit end differs from a Cleared-by-X end in claimBp.

*Readability.* Good. An overtime countdown on the top border row, plus an at-risk tint (one palette entry) on cells captured in overtime, repainted once on bank. Both are clear at 4x4 px and need only partial redraws.

## Short-clock sprint levels

`controls-time-and-pacing#11` · T1 Level data v1 · score 12 (core 4, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: unclear · D14: compliant

Short levels of 30 to 60 seconds with lower targets, where the countdown really matters. Running out of time keeps your claim but running out of lives zeroes it, so the last seconds become a deliberate gamble.

**Rule sketch.** Level data v1 only: the time limit T has always been a knob (1-2,700), but all 16 levels use 2,700, and neither search (at most 1,448 ticks) nor the human journals (163 and 333 ticks) come close to it. New levels, all v1 bytes: blitz-open (open field, B 5, period 2, no minions, T 600, 3 lives, 6,500 bp target), blitz-sentry (1 minion at period 2, R 90, T 750, 7,000 bp), countdown-comb (comb obstacles, B 5, period 1, 2 minions, T 900, 4 lives, 7,500 bp), last-minute-titan (B 8, period 1, 2 minions, T 900, 4 lives, 7,000 bp). A time-out ends the level at step 7 with the claim kept (R4.10-4), while running out of lives scores 0 (D5). With a life to spare near the end, a risky big cut costs at most a life, which gives each level a pacing arc. A season can swap a few sprint levels into its 16. Needs new level ids, a playtest rerun against targets.json, and recalibrated weights. Presentation: a countdown on the top border row in the last 10 s.

**Inspired by:** Lovely Girls (1998), Cuthbert Goes Walkabout (1983), Urbanix (2010), Clax (1985), Arcade Land Grab: Line Game (2026), Stix (1983), Zolyx (1987), KBounce (2000), Territory Raider (2026), Paparazzi (1996), Glowfield (2026), ZUBGUBBLERS (unknown), Jolly Jogger (1982), Jezzball Jam (2014). **Mechanic families:** [Per-level enemy ramp](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#per-level-enemy-ramp), [Timer-survival fallback clear](../taxonomy/level-structure-and-progression.md#timer-survival-fallback-clear), [Area-percentage clear threshold](../taxonomy/capture-rules.md#area-percentage-clear-threshold), [Level countdown timer](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#level-countdown-timer), [Very short rounds](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#very-short-rounds), [Life budget](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#life-budget). **Touches:** level-data, tools-bots, economy, presentation.

**Bot effect.** Unclear, so measure it. Search pads half the ticks a level can spare beyond 300, so on T = 600 it pads only 150, and its compute per level shrinks with the ticks (the §7.2 deadline follows the ticks used). Humans already clear in 160-340 ticks. It may narrow a little. If the clock makes the human-like bots time out more, it widens instead; the bot-advantage report decides.

**Budget.** No code. Shorter T caps those levels' worst-case ticks, and so their zk cycles and proving cost, by 67-78%. Shorter runs also shorten the commit deadline. Only the countdown digits are redrawn.

**Variety.** The clock becomes a real pressure and a late-level gamble (time-out keeps the claim, lives-out zeroes it), and short levels give a run a mix of rhythms. Today time never binds.

**Merged from:** `level-structure-and-progression#14`, `v1-levels#13`. Variants:

- Short-clock and sudden-death levels: New levels where the clock really matters: 30-60 s sprints in which starting a long cut late is a gamble, and one-life levels where every cut is a commit decision.
- Blitz: 40 seconds, nine lives: Forty seconds on the clock and nine lives: running out of lives is nearly impossible, so a failed cut costs time, not the level. Big risky cuts are cheap to fail, but every failure eats into a very short clock.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 1.** All four levels are valid v1 bytes (T 1-2,700, target 1-10,000, lives >= 1), so the core does not change. Checks:
(1) The clock binds less than the pitch implies. The reference mix's median ticks are 352 (open), 345 (sentry), 481 (comb) and 516 (titan) at today's higher targets, and the human open-field clear took 333 ticks. So T 600-900 with lower targets ends only the tail of the runs on time. A time-out is in effect an automatic Commit (R4.10-4), and the last-life gamble with X is the existing D5 decision. The new pressure is the forced end, which is real but modest at these T values.
(2) The scripted bots ignore the clock. Time-outs keep the claim, so calibration still works, but the mix never models the late gamble.
(3) Search accrues 892,857 units per tick and pads (T - 300) / 2. At T 600 its first search gets about 150 ticks of units, against about 1,200 at T 2,700. Under the real-time compute model, short clocks really do cap search, so the gap probably narrows.
(4) The budget claim is overstated. Proving price is about the same per proof ('a level's or a run's alike', M4.8). A 67-78% cut bounds the worst-case cycles, not the price, and real runs already end at 300-650 ticks.
(5) Countdown: a 4 px border row cannot hold digits, and drawing there hides the top rail that the player and minions walk. The browser HUD already shows ticksLeft.
(6) The tools hard-code SEASON_LEVELS = 16: source names NN 01-16, a 16-entry non-increasing clear_rate_pct, and calibrate expecting 01-16. Extra levels therefore need a per-set directory or a tools change. That is not 'no code'.

*Fixes.* Set T near 1.2-1.5x the mix's median ticks for each layout (for example 450-650) so that the clock actually binds, and confirm with the playtest's Time limit column. Drop the countdown digits on the border row: use the browser HUD's ticksLeft. Add per-set level directories to the level, playtest and calibrate tools before swapping levels into a season's 16, and keep the P11 curve non-increasing across the new slot order.

*Cost.* No core, format or vector change. Per tick: none. zk: caps the worst-case ticks of those levels at 600-900 (worst-case cycles down about 67-78%), but the per-proof price stays about flat. Offline: new level sources, a playtest at 1,000 seeds per level, calibration. Tools: SEASON_LEVELS = 16 generalization.

*Conflicts.* P11 (the 16-slot non-increasing curve must still hold with sprint levels swapped in)

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap unclear; D14 compliant.** The idea uses level data v1 only and leaves the seeded deflections untouched, so D14 holds. At T 600 and boss period 2 there are still about 300 boss moves and about 9 deflections, and two runs part after a median of 12 boss moves. Scripted bots play these levels like any other, and the usual playtest and calibrate path applies. Two effects pull the gap in opposite directions. (a) On one level, search's score over a human's is claim_search / claim_human, because the weight cancels. Search stays near the ceiling (9,580-9,918) whatever the target, while humans and the mix stop just past it. Lowering targets to 6,500-7,000 therefore lowers human claims and the mix median, which raises those slots' weights and widens the ratio. On open it is 9,881 / 7,740 = 1.28 today, against an estimated 9,880 / 6,750 = 1.46 at a 6,500 target. (b) A short T cuts the wall time per level from 180 s to 40-60 s, which shrinks any real-time search budget, including multi-core bots. The end of the clock is also a free roll for humans, since spare lives are worth nothing at a time-out; search does not need it. The mix's median ticks are 350-650 today (playtest-449f231636aa), so T 600-900 will bind on the period-1 sprints (countdown-comb, last-minute-titan). Some clears become below-target time-outs there, which widens the claim spread on those slots, and weights normalize only the median. The bots as written ignore the clock, so they will not model the late gamble humans play. The one-life sudden-death variant adds zero-score variance to whichever runs draw it.

*Fixes.* Keep sprint targets at 7,500 bp or higher, so the clock is the only change and a lower target does not widen claim_search / claim_human. Before calibrating, give bold and committer a time-aware rule: below target, with a spare life, in the last N ticks, go for a big cut. Rerun reports/bot-advantage.md with the sprint set. Ship only if each sprint slot's search / mix-median ratio stays within today's range and its claim IQR is no wider than comparable levels. Keep the one-life variant out of Challenge.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** T is the one level knob all 16 levels leave at its maximum, so a binding clock is new to the game and costs no code (T1). It adds real decisions. The slow shield now costs time as well as speed, and the fast cut gains value. A cut started late may be discarded at the buzzer: R4.10-4 keeps the claim but not the unfinished trail. A spare life loses its value as the clock runs down, so the late big cut becomes a deliberate gamble, while out of lives still scores 0 (D5). That is a pacing arc no current level has. The weakness is the proposed numbers. The human journals clear labyrinth in 163 ticks and open field in 333, so T 600-900 combined with lower targets (6,500-7,500) will rarely bind for a competent player. As specified, the clock may stay a decoration on easy draws. Bot effect is honestly 'measure it': search gets less padding and less compute when T is small, which may narrow the gap.

*Fixes.* Tune T from data, not feel. Set it near the reference mix's median clear tick and keep targets at 7,500 or more, so the clock binds for most players: for example T 300-450 on an open field and 600 on comb or titan variants. Lowering targets and shortening the clock pull against each other. In levels/README.md, report each sprint level's share of runs that end by time-out (end reason 4), and reject a sprint level where that share is near zero. Ship 2-3 sprint levels, not 4, and spread them across the difficulty ramp.

*Readability.* The browser HUD already shows ticksLeft, so it is fine. Digits on the top border row do not fit, because the row is 4 px high, and they would cover rail cells where the player and minions walk.

## Beat-pattern boss

`controls-time-and-pacing#10` · T3 New core rule (proposed as T2) · score 11 (core 3, fairness 4, play 4) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Instead of a steady pace, the boss moves in a rhythm: a burst of moves, then rests, repeating every bar. The rests are windows for a quick cut, and the beat is shown on the boss and heard in the browser.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 replace the boss move period (u8) with a beat: bar length Lb (u8, 1-32) and mask Mb (u32, non-zero, bits >= Lb zero). The minions get the same pair, Lm and Mm. A v1 period p decodes as L = p, mask = 1. Phase phi = (seed byte 4 >> 2) mod L, from the six unused bits of byte 4 (§6.4). R4.4-2 becomes: an entity moves on tick t iff bit ((t + phi) mod L) of its mask is set. Deflection draws still happen only on actual moves (R4.6-11 unchanged), so the boss's path stays seeded. State hash v2 replaces the move-period bytes of the boss and of each minion slot with L (u8) and mask (u32). Example patterns: gallop (6 moves, 10 rests in a 16-tick bar), heartbeat (2 moves, 10 rests), stutter (alternating 1-3 moves). Authors size the rest windows to corridor lengths. Presentation: the boss pulses on each move and dims during rests. The browser plays a click per move and a cue before each rest.

**Inspired by:** Orbis: Bubble Fill Reflex Game (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Rhythm timing](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#rhythm-timing). **Touches:** level-format, level-data, rules, state-hash, presentation, tools-bots.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.4-2; R4.3-3; R4.3-4.

**Bot effect.** Narrows slightly. The timing is already known to search, and the beat makes it perceivable to humans, who are good at rhythm. Space stays seeded, so there are safe moments, not safe zones (D14). The seeded phase keeps tick-timed scripts from carrying over between runs.

**Budget.** Level bytes grow by about 8 bytes and the state preimage by 4-5 bytes per entity. Per tick, a shift and a mask per entity replace a modulo, so a little cheaper. Screen: a pulse on the boss's cells on each move.

**Variety.** Opportunity moves from space to time: size each cut to fit a rest window, and read the bar rather than the bounce. Today every entity moves at one uniform pace for the whole level.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 3.** The mechanic is sound and costs almost nothing per tick. But it is not a clean T2. (1) The seeded phase phi changes the rule text of R4.4-2, R4.7-10 and the §6.4 seed use. A v1 level decoded as L = p, mask = 1 with phi = (byte4 >> 2) mod p moves on (t + phi) % p == 0 instead of t % p == 0. That changes every existing level's game and every committed vector, so it is a behavior change (RULES_VERSION 3), not a compatible decode. (2) v1 periods may be 1-255, but L is capped at 32 by the u32 mask, so the decode 'L = p' is not total. (3) D14 divergence (R4.6-12) is proven for move periods 1-6 with N = 400 boss moves. A sparse mask such as L = 32 with one bit set gives about 84 boss moves per level, the weak regime the README rationale flags (2e-5 probability of the same trajectory already at period 16). Validity must bound the mask density. (4) 'A shift and a mask replace a modulo' is wrong: (t + phi) mod L is still a modulo, followed by a shift and an AND. Negligible on RV32IM. (5) The phase reuses the 6 free bits of byte 4, which other ideas compete for. On the bots: search already knows the exact timing, so the gap is roughly unchanged, perhaps slightly narrower. The scripted mix ignores the rhythm, which calibration absorbs. Presentation is cheap: one redraw of the boss body per rest boundary.

*Fixes.* Apply phi only to v2 level bytes (phi = 0 for v1), or accept and document the bump. Decode a v1 period p > 32 as an error, or keep v1's decoding path separate. Add a validity rule: popcount(M) x 6 >= L for the boss (at least as dense as period 6). Extend the M1.5 divergence property test to masks. Take phi from seed byte 9 rather than byte 4's spare bits. Optionally drop the phase entirely: without it the idea is a true T2 parameterization.

*Cost.* Level bytes 472 -> 480 (v2). State hash preimage 948 -> 964 (v2: +4 for the boss, +4 x 3 minion slots). Per tick: one modulo, shift and AND per entity, about the same as today, so zk cycles change by about 0. Core RAM about +10 B. RULES_VERSION bump, all vectors regenerated, M1.5 test extended, playtest and recalibration.

*Conflicts.* R4.4-2; R4.3-3; R4.3-4; R4.7-10; R4.6-12; §5 level bytes v1; §6.4 seed byte use; state hash v1

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** Space stays seeded: the key from bytes 5-8 is unchanged, and deflection draws still happen only on actual moves, so no two runs share a game. The 6-bit phase uses free seed material correctly; 64 mod L is uniform when L divides 64 and only slightly biased otherwise, which is harmless. The D14 hole is move density. The format allows a u32 mask with one set bit in a 32-tick bar. That gives about 84 boss moves in 2,700 ticks, far under the 400 moves of R4.6-12, so two runs with the same boss start could keep the same trajectory with a probability of about 0.5 %. M1.5's divergence test also covers only periods 1-6. Rests are safe moments, not safe zones: while the boss rests it cannot bump any trail. A 10-tick rest therefore allows 20 fast-cut cells that can close right against the body. Search uses each rest to the exact tick, while humans keep a margin. Search already reaches 0.985 of a ceiling (claimable - B^2) that the beat does not raise, so most of the gain goes to humans, especially with the audio cue: the gap narrows. Scripted bots can play beat levels if greedy and bold compute the boss's reach from the mask (its moves over the next k ticks) rather than from a period. Without that, the mix misjudges rest windows, and the weights misprice beat levels. The cost is really T3, not T2: R4.4-2 changes, so RULES_VERSION must be bumped.

*Fixes.* Add a level-validity rule on mask density so the boss always makes at least 400 moves within T, for example popcount(M) x T / L >= 400 (apply the same rule to minions for consistency), and extend M1.5's divergence test to beat masks. Teach greedy and bold beat-aware reach before calibrating. Make the visual pulse readable on its own for players with sound muted, and show when the next rest starts, not only a cue just before it.

**Play: strong, 4 of 5.** A real new axis: opportunity in time rather than space. New situations: sizing a cut to a rest window, slipping a cut right past a resting boss, and, with minion beats, crossing the rail in a minion's rest. Every one of the 16 levels uses one uniform period, so this is clearly distinct. The seeded phase plus the unchanged deflections keep D14 (safe moments, not safe zones). The claim that it narrows the gap is plausible, because humans read rhythm well. Concerns: the dangerous moment is the start of a burst, and the sketch telegraphs the moves (pulse) rather than the end of a rest. Masks like 'stutter' are hard to read, and beats on the boss and the minions at once overload the player. Long rests next to a big boss make adjacent cuts trivially safe, so rest length becomes a balance knob authors must cap. The real cost is T2 plus T3 (format v2, R4.4-2 rewrite, state hash v2, vectors, recalibration), not T2 alone.

*Fixes.* Telegraph the burst: step the boss's colour up over the last 2-3 rest ticks, using one or two palette slots. Limit authored masks to at most 2 bursts per bar, with bars of 16 ticks or fewer. Introduce beats on the boss first and add minion beats on later levels only. Cap rest length per level relative to the cut lengths the layout allows.

*Readability.* Good. The boss is a 8-32 px square, and its stillness during a rest reads by itself. Dim and bright states redraw only the boss's cells.

## Restless boss: a capture clock that speeds the boss

`controls-time-and-pacing#2` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 4, fairness 3, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

The boss starts calm and steps up its tempo for every K ticks that pass without a capture. Any capture calms it back to the level's tempo. The player chooses between steady small claims and a long setup under a boss that keeps speeding up.

**Rule sketch.** Level bytes v2 add restless interval K (u16, 0 = off) and tempo floor F (u8, 1 \<= F \<= the level's boss period). State hash v2 adds idle u16, the ticks since level start or the last capture. The current boss move period, already a u8 in the state hash, now varies. Step 3: any capture sets idle = 0 and the boss period back to the level's period. Step 7, before the time-limit test: idle += 1. If K > 0, idle % K == 0 and period > F, then period -= 1. Movement stays R4.4-2 with the current period (tick % period == 0, global phase). The deflection draw of R4.6-7 runs on each actual move, as today, so D14 is untouched. Optional variant (from TIXONIX): a lost life also resets idle and the period. Authoring: today's hard levels already run the boss at period 1, so this mostly opens a new family of 'calm then frantic' levels whose bosses start at period 3-4. Presentation: the boss colour warms one palette step per tempo step.

**Inspired by:** Territory Raider (2026), Antix (1985), Xonix (PICO-8) (2024), Mamba (1991), Patchwork Heroes (2010), TIXONIX (unknown). **Mechanic families:** [Time-driven escalation](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#time-driven-escalation), [Per-capture deadline](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#per-capture-deadline), [Clock replenished by play](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#clock-replenished-by-play). **Touches:** rules, state-hash, level-format, level-data, tools-bots, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** R4.4-2; R4.3-3; R4.4-12.

**Bot effect.** Likely widens slightly. It makes levels harder for everyone. Search plans around a known, deterministic schedule and stays near the ceiling, while humans lose more claim and more lives. A partial offset: search pads on the rail to accrue its compute budget before its two-capture finisher, and padding now speeds the boss, so its plan gets costlier. Measure with the bot-advantage report on restless levels before adopting.

**Budget.** \+2 bytes of state, one increment and one compare per tick. A faster boss moves more often, but each move costs at most 2B edge lookups plus one fmix32, which is negligible against captures. Screen: a boss colour change per tempo step.

**Variety.** Tempo becomes a resource the player manages: chain small captures to keep the boss slow, or invest in a big setup while it accelerates. It also adds a new level family that escalates within a level. Today every boss keeps one fixed tempo for the whole level.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 3.** The boss move period is already a hashed u8 in state hash v1, so idle (u16) is the only new state. Movement keeps tick % period with the global phase. A tempo step can therefore make one gap between moves irregular, but never gives two moves in one tick. Each actual move still draws one deflection, so D14 is untouched; a faster boss simply draws more often. The design space is narrow: speed tops out at period 1, which levels 05 to 16 already use, and 01 to 04 run at period 2 or 3. So the mechanic exists only on new levels with a base period of 2 to 4, with at most 3 tempo steps. It cannot retune the current ramp. Level v2 changes the code of the committed level-unknown-version vector, so RULES_VERSION is bumped anyway. State hash v2 regenerates every trace. The gap likely widens, because search plans exactly around the known schedule; measure it with the bot-advantage report before adopting.

*Fixes.* None required. Decide the lost-life reset variant before generating vectors, and limit F and K in validity (F ≥ 1, F ≤ the base period, K ≥ some sensible minimum).

*Cost.* Level bytes +3 B (K u16, F u8). State +2 B (idle), state hash v2 with a 950-byte preimage. Per tick: one increment and one u16 remainder and compare. The boss still moves at most once per tick, so the worst tick is unchanged. zk under 1% (more boss moves, each about 2B edge reads plus one fmix32). RULES_VERSION bump. A new level family to author, playtest and calibrate.

*Conflicts.* R4.4-2; R4.3-3; R4.4-12; §5 level bytes v1; §6.4 state hash v1

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. The tempo schedule is a pure function of the idle counter, and the deflection draw still runs on every actual move keyed by tick, so nothing becomes precomputable. A shorter period means more boss moves, which strengthens divergence. Gap: widens, as the pitch admits. A known, deterministic acceleration is something search plans around while staying near its ceiling, while humans lose claim and lives. Weights are P x 10,000 / max(m, floor), so a lower human median on restless levels raises their weight, and search's near-ceiling claim then earns proportionally more, which widens the pot-share ratio. Any capture resets the clock, so a 1-cell nibble every K ticks keeps the boss calm. That softens the effect for humans too, but it also shows the rule mostly taxes slow play. The optional 'a lost life resets idle' variant rewards taking a deliberate hit when lives are spare, a perverse incentive. Scripted bots can learn 'nibble before idle hits K', so it can be calibrated. It adds no per-seed variance. Requires state hash v2, a level format v2 and a RULES_VERSION bump.

*Fixes.* Show the idle clock explicitly (a bar, not only the boss colour), so humans can read the schedule search computes. Keep K long and F close to the base period on paid levels. Drop the lost-life reset variant. Run bot-advantage on a restless level family and adopt only if the ratio does not rise.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** This makes tempo a resource and reaches the commit loop directly: a sped-up boss makes banking more attractive. It also gives levels a shape over time, where today every boss keeps one fixed pace. The boss's tempo is easy to read. Problems: (1) A degenerate strategy. Any capture resets the tempo, so the best play becomes nibbling a 1-cell capture next to the rail every K ticks. That is cheap and dull, and it is exactly the edge-nibbler pattern. (2) Steps are coarse. Period 4 to 3 to 2 to 1 means jumps of +33%, +50% and +100%, with at most 2 or 3 steps. (3) It needs new levels: 12 of the 16 levels already run the boss at period 1, so only a new calm-then-frantic family can use it. (4) It is mostly extra pressure, a mild echo of the fuse that D4 removed. That clashes a little with the decision to let players stop mid-cut, though it is global and not tied to a single cut. The search bot plans around the known schedule, so the gap widens slightly, as the pitch says.

*Fixes.* Calm the boss only on a capture of at least M cells or some bp threshold, or calm it by one tempo step per capture instead of a full reset, so nibbling stops working. Consider the variant where a lost life also resets the tempo, as a comeback valve. Author at least 3 or 4 restless levels whose base period is 4 or more, so the escalation has room. Check the bot-advantage report on them before adoption.

*Readability.* Very good. The boss is the largest object on screen, and a colour change per tempo step is obvious. It costs 3 or 4 palette entries. A small tempo meter in the HUD would show how close the next step is.

## Shield recharge by a long hold

`controls-time-and-pacing#4` · T3 New core rule · score 10 (core 4, fairness 4, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Once your single shield charge is spent, you can earn it back by holding B on the rail for six seconds. You crawl at half speed with minions closing in while the boss roams and the clock runs.

**Rule sketch.** No new state and no format change: the rule uses press_tick and shield charges, both already in state hash v1. Step 6, after the tick's hits are resolved: if B is held, the player is not cutting, shield charges = 0 and tick - press_tick == C, then shield charges = 1. C = 90 ticks (6 s) is a core constant in consts.rs, mirrored in README §4. press_tick only changes when B goes from released to held, so the equality fires at most once per press, and only after a continuous hold. The cap stays at 1 charge. While B is held the player moves only on every second tick (R4.5-7), and a minion contact with 0 charges costs a life as usual, so the six seconds are spent exposed. Optional v2 level field for C, where 0 = off. Presentation: a charge ring around the player cell, which the client computes from tick - press_tick.

**Inspired by:** Volfied (1989), Mokoko X (2022), Pokémon Ranger: Shadows of Almia (2008), Demolition Herby (1982). **Mechanic families:** [Draining border shield](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#draining-border-shield), [Charged capture](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#charged-capture). **Touches:** rules, tools-bots, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** D6; R4.9-15.

**Bot effect.** Narrows slightly. Humans spend the shield far more often than search, which rarely needs it, so a recoverable shield mostly helps humans clear (the D5 zeros are a large part of the 2.03x gap), while search, near the ceiling, gains little. Scripted bots need a recharge routine: recharge when the charge is spent and no minion is within d rail cells.

**Budget.** None: one compare per tick, no RAM, no change to the state hash or to the zk cycles to speak of. Screen: a small ring of dirty cells around the player while charging.

**Variety.** Adds a tempo-for-safety trade on the rail: spend six seconds and some exposure to rebuild the shield before a risky finisher, or press on unprotected. Today the shield is a once-per-level item with no further decision once it is spent.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 3.** press_tick changes only on a released-to-held edge (Player::input). So 'B held and tick − press_tick == C' fires at most once per press, and only after a continuous hold. shield and press_tick are already in state hash v1, so no format changes. The flaw: R4.9-15 spends a charge only while B is held, so every spend happens during a hold. The 90-tick window counts from the press, not from the spend. Any hit absorbed in the first 6 s of a hold is therefore refunded at press_tick + 90 if the player keeps holding, with no 'earning it back' after the spend. The shield becomes nearly free for a player who walks with B held. Also, 'not cutting' is tested only on the tick C, so the hold need not stay on the rail: a slow cut under B that closes before tick C still recharges. The rule revises D6, one charge per level. A consts.rs change syncs README §2, §4 and §5. The gap plausibly narrows: D5 zeros make up a large part of the human gap, and search rarely needs the shield.

*Fixes.* Restart the window when a charge is spent: when R4.9-15 spends a charge, set press_tick = tick, so press_tick means 'start of the current hold'. This shifts R4.5-7's half-speed parity after an absorbed hit, which the same RULES_VERSION bump covers. Optionally also require a rail cell on tick C. Amend D6. Give bold and committer a recharge routine before calibration.

*Cost.* State: 0 B, state hash v1 kept. Per tick: one subtraction and compare at step 6. zk about 0. RULES_VERSION bump: every game vector regenerated, plus new ones (refund during a hold, release and re-press, cutting on tick C). C = 90 in consts.rs, mirrored in README §2, §4 and §5. Playtest and calibration rerun.

*Conflicts.* D6; R4.9-15; R4.5-7

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** D14 holds. It uses press_tick and shield charges, both deterministic and already in state hash v1, and needs no seed. The equality test fires once per continuous hold. Gap: narrows slightly. Search almost never needs the shield, while humans do, and D5 zeros drive much of the 2.03x gap. A renewable shield turns some human out-of-lives zeros into scores, at a real cost: 90 ticks at half speed on the rail, exposed to minions. R4.9-15 absorbs a hit only while B is held, so the extra protection still costs slow cutting, and the player cannot abuse it to cut fast while protected. Scripted bots can recharge with a simple rule, so it can be calibrated, and it adds no per-seed variance. The pitch's 'no format change' is right, but it is still a behavior change: vectors that hold B for 90 or more ticks after the charge is spent will diverge, so RULES_VERSION must be bumped, vectors regenerated and the playtest and calibration rerun. It reverses D6 ('one charge per level'), an owner decision.

*Fixes.* State the RULES_VERSION bump and recalibration explicitly. Get the owner to approve the D6 change. Add a recharge routine to the reference mix (bold, committer) so weights reflect humans who use it. Keep the cap at 1 charge, and keep C long enough that a recharge is a real decision.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** It turns D6's single, precious charge into a cooldown ability, and the help lands in the wrong place. On minion-light levels (open, sentry and heavy, with 0 or 1 minion at period 3), a half-speed crawl easily outpaces or avoids the minion, so recharging is nearly free. An absorbed hit leaves the trail intact (R4.9-15), so a renewable charge makes 'slow cut with B held, take one free boss bump, close, recharge on the rail' a safe loop on exactly the levels that need no help. On the hard levels (3 minions at period 1) the 6 s exposed hold is mostly suicide, so it helps least where help would matter. As arcade design, a 6-second hold-the-button ritual before each risky cut adds downtime, not tension. The new decision ('rebuild or press on') is real but thin. The cost is attractive: no new state and no format change.

*Fixes.* Earn the charge back by playing, not waiting: refill it on a capture of at least N cells, or on stranding a minion with a capture (the bounty idea). If a hold is kept, require standing still with no direction, scale C with minion count, and allow it at most once or twice per level. Show progress in the HUD shield pip.

*Readability.* A charge ring around a 4x4 px player cell would overdraw the neighbouring rail and field cells and any minion there, which is confusing at this scale. A HUD pip filling in 3 or 4 stages, or the player cell blinking on completion, reads better.

## Sketch a cut, release to draw it

`controls-time-and-pacing#7` · T0 Presentation · score 10 (core 4, fairness 4, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

With a mouse or a finger, drag a path from your marker across the field. A preview shows the region it would capture and the claim it would give. Release, and your marker draws it at full speed until it closes or you take over.

**Rule sketch.** Browser only, no consensus change. A press on or near the player starts a sketch. The client snaps the drag to a 4-connected path of EMPTY cells from the player's rail cell to a CLAIMED cell. Preview: the core's capture procedure (R4.8) runs on a scratch copy of the grid with the path marked TRAIL, showing the cells and the claim_bp it would claim, the ticks needed at 2 cells per tick, and, with the forecast, the first forecast boss move that would cross the planned trail. On release, the client writes into the input mask, tick by tick, the direction of the next path step with A held, exactly as the keyboard would. It stops when the cut closes, when the next cell is no longer EMPTY, or at any key, touch or joystick input, which hands control back with the trail in place. Tapping a rail cell auto-walks there along the shortest rail path, found by a client BFS over rail cells. The journal holds ordinary masks.

**Inspired by:** Fezball (2014), SPLITRON (2019), Jezz Mania: Better Jezzball (2021), Snip It! (2024), Jintori Fighters!! (2026). **Mechanic families:** [Aim preview, build on release](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#aim-preview-build-on-release), [Drag and point-to-move](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#drag-and-point-to-move), [Swipe-defined walls](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#swipe-defined-walls). **Touches:** presentation, input.

**Bot effect.** Narrows strongly. It separates the plan from the execution: humans choose the geometry and get frame-perfect fast cuts, so the remaining gap is plan quality and timing, which the forecast also helps. A caveat, said plainly: the official client becomes an execution aid. The line it keeps is that it never chooses or proposes a path. Anyone can already write such a client, so shipping it levels the field rather than tilting it.

**Budget.** Browser only. Per drag update, one path search and one capture fill on a scratch grid (3.6 KB grid + 6.7 KB stack) in wasm, which is negligible. Zero zk cycles.

**Variety.** A geometry-first way to play on touch and mouse, close to puzzle planning, with the claim known before committing to a cut. It makes the large finisher cuts reachable for human hands.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 4 of 5, tier 0.** Client only. The masks enter the journal like keystrokes, so there is no consensus, vector or zk cost. The driver as stated has bugs. (a) 'The direction with A held' overshoots turns. A fast cut takes 2 sub-steps in one resolved direction (R4.5-6), so whenever the next two path cells are not in a straight line, the second sub-step leaves the path. A should be held only on ticks whose next two cells go straight. (b) 'Stops when the next cell is no longer EMPTY' misses the boss. Its body covers EMPTY cells, and stepping into it is a hit (R4.9-3). The driver must stop before body cells, checking both sub-steps on a fast tick. (c) Auto-walk on the rail walks into minions; it needs the same stop. (d) The preview is an estimate. Until the cut closes, the boss can cross the remaining gap, so the side that gets captured can flip. (e) It depends on 'the forecast', a separate and larger execution aid (simulating the boss on the client), which should be judged on its own. The gap narrows as claimed. The official client becomes a partial bot, which §7.1 already admits anyone can write. That is a product stance the owner should sign off on.

*Fixes.* Hold A only on ticks whose next two path cells are in a straight line. Stop before boss-body cells and minion cells. Label the preview 'if the boss stays on this side'. Drop the forecast dependency, or tie it explicitly to the forecast idea. Keep the hand-back on any key, touch or joystick input.

*Cost.* Browser only. Per drag update: a path snap and one capture fill on a scratch grid (3.6 KB grid plus 6.7 KB stack), about 0.1 ms in wasm. A rail BFS over at most 3,600 cells. On winding paths, up to one journal event per tick, within the 2,700 cap. zk 0, no vectors.

**Fairness: concerns, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** There is no consensus change and the journal holds ordinary masks, so D14 and the referee are untouched. Every browser Challenge player gets it, since Challenge is browser-only, and anyone could ship such a client anyway. The gap narrows strongly: humans keep the planning and lose the execution penalty. The preview with the boss forecast is effectively a one-rollout simulator of the core under the planned inputs, a slice of what search does, so it is a product-defining assistance policy for paid play that the owner should decide on. Calibration drifts: weights and targets come from the bold/committer mix as a stand-in for humans. If sketch-assisted humans play closer to greedy, the mix stops representing paid players and the P11 floor and targets go stale. One defect in the sketch: the auto-play stops only when the next cell is not EMPTY, but the boss body covers EMPTY cells and stepping onto it is a hit (R4.9-3), including on a fast cut's second sub-step. The client would walk the player into the boss. The 'forecast' is referenced but defined in another idea.

*Fixes.* Also stop auto-play when the next sub-step's cell is under the boss body, or when the forecast puts the boss there. Add a sketch-like policy (planned cut, exact execution) to the playtest mix, or reweight the reference mix, before calibrating seasons in which the client ships. Get the owner's decision on assisted execution and the forecast in paid mode, and specify the forecast here rather than by reference. Keep the rule that the client never proposes a path.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** It adds no new in-game situation. The boss, minions and rail are unchanged, and only the interface changes. It replaces the arcade execution layer with planning: drag a path, read the exact claim, then let the client drive frame-perfect fast cuts. With the forecast it goes further. The client knows the level seed, so it can simulate exactly whether a planned cut survives the boss, which hands humans the search bot's main tool. That narrows the gap, as the pitch says, but by turning Challenge in the browser into a deterministic puzzle with perfect foresight. That clashes with the game's arcade feel and with what D14's seeded deflections are meant to keep unknowable to the eye. Once it ships in the official client, sketching becomes the only competitive way to play, and keyboard skill stops mattering. The useful parts are touch accessibility, which the mobile browser needs anyway, and tapping a rail cell to walk there.

*Fixes.* Keep tap-to-walk on the rail and simple swipe or drag steering for touch, where the player still times every step. Make the claim preview and boss forecast Training-only teaching aids, or leave them out. If auto-execution of a sketched cut is kept, limit it to normal speed, so a fast cut still needs the player's input.

*Readability.* Browser-only, where an overlay of the path preview on the canvas is clear.

## Touch steering with a turn buffer

`controls-time-and-pacing#8` · T0 Presentation · score 10 (core 4, fairness 4, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

On a phone, swipe anywhere to set a direction that keeps going, and hold a second finger for the fast cut. Turns you press a moment early are held and taken on the first cell where they fit, so corners and gaps never need frame-perfect timing.

**Rule sketch.** Client only. Swipe anywhere (at least 12 px): the client keeps that direction bit set every tick until the next swipe or a tap, which stops (latched movement). A second finger held anywhere sets A. On-screen B and X buttons, where X needs a 0.5 s hold so a stray tap never commits. An optional 4-way on-screen pad. Turn buffer, for all platforms: a direction pressed while its target cell is not enterable (neither a rail cell nor EMPTY) is queued for up to 8 ticks. Each tick the client checks the queued direction against its grid shadow and, once the cell is enterable, writes the new direction alone into the mask. Under R4.5-1 the core would otherwise keep the old direction while both are held. The journal holds ordinary masks, so nothing changes in the core or the vectors.

**Inspired by:** Xonix32 (1997), The Filling (2013), Fill It! Arcade (2026), Xonix (Atari 8-bit) (2024), Photo Slice (2026), 国盗り Returns with 閃乱カグラ NewWave (2014). **Mechanic families:** [Swipe steering](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#swipe-steering), [Continuous (latched) movement](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#continuous-latched-movement), [Turn assist](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#turn-assist), [On-screen virtual pad](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#on-screen-virtual-pad), [Multiple input schemes](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#multiple-input-schemes). **Touches:** input, presentation.

**Bot effect.** Narrows slightly. It removes input-timing errors (missed one-cell gaps, late turns) that cost humans and never cost bots. Bots write masks directly and are unaffected.

**Budget.** Client JavaScript only. Zero core and zk cost.

**Variety.** Opens the browser Challenge to phones, and makes threading corridors on maze levels a question of reading the board rather than reflexes. It adds no rule decisions.

**Core engineer: feasible, 4 of 5, tier 0.** I found nothing that blocks it. The journal holds ordinary masks, and the touch latch and second-finger A are pure input synthesis. R4.5-1 is described correctly: if old and new are both held, the old one wins, so writing the new direction alone is the right lever. Two weak points. (1) The sketch's test for 'enterable' (a rail cell or EMPTY) is not the core's rule. Off the rail, any CLAIMED cell can be entered (R4.5-3). While cutting, CLAIMED closes the cut and TRAIL never opens. A cell under the boss body is EMPTY in the grid but is a hit (R4.9-3). Writing the rule again in JS duplicates R4.5-2 to R4.5-5, which the 'rules live once' principle forbids. And a buffer that fires up to 8 ticks after the press can walk the player into a boss body that has moved in since. (2) The buffer cannot fix a gap passed during the second fast-cut sub-step, because the mask applies per tick. In practice it rarely engages during a cut, since almost every cell is enterable there. Fairness: input aids are client-side and verification never sees them, so they are fine in Challenge. Bot effect is as stated: it only narrows.

*Fixes.* Don't re-encode enterability in the client. Export a pure, read-only core query, can_step(state, dir) -> moved|blocked|hit, from wasm. It needs no RULES_VERSION bump, because it is an API and not a rule. Treat 'hit' (a boss body cell) as not enterable. Optionally, don't let a buffered turn start a cut later than 2-3 ticks after the press.

*Cost.* Zero core rules, vectors, formats or zk cycles. Browser: a few hundred lines of touch and buffer JS.

**Fairness: ok, 4 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** The idea only changes the client. Journals still hold ordinary masks, so D14, the core, the vectors, the calibration weights and the proving cost are untouched. Bots write masks directly and gain nothing. The idea removes an error source that only humans have: late turns and missed one-cell gaps. That error costs more on corridor levels (09-cross, 10-maze, 11-labyrinth) than on open fields. With the buffer, weights calibrated on error-free bots fit humans better, so draw variance falls slightly. The pitch reads R4.5-1 correctly: when both the old and the new direction are held, the core keeps the old one, so the client must write the new direction alone. The sketch has two fairness gaps for paid runs. (a) 'Enterable = rail cell or EMPTY' includes the EMPTY cells under the boss body. A turn queued up to 8 ticks earlier can therefore step into the boss automatically, which is a hit under R4.9-3: the client, not the player, loses a life in a paid run. (b) The 0.5 s hold on X delays a phone player's commit by about 7-8 ticks compared with a keyboard player. That delay lands exactly when Commit matters, on the last life (D5), so phone players are disadvantaged in Challenge.

*Fixes.* Treat the cells under the boss body, and the player's own trail, as not enterable in the buffer. Drop a queued turn on any new input and after a hit. Replace the 0.5 s X hold with a guard that adds no delay at the moment of commit, for example a separate arm step followed by a tap, or a swipe on a dedicated commit zone; failing that, keep the hold at 0.2 s or less. Ship the buffer as a setting that is on by default on every platform, so keyboard and touch players get the same assist.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** Adds no new decisions or situations. It improves input feel and lets phones play, which is worth doing as a cheap client-only (T0) change, but it scores low on variety, the criterion being judged. A turn buffer is a familiar arcade comfort and fits the rail-cornering feel. On maze levels it moves the difficulty from reflexes to reading the board, as the pitch says. The sketch has two risks that could harm play. (1) A turn queued while cutting, blocked by the player's own TRAIL, can fire several ticks later once the cell is EMPTY: an unintended turn in the field. (2) The grid shadow shows EMPTY under the boss body, so a buffered turn can step the player into the boss, which is a hit (R4.9-3). Latched swipes still allow the D4 mid-cut stop, through a tap. Holding A with a second finger while B also needs a hold makes awkward three-touch chords on a phone.

*Fixes.* Buffer only while walking the rail and not cutting. Treat the boss body's cells as not enterable. Cancel the queue on any other direction press. Shorten it to about 4-6 ticks. Give B a held on-screen zone like A. Offer hold-to-move (finger down = direction held, lift = stop) as an alternative to latched swipes.

*Readability.* No change to the grid. On a phone in portrait, the on-screen buttons must sit below the square field, never over it.

## Focus meter: enemies at half tempo

`controls-time-and-pacing#13` · T4 Input or journal · score 8 (core 2, fairness 3, play 3) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Hold Y to make the boss and the minions move at half speed while a focus meter drains. Claiming land refills it, so you save it for the finisher or for the escape.

**Rule sketch.** Input mask bit 7 becomes Y (Focus): journal v2, where bit 7 is no longer required to be 0. Level bytes v2 add the focus maximum Fm (u16 ticks, 0 = off) and refill r (u8 meter ticks per 100 captured cells). State hash v2 adds focus u16, which starts at Fm. Steps 4 and 5: when Y is held and focus > 0, an entity of period N whose rule says it moves on tick t moves only if (t / N) is even, so at half tempo. A skipped boss move draws no deflection (R4.6-11). Respawn countdowns are unchanged, and a minion that does not move has a = b in the hit predicate. Step 7: if Y is held and focus > 0, focus -= 1. Step 3: a capture of c cells sets focus = min(Fm, focus + c x r / 100). Presentation: a meter overlaid on the top border row.

**Inspired by:** Filler 2 (2009), Glowfield (2026), VoidCutter (2026), Dancing Eyes (1996), Star Trigon (2002). **Mechanic families:** [Player-triggered time dilation](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#player-triggered-time-dilation), [Clock replenished by play](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#clock-replenished-by-play). **Touches:** input, journal, rules, state-hash, level-format, tools-bots, presentation.

**Decisions it reopens:** D8; R4.4-2; R4.4-9; R4.4-10.

**Bot effect.** Roughly keeps the gap, perhaps narrows. Search will spend focus with perfect timing on its finisher, but it already sits at 0.985 of a ceiling (claimable - B^2) that focus does not raise. Humans gain real reaction time, because enemies move half as often per wall-clock second while the player keeps full speed, which mostly helps them clear. The risk is that focus makes finishers so safe that the human-like bots' clear rates rise unevenly across levels, which recalibration must absorb.

**Budget.** \+2 bytes of state, two compares per tick, and journal format v2 (bit 7). Fewer enemy moves while focused slightly lower the cycles. Screen: a meter of a few border cells.

**Variety.** A resource spent at the critical moment and earned back by claiming, so when to focus, and whether a capture pays for itself in focus, become decisions. Today the player has no lever on enemy timing.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 4.** It is deterministic and cheap, but as written it widens the bot gap, contrary to the pitch. Focus is drained per tick held and acts per tick, at steps 4 and 5. A journal can therefore hold Y on exactly the one tick where a move would be fatal: the boss bumping TRAIL at step 4 of tick t with (t / N) odd. That skips that single move for 1 focus, and the cut closes at step 3 of the next tick. With Fm in the hundreds, search gets hundreds of frame-perfect single-move dodges. Pulsing Y only on move ticks also halves the cost of half tempo. Humans can't key Y in phase with 15 Hz tick parity. 'Search sits near a ceiling focus doesn't raise' misses the point: the issue is the reliability of clears and finishers, which focus makes nearly free for search. Other points. Under D8, Y was left out to avoid free thinking time. This does not pause, so the conflict is a decision revisit, not a contradiction. Overlaying the meter on the top border row draws over rail cells that the player and minions walk, which hurts legibility on 4 px cells. The scripted reference mix (bold, committer) must get a focus policy before calibration, or the weights measure a game that humans don't play. The remaining formulas check out. c x r / 100 fits u32 (3,364 x 255). A skipped boss move draws nothing, consistent with R4.6-11.

*Fixes.* Make focus a committed window instead of a held key. A rising edge of Y with focus >= W starts a W-tick window (for example W = 30, 2 s), debited at once, that cannot be cancelled. During the window an entity skips every other scheduled move counted from the window's start, not (t / N) parity. That removes both single-tick dodges and phase pulsing. Show the meter as a colour or blink on the player, or a strip outside the rail, not over border row 0. Add a focus policy to tools/src/bots before calibrating.

*Cost.* Level bytes +3 (475, v2). State preimage +2 (950, v2). Journal v2: bit 7 allowed, and decode rule 10 changes. Per tick: 2-3 compares. zk cycles slightly lower while focused. Core RAM +2-5 B. RULES_VERSION bump, vectors regenerated, a new bot policy, recalibration.

*Conflicts.* D8; R4.4-2; R4.4-9; R4.4-10; R4.7-10; §6.1 bit 7; §6.3 decode rule 10; state hash v1; level bytes v1

**Fairness: concerns, 3 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** Focus acts in game ticks, not wall time. The 15 Hz clock and the commit deadline do not change, so D8's actual worry, free thinking time, does not arise. The real conflict with D8 is the use of Y and mask bit 7, which needs an owner decision and journal v2. Space stays seeded and a skipped move draws nothing, so D14 holds. However, focus removes boss moves. A boss of period 5 or 6 held at half tempo for long stretches can fall under the 400 moves of R4.6-12, and Fm (u16) with a refill of up to 255 per 100 cells would let a level grant near-permanent focus. Search spends focus optimally, but it cannot exceed the ceiling it already reaches at 0.985, so the gain lands mostly on humans and the gap narrows slightly. The main fairness risk is calibration. The reference mix (bold, committer) has no focus policy, so its median would not show how humans use focus. The effect is largest on the fast, minion-heavy levels (titan, swarm, doom), which carry the highest weights, and mispricing exactly those levels raises draw variance.

*Fixes.* Bound focus per level so the boss keeps at least 400 moves: a validity rule tying Fm, r and the boss period together. Before calibrating, give bold and committer a focus behaviour, for example hold Y while a cut is within the boss's reach and save it for the finishing cut, then compare human and mix results level by level on the high-weight levels. Draw the meter outside the playfield in the browser.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** A genuine resource decision with new situations (a slowed boss during the finisher, an escape from minions), and nothing in the 16 levels resembles it. However, the decision likely collapses to 'focus on the closing cut near the boss', and refilling on captures snowballs: big captures pay for the next big capture. It compresses the difficulty ladder, since period-1 bosses become period 2 on demand, so the hard levels drift toward the middle ones and calibration must absorb that unevenly. It revisits D8, the owner's decision. It gives reaction time, not a wall-clock pause, but Y plus B held together slows the whole game: a bounded slow motion that edges toward the thinking time D8 rejected. It adds a third held modifier (A, B, Y). The meter on the top border row covers rail cells where the player and minions walk. Cost is T4: journal v2, format v2, state hash v2.

*Fixes.* Make Y and B mutually exclusive (Y ignored while B is held) to avoid slowing the whole game. Use a fixed per-level charge, or refill only from captures above a size threshold, to curb snowballing. Show focus by tinting the enemies while it is active. Draw the meter on the corner cells, or on the border with entities drawn over it.

*Readability.* A meter a few cells long is legible, but on the top border row it hides the rail unless entities are drawn over it. An enemy tint while focused reads well at 4 px.

## Clear-speed bonus

`controls-time-and-pacing#12` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts · score 7 (core 2, fairness 2, play 3) · gap: widens · D14: compliant

A cleared level pays a bonus for the time left on its clock. Every finisher becomes a choice: take the clear now with a smaller claim and a bigger bonus, or spend seconds setting up a bigger capture.

**Rule sketch.** Season parameter timeBonusBp (uint16, 0-5,000; 0 = today). On a Cleared level the core computes level_score = claim_bp x weight_bp / 10,000 + (T - ticks_used) x weight_bp x timeBonusBp / (T x 10,000), each term rounded down, where T is the level's time limit. Committed and timed-out levels get no bonus, and out of lives still scores 0. The value lands in Outcome.levelScore, and the struct is unchanged. IReferee.verify and verifyRun take timeBonusBp beside weightsBp. ZkReferee's public values add it (abi.encode(uint16 weightBp, uint16 timeBonusBp, Outcome)). Season stores it as a committed parameter, and calibration rescales the weights so that a median run still scores about P points per level. D10's fewer-ticks tie-break becomes mostly redundant but stays. Presentation: the HUD shows the bonus draining.

**Inspired by:** IceBreaker (2000), Time Runner (1982), Kix (BBC Micro) (1986), Rumba Lumber (1984), Gridder (1983), Dancing Eyes (1996), Q\*BALL (2025). **Mechanic families:** [Clear-speed bonus](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#clear-speed-bonus), [Box value drain](../taxonomy/scoring-and-risk.md#box-value-drain), [Level countdown timer](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#level-countdown-timer). **Touches:** level-format, rules, outcome, economy, tools-bots, presentation, contracts.

**Decisions it reopens:** §8.4 level_score; D10; §7.2 run score bound; P11; P4; PIN-08.

**Bot effect.** May narrow, but it raises the ceiling, so measure before adopting. Under §7.2 the deadline follows the ticks used, so a bot that clears fast also has less wall time to search. Search today uses 1,300-1,450 ticks, padding to accrue compute, where humans clear in 160-340, so the bonus taxes exactly that padding. Honestly, a bot with enough hardware can play fast and still search well, and the new points raise everyone's ceiling, so against a well-resourced bot it could widen.

**Budget.** One multiply-divide per level and no per-tick cost. Contracts: one Season parameter, the IReferee signature and the zk public values (+2 bytes per level). Economy and calibration documents change (README §8.4, docs/ECONOMY.md).

**Variety.** Speed against size at every finisher, and Commit against Clear gains a new trade-off. Today speed only breaks ties.

**Merged from:** `scoring-and-risk#7`. Variants:

- Clear-speed bonus: Clearing early pays a bonus that drains every tick, shown as a bar. A fast clear can then beat a slow perfect cage, and every second spent waiting for an opening has a visible price.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 2 of 5, tier 5.** The formula overflows as written. (T - ticks_used) x weight_bp x timeBonusBp reaches 2,699 x 65,535 x 5,000, about 8.8e11, against u32's 4.29e9. It needs u64, which is soft on RV32 but only once per level, plus a pinned evaluation order, since each term is rounded down. The §7.2 bound changes: a level can reach 65,535 + 32,755, so a run reaches about 589,700 (uint32 still holds it), and the README's 393,210 must be updated. As a season parameter, it changes IReferee.verify and verifyRun (P4), ZkReferee's public values (+2 B per level), Season and Challenge: a full contract generation. Bot effect: today humans clear in 163 to 333 ticks and search pads to 1,300 to 1,450, so against the current search policy the bonus favours humans. But it raises the ceiling, and a bot with more hardware can play fast and still search well, so it likely widens over time. The idea says so honestly. Calibration must fold the bonus into the medians.

*Fixes.* Compute in u64 and pin the order, for example bonus = (T - ticks_used) x timeBonusBp / T, then x weight_bp / 10,000, each step rounded down. Better still, make timeBonusBp a rules constant in consts.rs under a new RULES_VERSION: the core computes levelScore and contracts only sum it, so IReferee, ZkReferee and Season stay unchanged (T3). Update the §7.2 bound. Measure with search re-tuned for fast clears before adopting.

*Cost.* One u64 multiply-divide per level; no per-tick cost; zk ~0. As stated: IReferee signature, ZkReferee public values +2 B per level, Season parameter and Challenge pass-through, so a new contract generation and a new guest image. README §7.2, §7.3 and §8.4 and docs/ECONOMY.md change; recalibration. As a rules constant: core and calibration only.

*Conflicts.* P4 (IReferee signature); §7.2 run score bound 393,210; §7.3 zk public values; §8.4 level_score; §8.4 season parameters; D10; P11; PIN-08

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap widens; D14 compliant.** D14 holds: nothing is seeded. But the bonus pays for the thing search is best at. After the reveal, search knows the boss's entire seeded trajectory for any grid it plans, so it can find the earliest big cut the boss will never touch. Humans have to guess, and rushing on hard levels costs them D5 zeros. Taxing padding does cut the compute a single-core search accrues under §7.2, since the deadline follows ticks used. But a bot with more cores, or one that already finds strong cuts within a few hundred ticks, loses little. Humans already clear fast at times (labyrinth in 163 ticks), but not reliably. The bonus also raises the ceiling for everyone, and search will approach the new ceiling while humans don't, which widens the gap in absolute points. Draw variance grows: fast clears are realistic on 01-04 and rare on 13-16, so the bonus's spread varies by level, and a two-term score is harder to normalize with one weight per level. Calibration is still possible with a 'clear early' bot variant. Contracts: changes to the IReferee signature, the zk public values and the Season parameters. Its own pitch says to measure before adopting, and that it could widen.

*Fixes.* Don't adopt it for Challenge without a search-bot measurement at full compute and with parallel cores. If kept, cap the bonus small (timeBonusBp at most about 500), or award it only below a level-specific tick threshold that a skilled human reaches. Better, show clear speed as a non-scoring badge (T0) and leave the score untouched.

**Play: ok, 3 of 5.** Adds tempo, which the game lacks today. All 16 levels have T = 2700 and the clock almost never binds, so waiting mid-cut (D4) or on the rail is free. A draining bonus makes every pause for an opening a visible cost and gives the finisher a speed-against-size trade-off. Its point about §7.2 is sharp: a bot that must clear fast accrues less search compute, so the bonus taxes search's padding (1,300-1,450 ticks against humans' 160-340). It may narrow the gap in practice, though, as it admits, a well-resourced bot can play fast and also bank the raised ceiling. For humans the decision mostly collapses to 'play faster', which is closer to difficulty than to new situations. It creates nothing new on the board, applies uniformly per season and partly undercuts D4's deliberate patience. It also touches the IReferee signature and zk public values, a heavy T5 cost for a pressure layer.

*Fixes.* First try the cheap T1 version: levels with short T limits (the knob is unused, at 2700 everywhere) to create time-pressure levels and measure whether tempo adds variety. If the bonus is still wanted, scale it modestly (timeBonusBp of about 1,000-2,000) so a fast clear can't outscore a near-ceiling claim, and measure search's gap with the bonus on before any season.

*Readability.* Good. A draining bar on a border row is one palette entry with a cheap partial redraw. The board is unchanged.

## Run-wide slow-motion allowance

`controls-time-and-pacing#14` · T5 Mode, scoring or contracts · score 7 (core 3, fairness 2, play 2) · gap: narrows · D14: compliant

Each Challenge run gets a fixed budget of wall-clock seconds. While you hold Focus, the game runs at half speed and draws on that budget, shown on screen, so you choose where in the six levels to buy thinking time.

**Rule sketch.** No core change: ticks, journals, vectors and the state hash are untouched, and Focus is not journaled. A new season parameter, focusAllowance (uint64 seconds, 0-300, 0 = today). The on-time rule of §7.2 becomes committedAt \<= revealedAt + (sum ticksUsed + 14) / 15 + commitSlack + focusAllowance, and commitRun's bound becomes revealedAt + 1,080 + commitSlack + focusAllowance. Client: while the Focus key is held, the browser runs the tick loop at 7.5 Hz (or pauses), counts the wall time beyond 15 Hz pace, and debits it from the allowance shown on screen. It refuses to spend into the last 30 s, which stay reserved for transitions and the commit transaction. Training gets the same key with no limit.

**Inspired by:** Glowfield (2026), Stix (1983), Gotcha (1993), 似非クイックス (2003). **Mechanic families:** [Player-triggered time dilation](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#player-triggered-time-dilation), [Pause](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#pause), [Adjustable game speed](../taxonomy/controls-time-and-pacing.md#adjustable-game-speed). **Touches:** contracts, economy, presentation, input.

**Decisions it reopens:** D8; P6.

**Bot effect.** Narrows. Wall-clock thinking time is a human bottleneck, not a bot one: search needs at most 749 ms per game second and is already near the ceiling in real time. Honest caveat: like commitSlack today, the allowance is unverifiable wall time, so a modified client can spend it practising a level whose seed it already knows (§7.2, 'Why the actual duration'), bounded by the allowance. Fresh entropy per level (§7.4) would remove that.

**Budget.** Contracts only: one addition in Challenge's deadline check and one Season parameter. Docs: README §7.2 and §8.4 and docs/ECONOMY.md's threat table. Zero core or zk cost.

**Variety.** Thinking time becomes a budget the player allocates across the six drawn levels: spend it on the hard draw's finisher, or save it. It directly revisits the paid-mode reason for D8 by making that time bounded and equal for everyone.

**Core engineer: feasible with changes, 3 of 5, tier 5.** It works, but the contract change is unnecessary. Onchain, focusAllowance is indistinguishable from commitSlack: both are unverifiable seconds added to the same deadline sum and to commitRun's bound. commitSlack already has a 1-3,600 s range, so 120 + 300 fits without a new Season parameter, a new Season generation or a Challenge redeploy. The client can show 'commitSlack minus a reserved margin' as the slow-motion budget. Trust model: §7.2 'Real-time play' says the deadline 'stops slow-motion play'. This deliberately gives some back, which needs an explicit decision revisiting D8 and P6. The honest caveat understates the threat. A modified client does more than practise a known seed: within the allowance it can save the state and rewind mistakes on the level being played, which is far more valuable than half-speed play. So the bounded allowance mainly widens the modified-client vs honest-human gap, about 3.5x today's 120 s residual at 300 s. Search barely benefits (it runs in real time already), so the bot vs honest-human gap does narrow. The grace bound 'reveal window + 1,080 + commitSlack' grows by the allowance too. An honest client that misjudges wall time gets a late run that earns nothing, so the 30 s reserve is needed.

*Fixes.* Drop focusAllowance and the contract edits. Raise commitSlack in the season file (for example 120 -> 420) and have the client reserve 120 s of it, treating the rest as the slow-motion budget. Record the decision: amend §7.2 'Real-time play' and D8. Add rewinds within commitSlack to docs/ECONOMY.md's threat table. Check grace >= reveal window + 1,080 + commitSlack.

*Cost.* With the fix: a season-file value, README §7.2 and §8.4, docs/ECONOMY.md, and client JS (a 7.5 Hz loop and a wall-clock debit). As stated: one Season parameter (a uint64 in the open() params, a new Season generation) and one addition in Challenge's deadline and commit bound. Zero core, zk or format cost either way.

*Conflicts.* D8; P6; §7.2 Real-time play

**Fairness: concerns, 2 of 5; gap narrows; D14 compliant.** There is no core change, so D14, the vectors and the calibration are untouched. Honest humans get bounded thinking time that search does not need, so the gap between search and honest humans narrows. But the allowance is wall time that nobody can verify, which is exactly what the §7.2 deadline exists to remove. Human clears take 11-22 s (playtest-human-1: 163 and 333 ticks). A 300 s allowance therefore lets a modified browser client, which is trivial to make, replay a level whose seed it already knows about 13 to 27 more times and commit the best journal; with commitSlack added, the count is higher still. The same client can practise all six drawn levels before playing them for real. The pitch says fresh entropy per level would remove this, but that only stops practice on later levels, not best-of-N retries of the level being played. The idea turns the accepted 120 s residual into a 420 s one. It opens a large gap between honest and modified clients, which is the fairness between humans that matters. The allowance is equal on paper but not in practice.

*Fixes.* Keep focusAllowance at 0 in Challenge and offer slow motion in Training only. If it ships in Challenge, do it only after fresh entropy per level with a commit per level, and with a small cap (30-60 s at most). Record in docs/ECONOMY.md's threat table that the allowance is a best-of-N budget for modified clients.

**Play: weak, 2 of 5.** Mostly a wall-clock pause budget. No in-game decision changes: ticks, rules and the board are identical. The only new choice is a meta allocation across six levels, and it will mostly collapse to 'spend it all on the hardest finisher'. It dissolves the core tension, real-time timing against a seeded boss, which is the reason D8 gives for keeping Y unused in paid modes. It also widens a cheating surface it admits itself: a modified client can spend the allowance practising a level whose seed is known. That is a contracts and economy change (§7.2 deadline, Season parameter, threat table) for something that adds no variety. The Training half, an unlimited pause, is harmless and useful.

*Fixes.* Ship only a Training pause on Y, as a client-only (T0) change. Drop the Challenge allowance, or defer it until fresh entropy per level (§7.4) removes the practice exploit. If it is ever pursued, use slow motion only (7.5 Hz, never a pause), so time still pressures the player.

*Readability.* A seconds counter in the browser HUD only.
